Quick Answer

Does an MOT Check Wheel Alignment?

No. An MOT test does not include a computerised wheel alignment or tracking check. The tester does not measure toe, camber or caster angles using specialist alignment equipment. Those measurements require dedicated wheel alignment machinery and fall outside the scope of the MOT inspection.

However, poor wheel alignment can cause problems that are inspected during an MOT. Uneven tyre wear, damaged suspension components, steering defects and unsafe vehicle handling may all result in advisories or MOT failures depending on their severity.

Quick Answer

✔ The MOT does inspect tyres, steering and suspension.

Mechanic's Answer

Why Wheel Alignment Still Matters Before an MOT

Many drivers believe an MOT includes a full tracking check, but this is one of the most common misconceptions. In reality, MOT testers inspect the results of poor wheel alignment rather than the alignment measurements themselves. If incorrect tracking has caused rapid tyre wear, damaged suspension components or steering problems, the vehicle may fail even though no alignment measurements have been taken.

For example, a tyre worn through to the cords on its inner edge because of incorrect toe settings is likely to fail the MOT due to its condition, not because the wheel alignment itself has been measured. Likewise, damaged steering or suspension components discovered during the inspection may lead to an MOT failure if they affect the vehicle's safety.

Mechanic Tip

If your steering wheel sits off-centre, the vehicle pulls to one side, or you notice uneven tyre wear, have the wheel alignment checked before booking your MOT. Correcting the problem early is usually far cheaper than replacing tyres damaged by poor alignment.

MOT vs Wheel Alignment

Wheel Alignment vs MOT Inspection

An MOT inspection and a wheel alignment service are not the same thing. The MOT checks roadworthiness, tyre condition, steering and suspension safety. A wheel alignment service measures and adjusts the vehicle's steering geometry using specialist equipment.

Quick Summary

An MOT does not measure wheel alignment angles. It checks whether poor alignment has already caused unsafe tyre wear, steering play, suspension defects or other roadworthiness problems.

Toe Angle

MOT: Not measured

Wheel alignment: measured and adjusted using alignment equipment.

Camber Angle

MOT: Not measured

Wheel alignment: measured where applicable, depending on vehicle and equipment.

Caster Angle

MOT: Not measured

Wheel alignment: measured on many four-wheel alignment systems.

Tyre Wear

MOT: Checked

Wheel alignment: checked because uneven tyre wear can point to tracking or suspension issues.

Steering Play

MOT: Checked

Wheel alignment: steering components should be inspected before adjustment.

Suspension Condition

MOT: Checked

Wheel alignment: worn parts must be repaired before accurate alignment.

Alignment Printout

MOT: Not provided

Wheel alignment: often provides a before-and-after geometry report.

Adjustment Work

MOT: No adjustment

Wheel alignment: adjusts tracking or geometry settings where possible.

MOT Failure Risk

Only if defects exist

Bad alignment itself is not measured, but tyre, steering or suspension defects can fail.

Mechanic's Tip

If your car pulls to one side, has uneven tyre wear or the steering wheel is off-centre, inspect tyres, steering and suspension before paying for alignment. Alignment only makes sense once the mechanical parts are sound.

Alignment vs Tracking

Wheel Alignment Is Not the Same as an MOT Check

A proper wheel alignment check uses specialist equipment to measure wheel angles. The garage looks at settings such as toe, camber and sometimes caster depending on the equipment and vehicle.

An MOT is different. It is not designed to fine-tune the way the car drives. It checks whether important parts are safe and roadworthy on the day of the test.

Alignment

What alignment measures

Wheel alignment measures steering geometry and adjusts tracking where possible to reduce tyre wear and improve vehicle stability.

Tracking

What drivers usually mean by tracking

When most UK drivers say “tracking”, they usually mean front wheel toe adjustment. Poor tracking can make the steering wheel sit off-centre, make the car pull, or scrub the tyre edges.

Important distinction

If tyres are already badly worn because of tracking, the MOT issue becomes the tyre condition rather than the tracking measurement itself.

Real experience

What I See in Real Garage Work

A lot of drivers only notice tracking problems when the MOT is due. They look at the tyre from the outside and think it is fine, but when the steering is turned or the car is lifted, the inside edge is badly worn.

In many cases, the tracking is only part of the story. A worn lower arm bush, loose track rod end, bent suspension arm or damaged tyre can be the real reason the car is wearing tyres unevenly.

That is why I would not just say “get tracking done” without checking the front suspension and steering first. If there is play in the parts, alignment may not hold properly.

Mechanic tip

Do not fit new tyres before checking the cause. Inspect steering and suspension first, repair worn parts, then align the wheels, then fit or protect the tyres depending on condition.

Used Car Buying

Buying a Used Car With Wheel Alignment Problems

Poor wheel alignment is often a symptom rather than the root cause of the problem. While a simple tracking adjustment may solve minor steering issues, persistent pulling, uneven tyre wear or an off-centre steering wheel can indicate worn suspension components, accident damage or steering faults that require more extensive repairs. A careful inspection before buying can save hundreds of pounds in unexpected repair costs.

Many buyers focus only on tyre condition, but tyres often tell the story of the suspension and steering underneath. A vehicle with rapid inner-edge tyre wear, repeated MOT advisories or evidence of recent kerb impacts deserves a much closer inspection before money changes hands.

Buying Advice

Never assume uneven tyre wear simply means the tracking needs adjusting. Always inspect the tyres, steering and suspension together, check the vehicle's MOT history for repeated advisories and ask whether any suspension or steering repairs have recently been carried out.

FAQs

Does MOT Check Wheel Alignment? Frequently Asked Questions

Clear answers to the questions UK drivers most commonly ask about MOT tests, wheel alignment, tracking, tyre wear and steering-related MOT failures.

Does an MOT check wheel alignment?

No. An MOT does not perform a computerised wheel alignment or tracking measurement. Instead, it checks the condition of tyres, steering, suspension and other safety-related components that may be affected by poor alignment.

Can bad tracking fail an MOT?

Not directly. However, poor tracking can cause excessive tyre wear, exposed cords, steering defects or suspension problems that may result in an MOT failure.

Can uneven tyre wear fail an MOT?

Yes. A tyre can fail if tread depth is below the legal limit, cords are exposed, or the tyre has dangerous damage such as cuts or bulges.

Can a car pass an MOT with the steering wheel off-centre?

Possibly, but the underlying cause should always be investigated. An off-centre steering wheel often indicates wheel alignment, steering or suspension problems that may require repair.

Should I get wheel alignment done before an MOT?

Yes, if the vehicle pulls to one side, the steering wheel is not centred or the tyres are wearing unevenly. Any worn steering or suspension components should be repaired before alignment is carried out.

Will an MOT tester tell me if my tracking is out?

The tester may identify uneven tyre wear or steering and suspension defects, but they do not perform a full wheel alignment report or provide alignment measurements.

Is wheel balancing checked during an MOT?

No. Wheel balancing is not part of the MOT inspection, although damaged wheels, unsafe tyres and related defects may still be identified during the test.

What is the best thing to check before an MOT?

Inspect tyre condition, tread depth, tyre pressures, steering feel, suspension noises and any signs of uneven tyre wear. Fixing problems early often prevents MOT advisories and expensive repairs later.

Final Mechanic's Advice

Think of poor wheel alignment as an early warning rather than the fault itself. The alignment may not be measured during an MOT, but the tyre wear, steering defects and suspension problems it creates are carefully inspected. Dealing with these issues early usually improves handling, extends tyre life and reduces the risk of MOT advisories or failures.
