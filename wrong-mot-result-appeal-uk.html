Quick Answer

Can You Appeal a Wrong MOT Result in the UK?

Yes. In England, Scotland and Wales, you can appeal to the Driver and Vehicle Standards Agency (DVSA) if your vehicle failed its MOT and you believe it should have passed, or if it passed when you believe it should have failed. You should first discuss the result with the MOT test centre, avoid repairs that could affect the evidence and submit the official VT17 appeal form within the relevant deadline.

Wrong MOT Failure

Appeal within 14 working days of the test. DVSA may arrange a full MOT retest and charge the applicable appeal fee.

Wrong MOT Pass

Appeal within 28 calendar days for non-corrosion defects, or within three months for corrosion-related problems. DVSA reinspection is free for this appeal type.

Official DVSA Form

Use form VT17, available through GOV.UK. Submit it to DVSA, explaining why you believe the original MOT result was incorrect.

Mechanic-style advice: preserve the original condition

The strongest MOT appeal is usually one where the vehicle remains in substantially the same condition as it was during the disputed test. Photograph the relevant area safely, keep the original test result and record what the tester said. Do not dismantle or repair components before discussing the appeal with DVSA, unless urgent safety needs make intervention unavoidable.

Understanding MOT Appeals

What Types of MOT Results Can You Appeal?

In England, Scotland and Wales, the Driver and Vehicle Standards Agency (DVSA) handles formal appeals against MOT test results for cars, motorcycles and vans. You can challenge a failure you believe was incorrect or complain about a pass when you believe the vehicle should have failed.

These are two different situations. A disputed failure involves a vehicle that the MOT tester considered below the required standard. A disputed pass involves a vehicle that received an MOT certificate despite a suspected testable defect.

1. Incorrect MOT Failure

Your vehicle failed its MOT, but you believe the tester incorrectly assessed a component, applied the wrong rejection standard or recorded a defect that was not present.

Appeal within 14 working days

2. Incorrect MOT Pass

Your vehicle passed its MOT, but you believe the tester missed a defect that should have resulted in failure under the MOT inspection standards.

Different deadlines apply

3. MOT Record Error

The MOT record contains incorrect mileage, vehicle details, an expiry-date problem or a missing test. These issues normally follow a separate record-correction procedure rather than a VT17 appeal.

Use the correction route

Important distinction

The VT17 appeal process is designed to challenge the MOT test decision itself. It is not the normal route for correcting a mistyped mileage reading, an incorrect vehicle detail or a missing online record. Choosing the correct process avoids unnecessary delays.

Disputed MOT Failure

How to Appeal an MOT Failure You Believe Is Wrong

An MOT failure means the tester identified one or more major or dangerous defects, or another reason requiring refusal of an MOT certificate.

If you believe the decision was incorrect, you can challenge it through DVSA. However, disagreeing with the result does not automatically mean the tester made a mistake. The disputed item must be assessed against the applicable MOT inspection standard.

Examples of MOT Failures That May Be Disputed

Brake Component Assessment

A brake disc, pad, pipe or other component was failed, but you believe its condition did not meet the relevant failure criteria at the time of testing.

Suspension or Steering

A tester recorded excessive movement, wear or deterioration, but you believe the assessment was inconsistent with the MOT inspection manual.

Lighting or Visibility

A lamp, windscreen or visibility defect was recorded, but you believe the applicable MOT rejection criteria were not satisfied.

What to Do After a Disputed Failure

1. Read the Failure

Check the exact defect wording and whether the tester recorded a major or dangerous problem.

2. Speak to the Garage

Ask the MOT test centre to explain why the component failed and which inspection standard applies.

3. Preserve Evidence

Keep the original result, photographs and relevant supporting information. Avoid repairs or adjustments.

4. Submit VT17

If you still disagree, submit the official appeal form to DVSA within 14 working days.

What Happens if DVSA Rechecks a Failed Vehicle?

DVSA may arrange for a vehicle examiner to carry out a full MOT test. You must pay the applicable appeal fee before the examination. The examiner assesses the vehicle in its current condition, explains the result and provides a report showing any defects and advisory items.

The original MOT result remains in the vehicle's MOT history. DVSA adds the result of its retest to that history. If the vehicle passes the appeal retest, the appeal fee is refunded.

Do not arrange repairs before the appeal is considered

Replacing the disputed component, adjusting a mechanism or repairing the vehicle before DVSA examines it may make it impossible to assess the original complaint properly. If the vehicle has a dangerous defect, do not drive it. Discuss safe transport and evidence preservation with DVSA.

Disputed MOT Pass

How to Appeal an MOT Pass When the Vehicle Should Have Failed

A vehicle may pass its MOT even though a driver later discovers a fault. This does not automatically prove that the original MOT was incorrect. The key question is whether the relevant defect existed at the time of the test and should have been identified under the MOT inspection rules.

If you believe the tester missed a qualifying defect, you can submit a complaint through the DVSA VT17 appeal process.

Examples of Concerns After an MOT Pass

Brake Safety Defects

Significant brake deterioration, an unsafe brake component or another testable defect may raise concerns if evidence suggests it was present during the original test.

Steering and Suspension

Excessive play, serious wear or unsafe component condition may justify investigation when supported by evidence relating to the test date.

Structural Corrosion

Serious corrosion affecting a prescribed area or safety component may justify an appeal against a pass. A longer complaint period applies.

Why the Date of the Original MOT Matters

Mechanical defects can develop or worsen after a test. Tyres, brakes, suspension components and other parts may change condition through use, impact or deterioration. DVSA therefore considers whether the evidence can establish the vehicle's condition at the original MOT examination.

Corrosion generally develops over a longer period, which is why the official complaint deadline for corrosion-related pass concerns is different.

What DVSA Does After a Disputed Pass

Vehicle Reinspection

If DVSA decides a reinspection is appropriate, a vehicle examiner will assess the relevant condition and provide a report showing defects and advisory items.

No New MOT Result

For an appeal against a pass, DVSA does not issue a new MOT test result or update the vehicle's MOT history following its reinspection.

DVSA reinspection fee

You do not pay an additional MOT test fee for a DVSA reinspection following an appeal against a passing result.

A recent MOT pass is not a safety guarantee

If a serious defect is discovered after an MOT pass, do not continue driving simply because the certificate remains valid. Roadworthiness is a separate legal requirement. Arrange a safe inspection and preserve relevant evidence.

Official DVSA Deadlines

MOT Appeal Deadlines UK: 14 Working Days, 28 Days or 3 Months?

The deadline depends on whether you are challenging a failed MOT or complaining about a vehicle that passed.

The official VT17 appeal form must reach DVSA within the relevant time limit. Missing the deadline can prevent the complaint from being considered through the normal appeal procedure.

DVSA MOT Appeal Deadline Comparison

Appeal type Time limit How the deadline works
Appeal against an MOT failure 14 working days Submit the VT17 form within 14 working days of the test.
Appeal against an MOT pass — mechanical or other defects 28 calendar days Submit the appeal within 28 calendar days of the original test.
Appeal against an MOT pass — corrosion-related defects 3 months Submit the complaint within three months of the original MOT.
Incorrect MOT mileage or other record information Separate correction rules Follow the appropriate MOT record correction procedure instead.

Working Days vs Calendar Days

14 Working Days

The deadline for appealing an MOT failure is measured in working days rather than ordinary calendar days. Do not assume that 14 working days means exactly two weeks.

28 Calendar Days

The deadline for challenging an MOT pass involving non-corrosion defects is 28 calendar days. Weekends are included in this period.

Why You Should Appeal as Soon as Possible

Even when the formal deadline has not expired, waiting can make an appeal more difficult. The vehicle may accumulate mileage, components may deteriorate further and repairs may change the condition DVSA needs to inspect.

If you disagree with the MOT result, discuss the issue promptly with the original test centre and submit the appeal well before the deadline.

Do not wait until the final day

The deadline applies to submission of the appeal, not simply to the date you begin preparing it. Download the VT17 form, complete the required information and send it to DVSA promptly.

Official source: GOV.UK — Appeal an MOT Test Result (VT17) .

Before Submitting VT17

What Should You Do Before Appealing an MOT Result?

Before submitting an appeal, establish exactly what you disagree with and preserve the information DVSA may need to investigate the original test decision.

You should discuss the disputed result with the MOT test centre first. The tester may be able to explain the inspection standard, defect classification or reason for the result.

Five Steps to Take Before an MOT Appeal

1. Check the MOT Record

Review the recorded test date, result, defect wording and advisory information.

2. Identify the Disputed Item

Record the exact component or defect you believe was incorrectly assessed.

3. Contact the Test Centre

Ask for an explanation of the test decision and the relevant MOT inspection rule.

4. Preserve the Vehicle

Avoid repairs, adjustments or component replacement that could affect the appeal evidence.

5. Prepare Supporting Evidence

Keep the MOT result, photographs, written explanations and any relevant records. Note when the evidence was obtained.

6. Submit Within the Deadline

Complete the official VT17 form and email it to DVSA within the applicable appeal period.

What to say to the MOT test centre

Explain that you disagree with the recorded MOT result and would like to understand the exact inspection requirement used to assess the disputed component. Ask for the explanation before authorising any repairs.

Vehicle safety takes priority over evidence

Do not drive a vehicle with a dangerous defect merely to preserve its condition for an appeal. Contact DVSA for guidance about the inspection process and arrange recovery if necessary.

Official DVSA Appeal Process

How to Appeal a Wrong MOT Result Using Form VT17

If you believe your vehicle received an incorrect MOT pass or failure, you can challenge the result through the Driver and Vehicle Standards Agency (DVSA). The official process uses form VT17, which is available from GOV.UK.

Before submitting the appeal, discuss the disputed result with the original MOT test centre. Ask the tester to explain the relevant inspection standard and why the vehicle passed or failed.

If you still disagree, complete the VT17 form and send it to DVSA within the applicable appeal deadline.

Step-by-Step MOT Appeal Process

1. Download VT17

Visit the official GOV.UK appeal page and download the VT17 form for cars, motorcycles and vans.

3. Email DVSA

Save the completed form and email it to the official DVSA complaints address before the appeal deadline.

4. Await DVSA Contact

A DVSA vehicle examiner should contact you within five working days to discuss the appeal and possible reinspection.

VT17 Form Guidance

How to Complete the DVSA VT17 MOT Appeal Form

Form VT17 allows DVSA to identify the vehicle, the MOT test being challenged and the reason you believe the result was incorrect.

Complete the form accurately and explain the disputed test decision clearly. Avoid general statements such as "the garage was wrong" without identifying the relevant defect or inspection issue.

How to Explain the Disputed MOT Result

Describe the original MOT decision and explain why you believe it was incorrect. Identify the specific component, defect wording or inspection requirement involved.

For example, if a suspension component failed because of excessive movement, explain which component was assessed, what the MOT result recorded and why you believe the condition did not meet the failure standard.

If you are challenging an MOT pass, explain the defect you believe the tester missed and why you believe it was present at the time of the original test.

Useful Appeal Explanation

Identify the test date, disputed defect, relevant component and reason for disagreement. Keep the explanation factual and specific.

Clear and evidence-based

Weak Appeal Explanation

Simply stating that the tester was unfair, the repair was expensive or another garage disagreed may not establish an incorrect MOT assessment.

DVSA Vehicle Examination

What Happens During a DVSA MOT Appeal Reinspection?

After receiving your VT17 appeal, DVSA will review the complaint and contact you to discuss the disputed test result.

If a reinspection is appropriate, DVSA will arrange a location, date and time for a vehicle examiner to assess the vehicle.

The examination procedure depends on whether you are challenging a failed MOT or a passing result.

What the DVSA Examiner May Check

Original Defect

The examiner considers the component or condition involved in the original disputed MOT result.

Current Vehicle Condition

The vehicle's condition at reinspection is relevant, particularly if parts have deteriorated or been repaired since the original test.

MOT Inspection Standards

The examiner applies the relevant MOT inspection requirements when assessing the vehicle.

Changes to the vehicle can affect the appeal

Repairs, adjustments, replacement components or further deterioration may make it difficult to establish whether the original MOT decision was correct. Discuss any necessary changes with DVSA before the examination where possible.

MOT Appeal Fees

How Much Does It Cost to Appeal an MOT Result in the UK?

The cost of an MOT appeal depends on whether you are challenging a failure or complaining about a vehicle that passed.

For an appeal against a failed MOT, DVSA charges the applicable full MOT test fee before the vehicle examination.

For a complaint against a passing result, DVSA does not charge a reinspection fee.

Are Other Costs Possible?

Although the DVSA appeal fee is fixed by the relevant MOT test class, you may face other expenses depending on your circumstances.

These could include vehicle recovery, transport to the examination location, independent inspection costs or repairs needed to make the vehicle safe.

These expenses are separate from the DVSA appeal fee and should not be assumed to be recoverable through the appeal process.

Check the latest official MOT fees

The maximum MOT fee for a standard car is £54.85 under the published DVSA fee schedule. Fees for other vehicle classes can differ.

DVSA does not award garage compensation

An MOT appeal is not a compensation claim against the original test centre. DVSA cannot obtain a refund of the garage's original MOT charge or compensation for repairs and other losses. Separate consumer or legal action may be necessary.

Appeal Results

What Happens if Your MOT Appeal Is Successful or Unsuccessful?

The outcome depends on the original MOT result and the findings of the DVSA vehicle examiner.

An appeal against a failure involves a full MOT retest. A complaint against a pass involves a reinspection and a report rather than a new MOT result.

Possible Outcomes of a DVSA MOT Appeal

Vehicle Passes DVSA Retest

If you appealed a failed MOT and the vehicle passes the DVSA examination, the appeal fee is refunded and the new result is recorded.

Appeal fee refunded

Vehicle Fails DVSA Retest

If the vehicle fails the DVSA examination, the examiner records the new failure and provides a report of defects and advisory items.

Repairs may be required

Disputed Pass Reinspection

DVSA provides an inspection report explaining the defects and advisory items found. No new MOT test result is issued.

Report, not new MOT

Does a Successful Appeal Mean the Garage Must Pay You?

No. A successful DVSA appeal does not automatically create an entitlement to compensation from the original MOT centre.

If you believe the garage caused a financial loss, you may need to pursue the matter separately through consumer advice, Trading Standards or legal proceedings.

Keep the DVSA inspection report

The DVSA examiner's report may be useful when discussing the result with the garage or seeking independent advice about any further action.

Official MOT Records

Does an MOT Appeal Remove or Change the Original MOT History?

Appealing an MOT result does not automatically remove the original test from the vehicle's official MOT history.

DVSA treats appeals against failures differently from complaints against passes. Understanding this distinction is particularly important when checking a vehicle's history before buying.

What Happens to MOT History After an Appeal?

Situation Original MOT record New record
Failed MOT appealed and DVSA retest passes Original failure remains in MOT history DVSA pass result is added
Failed MOT appealed and DVSA retest fails Original failure remains in MOT history DVSA failure result is added
Passed MOT challenged and DVSA reinspects Original pass remains in MOT history No new MOT result is added
Incorrect mileage or administrative detail Handled through the relevant correction process Depends on the type of accepted correction

Why Does the Original Failed MOT Stay Visible?

DVSA records the result of the original test and the subsequent appeal retest as separate examinations.

This means a vehicle can show an original failure followed by a DVSA pass, even when the appeal fee was refunded.

When reviewing a used car's MOT history, consider the dates and sequence of results rather than assuming every recorded failure represents an unresolved defect.

Can You Remove a Failed MOT From the Online Record?

The official MOT record correction service cannot be used simply to remove a failure, defect or advisory because the vehicle owner disagrees with the assessment.

If the test decision itself is disputed, the appropriate route is the formal DVSA appeal procedure.

For genuine administrative mistakes, use the separate official MOT record correction process.

Do not confuse an MOT appeal with a record correction

A DVSA appeal investigates the MOT test decision. A record correction deals with qualifying mistakes in the recorded information. Neither process should be used to conceal a genuine vehicle defect or test result.

MOT Appeal Evidence

What Evidence Should You Keep When Appealing an MOT Result?

Evidence can help DVSA understand why you believe an MOT result was incorrect. The most useful information identifies the original test, the disputed defect and the vehicle's condition at the time of the examination.

However, photographs or a second garage's opinion do not automatically prove that the original tester made an error. DVSA considers the circumstances and decides whether a reinspection is appropriate.

How to Photograph a Disputed MOT Defect

Show the Component

Take an overall photograph identifying the relevant component and its location on the vehicle.

Show the Concern

Where safe and practical, take a closer photograph of the wear, corrosion, damage or condition being disputed.

Keep Original Files

Preserve the original photographs and record when they were taken. Avoid misleading edits or altered evidence.

Do not take risks to obtain photographs

Never crawl beneath a vehicle supported only by a jack, touch hot or moving components, or dismantle safety-critical parts to obtain appeal evidence. Use a suitably equipped professional where necessary.

Can a Second Garage Prove the MOT Was Wrong?

An independent mechanic may identify information relevant to the appeal, but another opinion does not automatically overturn the original MOT decision.

The vehicle may have changed condition between inspections. A useful report should explain the findings, inspection date, vehicle mileage and relevant MOT standard.

Before arranging an inspection that involves dismantling, adjustment or repair, discuss the potential effect on your appeal with DVSA.

Best evidence is relevant and traceable

Keep a clear timeline showing when the MOT took place, when the defect was identified, when photographs were taken and whether the vehicle was used or repaired between those events.

Preserving the Vehicle

Can You Repair Your Car Before Appealing an MOT Result?

DVSA advises drivers not to have repairs carried out until the appeal process has finished. Repairs and adjustments can change the condition of the vehicle and affect the outcome of an appeal.

This is particularly important when challenging a failed MOT. If the disputed component is replaced, DVSA may no longer be able to assess the condition that led to the original failure.

How Repairs Can Affect Your Appeal

Action after the MOT Potential effect Recommended approach
Replace the disputed component Removes the original component from examination Speak to DVSA before authorising replacement
Adjust a failed component Changes the condition assessed during the test Avoid adjustments until the appeal is considered
Carry out unrelated repairs May or may not affect the disputed assessment Discuss planned work with DVSA first
Continue using the vehicle Additional wear or damage may change its condition Minimise unnecessary use and follow driving rules
Repair an immediate safety hazard May change or remove important appeal evidence Prioritise safety, avoid driving and seek DVSA guidance promptly

What if the Car Is Unsafe and Needs Urgent Repairs?

Preserving evidence does not mean you should drive an unsafe vehicle. If a dangerous defect is present, do not use the vehicle on the road.

Arrange recovery or suitable transport and contact DVSA promptly to explain the circumstances. If repairs are unavoidable for safety, document the condition before work begins where it is safe to do so.

Keep photographs, inspection findings, invoices and any removed components where practical and appropriate. Understand that repairs may still affect DVSA's ability to assess the original result.

Never drive a dangerous vehicle to preserve appeal evidence

An MOT appeal does not remove your responsibility to keep the vehicle roadworthy. If a defect makes the vehicle dangerous, arrange safe transport rather than driving it.

Driving Legality & Safety

Can You Drive a Car While Appealing a Failed MOT?

Submitting an MOT appeal does not suspend the original test result or automatically make the vehicle legal to drive.

Whether you can drive depends on the vehicle's current MOT validity, the defects recorded and its actual roadworthiness.

Driving After a Failed MOT: Decision Table

Vehicle situation Can you drive? Important condition

Failed MOT, existing MOT still valid, no dangerous defect Potentially, if roadworthy The vehicle must still meet minimum legal roadworthiness standards
Failed MOT and previous MOT expired Ordinary driving is not permitted Only applicable legal exemptions may allow a qualifying journey; the vehicle must remain roadworthy
MOT appeal submitted but not decided No automatic exemption Follow the same MOT validity and safety rules
Valid MOT pass but a dangerous fault is discovered Do not drive while unsafe A valid certificate does not make a dangerous vehicle roadworthy

Does a Previous MOT Certificate Remain Valid After a Failure?

An earlier MOT certificate may still be within its validity period when a vehicle fails an early renewal test.

GOV.UK explains that a vehicle can be taken away after a failed MOT if the existing MOT remains valid and no dangerous problems were recorded.

However, the vehicle must still meet the minimum standards of roadworthiness. A remaining MOT certificate does not excuse unsafe brakes, tyres, steering or other defects.

Can You Drive to a DVSA Appeal Reinspection?

Do not assume that an appeal appointment automatically permits you to drive a vehicle without a valid MOT or with unsafe defects.

If the vehicle has no valid MOT, the law provides limited exemptions for certain qualifying journeys, such as travelling to a pre-arranged MOT test or to have defects repaired.

Confirm the arrangements and applicable legal position with DVSA before travelling. If the vehicle is dangerous, arrange recovery instead.

Dangerous defects carry serious penalties

GOV.UK warns that driving a vehicle that has failed its MOT because of a dangerous problem can lead to a fine of up to £2,500, a driving ban and three penalty points.

Official driving guidance: GOV.UK — MOT Test Result and Driving Rules .

Appeal or Record Correction?

Should You Appeal an MOT Result or Correct the MOT Record?

An MOT appeal and an MOT record correction are different procedures.

A formal appeal challenges whether the tester made the correct decision about the vehicle's condition.

A record correction deals with qualifying errors in the information held on the MOT database.

Can You Remove an MOT Failure or Advisory Through a Correction Request?

No. The official MOT record correction service cannot be used to change or remove MOT results, defects or advisory problems simply because you disagree with them.

If you believe the test result itself was wrong, follow the official DVSA appeal process.

Garage Complaints & Consumer Rights

Can You Complain About an MOT Garage or Claim Compensation?

If you believe an MOT garage made an incorrect test decision, the DVSA appeal process provides a way to challenge that result.

However, an MOT appeal is not the same as a claim for compensation, a refund of the original garage charge or reimbursement for repairs.

DVSA cannot obtain compensation from the MOT centre or vehicle seller on your behalf.

What if the MOT Appeal Is Successful?

A successful appeal against a failed MOT can result in a refund of the DVSA appeal test fee.

This does not automatically entitle you to recover the original MOT charge, repair costs, lost wages or other expenses.

If you want to pursue financial compensation, obtain independent consumer or legal advice.

Can DVSA Tell You if the Garage Was Disciplined?

DVSA may investigate concerns about MOT testing standards. However, it does not disclose details of action it takes against an MOT test centre to the complainant.

Do not assume a disputed MOT result proves fraud

A difference of opinion, recording error or incorrect assessment does not automatically establish deliberate dishonesty. Report suspected criminal conduct only where there is a genuine evidential basis for the concern.

Northern Ireland MOT Appeals

Are MOT Appeal Rules Different in Northern Ireland?

Yes. The DVSA VT17 procedure described in this guide applies to England, Scotland and Wales.

Northern Ireland operates a separate vehicle testing system through the Driver and Vehicle Agency (DVA). Drivers should follow the DVA appeal procedure rather than using the Great Britain VT17 form.

How to Challenge a Northern Ireland MOT Result

At the Test Centre

Ask the DVA test centre manager to review the result before leaving. If the manager upholds the decision, no further appeal is available through that on-site route.

After Leaving

Contact DVA within 14 days of the original test. Provide the registration, test centre and your contact details.

DVA Recheck

DVA can arrange a vehicle recheck. Do not have the vehicle repaired before the appeal is considered, as changes may affect the outcome.

Frequently Asked Questions

Wrong MOT Result Appeal UK — FAQs

These answers cover the most common questions about appealing an MOT failure or pass, DVSA deadlines, form VT17, fees, vehicle reinspections and driving rules during an appeal.

Can I appeal an MOT failure if I think the tester was wrong?

Yes. In England, Scotland and Wales, you can appeal an MOT failure to DVSA if you believe the vehicle should have passed. Discuss the result with the test centre first, avoid repairs that could affect the evidence and submit form VT17 within 14 working days of the test.

How long do I have to appeal a failed MOT?

You must submit your appeal within 14 working days of the original MOT test. This is a working-day deadline, not 14 calendar days. Submit the official VT17 form promptly and keep a record of when you sent it.

Can I appeal an MOT pass if the garage missed a defect?

Yes. You can appeal a passing MOT result if you believe the vehicle should have failed. For defects other than corrosion, the deadline is 28 calendar days from the test. For corrosion-related defects, the deadline is three months. DVSA may arrange a vehicle reinspection.

What is the DVSA VT17 form?

VT17 is the official form used to appeal an MOT test result for cars, motorcycles and vans in England, Scotland and Wales. It asks for your details, vehicle information, the disputed MOT result and your reasons for appealing. The form is available through GOV.UK.

How do I submit an MOT appeal to DVSA?

Download and complete form VT17 from GOV.UK, explain the MOT decision you disagree with and submit it to DVSA within the relevant deadline. The official guidance provides email and postal submission details. Keep a copy of the completed form and your submission.

How much does it cost to appeal a failed MOT?

For an appeal against an MOT failure, DVSA charges the applicable full MOT test fee before the examination. For a standard car, the published maximum MOT fee is £54.85. The appeal fee is refunded if the vehicle passes the DVSA retest. Fees differ for some vehicle classes.

Is it free to appeal an MOT pass?

DVSA does not charge a reinspection fee for an appeal against a passing MOT result. If DVSA decides a vehicle examination is appropriate, the examiner provides a report of the findings rather than issuing a new MOT test result.

Can I repair my car before an MOT appeal?

DVSA advises against repairs or adjustments before the appeal is considered because changes to the vehicle may affect the examination. If the vehicle is dangerous, do not drive it. Contact DVSA promptly about the appeal and arrange safe transport or necessary safety measures.

Can I drive my car while appealing a failed MOT?

An MOT appeal does not automatically permit driving. You must follow the normal MOT validity and roadworthiness rules. A dangerous vehicle must not be driven. If the previous MOT remains valid and no dangerous defects were recorded, driving may be possible only if the vehicle is still roadworthy.

What happens during a DVSA MOT appeal retest?

For an appeal against a failure, DVSA may arrange a full MOT test carried out by a vehicle examiner. For an appeal against a pass, DVSA may arrange a reinspection and provide a report. The vehicle's condition at the examination is important, particularly if it has changed since the original test.

Will a successful MOT appeal remove the original failure from history?

No. When a failed MOT is appealed, the original result remains in the vehicle's MOT history. DVSA adds the result of its retest as a separate entry. If the vehicle passes the DVSA retest, the appeal fee is refunded, but the earlier failure is not simply deleted.

Can I appeal an MOT advisory or have it removed?

The MOT record correction service cannot be used simply to remove advisory items or defects because you disagree with them. Discuss the advisory with the test centre and seek DVSA guidance about the appropriate complaint route. A formal VT17 appeal concerns a disputed MOT test result.

What evidence do I need for an MOT appeal?

Keep the original MOT result, relevant test details, photographs, garage correspondence and any useful independent inspection findings. Record the vehicle's mileage and any changes since the test. Supporting evidence may help DVSA investigate, but it does not automatically prove that the original result was incorrect.

Can I claim compensation from an MOT garage after a successful appeal?

A successful MOT appeal does not automatically entitle you to compensation from the original garage. DVSA cannot obtain a refund of the garage's MOT charge or compensation for repair costs and other losses. Financial disputes may require separate consumer advice or legal action.

Are MOT appeal rules different in Northern Ireland?

Yes. Northern Ireland uses the Driver and Vehicle Agency (DVA) rather than the DVSA VT17 process. Drivers should follow the official DVA appeal guidance, including its separate deadlines, review procedure and driving rules. The Great Britain VT17 instructions should not be used for a Northern Ireland test.

What is the difference between an MOT appeal and an MOT record correction?

An MOT appeal challenges the tester's pass or fail decision. An MOT record correction addresses qualifying errors in recorded information, such as mileage or vehicle details. Missing tests and expiry-date discrepancies may require separate investigation. Choose the process that matches the actual problem.

Final Thoughts

Challenging an Incorrect MOT Result With Confidence

An MOT result can affect whether you can use your vehicle, the repairs you are advised to undertake and the information future buyers see in its MOT history. If you genuinely believe the test decision was incorrect, the official DVSA appeal process provides a route to challenge it.

The most important step is understanding exactly what you are disputing. A failed component assessment, a missed safety defect and an incorrect mileage entry are different problems requiring different procedures.

For a disputed MOT pass or failure in England, Scotland or Wales, check the deadline, discuss the decision with the test centre and use the official VT17 form. Preserve evidence and avoid changing the vehicle's condition before DVSA considers the complaint.

Remember that an MOT appeal does not make an unsafe vehicle roadworthy and does not automatically remove the original result from the official database.
