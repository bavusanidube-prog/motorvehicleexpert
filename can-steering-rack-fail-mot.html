UK Steering Rack MOT Guide

Can Steering Rack Fail MOT?

Yes. A steering rack can fail the MOT for insecure mounting, excessive steering roughness, loss of gaiter protection, power steering leaks, failed assistance or excessive steering free play. If attachment or steering control becomes seriously compromised, the defect can become Dangerous.

Steering rack faults can affect vehicle control

Heavy or notchy steering, obvious rack movement, rapidly falling power steering fluid, severe free play or a sudden loss of steering assistance should not be treated as routine wear. Steering faults need proper diagnosis before test day because the seriousness depends on how securely and accurately the system can still control the road wheels.

Rack gaiter deteriorated Minor
Rack insecure / gaiter protection lost Major
Power steering leak / assistance fault Major
Attachment or safe steering seriously affected Dangerous

This guide is written for UK motorists who want to understand the difference between steering rack insecurity, rack gaiter damage, hydraulic leaks, failed assistance, excessive steering play and faults caused by inner tie rods or outer track rod ends. The MOT defect and the workshop diagnosis are treated separately.

Quick Answer

Will a Steering Rack Fail an MOT?

Yes. A steering rack can contribute to an MOT failure when the steering gear is not securely attached, steering operation is excessively rough, the rack gaiter has stopped protecting against contamination, the power steering system leaks or malfunctions, assistance is inoperative or excessive steering free play exists.

The severity depends on the actual condition. A deteriorated rack gaiter can remain a Minor defect while it still protects the steering components, whereas dangerous rack movement, seriously affected attachment or steering that can no longer be controlled safely can move the defect into the Dangerous category.

Minor

Rack Gaiter Deteriorated

Pass Possible

A gaiter that is damaged or deteriorated can remain Minor while it still performs its protective role against dirt and contamination.

Major

Rack or Assistance Fault

MOT Failure

Insecure steering gear, loss of gaiter protection, excessive roughness, hydraulic leakage, power steering malfunction or excessive free play can cause a Major failure.

Dangerous

Steering Control Seriously Affected

Urgent Steering Fault

Seriously affected rack attachment, dangerously loose retaining hardware or steering deterioration that compromises safe control can reach Dangerous severity.

A steering rack, inner tie rod and outer track rod end are not the same component

Steering looseness can be transmitted through the whole linkage. Before a rack is condemned, the technician should establish whether the movement originates inside the rack, at the rack mounting, in an inner tie rod or at the outer track rod end.

Cleaning a hydraulic leak does not repair the steering fault

Fresh power steering fluid around the rack, pipes or inside a rack gaiter should be traced to its source. Wiping away fluid before the test does not restore a failed seal, refill a losing system or repair reduced steering assistance.

Current MOT Classifications

Steering Rack MOT Defects: Minor, Major and Dangerous

The most useful way to understand steering-rack MOT treatment is to separate rack condition, rack attachment, gaiter protection, power steering operation and steering free play. They are related, but they are not the same failure route.

Condition Classification MOT Result Practical Meaning
Steering operation excessively rough Major Fail Steering needs diagnosis for rack, joint, assistance or other mechanical restriction.
Steering gear casing not properly attached Major Fail Rack mountings, bolts, bushes and supporting structure need inspection.
Retaining devices dangerously loose or rack movement relative to body/chassis visible Dangerous Fail Steering gear attachment has deteriorated into a serious control risk.
Steering rack gaiter damaged or deteriorated Minor Pass possible Protective rubber needs attention before contamination protection is lost.
Rack gaiter missing or no longer preventing dirt ingress Major Fail The protective barrier around the steering components is no longer effective.
Power steering fluid leaking or system malfunctioning Major Fail Trace the failed component, joint, seal or assistance fault.
Power steering fluid below minimum Minor Pass possible Investigate why fluid level has fallen rather than topping up repeatedly.
Power steering reservoir empty Major Fail Hydraulic assistance system requires leak and operation checks.
Power steering inoperative Major Fail Determine whether the fault is hydraulic, electrical or mechanical.
Power steering inoperative and steering adversely affected Dangerous Fail Loss of assistance is now materially affecting steering control.
Steering free play excessive Major Fail Locate the wear or maladjustment within the steering system.
Steering free play so excessive that safe steering is affected Dangerous Fail Steering looseness has progressed into a direct control problem.
Mechanic distinction: a hydraulic leak and a rack gaiter fault are different problems

The rubber gaiter protects steering components from contamination. On a hydraulic rack, fluid appearing inside that gaiter can point towards an internal rack seal failure. Replacing only the rubber boot will not repair the hydraulic leak.

Dangerous means steering control or attachment has become seriously compromised

A Dangerous result is not simply a louder knock or a slightly heavier steering wheel. It reflects advanced deterioration where steering attachment, safe operation or vehicle control is seriously affected.

Tester Checks

What Does the MOT Tester Check on a Steering Rack?

The steering rack is assessed as part of the complete steering system. The tester is not simply looking for a leak or listening for a knock. The inspection covers operation, security, free play, gaiter condition, assistance and related steering components.

Operation

Does the Steering Move Smoothly?

Steering is operated through its range to identify excessive roughness, restriction or abnormal mechanical behaviour.

Rack Security

Is the Steering Gear Firmly Attached?

The rack casing, mounting points, fixing bolts and surrounding load-bearing structure are checked for secure attachment.

Movement

Does the Rack Move Against the Body?

Steering input can reveal unwanted movement between the steering gear and the body or chassis.

Gaiters

Are the Rack Gaiters Still Protective?

Rubber gaiters are checked for deterioration, damage, absence and whether they still prevent road contamination reaching the steering components.

Hydraulic System

Is Power Steering Fluid Leaking?

Where hydraulic assistance is fitted, failed seals, joints and steering components are checked for leakage and correct operation.

Fluid Level

Is the Reservoir Below Minimum or Empty?

A low level and an empty reservoir receive different classifications, making the actual level important.

Assistance

Is Power Steering Working?

Hydraulic or electric assistance is checked for correct operation, with greater severity where failed assistance adversely affects steering.

Free Play

How Much Steering Movement Exists?

Free movement at the steering-wheel rim is assessed before the road wheels respond, while built-in movement must not be mistaken for wear.

Related Components

Is the Fault Actually in the Linkage?

Inner tie rods, outer track rod ends and other steering components can create free play or knocking that may initially be blamed on the rack.

Rack-and-pinion steering has a specific free-play reference

For a standard 380 mm steering wheel, the current inspection guidance uses 13 mm maximum free play for rack-and-pinion steering, or 48 mm where several joints exist between the steering wheel and rack. Different steering-wheel diameters require the limit to be adjusted proportionally.

Do not diagnose a complete rack because the steering knocks

Knocking can come from rack mountings, inner tie rods, outer track rod ends, suspension joints or the rack itself. The correct workshop diagnosis must identify where the movement begins before an expensive steering rack is authorised.

The MOT result identifies the inspection defect; workshop testing identifies the repair

A rack-related MOT problem can lead to very different repairs depending on whether the fault is a gaiter, mounting, hydraulic seal, inner joint, assistance system or complete steering gear. Parts should follow evidence, not the wording “steering rack” alone.

Major MOT Defect

Can an Insecure Steering Rack Fail an MOT?

Yes. The steering rack must remain securely attached to the vehicle structure. If the steering gear casing is not properly fixed in position, the defect is Major.

This can involve loose mounting bolts, damaged mounting points, failed rack bushes or another condition allowing movement where the steering gear should remain firmly located.

Mounting

Rack Moves at Its Mounts

Steering input should move the internal rack and linkage, not the complete steering gear against the vehicle structure.

Fixings

Bolts or Retainers Loose

Loose or damaged fixing hardware can allow unwanted rack movement under steering load.

MOT Result

Steering Gear Not Properly Attached

Major

Once the rack is no longer properly secured, the vehicle fails the MOT.

A worn mounting bush and a loose steering rack are not always the same severity

Rubber deterioration should be judged by its effect on steering-gear security. The important question is whether the rack remains correctly and firmly attached when steering load is applied.

Watch the rack body while steering load is applied

Controlled steering movement can help separate movement inside the rack from movement of the complete rack casing against its mountings.

Dangerous MOT Defect

When Can Steering Rack Movement Become Dangerous?

Rack security moves beyond a Major defect when the attachment has deteriorated far enough to create a serious steering-control risk.

Dangerously loose retaining devices or visible relative movement between the steering gear and the body or chassis can be classified as Dangerous.

Severe Looseness

Rack Movement Is Obvious

The steering gear visibly shifts against the body or chassis when steering force is applied.

Retention

Mounting Security Seriously Reduced

Fixings or retaining components are no longer reliably keeping the steering gear in its intended position.

MOT Severity

Steering Attachment Compromised

Dangerous

The fault has progressed into a condition where steering control may no longer remain safely predictable.

Dangerous rack movement is not a routine MOT-preparation fault

If the steering gear is visibly moving against the vehicle structure, arrange repair or appropriate recovery rather than continuing normal driving.

Inspect the structure as well as the rack mounts

Loose fixings are not the only possible cause. Corrosion, elongated mounting holes or damaged supporting structure can also reduce rack security.

Minor vs Major

Can a Damaged or Split Steering Rack Gaiter Fail an MOT?

A damaged rack gaiter does not automatically mean a Major failure. The classification depends on whether the protective function remains effective.

A gaiter that is damaged or deteriorated but still keeps contamination away can be recorded as Minor. If it is missing or no longer prevents dirt entering, the defect becomes Major.

Deteriorated

Rubber Aged or Cracked

Ageing, cracking or surface deterioration can appear before the seal has completely failed.

Minor

Protection Still Effective

Where the gaiter remains protective despite deterioration, the defect can remain Minor.

Major

Dirt Protection Lost

A missing or open gaiter that no longer keeps contamination away from the steering components causes a Major failure.

A rack gaiter is not a hydraulic seal

The gaiter protects steering components from road contamination. If hydraulic power steering fluid is found inside the gaiter, suspect an internal rack seal leak rather than treating the rubber boot as the source.

Do not fit a new gaiter over an unresolved hydraulic leak

A fresh boot may hide the evidence temporarily, but it will not repair a leaking rack seal or stop hydraulic fluid collecting inside the gaiter.

Hydraulic Steering

Can a Power Steering Leak Fail an MOT?

Yes. Where a power steering component, joint or seal has failed and fluid is leaking, the defect can be classified as Major.

The fluid level itself is also important. A level below the minimum mark is treated differently from a completely empty reservoir.

Leak

Component or Seal Failed

Major

Hydraulic fluid escaping because a steering component, connection or seal has failed can cause an MOT failure.

Low Fluid

Below Minimum Mark

Minor

A low fluid level is a warning that the hydraulic system should be checked for loss or leakage.

Empty

Reservoir Has No Fluid

Major

An empty power steering reservoir moves the condition into the Major category.

Repeated topping-up is not a repair

If the fluid level continues falling, the system is losing fluid somewhere. Check the rack, hoses, pipes, pump connections and seals rather than repeatedly restoring the level without fixing the cause.

Clean the area only after recording where the leak originates

Hydraulic fluid can spread along the rack, subframe and gaiter. Trace the highest fresh wet point before cleaning so the failed component is not misidentified.

Steering Assistance

Can Failed Power Steering Cause an MOT Failure?

Yes. If the fitted power steering system is inoperative, the condition is normally a Major defect.

If the failure of assistance has progressed far enough that steering is adversely affected, the severity can become Dangerous.

Hydraulic

Pump or Pressure Lost

Hydraulic systems can lose assistance through fluid loss, pump failure, pressure faults or rack problems.

Electric

EPS Assistance Failed

Electric steering does not need hydraulic fluid, but failure of the assistance system can still create an MOT defect.

Dangerous

Steering Adversely Affected

Where loss of assistance materially affects safe steering control, the condition can reach Dangerous severity.

Heavy steering does not prove the rack itself has failed

Assistance faults can come from a hydraulic pump, electric motor, control module, low voltage, sensor fault, steering joint or mechanical rack problem. Diagnose the system before replacing the complete rack.

Sudden major steering effort deserves prompt inspection

If assistance disappears suddenly or steering becomes abnormally difficult to control, treat it as a steering-system fault rather than simply an inconvenience.

Steering Free Play

Can Excessive Steering Play Fail an MOT?

Yes. Excessive free movement at the steering wheel before the road wheels respond is a Major MOT defect.

If the free play becomes so great that safe steering is affected, the defect can become Dangerous.

Rack & Pinion

13 mm Reference

For a standard 380 mm steering wheel, rack-and-pinion steering uses a 13 mm free-play reference.

Multiple Joints

48 mm Reference

Where several joints exist between the steering wheel and rack, the current reference for the same steering-wheel diameter is 48 mm.

Dangerous

Safe Steering Affected

Extremely excessive free play that compromises safe steering moves the defect into the Dangerous category.

Steering Condition MOT Meaning Diagnostic Direction
Small designed movement Not automatically a defect Distinguish built-in clearance from wear
Excessive steering free play Major Trace wear through rack, inner joints, outer joints and column
Free play affecting safe steering Dangerous Repair before further normal driving
Steering-wheel free play does not automatically mean the rack is worn internally

Movement can come from the column, universal joints, rack, inner tie rods, outer track rod ends or other linkage components. Trace the movement before approving a rack replacement.

Measure free play before dismantling

Recording the steering movement and then watching each joint while the wheel is gently moved can show where the delay begins and prevent a serviceable rack being replaced unnecessarily.

Steering Rack Symptoms

What Are the Common Signs of a Steering Rack Problem?

Steering rack faults can show up as knocking, vague steering, heavy assistance, fluid loss, uneven tyre wear or unwanted movement in the steering linkage.

None of those symptoms proves the rack itself has failed. The same behaviour can come from inner tie rods, outer track rod ends, column joints, power steering components or suspension wear.

Noise

Knock During Steering

Rack mountings, inner joints or internal steering-gear wear can produce a knock as steering load changes direction.

Steering Feel

Vague or Delayed Response

Free movement somewhere in the steering system can introduce a delay between steering-wheel input and road-wheel movement.

Assistance

Heavy Steering

Hydraulic pressure loss, EPS faults, mechanical binding or rack damage can all increase steering effort.

Hydraulic

Power Steering Fluid Loss

Fluid around the rack, pipes or inside a gaiter can point towards an internal or external hydraulic leak.

Tyres

Uneven Front Tyre Wear

Steering looseness or incorrect geometry can contribute to accelerated inner- or outer-edge tread wear.

Movement

Visible Rack or Joint Play

Physical movement seen while steering load is applied gives far stronger evidence than noise alone.

Do not condemn a complete steering rack from symptoms alone

A comparatively inexpensive inner tie rod, outer track rod end, mounting bush or hydraulic pipe can mimic a much more expensive rack fault. Identify where the problem starts before ordering the steering gear.

Diagnose the steering system as a chain

Follow movement from the steering wheel through the column, steering gear, inner tie rods and outer joints to the road wheels. The component that fails to transfer movement accurately is the one that deserves closer inspection.

Steering Knock

Can a Steering Rack Cause Knocking or Clunking?

Yes, but the source needs to be isolated carefully. A knock around the steering rack area can come from rack mounting movement, worn inner tie rods, internal rack wear or another front suspension component.

Mounting

Rack Moves Against the Body

Failed bushes, loose fixings or damaged mounting points can allow the complete steering gear to shift when steering load changes.

Inner Joint

Inner Tie Rod Knocks

Free play in the inner joint can produce a knock close to the rack even when the steering gear itself remains serviceable.

Internal Rack

Mechanical Wear Inside the Gear

Internal wear can create movement or roughness, but this should only be diagnosed after external joints and mountings have been checked.

A knock near the rack is a location clue, not a diagnosis

Sound travels through the subframe and steering linkage. A worn ball joint, lower-arm bush or anti-roll-bar link can sound as if it comes directly from the rack.

Watch the rack body and inner joints at the same time

Small steering inputs can reveal whether the rack casing moves against its mounts or whether the movement is concentrated in an inner steering joint.

Steering Effort

Does Heavy Steering Mean the Steering Rack Is Faulty?

Not necessarily. Heavy steering tells you that the steering system needs diagnosis, but it does not identify the rack by itself.

The increased effort can come from hydraulic pressure loss, an electric assistance fault, low fluid, pump failure, mechanical binding, tyre pressure or a seized steering or suspension joint.

Hydraulic

Assistance Pressure Low

A leaking system or weak pump can reduce assistance even when the rack itself remains mechanically intact.

Electric

EPS Assistance Fault

Electric steering systems can become heavy because of motor, sensor, voltage or control faults without any hydraulic leak being present.

Mechanical

Steering Binding

Roughness or restriction inside the rack, column joints or suspension steering points can increase effort mechanically.

Heavy steering that suddenly worsens should be investigated promptly

A sudden assistance loss can change how much force is needed to control the vehicle, especially at low speed. Do not assume it is simply an ageing rack.

Check basic causes before condemning the rack

Tyre pressures, fluid level, warning lights and assistance operation are quick checks that can narrow the diagnosis before deeper steering work is authorised.

Hydraulic Rack Diagnosis

What Does Power Steering Fluid Inside a Rack Gaiter Mean?

On a hydraulic steering rack, power steering fluid collecting inside the gaiter can indicate an internal rack seal leak.

The rubber gaiter itself is a protective cover around the rack end and inner steering joint. It is not designed to contain hydraulic system pressure.

Dry Gaiter

Normal Protective Boot

The gaiter should primarily protect steering components from dirt, road water and debris.

Hydraulic Fluid

Fluid Collected Inside

Hydraulic fluid behind the boot suggests leakage from inside the rack rather than ordinary external contamination.

Diagnosis

Inspect Internal Rack Seal

The rack needs further assessment to establish whether the internal seal has failed and whether replacement or specialist repair is appropriate.

Do not puncture or loosen the gaiter simply to drain the fluid

Releasing trapped hydraulic fluid may remove visible evidence temporarily, but it does not repair the internal leak and can compromise the gaiter's protective function.

Fluid inside one gaiter can reveal a leak before the driveway does

The boot can temporarily contain leaking fluid around the rack end, so a vehicle may have an internal steering-rack leak without leaving a large obvious puddle underneath.

Confirm the fluid type before blaming the rack

Steering components can become contaminated by engine oil, gearbox oil, grease or road residue from elsewhere. Identify whether the liquid is actually power steering fluid before authorising rack replacement.

Differential Diagnosis

Steering Rack vs Inner Tie Rod: Where Is the Play Coming From?

The inner tie rod attaches directly to the rack and sits beneath the rack gaiter, so wear there can easily be described incorrectly as “steering rack play”.

Inner Tie Rod

Joint Wear at Rack End

The steering rack itself can remain secure while the inner articulation develops free movement.

  • Movement occurs at the inner steering joint.
  • Outer track rod end may still be tight.
  • Knocking can appear close to the rack.
  • Toe and steering accuracy can be affected.
Steering Rack

Gear or Mounting Fault

A rack fault may involve internal gear wear, rough operation, mounting movement, hydraulic leakage or an assistance-related problem.

  • Rack casing may move at mountings.
  • Hydraulic fluid may leak from internal seals.
  • Steering can feel rough or bind.
  • Internal steering play may remain after joints are confirmed sound.
Do not replace the whole steering rack for an isolated inner-joint fault

Where the rack itself is secure and operating correctly but an inner tie rod has excessive play, repair should target the worn joint rather than automatically replacing the complete steering gear.

Hold the rack movement and joint movement separately

Controlled steering input while observing the inner joint can show whether free movement occurs before the rack bar itself responds.

Outer Steering Joint

Steering Rack vs Track Rod End: Which Part Is Actually Worn?

Outer track rod end wear is one of the most common steering faults that can be mistaken for a rack problem because the looseness is transmitted through the whole linkage.

Outer Joint

Visible Play at Track Rod End

If the ball stud moves excessively inside the outer joint housing, the fault is at the track rod end rather than the rack.

Rack

Outer Joint Tight but Play Remains

Trace the movement further inboard towards the inner tie rod and steering gear when the outer joint is serviceable.

Alignment

Both Repairs Affect Toe

Track rod end or inner steering work can alter wheel toe, so geometry should be checked after the mechanical fault is repaired.

Start with the component you can see moving

If the outer joint visibly moves before the rest of the linkage responds, there is little value in diagnosing a rack fault until that confirmed steering-joint play is addressed.

Steering free play can come from more than one component

On a neglected front end, an outer track rod end and an inner joint can be worn at the same time. Continue checking the complete linkage even after finding one obvious fault.

Repair Confirmation

What Should Be Checked After Steering Rack or Tie Rod Repair?

Steering work is not complete simply because a replacement part has been fitted.

The repair should be confirmed through rack security, joint play, assistance operation, leak checks, steering-wheel position and wheel alignment.

Security

Rack Firmly Mounted

Recheck mounting bolts, bushes and supporting structure so the steering gear remains fixed under load.

Free Play

Original Looseness Removed

Confirm unwanted movement has disappeared from the rack, inner joints or outer steering joints that were repaired.

Hydraulic

No Fresh Fluid Leakage

On hydraulic systems, clean the repaired area and recheck for fresh fluid around the rack, pipes and gaiters.

Assistance

Steering Effort Normal

Hydraulic or electric assistance should operate consistently without abnormal heavy spots or warning indications.

Alignment

Toe Correctly Set

Rack, inner tie rod or outer track rod work can alter geometry, making alignment an important final step.

Road Test

Steering Tracks Normally

Confirm that knocking, wandering, heavy steering or off-centre steering behaviour has genuinely been resolved.

Persistent steering play after rack replacement means the diagnosis needs revisiting

A new steering rack will not repair worn outer track rod ends, suspension ball joints, wheel bearings or other steering linkage faults that were present at the same time.

Confirm the original symptom before considering the repair complete

If the steering is still heavy, knocks remain or the vehicle still wanders after repair, return to the diagnostic process rather than replacing another major steering component by assumption.

Pre-MOT Checks

What Can You Check Before the MOT if You Suspect a Steering Rack Fault?

An owner can gather useful evidence before the test without trying to dismantle or condemn the steering rack.

Concentrate on steering effort, fluid loss, knocking, steering-wheel free movement, warning lights and how accurately the car follows steering input.

Check 1

Check Steering Effort

Note whether the steering has become unusually heavy, inconsistent or noticeably harder in one direction.

Check 2

Look for Fluid Loss

On hydraulic systems, inspect the reservoir level and look for fresh fluid around the rack, pipes, subframe and steering gaiters.

Check 3

Listen for Steering Knocks

Note whether knocking appears during low-speed steering, direction changes or driving over uneven surfaces.

Check 4

Note Steering Free Movement

Excessive movement at the steering wheel before the road wheels respond deserves inspection, but the source still needs to be traced.

Check 5

Check Warning Lights

Electric power steering faults can illuminate a steering warning lamp even where no hydraulic leak exists.

Check 6

Review MOT History

Previous steering, gaiter, power steering or linkage advisories can show whether the fault has been developing across several tests.

Do not put hands around steering components while the system is moving

Rack and linkage checks involving steering movement should be carried out with the vehicle safely positioned and supported. Leave underside movement checks to a workshop if safe access is not available.

Give the workshop symptoms rather than a pre-selected repair

“The steering is heavy at parking speed, fluid level is dropping and the left gaiter looks wet” is more useful than simply asking for a steering rack replacement.

Workshop Confirmation

What Is the Correct Steering Rack Diagnostic Process?

The diagnosis should establish whether the rack itself is faulty or whether the symptom is being created by a mounting, inner tie rod, outer track rod end, hydraulic component or assistance fault.

Step 1

Confirm the Driver Complaint

Identify whether the main problem is looseness, knocking, heavy steering, leakage, warning lights or inconsistent assistance.

Step 2

Inspect Rack Security

Watch the steering gear and its mountings while controlled steering load is applied.

Step 3

Trace Steering Play

Follow free movement through the column, rack, inner joints and outer track rod ends rather than assuming the rack is the source.

Step 4

Inspect Rack Gaiters

Check deterioration, splitting, sealing and whether hydraulic fluid has collected inside either gaiter.

Step 5

Check Hydraulic System

Confirm reservoir level, hose condition, rack seals, joints and pump-side leakage where hydraulic assistance is fitted.

Step 6

Check Electric Assistance

On EPS systems, scan relevant steering modules and assess voltage, sensor, motor and assistance faults before condemning the rack.

Step 7

Check for Mechanical Binding

Separate genuine rack roughness from seized joints, column problems or suspension components that make steering heavy.

Step 8

Confirm the Failed Component

Decide whether the repair is a gaiter, mounting, inner joint, hydraulic line, assistance component or complete steering rack.

Step 9

Plan Alignment and Calibration

Rack removal or steering-joint replacement can affect wheel geometry, and some electric systems may also require vehicle-specific setup after repair.

A complete steering rack should be the diagnosis, not the starting assumption

Rack replacement is one of the more expensive steering repairs. Confirm mountings, tie rods, leakage source and assistance operation first.

Mechanical evidence should lead the repair decision

If only one inner joint has free play or one hydraulic pipe is leaking, replacing the entire rack may not be necessary.

Before the Test

What Steering Rack Faults Should Be Repaired Before the MOT?

Repair confirmed Major or Dangerous steering defects before presenting the vehicle rather than relying on cleaning, topping up fluid or clearing a warning light.

Security

Insecure Steering Rack

Restore proper steering-gear attachment and inspect the mountings and supporting structure.

Gaiter

Protection Lost

Replace a gaiter that no longer protects steering components and inspect the joint beneath it for contamination or wear.

Hydraulic

Active Steering Leak

Trace and repair the failed seal, pipe, joint or rack component causing hydraulic fluid loss.

Assistance

Power Steering Inoperative

Diagnose and restore the assistance system rather than presenting the vehicle with a known power steering failure.

Play

Excessive Steering Free Play

Locate and repair the worn rack, joint or linkage component before the test.

Dangerous

Safe Steering Compromised

Dangerous rack movement, attachment failure or steering control problems require urgent repair rather than normal road use.

A Minor gaiter or low-fluid defect is still worth addressing early

Minor deterioration can become more expensive once contamination reaches a steering joint or a small hydraulic loss develops into a larger leak.

Repair Decision

Gaiter, Inner Tie Rod, Mounting, Seal or Complete Rack: What Should Be Repaired?

“Steering rack fault” covers several very different repairs. The correct choice depends on what testing actually proves.

Gaiter

Protective Boot Only

Appropriate where the rubber gaiter has failed but the steering joint and rack underneath remain mechanically sound.

Inner Joint

Inner Tie Rod

Replace the inner steering joint where play is isolated there and the rack itself remains serviceable.

Mounting

Rack Bush or Fixing

Where the steering gear is moving because a mounting or retaining component has failed, repair the attachment rather than the internal rack.

Hydraulic

Pipe, Joint or Seal

External hydraulic leaks should be traced to the failed connection, hose, pipe or steering component before the rack is condemned.

Reconditioned Rack

Internal Rack Fault

A professionally reconditioned rack can be a practical option where internal wear or hydraulic seal failure makes rack replacement necessary.

Complete Rack

New or Replacement Assembly

Appropriate where the steering gear itself is damaged, excessively worn, internally leaking or has an assistance fault that cannot economically be repaired separately.

Confirmed Finding Likely Repair Why
Gaiter damaged, rack dry and joint sound Rack gaiter Protective fault only
Inner joint has excessive play Inner tie rod Wear isolated outside the steering gear mechanism
Rack moves at mounting Bush, fixing or structural repair Attachment fault rather than internal rack wear
Hydraulic fluid inside gaiter Rack seal repair or rack replacement Internal hydraulic leakage suspected

Electric rack assistance fault Vehicle-specific EPS repair or rack assembly Depends on motor, sensor, electronics and rack design
Do not fit a replacement rack until the adjacent joints have been checked

A new rack will not remove free play in worn outer track rod ends, suspension joints or wheel bearings.

Reconditioned and new racks should be compared on total fitted cost

Part price is only one factor. Labour, alignment, coding or calibration, replacement tie rods and warranty terms can change which option offers better value.

After Steering Repair

Do You Need Wheel Alignment After Steering Rack Replacement?

Yes — wheel alignment should normally be checked after steering rack replacement or tie-rod work.

Removing the rack or disturbing inner and outer steering adjustments can alter wheel toe and steering-wheel position.

Toe

Steering Geometry Changes

Tie-rod length directly affects where each front wheel points relative to the vehicle centreline.

Steering Wheel

Centre Position Matters

Alignment should leave the steering wheel correctly positioned while the vehicle travels straight ahead.

Tyres

Protect the New Repair

Incorrect toe after steering work can rapidly damage front tyres even though the replacement rack itself is functioning correctly.

Do not use alignment to hide mechanical steering play

Wheel geometry should only be finalised once rack security, steering joints and suspension components are mechanically sound.

Include alignment in the repair quote from the beginning

Treating alignment as part of the steering-rack repair avoids completing major steering work only to leave the vehicle with incorrect toe or an off-centre steering wheel.

UK Repair Costs

How Much Does Steering Rack Repair or Replacement Cost in the UK?

Steering-rack costs vary much more than a simple outer steering-joint repair. For planning purposes, a complete rack replacement can commonly fall around £400–£1,200+, with some straightforward applications lower and complex electric, premium or new-OEM systems significantly higher.

These are Motor Vehicle Expert planning figures rather than fixed quotations. The real bill depends on the failed component, rack type, labour access, whether the subframe needs lowering, parts choice, alignment and any coding or calibration required afterwards.

MOT Retest

What Happens After a Steering Rack MOT Failure?

Once the failed steering defect has been repaired, the vehicle can return for the appropriate MOT retest.

Vehicle Stays

Repaired at the Test Centre

Free Partial Retest

If the vehicle remains at the same test centre for repair and is retested within 10 working days, only a partial retest is required and no retest fee is charged.

Vehicle Leaves

Return Within 10 Working Days

Partial Fee Can Apply

If the vehicle is taken away for repair and returned to the same test centre within 10 working days, only a partial retest is required but a partial fee can be charged.

Outside the Window

Full MOT Again

Full Fee Can Apply

Outside the qualifying retest arrangements, another full MOT test is normally required.

A steering rack is not one of the specifically listed next-working-day free-return items

Do not assume that taking the car away for rack repair and returning the next working day automatically produces a free partial retest. The normal retest arrangements apply unless another failed item separately qualifies.

Dangerous steering defects remain roadworthiness problems before the retest

Seriously insecure steering gear or steering that cannot be controlled safely should be repaired or recovered appropriately rather than driven simply because a previous MOT certificate has not yet expired.

Used-Car Buying

Should You Buy a Used Car With a Steering Rack Fault?

A steering rack warning on a used car deserves proper diagnosis before purchase because the cost exposure can range from a relatively small gaiter or tie-rod repair to a complete hydraulic or electric steering-rack replacement.

Steering Feel

Check for Play or Binding

Steering should respond accurately without excessive dead movement, notchiness or unusually heavy sections.

Hydraulic

Look for Fluid Loss

On hydraulic systems, inspect the reservoir and rack area rather than accepting repeated topping-up as normal.

Electric Steering

Check Warning Lights

EPS warnings can point towards sensor, motor, voltage, electronics or complete rack-assistance faults.

Tyres

Inspect Front Tyre Wear

Uneven wear can reveal poor alignment or steering looseness that has existed for some time.

MOT History

Check Repeat Steering Advisories

Repeated steering, power steering, tyre or suspension notes can reveal a problem that has been deferred rather than recently developed.

Repair Evidence

Ask Exactly What Was Diagnosed

“Steering rack issue” is too vague. Ask whether the confirmed problem is the rack itself, inner joint, mounting, gaiter, leak or power assistance.

Do not price every steering-rack symptom as a small repair

A split gaiter or worn inner tie rod can be manageable. An electric rack, internal hydraulic failure or badly insecure steering gear can expose the buyer to a much larger bill.

A diagnosed isolated steering fault can still be an acceptable purchase

If the rest of the steering and suspension is sound, the repair is clearly identified and the price reflects the work required, a steering fault does not automatically make the car a poor buy.

Frequently Asked Questions

Steering Rack & MOT FAQs

Can a steering rack fail an MOT?

Yes. A steering rack can fail an MOT if the steering gear is insecure, operation is excessively rough, the rack gaiter no longer protects against dirt, the power steering system leaks or malfunctions, steering assistance fails or excessive steering free play is present.

Can an insecure steering rack fail an MOT?

Yes. A steering gear casing that is not properly attached is a Major MOT defect. If retaining devices are dangerously loose or visible movement exists between the steering gear and the body or chassis, the defect can be Dangerous.

When can steering rack movement become Dangerous?

Steering rack security becomes Dangerous when the attachment has deteriorated beyond ordinary insecurity, such as dangerously loose retaining devices or visible movement between the steering gear and the vehicle structure that creates a serious steering-control risk.

Can a damaged steering rack gaiter fail an MOT?

A steering rack gaiter that is damaged or deteriorated can be recorded as a Minor defect while it still protects the steering components from contamination. If the gaiter is missing or no longer keeps dirt out, the classification becomes Major.

Can a split steering rack gaiter fail an MOT?

A split gaiter does not automatically receive the same result in every case. Damage that still leaves the protective function effective can be Minor, while a split that means dirt can enter the steering joint or rack area can become Major.

Will a missing steering rack gaiter fail an MOT?

Yes. Where a steering rack gaiter is missing and the steering components are no longer protected from dirt entering, the defect is Major.

Can a leaking power steering rack fail an MOT?

Yes. A power steering fluid leak caused by a failed component, joint or seal can be a Major defect. The leak should be traced properly because fluid around a rack gaiter may come from an internal rack seal rather than the gaiter itself.

Is low power steering fluid an MOT failure?

Power steering fluid below the minimum mark is currently a Minor defect. If the reservoir is empty, the defect becomes Major. A falling fluid level should still be investigated because it normally indicates leakage somewhere in the hydraulic system.

Can power steering failure cause an MOT failure?

Yes. Inoperative power steering is a Major defect. If the loss of assistance has progressed far enough that steering is adversely affected, the defect can become Dangerous.

Can heavy steering fail an MOT?

Heavy steering can lead to an MOT failure where it results from inoperative assistance, excessive roughness or another steering-system defect. The cause should be confirmed because tyres, suspension joints, hydraulic faults and electric power steering faults can all make steering heavy.

Can excessive steering rack play fail an MOT?

Yes. Excessive free play in the steering system is a Major defect. If the free play becomes so excessive that safe steering is affected, the classification becomes Dangerous.

How much steering wheel free play is allowed on rack and pinion steering?

For a standard 380 mm steering wheel, the current MOT manual gives a maximum of 13 mm free play for rack and pinion steering, or 48 mm where several joints exist between the steering wheel and rack. The permitted amount is adjusted for different steering wheel diameters.

Can steering rack mounting bushes fail an MOT?

They can contribute to failure if the rack or steering gear is no longer properly attached. The key issue is whether the steering gear remains securely fixed to the vehicle rather than whether a rubber bush merely looks aged.

Can fluid inside a steering rack gaiter mean the rack is leaking?

Yes. On a hydraulic rack, power steering fluid collecting inside a gaiter can indicate that an internal rack seal is leaking. The gaiter itself normally contains grease and protects steering components; it should not be treated as the source of hydraulic fluid.

Can an electric steering rack fail an MOT?

Yes. Electric steering racks do not use hydraulic power steering fluid, but steering assistance, rack security, steering play, mechanical operation and electronic power steering faults can still affect the MOT.

Should I repair a steering rack fault before the MOT?

Yes if diagnosis confirms excessive play, rack insecurity, a failed gaiter seal, a hydraulic leak, loss of steering assistance or another defined steering defect. Steering faults should be repaired according to their mechanical condition rather than simply cleared or cleaned before test day.

How much does steering rack repair cost in the UK?

Steering rack repair cost varies widely because the fault may involve a gaiter, inner tie rod, mounting, hydraulic pipe, rack seal, electric assistance component or complete rack. Labour access, wheel alignment and whether a new, reconditioned or replacement rack is used can significantly affect the total.

Should you buy a used car with a steering rack fault?

A steering rack fault should be diagnosed before purchase because the repair exposure can range from a relatively small gaiter or joint problem to an expensive hydraulic or electric rack replacement. Check steering play, assistance, leaks, tyre wear, alignment and previous MOT history before deciding.
