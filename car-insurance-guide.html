Car Insurance Guide UK

Quick answer

How does car insurance work in the UK?

Car insurance provides financial protection against risks connected with using a vehicle. The exact protection depends on the policy you buy. The three main levels are third party, third party fire and theft, and comprehensive cover.

Your premium is the price you pay for the policy. The insurer calculates that price using information about you, the car and how it will be used. Two drivers buying the same model can therefore receive very different quotes.

Third party

The basic level of cover protects against liability for injury to other people and damage to their property, subject to the policy terms.

Third party, fire & theft

Adds protection relating to fire and theft of your own vehicle alongside third-party cover.

Comprehensive

Normally provides broader protection and may include damage to your own vehicle, but exact cover and exclusions vary between insurers.

Money-saving starting point:

Compare all suitable levels of cover. Do not automatically assume third-party insurance will cost less than comprehensive insurance.

UK legal basics

Do you legally need car insurance in the UK?

If you drive a vehicle on UK roads or in public places, you normally need valid motor insurance providing at least the legally required third-party protection.

Vehicle insurance and vehicle tax are separate requirements. Having one does not automatically mean the other is in place.

The driver

Make sure you are properly covered to drive the vehicle. Never assume somebody else's policy automatically covers you.

The vehicle

Check that the policy details correctly identify the car and its use.

The information

Information supplied to the insurer should be accurate. Incorrect details can create serious problems if you need to claim.

Never drive on an assumption.

If you are borrowing, collecting or test-driving a vehicle, confirm that valid insurance is actually in place before driving it.

Policy types

The three main types of UK car insurance

The names of the three main cover levels sound simple, but the details inside individual policies can vary considerably. Always read what is actually included.

Third Party

Basic level
Check your own-car protection

Covers your legal liability towards other people and their property according to the policy. It does not normally pay to repair your own car after an accident where you are responsible.

Third Party, Fire & Theft

Extra protection
More than basic third party

Includes third-party protection and normally adds cover if your own vehicle is stolen or damaged by fire, subject to policy conditions.

Comprehensive

Broadest main level
Compare carefully

Usually includes third-party, fire and theft protection plus accidental damage to your own vehicle. Extras and exclusions still vary.

Comprehensive insurance

Is comprehensive insurance always best?

Comprehensive insurance provides the broadest of the three main levels of cover, but the word “comprehensive” does not mean every possible situation is automatically covered.

Policies can differ in areas such as courtesy cars, windscreen protection, personal belongings, driving abroad, legal assistance and replacement vehicle conditions.

Lower cover levels

Is third-party insurance cheaper?

Not necessarily. It sounds logical that less cover should cost less, but insurance pricing is based on risk data rather than simply counting how many benefits a policy contains.

For that reason, comprehensive cover can sometimes produce a lower quote than third-party or third-party fire and theft cover for the same driver.

Compare instead of assuming

Run suitable quotes and compare the total price, excess and actual protection. The cheapest-sounding level of cover is not guaranteed to produce the cheapest premium.

Insurance pricing

What affects the cost of car insurance?

There is no single price for insuring a particular car. Insurers assess risk using information about the driver, vehicle, location and intended use.

Your age and experience

Newly qualified and younger drivers can face higher premiums because insurers have less driving history on which to assess them and may view the risk differently.

The car

Value, performance, repair cost, parts prices, security and claims experience can all affect how a vehicle is rated.

Your address

Insurers can consider claims and theft risk associated with where a vehicle is normally kept.

Your occupation

Occupation can be part of the risk assessment. Describe your job accurately rather than choosing an inaccurate description purely because it produces a lower quote.

Annual mileage

Expected mileage affects how much exposure the vehicle has to road risk. Give a realistic estimate.

Driving history

Claims, convictions and other driving-history information requested by the insurer can affect the quote.

Where the car is kept

Parking arrangements can form part of the insurer's assessment. State where the vehicle is genuinely kept overnight.

Modifications

Changes from standard specification may affect the insurer's willingness to cover the car or the price charged.

Cover and excess

The protection selected and the amount you agree to contribute towards claims can affect the premium.

Choosing a car

How your choice of car affects insurance

This is especially important for first-time buyers. Two cars with similar purchase prices can produce very different insurance quotes.

Performance

More powerful or performance-focused cars may be rated differently because of their speed, value and claims profile.

Repair costs

Expensive body panels, lights, sensors and specialist components can increase the potential cost of an accident claim.

Security

Theft risk and factory security equipment can form part of how insurers assess the vehicle.

Insurance groups

Car insurance groups explained

Insurance groups are often used as a quick way of comparing how cars may differ in insurance risk. Cars are generally placed into numbered groups, with lower groups often associated with vehicles that may be less expensive to insure.

But the group is only one part of the calculation. It does not tell you what your personal premium will be.

Lower group

Useful starting point
Not a guaranteed price

Can help first-car buyers create a shortlist, but you still need an actual quote.

Higher group

Potentially higher risk
Compare carefully

Performance, vehicle value and repair costs can contribute to a higher rating.

Your quote

The number that matters
Check before buying

Your individual quote combines the vehicle with your own circumstances.

Do not buy a car from its insurance group alone.

Always quote the exact vehicle. A supposedly sensible low-group car can still be expensive for an individual driver.

Policy excess

What does excess mean on car insurance?

The excess is the amount you may have to contribute towards a claim. When comparing policies, look beyond the headline premium and check the total excess that could apply.

Compulsory excess

This is set by the insurer. The amount can vary depending on the policy, vehicle and driver.

Voluntary excess

This is an additional amount you agree to contribute. Choosing a larger voluntary excess can sometimes reduce the premium, but it increases what you may need to pay following a claim.

Do not choose an excess you cannot realistically afford.

A cheaper annual premium is not much help if the total excess leaves you unable to make a claim when you need to.

Telematics insurance

How does black-box car insurance work?

Black-box or telematics insurance uses driving information as part of the policy. Depending on the insurer, data may come from a fitted device, plug-in device, smartphone app or technology already built into the vehicle.

The information considered can vary, which is why you should read the individual policy rather than assuming every black-box product works in the same way.

Driving behaviour

Some policies may assess aspects of how the car is driven, such as speed, braking or acceleration.

When you drive

Some products may consider journey times or have policy conditions connected with when the vehicle is used.

Mileage

Distance travelled can form part of telematics-based policies, especially products designed around limited mileage.

Check the conditions before buying.

Telematics can be worth comparing for a new driver, but understand exactly what is monitored, whether restrictions apply and what happens if the policy's conditions are not followed.

New driver insurance

Car insurance for newly qualified drivers

Insurance can be one of the hardest costs for a newly qualified driver to budget for. This is why the insurance quote should be checked before choosing the first car, not after.

1. Shortlist cars

Choose several sensible cars rather than becoming fixed on one particular model.

2. Compare quotes

Check what the different cars actually cost to insure using accurate details.

3. Compare total cost

Add purchase price, insurance, fuel, tax where applicable and likely maintenance.

4. Inspect the car

Only then decide whether the individual used car is worth buying.

Named drivers

Adding a named driver — and why fronting is different

A policy can allow additional named drivers to use the vehicle. For example, a new driver may legitimately have a parent added if that parent genuinely drives the car.

That is different from giving false information about who is actually the main driver simply to obtain a cheaper premium.

Legitimate named driver

Accurate information

The policy correctly identifies the person who mainly uses the vehicle and any genuine additional drivers.

Fronting

Do not do this

Fronting generally describes falsely presenting somebody else as the main driver when another person actually uses the car most. Giving false information can invalidate cover and create serious consequences.

Accuracy matters.

Never change factual information purely to force a cheaper quote. Tell the insurer who genuinely owns, keeps and mainly drives the vehicle when those questions are asked.

Vehicle modifications

Do modifications affect car insurance?

They can. A modification changes the vehicle from its standard specification and may affect value, performance, theft risk, repair cost or how an insurer assesses it.

Performance changes

Engine tuning, remapping and similar changes can affect performance and may need to be declared.

Suspension and wheels

Lowering, non-standard wheels and suspension changes may alter the car from factory specification.

Cosmetic changes

Do not assume a change is irrelevant simply because it does not make the car faster. Ask the insurer when unsure.

Buying a modified used car?

Find out exactly what has been changed before purchasing and check whether your chosen insurer will cover those modifications.

Vehicle use

Annual mileage and how you use the car

Insurers ask how far you expect to drive and what the car will be used for because different patterns of use can create different levels of exposure.

Social use

Policies may describe social, domestic and pleasure use in different ways. Check the exact wording.

Commuting

If you use the car to travel to work, make sure the policy provides the type of use you require.

Business use

Driving connected with work beyond normal commuting can require different cover. Tell the insurer how the car will actually be used.

Estimate mileage honestly.

Use previous mileage, expected commuting distance and regular journeys to make a realistic estimate rather than choosing an artificially low figure.

Optional cover

Car insurance extras — which ones should you check?

The headline policy price does not always include every feature you want. Some benefits may be optional extras, while others may already be included.

Breakdown cover

Check whether you already have breakdown protection elsewhere before paying for duplicate cover.

Legal expenses

Understand what the legal cover actually provides and any limits or conditions.

Courtesy car

Check when a courtesy vehicle is supplied. Conditions can differ depending on whether your car is repairable, stolen or written off.

Windscreen cover

Look at the excess and whether repairs and replacements are treated differently.

Protected no-claims discount

Understand what protection means. Protecting a discount does not necessarily mean the overall premium can never rise after a claim.

Personal accident cover

Check the amount of cover, who qualifies and the circumstances in which it applies.

Compare policies

How to compare car insurance quotes properly

The lowest number on the screen does not necessarily represent the best deal. Compare like with like.

1. Check cover

Make sure each quote provides the protection you actually need.

2. Check excess

Compare compulsory and voluntary excess, not just the annual premium.

3. Check extras

Look for differences in breakdown, windscreen, courtesy-car and legal cover.

4. Check conditions

Understand restrictions, telematics requirements, mileage and vehicle-use conditions.

Compare the total package.

Paying slightly more for a policy with an affordable excess and the protection you need can be better value than buying the cheapest headline quote.

Renewal

Should you automatically renew your car insurance?

Renewal is a good time to check whether the policy still suits your circumstances and whether other insurers offer suitable alternatives.

Check your details

Make sure address, occupation, mileage, vehicle details, modifications and other relevant information remain accurate.

Review the cover

Your needs may have changed since the policy started. Check the excess and optional benefits again.

Compare alternatives

Do not assume your existing provider will always remain the best choice for your circumstances.

Claims and accidents

What should you do after an accident?

Safety comes first. Stop where required, check whether anybody is injured and follow the legal requirements that apply to the incident.

When safe, gather useful information and follow your insurer's reporting instructions.

Record details

Note relevant vehicle, driver, location and incident information while it is fresh.

Take photographs

If it is safe to do so, photographs of vehicle damage and the scene can help document what happened.

Contact your insurer

Follow the reporting requirements and timescales in your policy, even if you are unsure whether you intend to claim.

Do not put yourself in danger to collect evidence.

On fast or busy roads, personal safety is more important than taking photographs or inspecting vehicle damage.

Common mistakes

Car insurance mistakes to avoid

Buying the car first

Avoid

Never assume a car will be affordable to insure just because it has a small engine or low purchase price.

Looking only at premium

Compare properly

Check excess, cover, restrictions and extras as well as the annual price.

Giving inaccurate information

Serious mistake

Incorrect information about drivers, mileage, occupation, vehicle use or modifications can cause major problems.

Choosing excessive voluntary excess

Think ahead

A lower premium is not worthwhile if you could not afford the excess after an accident.

Ignoring modifications

Declare correctly

Find out what your insurer considers a modification and tell them about relevant changes.

Assuming all policies are equal

Read the details

Two comprehensive policies can have different excesses, benefits, exclusions and conditions.

Frequently Asked Questions

Car Insurance FAQs

These are some of the most common questions UK drivers ask when comparing car insurance.

What are the main types of car insurance in the UK?

The three main levels are third party, third party fire and theft, and comprehensive insurance. Individual policies can differ, so always check the exact protection, excess, exclusions and optional extras.

Is comprehensive car insurance always more expensive?

No. Comprehensive insurance can sometimes produce a cheaper quote than lower levels of cover. Compare suitable policies rather than assuming third-party cover will cost less.

What is a car insurance excess?

The excess is the amount you may need to contribute towards a claim. A policy can contain compulsory excess set by the insurer and voluntary excess chosen by you.

What affects the price of car insurance?

Factors can include the driver, vehicle, address, occupation, annual mileage, driving and claims history, where the car is kept, modifications, vehicle use, cover level and excess.

Should I get an insurance quote before buying a car?

Yes. This is particularly important for first-time buyers. A car with a low purchase price can still be expensive to own if the insurance premium is high.

What is black-box car insurance?

Black-box or telematics insurance uses driving information as part of the policy. Exactly what is monitored and how the information is used varies between insurers, so read the policy conditions carefully.

Final Thoughts

Good Car Insurance Is About More Than Finding the Cheapest Quote

Car insurance can feel complicated because there is no single price or policy that is best for every driver. Your vehicle, age, experience, location, mileage and many other factors can change the quote.

The best approach is simple: give accurate information, compare suitable policies, understand the excess and check what you are actually buying before paying.

For a new driver, do this before choosing the car. A vehicle that costs slightly more to buy but is much cheaper to insure and maintain may be the better first-car decision overall.
