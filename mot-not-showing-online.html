Quick Answer

Why is my MOT not showing online?

If your MOT has been completed but the result is missing from GOV.UK, the first step is to check the vehicle's official MOT history using its correct registration number. If the recent test still does not appear, contact the MOT test centre and ask it to confirm that the result was correctly recorded against your vehicle.

A missing result does not automatically mean the car failed, that the garage did anything wrong or that the test never happened. The cause needs to be established. If an actual test record is missing, DVSA provides an official process for having it investigated and corrected.

1. Check GOV.UK

Search the official MOT history using the vehicle registration. Confirm the vehicle details, most recent test date, result and expiry date.

2. Contact the garage

Ask the test centre to confirm the MOT test number, result, registration and whether the completed test was recorded against the correct vehicle.

3. Escalate if necessary

If the record is genuinely missing or assigned incorrectly, follow DVSA's official process for correcting MOT records.

Important distinction: missing result versus missing certificate

A lost paper certificate is not the same as a missing electronic MOT test record. In Great Britain, a recorded MOT result can normally be viewed through the official MOT history service, even if you do not have a printed certificate. If the electronic test entry itself is missing, the problem requires further investigation.

What should you do immediately?

  • ✓ Check the official MOT history, not just a third-party vehicle checker.
  • ✓ Confirm that you entered the correct registration number.
  • ✓ Compare the result with any certificate or test paperwork.
  • ✓ Contact the test centre if the result is still missing.
  • ✓ Keep your booking confirmation, invoice and MOT test number.
  • ✓ Do not rely on an assumed pass or renewal date when deciding whether to drive.

When does a missing MOT result become a serious problem?

The situation is particularly important when the previous MOT has expired, a vehicle has just failed a test, the registration details are wrong or a seller claims a recent pass that cannot be verified. In those cases, the missing record can affect decisions about lawful road use, repairs and buying the vehicle.

Do not confuse a valid MOT with roadworthiness

A vehicle can be unsafe even when it has a current MOT certificate. If there are dangerous defects, do not drive it simply because an earlier MOT still appears valid.

How to check whether your MOT is recorded online

Before assuming your MOT result is missing, check the official government record. A vehicle's current MOT status and its test history are related, but they answer different questions. The status service shows whether an MOT is currently recorded as valid, while the history service lets you inspect previous tests, results, recorded mileage and expiry dates.

Check current MOT status

Use the official GOV.UK service to check whether the vehicle currently has a valid MOT and when it expires. This is the first check if you need to establish the vehicle's current recorded status.

1. Enter the registration

Enter the vehicle registration exactly as it appears on the number plate or registration document. Check similar-looking letters and numbers before proceeding.

2. Confirm the vehicle

Check that the vehicle description corresponds to the car you own or are inspecting. If the registration produces unexpected details, investigate before relying on the result.

Record exactly what is wrong before contacting the garage. "The latest test is missing", "the test appears but the expiry date looks wrong" and "the registration shows a different vehicle" are three different problems and may require different investigations.

Possible causes

Why is my MOT test not showing online?

An MOT result can appear to be missing for several different reasons. The driver may be checking the wrong vehicle record, looking at an older expiry date rather than the latest test entry, or dealing with a genuine recording problem.

It is important not to diagnose every missing result as a DVSA system delay. The available evidence should be checked first, particularly when the previous MOT has already expired.

Wrong registration entered

A typing mistake or incorrect registration can lead to an unrelated record or prevent the expected vehicle from being found.

Looking at the wrong date

The most recent test date and the next MOT expiry date are not the same thing. An early MOT renewal may retain the previous renewal anniversary.

Test record needs checking

If the completed test is absent from the official history, the test centre needs to verify how the result was recorded.

Incorrect vehicle details

An error involving the registration or vehicle identification details may require an official record correction.

Online service interruption

A service fault or maintenance period may prevent normal access to MOT information. Check official service updates rather than relying on speculation.

Third-party information is stale

A commercial vehicle-check website may display older information. Compare its result with GOV.UK before deciding that the official MOT record is wrong.

What does a missing result actually prove?

A missing online entry is a reason to investigate, not proof that a test passed or failed. It also does not establish whether a particular garage made a mistake. The test centre and, where necessary, DVSA must establish what happened to the official record.

Do not use a garage receipt as proof of a valid MOT

A booking confirmation, payment receipt or service invoice can help establish that you attended the garage, but it does not by itself prove that the vehicle passed an MOT or holds a valid certificate. Ask for the actual MOT test result and its identifying details.

Recent MOT pass

MOT passed but not showing online: what should you do?

If the garage has told you that your vehicle passed its MOT but the pass does not appear in the official MOT history, contact the test centre that carried out the inspection. The centre is the best starting point because it can check the completed test against its own MOT testing records.

First, check the evidence you received

  • Confirm the registration number on your MOT certificate or test paperwork.
  • Check the date the MOT was completed.
  • Look for the MOT test number and result.
  • Check whether the paperwork relates to the same vehicle you entered on GOV.UK.
  • Compare the latest online test entry with the garage's recorded result.

Then ask the test centre to confirm the result

Explain that the test appears to have been completed but cannot be found in the official online history. Ask the centre to check the registration, test number, test date and recorded outcome.

If the garage confirms a valid pass

Ask it to investigate why the pass is not appearing against the correct vehicle. Keep the certificate and test details while the discrepancy is resolved.

If the garage cannot confirm the pass

Do not assume the vehicle has a valid MOT. Request clarification of the actual test outcome and follow the official correction process if a record is missing or wrong.

What if your previous MOT has expired?

Treat this as a priority. Until the vehicle's valid MOT status is established, do not rely on an unverified verbal assurance when deciding whether ordinary road use is lawful. The limited legal exceptions for driving without a current MOT are covered later in this guide.

A recent failure creates a different situation. See our guide to driving after a failed MOT for the separate rules concerning dangerous defects and the validity of an earlier certificate.

Renewal date problems

Why has my MOT expiry date not updated?

An unchanged MOT expiry date can have more than one explanation. Before reporting a database problem, check whether the latest test actually appears in the MOT history and whether you are comparing the correct dates.

Understand the difference between test date and expiry date

The test date is when the MOT inspection took place. The expiry date is the deadline by which the next MOT must be completed. These dates do not necessarily fall on the same day of the month.

Early renewal within the permitted window

In Great Britain, you can normally renew an MOT up to one month minus one day before its expiry without losing the existing annual renewal date.

A successful test in this window usually extends the expiry to the following year while preserving the original anniversary.

Renewal outside that window

If you have the MOT carried out earlier than the permitted renewal window, the new expiry is normally calculated from the date of the new test.

This can produce a different expiry date from the one you expected, without necessarily indicating a database error.

Worked example: renewing an MOT early

Suppose a car's existing MOT expires on 30 November 2026. In Great Britain, the earliest date for renewing while keeping that anniversary is normally 31 October 2026.

If the vehicle passes on 10 November 2026, the new expiry would normally be 30 November 2027. The renewal anniversary remains the same even though the test was carried out earlier.

Check the actual new expiry

An early MOT pass should not leave the certificate expiring on the old date. What may stay the same is the day and month of the annual renewal anniversary. Always compare the full expiry date, including the year.

What if the expiry year really has not changed?

If the latest test is recorded as a pass but the expiry date appears inconsistent, contact the test centre and ask it to check the certificate details. If the test itself is absent, follow the missing-record investigation process instead of assuming this is only a renewal-date calculation issue.

For the full renewal-window rules, see When Is My MOT Due? — Deadlines and Fines .

Vehicle record errors

What if the MOT shows the wrong registration or vehicle details?

A registration or vehicle identification error should be treated differently from an ordinary missing test result. The first task is to establish whether the mistake is in the information entered into the checker, the MOT test record or the vehicle's registration details.

Check the information against your documents

  • Compare the registration on the number plate with the registration on the V5C.
  • Compare the MOT paperwork with the vehicle you actually presented for testing.
  • Check the test date and test number before contacting the garage.
  • Identify whether the problem concerns the registration, vehicle identity, mileage or test result.

Incorrect registration

Contact the test centre to confirm which registration was used and whether the test was recorded against the correct vehicle.

Incorrect mileage

A mileage recording mistake has its own DVSA correction procedure. The route depends partly on how long ago the test took place.

Missing test entry

If no entry can be found for a completed test, ask the garage to verify the result and follow the official missing-record process.

Do not alter or recreate MOT paperwork yourself

Corrections must be handled through the authorised test centre or the appropriate DVSA process. Keep the original paperwork and any evidence of the discrepancy.

Official guidance is available through GOV.UK's MOT certificate correction service . We cover the different correction routes in the dedicated section later in this guide.

Online service problems

Could a DVSA system problem stop your MOT showing online?

Yes, an online service interruption or planned maintenance can affect access to MOT services. However, a temporary website problem is not the same as proof that a completed MOT result has been recorded correctly.

Check the official MOT service status

DVSA publishes information about MOT service availability, incidents and planned maintenance. If the online checker is unavailable, check the official service status before repeatedly submitting the same registration.

The website is not loading

Check the official status page. If a service incident is reported, retry the GOV.UK checker when service is restored.

The website works but your test is absent

Contact the MOT test centre. A working website that does not display your test requires investigation of the specific vehicle record.

How long does an MOT take to show online?

There is no universal waiting period that drivers should treat as proof that a missing MOT result is normal. The appropriate action depends on whether the test has been completed, whether the result was recorded and whether the online service is functioning correctly.

If your test has been completed but no corresponding entry appears, contact the test centre rather than relying on an assumed delay of a few hours or several days.

A system outage does not automatically permit driving

An unavailable MOT checker does not create a general exemption from MOT requirements. If your previous MOT has expired and you cannot establish a valid replacement, seek clarification before using the vehicle on public roads.

The next stage is a structured diagnostic process: establish what the online record shows, confirm what the test centre recorded and decide whether the matter needs DVSA involvement.

Step-by-step diagnosis

How to diagnose an MOT result that is not showing online

When a recent MOT result is missing, the aim is to establish exactly where the information differs. Start with the official online record, compare it with the test paperwork and then ask the test centre to confirm what was recorded.

This process helps distinguish a genuine missing test from an incorrect registration, an unexpected expiry date or an online service problem. It also prevents unnecessary assumptions about whether the vehicle has passed.

Step 1. Check the official MOT history

Enter the registration on GOV.UK and locate the most recent test. Check its date, result and recorded mileage.

If the latest test is missing, record the date of the last test that does appear.

Open the official MOT history checker →

Step 2. Confirm the registration

Compare the registration entered online with the number plate, V5C and MOT paperwork. Check for incorrect letters or numbers.

If the registration has changed, establish which registration was used when the vehicle was tested.

Step 3. Compare the test documents

Look at the certificate or test result supplied by the garage. Confirm the test date, registration, result and MOT test number.

Keep the original documents. They may help the test centre or DVSA identify the relevant test.

Step 4. Check the actual expiry date

If the latest pass is visible, compare the full expiry date, including the year. An early renewal may preserve the existing annual anniversary.

Do not confuse an unchanged day and month with an expiry date that has failed to advance.

Step 5. Contact the MOT test centre

If the test is absent or the details are inconsistent, ask the garage to verify the result in the MOT testing service and confirm the vehicle against which it was recorded.

Request the correct test details rather than relying on a verbal statement that the car passed.

Step 6. Escalate an unresolved error

If the test centre cannot resolve a missing or incorrect record, use the relevant GOV.UK guidance and contact DVSA through its official channels.

Explain the discrepancy clearly and provide the test details and supporting evidence requested.

What evidence should you keep?

A clear record of the discrepancy can make the investigation easier. Keep the original paperwork and record the information shown by the official checker before contacting the test centre.

Vehicle details

Registration number, make, model and relevant V5C details.

MOT test details

Test date, MOT test number, recorded result and certificate, where available.

Supporting evidence

Garage invoice, booking confirmation, screenshots of the discrepancy and correspondence with the centre.

Mechanic insight: identify the exact mismatch

When speaking to the test centre, avoid simply saying "my MOT is not working". Explain whether the entire test is missing, the pass is recorded against the wrong vehicle, the mileage is incorrect or the expiry date appears inconsistent. This gives the garage a specific issue to investigate.

Do not drive solely because you expect the record to update

If the vehicle's previous MOT has expired and a new valid certificate cannot be established, do not assume that a recent garage visit permits normal driving. Resolve the status before using the vehicle, unless a specific legal exception applies.

Test centre verification

What should you ask the MOT garage when the result is missing?

The test centre that carried out the MOT should be your first point of contact when a completed test does not appear in the official online record. It can investigate the test details and identify whether the result was recorded correctly.

Ask the garage these questions

  • Was the MOT test completed? Confirm that the vehicle underwent a completed MOT test rather than only a pre-MOT inspection or repair appointment.
  • What was the recorded result? Ask whether the test was recorded as a pass or a fail.
  • What is the MOT test number? Request the identifying test number so the specific result can be traced.
  • Which registration was recorded? Check that the result was entered against the correct vehicle.
  • What expiry date was issued? If the vehicle passed, ask the centre to confirm the recorded certificate expiry date.
  • Does the record require correction? If the details are wrong, ask which official correction procedure applies and whether the test centre can resolve the issue.

Understand what the garage may discover

The test is correctly recorded

The centre may confirm a completed test and provide details that help locate the correct record. Recheck the official history using the confirmed registration.

The details contain an error

The centre may identify an incorrect registration, mileage or other entry. The appropriate correction procedure depends on the type of mistake.

No completed test is confirmed

A garage booking, inspection or payment does not necessarily mean a completed statutory MOT test was recorded. Establish what service actually took place.

The centre cannot resolve the discrepancy

Ask for the available test information and follow the relevant DVSA guidance. Do not accept an invented expiry date or an informal replacement record.

Useful wording when contacting the garage

"My vehicle was tested at your centre on [test date], but I cannot find the result in the official GOV.UK MOT history. Please confirm the registration, MOT test number, recorded result and expiry date, and advise how the discrepancy can be corrected."

Should you pay for another MOT immediately?

Not necessarily. First establish whether the original test was completed and recorded. A missing online entry does not, by itself, establish that another test is required.

However, if no valid MOT can be established, the vehicle must not be used for ordinary road journeys that require a current MOT. The garage or DVSA should clarify the recording problem before you make further decisions.

If your MOT result is recorded incorrectly, the next step depends on the type of mistake. A missing test, incorrect registration, wrong mileage and disputed test result are not the same problem.

Start by contacting the MOT test centre. Explain the discrepancy and ask it to confirm the original test details. Where the garage cannot resolve the issue, follow the appropriate official DVSA correction process.

MOT law and road safety

Can you drive if your MOT is not showing online?

Whether you can legally drive depends on the vehicle's actual MOT status, not simply whether a particular website is displaying the information you expect. A temporary online access problem does not automatically invalidate a genuine MOT, but it also does not create an exemption for a vehicle without a valid certificate.

If the previous MOT has expired and a new pass cannot be confirmed, do not assume the vehicle is legal for ordinary road use. Contact the test centre and establish the result before driving.

Driving situations and their legal implications

Situation What it means Recommended action
Valid MOT confirmed, but online access is unavailable A website outage does not itself cancel an otherwise valid MOT. Keep the test details and verify the online record when the service becomes available.

Is there an MOT grace period?

There is no general grace period allowing a driver to continue using a vehicle normally after its MOT expires. A missing online result does not extend the previous certificate.

The limited exemptions for travelling to a pre-arranged MOT test or certain repair journeys should not be confused with permission for everyday driving.

What happens if you drive without a valid MOT?

In Great Britain, driving a vehicle that requires an MOT without a valid certificate can result in a fine of up to £1,000. Separate offences and potentially more serious penalties may apply where the vehicle is in a dangerous condition.

Roadworthiness is a separate legal requirement

A current MOT does not guarantee that a vehicle is safe to drive. Dangerous tyres, brakes, steering or other serious defects must be addressed regardless of the certificate's expiry date.

What about insurance and vehicle tax?

MOT validity, insurance and vehicle tax are separate requirements. A vehicle must satisfy the applicable legal requirements before being used on public roads.

Do not assume that insurance automatically becomes void solely because an MOT has expired. Cover depends on the policy wording and circumstances, while the legal obligation to hold a valid MOT remains separate.

Used car buying protection

Buying a used car when the latest MOT is not showing online

A missing recent MOT result is particularly important when buying a used car. If a seller advertises a vehicle as having a fresh MOT but the official history does not show the claimed test, investigate before paying.

A missing result does not automatically prove that the seller is dishonest. However, an unverified MOT pass should not be treated as established simply because the seller provides a photograph, invoice or verbal assurance.

Check the MOT claim before buying

Check the official history

Enter the registration on GOV.UK. Check the most recent result, mileage, expiry date and recorded defects.

Ask for the test details

Request the MOT test number, test date and details of the garage that carried out the inspection.

Verify unresolved discrepancies

Ask the seller to have the test centre confirm or correct the record before completing the purchase.

Warning signs to investigate

  • The seller advertises a fresh MOT, but only an older test appears on GOV.UK.
  • The MOT paperwork shows a different registration from the vehicle.
  • The seller cannot provide the test date or test number.
  • The claimed expiry date conflicts with the official record.
  • The seller refuses to allow the discrepancy to be checked.
  • The vehicle has unresolved serious defects or a concerning MOT history.

If your recent MOT is not showing online, check the official history, then contact the test centre with the registration, test date and MOT test number. Escalate genuine recording errors through the appropriate DVSA procedure.

Frequently Asked Questions

MOT Not Showing Online — Frequently Asked Questions

These answers cover the most common questions UK drivers ask when an MOT test result is missing, delayed or recorded incorrectly.

Why is my MOT not showing online?

Your MOT may appear to be missing because you entered the wrong registration, checked an outdated third-party service, encountered an online service problem or have a genuine MOT record discrepancy. Check the official GOV.UK MOT history first, then contact the test centre if the completed test is absent.

How long does it take for an MOT to show online?

Do not rely on a fixed waiting period. If the MOT has been completed but the result does not appear in the official history, contact the test centre to confirm the recorded test details. Check DVSA's service status if the online system is unavailable.

My car passed its MOT but it is not showing online. What should I do?

Check the registration and latest MOT history entry. Compare the result with your MOT paperwork, then ask the test centre to confirm the test number, registration, result and expiry date. If the discrepancy remains unresolved, follow the relevant official DVSA guidance.

Why has my MOT expiry date not changed?

An early MOT renewal can preserve the existing annual renewal anniversary. Check the full expiry date, including the year. If the latest pass is recorded but the expiry date has not advanced correctly, ask the test centre to investigate the discrepancy.

Can I drive if my MOT is not showing online?

It depends on whether the vehicle actually has a valid MOT and is roadworthy. An unavailable website does not cancel a valid MOT, but a missing result does not create permission to drive without one. If your previous MOT has expired and the new pass is unconfirmed, establish the status before ordinary road use.

Can the MOT garage correct a missing test result?

The garage that carried out the test should be contacted first. It can investigate the original test details and advise on the appropriate correction procedure. Some problems may require involvement from DVSA.

What if the wrong registration is on my MOT record?

Compare the MOT details with your vehicle registration and V5C. Contact the test centre to check which vehicle was recorded. Follow the official correction procedure rather than attempting to change the paperwork yourself.

How do I correct incorrect mileage on my MOT?

For MOT mileage errors in Great Britain, contact the test centre if the test was within the previous 28 days. For older tests, use the official DVSA correction process and provide the supporting evidence required by GOV.UK.

Does an MOT certificate prove the car is roadworthy?

No. An MOT certificate confirms the result of the statutory inspection at the time of testing. It does not guarantee that the vehicle will remain roadworthy afterwards. Drivers must still ensure that their vehicle is safe to use.

Should I buy a used car if its latest MOT is missing?

Do not rely on an unverified claim that the vehicle has passed. Check the official MOT history and ask the seller to resolve any discrepancy with the test centre before completing the purchase.

Does an MOT system outage mean I can drive without an MOT?

No. An online system outage does not create a general exemption from MOT requirements. A vehicle that requires an MOT must have a valid certificate unless a specific legal exception applies.

Where can I check whether the DVSA MOT service is working?

Check the official DVSA MOT service status page for information about incidents and planned maintenance.

Final Thoughts

Resolving a Missing MOT Result With Confidence

Discovering that your MOT is not showing online can be concerning, particularly when the vehicle has recently passed its test or the previous certificate has expired. The important thing is to identify the exact problem rather than assume that the MOT database will eventually update.

A missing test entry, incorrect expiry date, wrong registration and online service outage each require a different response. Checking the official history, reviewing the test paperwork and contacting the original test centre will usually establish where further investigation is needed.

Where the issue involves an incorrect official record, use the appropriate DVSA procedure. If you are unsure whether the vehicle currently has a valid MOT, resolve that uncertainty before ordinary road use.
