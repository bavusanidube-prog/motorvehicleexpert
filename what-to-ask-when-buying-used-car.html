What to Ask When Buying a Used Car in the UK

Questions to ask before viewing, test driving or paying for a second-hand car.

Quick Answer

Before buying a used car, ask about its service history, MOT advisories, outstanding finance, accident damage, mechanical faults, previous ownership and reasons for sale. Verify important claims independently rather than relying on the seller's answers alone.

Questions to Ask Before Viewing

  • How long have you owned the vehicle?
  • Why are you selling it?
  • Is the recorded mileage genuine and supported by records?
  • Are there any known mechanical or electrical faults?
  • Has the car been involved in an accident or insurance write-off?
  • Is there any outstanding finance?
  • Can I see the V5C registration certificate and service records?

Questions About MOT History

Ask whether previous MOT advisories have been repaired, particularly corrosion, brake, tyre and suspension defects. Check the vehicle's MOT history independently using the official GOV.UK service.

Questions About Servicing and Repairs

  • When was the vehicle last serviced?
  • Is there evidence of oil and filter changes?
  • Has the timing belt or chain required attention?
  • Have the clutch, gearbox or major engine components been repaired?
  • Are there invoices supporting significant repairs?

Questions During the Test Drive

  • Does the engine start smoothly from cold?
  • Are any dashboard warning lights illuminated?
  • Does the clutch or automatic gearbox operate correctly?
  • Are there unusual knocks, rattles or vibrations?
  • Does the car brake and steer without pulling to one side?
  • Does the engine reach a normal operating temperature?

If possible, arrange an independent vehicle inspection before committing to the purchase.

Questions Before Paying

  • Does the seller have the right to sell the vehicle?
  • Do the VIN and registration details match the paperwork?
  • Have outstanding finance and theft records been checked?
  • What is included in the agreed price?
  • What warranty or statutory rights apply to this sale?
  • Will you receive a dated receipt identifying the vehicle and seller?

When Should You Walk Away?

Be cautious if the seller refuses reasonable inspections, cannot explain discrepancies in the vehicle's history, pressures you into paying immediately, or provides inconsistent ownership information.
