Quick answer

How do you prepare your car for winter in the UK?

The best way to prepare your car for UK winter is to check the battery, antifreeze protection, tyre tread and pressures, engine starting performance, screen wash, wipers, exterior lights and heating system before freezing temperatures arrive. These checks help reduce breakdown risks and improve safety during cold, wet and icy conditions.

Cold weather reduces the available starting power of conventional 12V batteries, increases the effort required to turn some engines over and can expose existing electrical or mechanical faults. Low temperatures can also reduce tyre pressures, freeze unsuitable washer fluid and make worn wiper blades less effective. Wet roads, early darkness and road salt create additional safety and maintenance challenges.

What should you check first?

Start with the 12V battery, coolant antifreeze protection and tyre condition. These are among the most important systems to assess before the first frosty mornings.

What can cause a winter breakdown?

A weak battery, charging fault, cold-starting problem, coolant leak or neglected maintenance can leave a vehicle difficult or unsafe to use in cold weather.

When should you prepare?

October and November are sensible months for a pre-winter inspection in much of the UK. Do not wait for snow or freezing temperatures before checking known problems.

  • ✓Investigate slow cranking or difficult cold starting.
  • ✓Use winter-rated screen wash and inspect wiper blades.

  • ✓Clean road salt from vulnerable areas and inspect corrosion.

Mechanic's Tip

Do not assume a battery is healthy simply because the engine starts normally in mild weather. Cold temperatures reduce its available starting power, while thicker engine oil and increased mechanical resistance can demand more from the starter motor. A battery health test before winter can identify a weakening battery before it leaves you stranded.

Winter maintenance and MOT safety

A valid MOT certificate does not confirm that your battery is healthy, your antifreeze concentration is correct or your car is fully prepared for winter. The MOT assesses specific roadworthiness and environmental requirements at the time of the test. Routine winter maintenance remains the owner's responsibility.

Cold weather risks

Why Winter Weather Affects Your Car

Winter places additional demands on a vehicle's electrical, cooling, braking and tyre systems. In the UK, drivers often face a combination of freezing mornings, heavy rain, standing water, road salt, shorter daylight hours and occasional snow or ice. These conditions can expose developing faults that were less noticeable during warmer weather.

A car does not have to be old or poorly maintained to experience winter problems. Even a relatively modern vehicle can struggle if its battery is undercharged, tyre pressures are incorrect, coolant protection is inadequate or the windscreen washer system freezes.

Cold Temperatures

Cold weather reduces the available starting power of conventional lead-acid batteries and increases the resistance of engine oil to movement. Existing starting-system weaknesses may become more noticeable.

Starting risk

Wet and Icy Roads

Rain, frost, snow and ice reduce available tyre grip. Worn tyres, incorrect pressures and sudden driver inputs can make maintaining control more difficult.

Safety priority

Road Salt and Moisture

Salt and moisture can accelerate corrosion of exposed metal components, including brake pipes, suspension mountings, subframes and vulnerable underbody areas.

Corrosion risk

Reduced Visibility

Shorter daylight hours, dirty headlights, condensation, frozen washer jets and damaged wiper blades can make it harder to see hazards and other road users.

Visibility priority

Short Journeys

Repeated short trips may not replace the energy used during starting, especially when heated windows, blowers, lights and other electrical equipment are operating.

Battery charging

Existing Mechanical Faults

Weak starter motors, coolant leaks, poor electrical connections, worn tyres and heating faults may become more troublesome as winter conditions worsen.

Inspect early
Mechanic's Tip

Winter rarely creates every fault from nothing. It often reveals a weakness that was already developing. If your car has been slow to start, losing coolant, struggling to demist or showing intermittent electrical warnings, investigate the symptoms before the first prolonged cold spell.

1. Winter battery care

Car Battery Problems in Winter: Why Cold Weather Causes Breakdowns

A weak 12V battery is one of the most important causes of cold-weather starting trouble. The battery must provide sufficient electrical current to operate the starter motor and power the vehicle's electronic systems. When temperatures fall, conventional lead-acid batteries become less capable of delivering their available starting current.

At the same time, the engine may require more effort to turn over because engine oil becomes more viscous at low temperatures. This combination explains why a battery that starts the car normally in September may struggle during a frosty December morning.

Why Car Batteries Struggle in Cold Weather

1. Reduced Battery Output

Low temperatures slow the chemical reactions inside a conventional lead-acid battery, reducing the current available for starting.

2. Increased Starting Demand

Cold engine oil and additional mechanical resistance can make the engine harder to crank, increasing the load on the battery and starter motor.

3. Electrical Equipment

Heated screens, lights, cabin blowers and heated seats increase electrical demand, particularly during short journeys.

4. Incomplete Recharging

Frequent short trips, prolonged parking and existing charging faults can leave the battery with insufficient charge for reliable cold starting.

Signs Your Car Battery May Be Weak

Battery problems often develop gradually. Recognising the early warning signs can help you arrange testing before the vehicle refuses to start.

Slow Engine Cranking

The starter turns the engine more slowly than usual, particularly after the vehicle has been parked overnight.

Test battery

Clicking When Starting

A clicking sound without normal engine cranking may indicate insufficient battery power, poor electrical connections or a starter-system fault.

Diagnosis required

Dim Dashboard Lights

Dashboard lights that become unusually dim during starting can indicate excessive voltage drop under electrical load.

Check voltage

Repeated Jump-Starting

A battery that repeatedly needs assistance may be discharged, deteriorating or affected by a charging-system or electrical-drain fault.

Find the cause

Stop-Start Unavailable

Some vehicles disable automatic stop-start operation when battery charge or condition is unsuitable. Temperature and other operating conditions can also affect it.

Check battery health

Battery Warning Light

A battery or charging warning light while the engine is running may indicate an alternator, charging-control, belt or electrical-system problem rather than the battery alone.

Investigate promptly
Important Battery Warning

If the battery or charging-system warning light remains illuminated while driving, do not assume that replacing the battery will solve the problem. The alternator or another charging component may have failed. Electrical power can eventually become insufficient to operate essential vehicle systems. Arrange prompt diagnosis and stop safely if the vehicle begins to malfunction.

How Long Does a Car Battery Last?

Many conventional 12V car batteries provide approximately three to five years of service, although some last longer and others fail earlier. Battery life depends on temperature exposure, charging conditions, journey patterns, electrical demand and vehicle specification.

Age alone does not prove that a battery requires replacement. A properly performed battery health test is more useful than replacing a functioning battery solely because it has reached a particular age.

Mechanic's Tip

Check the battery terminals as well as the battery itself. Loose connections, corrosion around terminals and damaged earth straps can produce symptoms similar to a weak battery. A technician should assess voltage drop and connection condition before condemning the battery.

Battery diagnostics

How to Test Your Car Battery Before Winter

A battery test should establish both its state of charge and its ability to deliver starting current. A simple voltage reading can provide an initial indication, but a battery can show an acceptable resting voltage and still fail under the heavy load of engine starting.

Step-by-Step Winter Battery Check

1. Inspect the Battery

With the vehicle safely switched off, look for obvious damage, corrosion, loose terminals or signs of battery leakage. Do not touch damaged or leaking battery components.

2. Check Resting Voltage

Where safe and accessible, use a suitable multimeter to measure the rested battery voltage according to the vehicle manufacturer's instructions.

3. Perform a Health Test

A garage can use a compatible battery tester to assess starting-current capability, internal condition and whether charging or replacement is appropriate.

4. Check the Charging System

If the battery repeatedly becomes discharged, test the alternator, charging controls, electrical connections and possible parasitic current drain.

Understanding 12V Battery Voltage Readings

The following figures are approximate guides for a conventional, rested 12V lead-acid battery. Actual readings vary with battery type, temperature, recent charging and vehicle electrical activity.

Resting voltage Possible interpretation Recommended action
About 12.6–12.8V Generally consistent with a well-charged conventional lead-acid battery. Carry out a health test if starting performance is poor.
About 12.4V Battery may be partially discharged. Check charging history and recharge appropriately if required.
About 12.2V Indicates a lower state of charge under typical conditions. Investigate undercharging and test battery condition.
About 12.0V or below Battery may be substantially discharged. Recharge and test; investigate repeated discharge.

These readings are not replacement thresholds. A proper battery assessment also considers the vehicle's battery management system, starting current, temperature and charging behaviour. On some modern vehicles, battery terminals may not be the recommended testing points.

Do Short Journeys Damage Your Car Battery?

Repeated short journeys can gradually leave a battery undercharged. Starting an engine requires a burst of electrical energy, and the charging system needs sufficient operating time to replenish that energy. During winter, headlights, heated windows, ventilation fans and other equipment can increase the load.

Cars Used for Short Journeys

Frequent trips of only a few minutes may not provide enough charging time, especially if the battery is already weak or the vehicle has significant electrical demand.

Monitor battery charge

Cars Parked for Long Periods

Alarm systems, electronic modules and natural battery self-discharge can reduce available charge during extended periods without driving.

Consider suitable maintenance charging

2. Antifreeze and coolant

Antifreeze and Coolant: Protect Your Engine Against Freezing

Engine coolant protects the cooling system against freezing, overheating and internal corrosion. In most conventional liquid-cooled vehicles, coolant contains a mixture of water, antifreeze and corrosion-inhibiting additives. Using the wrong mixture or allowing coolant protection to deteriorate can expose the engine to expensive damage during freezing weather.

Coolant is not simply a fluid that prevents overheating in summer. Its antifreeze properties are equally important during winter, particularly when a vehicle is parked outdoors overnight.

What Happens If Engine Coolant Freezes?

Radiator Damage

Freezing coolant can expand and damage radiator passages, plastic end tanks and vulnerable cooling-system connections.

Potential major repair

Hose and Component Damage

Ice formation can place excessive pressure on hoses, housings and other cooling-system components.

Inspect before restarting

Engine Damage

Severe freezing can damage engine castings or other components, potentially resulting in costly mechanical repairs.

Serious risk

What Antifreeze Concentration Do You Need in the UK?

For many conventional glycol-based cooling systems, a mixture containing approximately 50% suitable antifreeze concentrate and 50% demineralised water provides freeze protection around −35°C to −37°C. Exact protection depends on the product and formulation.

However, a 50/50 mixture is not suitable for every vehicle or every coolant product. Some manufacturers specify different concentrations, and ready-mixed coolants are designed to be used without further dilution. Always follow the manufacturer's instructions.

Coolant situation What it means Correct action
Correct approved 50/50 mixture Often provides substantial freeze protection in conventional glycol-based systems. Confirm the specification and actual protection level.
Approved ready-mixed coolant Already diluted to the manufacturer's or product's stated concentration. Use as directed without unnecessary dilution.
Unknown coolant concentration Freeze protection cannot be established by appearance alone. Arrange a suitable coolant concentration test.
Incorrect or incompatible coolant May reduce corrosion protection or cause compatibility problems. Identify the correct specification and obtain professional advice.
Repeated coolant loss May indicate a leak or another cooling-system fault. Investigate the cause rather than repeatedly topping up.

How to Check Your Coolant Before Winter

1. Allow the Engine to Cool

Park safely on level ground and allow the engine and cooling system to cool fully before inspecting the coolant reservoir.

2. Locate the Expansion Tank

Identify the correct coolant expansion tank using the vehicle handbook. Do not confuse it with the washer-fluid or brake-fluid reservoir.

3. Check the Cold Level

Compare the coolant level with the cold MIN and MAX markings where fitted. The correct checking procedure may differ between vehicles.

4. Confirm Freeze Protection

Use a compatible antifreeze tester or ask a garage to measure protection and verify that the coolant meets the vehicle's specification.

Can You Top Up Coolant With Water?

The correct approved coolant mixture should be used for routine topping up. Adding plain water reduces antifreeze concentration and may reduce corrosion protection. In an emergency, water may be permitted temporarily on some vehicles, but this depends on manufacturer guidance and must not be treated as a permanent winter solution.

Coolant colour alone does not reliably identify its chemistry or compatibility. Two products with similar colours may have different specifications, while compatible products may be sold in different colours.

Important Coolant Safety Warning

Never remove a pressurised coolant cap while the engine is hot. Hot coolant and steam can escape suddenly and cause serious burns. If the engine overheats, stop safely, switch it off where appropriate and allow it to cool. Do not continue driving an overheating engine simply because the coolant reservoir still contains fluid.

Warning Signs of Cooling-System Problems

Coolant Level Keeps Falling

Repeated coolant loss may indicate leaking hoses, a radiator, water pump, expansion tank or another cooling-system component.

Leak investigation

Temperature Warning Light

A high-temperature warning may indicate overheating, insufficient coolant circulation or another serious fault.

Stop safely

Coolant Bubbling

Bubbling or coolant being forced out of the expansion tank can result from overheating, trapped air, pressure-control faults or combustion gases entering the system.

Diagnosis required

Heater Blowing Cold

Unexpected loss of cabin heat can be associated with low coolant, trapped air, thermostat problems or other heating-system faults.

Check cooling system

Visible Coolant Leaks

Wet hoses, staining around joints or fluid beneath the vehicle may indicate an external coolant leak.

Find leak source

Sweet Smell or Vapour

A sweet smell or unusual vapour may indicate coolant escaping onto hot components or leaking inside the heater system.

Inspect promptly
Mechanic's Tip

If coolant repeatedly falls below the minimum mark, do not keep topping it up without investigating the cause. A cooling-system pressure test can help locate external leaks, while further diagnostic checks may be needed if pressure rises abnormally or coolant is being lost internally.

3. Winter tyres and road grip

Winter Tyre Checks: Tread Depth, Pressures and Snow Grip

Tyres are the only parts of your vehicle that remain in direct contact with the road. During winter, they must cope with cold surfaces, standing water, slush, snow and ice. Worn tyres or incorrect pressures can reduce available grip and make steering, braking and acceleration less predictable.

UK winters often involve more wet-road driving than prolonged snowfall. This makes tread depth, tyre condition and suitable tyre selection important even for drivers who rarely encounter snow.

What Is the Legal Tyre Tread Depth in the UK?

For most passenger cars, the legal minimum tread depth is 1.6mm across the central three-quarters of the tread around the entire circumference of the tyre. Tyres must also be free from other defects that make them unsafe.

Although 1.6mm is the legal minimum, replacing tyres before reaching this point can be sensible. Around 3mm is a practical winter replacement consideration, particularly where wet-road performance is important. It is not a separate legal winter limit.

  • ✓Check several positions around the circumference.

  • ✓Look for cuts, bulges, exposed cords or other damage.
  • ✓Check for objects embedded in the tread.
  • ✓Inspect the spare tyre or tyre inflation kit where provided.

Why Tyre Pressures Change in Cold Weather

Tyre pressure generally falls as the temperature decreases. A tyre that was correctly inflated during warm weather may therefore show a lower pressure on a cold morning without having a puncture.

Check pressures when the tyres are cold and use the vehicle manufacturer's recommended settings. These are usually shown in the handbook, door aperture or another manufacturer-specified location. Some vehicles require different pressures depending on load and tyre size.

Mechanic's Tip

Never inflate tyres to the maximum pressure printed on the sidewall as a substitute for the vehicle manufacturer's recommendation. The sidewall figure is not the normal operating pressure for every vehicle. Also investigate any tyre that repeatedly loses pressure.

Winter Tyres vs All-Season Tyres vs Summer Tyres

Choosing the correct tyre depends on where you drive, typical temperatures, snowfall, vehicle requirements and whether you can store a second set of wheels or tyres.

Tyre type Main advantage Winter limitation Best suited to
Summer tyres Designed primarily for mild and warm conditions. Generally less capable in snow and severe cold than suitable winter tyres. Drivers mainly using roads in milder conditions.
All-season tyres Offer a compromise across a broad range of weather conditions. Performance varies by model and may not match specialist winter tyres in demanding snow conditions. Many UK drivers seeking one set of tyres year-round.
Winter tyres Designed to improve performance in cold weather and winter conditions. Seasonal tyre changes and storage may be required. Drivers regularly encountering snow, ice or sustained cold conditions.

What Does the Three-Peak Mountain Snowflake Symbol Mean?

The three-peak mountain snowflake symbol, often abbreviated to 3PMSF, identifies tyres that have met a defined snow-traction performance test. It is found on qualifying winter tyres and some all-season tyres.

The marking does not guarantee grip on every icy surface. Driving speed, road conditions, tyre condition and driver inputs remain important.

How to Drive Safely in Snow and Ice

1. Accelerate Gently

Apply power progressively to reduce the chance of wheelspin. Use suitable driving modes where the manufacturer provides them.

2. Increase Following Distance

Allow considerably more stopping distance than on a dry road. Ice can dramatically reduce available grip.

3. Avoid Sudden Inputs

Brake, steer and accelerate smoothly. Abrupt movements can exceed the limited grip available on slippery surfaces.

4. Assess the Journey

Check weather and road conditions before setting off. Postpone non-essential travel when conditions are particularly hazardous.

Important Winter Tyre Safety Advice

Four-wheel drive does not eliminate the need for suitable tyres or careful driving. It may help a vehicle move away on slippery surfaces, but it cannot overcome the limited grip available when braking or cornering on ice.

Mechanic's Tip

If one tyre is wearing significantly faster than the others, do not assume wheel alignment is the only possible cause. Incorrect pressure, suspension wear, damaged components and driving conditions can all contribute. Diagnose the wear pattern before replacing the tyre so the same problem does not quickly return.

4. Cold-weather starting problems

Car Struggling to Start in Cold Weather: Causes and Checks

A car that starts normally during warmer weather may crank slowly, hesitate or refuse to start on a freezing morning. Cold temperatures place additional demands on the battery, starter motor and engine, but they can also expose existing ignition, fuel-system and sensor faults.

The most useful first step is to identify exactly what happens when you try to start the vehicle. An engine that turns over slowly requires a different diagnostic approach from one that cranks at normal speed but fails to fire.

Why Cars Struggle to Start on Cold Mornings

Weak 12V Battery

Cold temperatures reduce the available starting power of conventional lead-acid batteries. An ageing or undercharged battery may no longer supply sufficient current.

Test battery health

Thicker Engine Oil

Engine oil becomes more viscous as temperatures fall. Oil that does not meet the manufacturer's cold-temperature requirements can increase cranking resistance.

Check oil specification

Starter Motor Problems

A worn starter motor, poor earth connection or excessive voltage drop may cause slow or intermittent cranking.

Starting-system test

Diesel Glow Plug Faults

Faulty glow plugs or their control system can make some diesel engines difficult to start in cold conditions.

Glow plug diagnosis

Fuel Delivery Problems

Low fuel pressure, fuel contamination, a restricted filter or another delivery fault may prevent reliable starting.

Fuel-system checks

Engine Sensor Faults

Faults involving engine speed, temperature or other control inputs can affect starting and fuel delivery.

Diagnostic scan

Cold-Starting Symptoms and What They May Mean

Cold-start symptom Possible causes Recommended next step
Engine turns over slowly Weak battery, poor connections, starter fault or excessive mechanical resistance. Test battery condition, voltage drop and starter performance.
Rapid clicking when starting Low battery voltage, poor connections or a starting-system fault. Check battery state of charge and electrical connections.
Single click but no cranking Starter motor, solenoid, electrical connection or battery problem. Arrange starting-circuit diagnosis.
Engine cranks normally but will not start Ignition, fuel delivery, engine sensor or control-system fault. Check fault codes and diagnose fuel and ignition systems as applicable.
Diesel starts after prolonged cranking Glow plug system, battery, fuel supply or another engine fault. Test glow plug operation and starting conditions.
Engine starts then stalls Fuel supply, air intake, engine management or idle-control problem. Investigate stored faults and live operating data.

Mechanic's Tip

Listen carefully to the engine during the first starting attempt. Slow cranking points towards insufficient starting power or increased resistance. Normal-speed cranking without combustion suggests a different problem. This distinction can prevent unnecessary battery replacement.

Does Engine Oil Become Too Thick in Winter?

Engine oil becomes more resistant to flow as temperatures decrease. Modern multigrade oils are designed to provide suitable performance across specified temperature ranges, but the correct viscosity and manufacturer approval remain essential.

The first number in a multigrade oil classification, such as 0W-30 or 5W-30, relates to specified low-temperature performance. However, two oils with the same viscosity grade may not have the same manufacturer approvals.

Always use oil that meets the exact specification in your vehicle handbook. Do not change to a thinner oil simply because winter is approaching unless the manufacturer permits it.

Diesel Glow Plugs and Winter Starting

Diesel engines rely on compression ignition, and many use glow plugs to assist starting and combustion in cold conditions. Modern systems may also operate glow plugs after the engine starts to support smoother running and emissions control.

A defective glow plug system can cause prolonged cranking, rough running immediately after starting or increased exhaust smoke. These symptoms can also result from other faults, so testing is preferable to replacing every glow plug without diagnosis.

  • ✓Follow the manufacturer's glow plug starting procedure.
  • ✓Check for relevant engine-management fault codes.
  • ✓Test glow plug operation and the control circuit.
  • ✓Check battery and starter performance before blaming the glow plugs.
  • ✓Use fuel suitable for the prevailing conditions and follow fuel-system maintenance guidance.

What NOT to Do When Your Car Will Not Start

Do Not Crank Continuously

Repeated prolonged starting attempts can overheat the starter motor, drain the battery and place unnecessary stress on electrical components.

Stop and assess

Do Not Keep Jump-Starting

Repeated jump-starting without investigating the underlying fault may leave you stranded again and can conceal charging or electrical-drain problems.

Find the cause

Do Not Use Unapproved Starting Sprays

Starting sprays can be hazardous and may damage some engines, particularly where glow plugs or other intake-heating systems are fitted.

Avoid unsafe methods

Do Not Ignore Abnormal Noises

Grinding, severe knocking or other unusual mechanical noises during starting may indicate a fault requiring immediate investigation.

Professional diagnosis
Jump-Starting Safety

Always follow the manufacturer's jump-starting procedure. Incorrect connections can damage electrical systems or cause injury. Hybrid and electric vehicles may have specific 12V battery procedures and restrictions. Never attempt to jump-start or work on high-voltage components.

When Should You Call for Breakdown Assistance?

Arrange professional help if the engine repeatedly fails to start, the battery will not retain charge, the starter makes abnormal noises or you suspect a fuel-system or electrical fault. If the vehicle is in an unsafe location, prioritise personal safety and follow the appropriate breakdown guidance.

Mechanic's Tip

If the car starts after being jump-started, that does not automatically prove the battery itself is defective. A charging-system test and battery health assessment can help determine whether the battery is weak, simply discharged or affected by another electrical fault.

5. Screen wash and wipers

Winter Screen Wash and Wiper Blades: Keep Your Windscreen Clear

Winter roads often leave windscreens covered in salt, dirt and spray. At the same time, frost can freeze unsuitable washer fluid and cause worn wiper blades to smear across the glass. Maintaining the washer and wiper systems is essential for safe visibility.

Plain water is not suitable as the only fluid in a winter washer system. It can freeze inside the reservoir, pump, hoses or washer jets, preventing the system from cleaning the windscreen when you need it.

What Strength Winter Screen Wash Should You Use?

Choose a screen wash product with freeze protection suitable for the temperatures you may encounter. The stated protection depends on the formulation and dilution ratio.

Freeze protection Typical use Important consideration
−5°C Milder winter conditions with occasional light frost. May not provide sufficient protection during colder spells.
−10°C More frequent freezing temperatures. Check the product's required dilution ratio.
−20°C Colder conditions or a greater freeze-protection margin. Protection applies only at the stated concentration.

A concentrate advertised as providing protection down to −20°C may offer substantially less protection once diluted. Read the product instructions carefully and consider any remaining fluid already in the reservoir.

How to Prepare Your Washer System for Winter

1. Check the Reservoir

Identify the correct washer-fluid reservoir and check the level. Some vehicles provide an electronic low-fluid warning rather than a visible level marking.

2. Use Suitable Screen Wash

Fill with a winter-rated product mixed according to the instructions and suitable for the expected temperatures.

3. Check Washer Jets

Confirm that the washer jets operate correctly and direct fluid onto the windscreen. Do not operate a frozen pump repeatedly.

4. Inspect the Wipers

Check the rubber for splits, hardening, missing sections and poor contact with the glass.

Signs Your Wiper Blades Need Replacing

Streaking

The blades leave lines or unwiped areas even when the windscreen is clean.

Inspect blades

Juddering

The blades skip or vibrate across the glass instead of moving smoothly.

Check rubber and glass

Damaged Rubber

Visible splits, torn edges or sections separating from the blade can prevent effective wiping.

Replace defective blades

What to Do If Your Wipers Are Frozen to the Windscreen

  • ✓Switch off automatic wiper operation before leaving the vehicle overnight where appropriate.
  • ✓Use a suitable de-icer or the vehicle's windscreen defrosting system.
  • ✓Allow ice around the blades to thaw before operating them.
  • ✓Clear snow and ice from the glass before setting off.
  • ✓Inspect the blades if they have been pulled away from frozen glass.
Never Pour Boiling Water on a Frozen Windscreen

Sudden temperature changes can crack automotive glass, particularly if it already contains chips or other damage. Use an appropriate scraper, suitable de-icer and the vehicle's demisting or defrosting system instead.

Screen Wash, Wipers and the UK MOT

Windscreen washers and wipers are assessed under the applicable MOT inspection requirements. Defects affecting the driver's ability to see the road can result in an MOT failure, depending on the defect and its effect on visibility.

The MOT does not routinely certify that your screen wash has sufficient antifreeze protection for the coming winter. That is a maintenance check you should perform separately.

Mechanic's Tip

If the washer pump can be heard but little or no fluid reaches the glass, inspect the system for frozen fluid, blocked jets, disconnected hoses or leaks. Do not keep operating a pump against a frozen or blocked system because this may cause damage.

6. Lights and winter visibility

Winter Lights, Windscreen Demisting and Visibility Checks

Shorter daylight hours, heavy rain, fog, road spray and low winter sun can make visibility particularly challenging. Dirty headlights, defective bulbs, condensation and poor windscreen demisting can reduce your ability to see hazards and make your vehicle less visible to other road users.

Which Car Lights Should You Check Before Winter?

Dipped and Main Beam

Check that the headlights operate correctly and that their lenses are clean and undamaged. Incorrect aim can reduce visibility or dazzle other drivers.

Brake Lights

Confirm that the brake lights illuminate when the pedal is pressed. Ask someone to help or use a safe reflective surface.

Indicators and Hazard Lights

Check all direction indicators and hazard warning lights. Unusual flashing speed can indicate a fault on some vehicles.

Rear Fog Lights

Ensure the rear fog lights operate correctly. Use them only when visibility is seriously reduced, and switch them off when conditions improve.

Rear and Number Plate Lights

Inspect rear position lamps and number plate illumination so the vehicle remains appropriately visible in darkness.

Reversing Lights

Check reversing lights where fitted. A fault may affect manoeuvring visibility and may have MOT implications depending on vehicle age and requirements.

How to Demist Your Windscreen Quickly and Safely

Windscreen misting occurs when moisture in the cabin condenses on cooler glass. During winter, damp clothing, wet carpets, passengers and water leaks can increase interior humidity.

1. Select Windscreen Demist

Direct the ventilation towards the windscreen using the manufacturer's demisting setting.

2. Use Warm Air

Use the heating system as appropriate to help warm the glass and remove condensation.

3. Use Air Conditioning

Where available and operating, air conditioning can help remove moisture from cabin air, even during colder weather.

4. Introduce Fresh Air

Switch off prolonged air recirculation if it is trapping humid air inside the vehicle.

7. Road salt and corrosion

How to Protect Your Car From Road Salt and Winter Corrosion

Road salt helps manage icy roads, but salt and moisture can accelerate corrosion of exposed metal components. During winter, salty water and road grime collect around wheel arches, suspension components, brake pipes, underbody panels and other vulnerable areas.

Corrosion is not simply a cosmetic problem. Severe deterioration affecting structural components, brake pipes or suspension mountings can compromise vehicle safety and lead to an MOT failure.

Which Car Parts Are Most Vulnerable to Road Salt?

Brake Pipes

Metal brake pipes can corrode where protective coatings deteriorate, particularly around clips, bends and exposed underbody sections.

Safety-critical

Suspension Components

Suspension arms, springs, mountings and subframes can develop corrosion after repeated exposure to moisture and road salt.

Inspect condition

Wheel Arches and Sills

Mud, salt and trapped moisture can encourage corrosion around wheel arches, sill edges and vulnerable body seams.

Clean regularly

Underbody and Subframes

Exposed metal, damaged coatings and areas where water collects may deteriorate over time.

Check underneath

Exhaust Components

Exhaust pipes, brackets and joints can corrode through exposure to moisture, heat cycles and road contamination.

Inspect for leaks

Door Seals and Drainage

Dirt, trapped water and deteriorating seals can encourage moisture accumulation and freezing around doors.

Clean and inspect

How to Reduce Road Salt Damage

1. Wash the Car Regularly

Remove road salt and grime after heavily salted journeys, paying attention to lower body panels and wheel arches.

2. Clean Accessible Underbody Areas

Use suitable cleaning equipment or a professional underbody wash. Avoid directing high-pressure water at vulnerable electrical or mechanical components.

3. Inspect Paint and Coatings

Repair damaged paint and investigate deteriorating protective coatings before corrosion spreads.

4. Check Drainage and Seals

Remove trapped leaves and dirt from accessible drainage areas and inspect door seals for damage or freezing problems.

Surface Rust vs Structural Corrosion

Not every rusty component is immediately unsafe. Light surface corrosion may be limited to an outer layer, while deeper corrosion can weaken metal, reduce component strength or affect critical mounting points.

Can Road Salt Corrosion Cause an MOT Failure?

Yes. Corrosion can contribute to an MOT failure when it affects components or structural areas covered by the inspection standards. The outcome depends on the location, extent and effect of the deterioration.

Corrosion affecting brake pipes, suspension components, prescribed structural areas or important mountings can be particularly serious. An MOT advisory for corrosion should not automatically be dismissed as harmless simply because the vehicle passed the test.

Do Not Hide Serious Corrosion With Underseal

Applying underseal over heavily corroded metal without first assessing and treating the damage can conceal deterioration rather than repair it. Safety-critical corrosion requires proper inspection and, where necessary, an appropriate repair or component replacement.

Mechanic's Tip

Pay particular attention to brake pipe clips, suspension mounting points and areas where mud and salt become trapped. These locations may deteriorate more quickly than exposed panels that are regularly washed. If corrosion is mentioned on an MOT advisory, inspect it before the next winter rather than waiting for the following test.

What Should You Do If Your Car Breaks Down in Winter?

Your first priority is personal safety. If the vehicle develops a serious problem, move to a safe location where possible and follow the appropriate breakdown guidance for the type of road.

On a motorway, if you can safely leave the vehicle and reach a place of safety behind a barrier, do so using the side away from moving traffic where possible. Keep away from the carriageway and contact the appropriate recovery or emergency service.

If you cannot safely leave the vehicle, remain inside with your seat belt fastened and contact emergency assistance. Follow instructions from emergency services.

Motorway Breakdown Safety

Never place a warning triangle on a motorway. Do not attempt roadside repairs in a live traffic lane or put yourself at risk retrieving equipment. If the vehicle is stranded in a dangerous position or there is an immediate danger, contact emergency services.

Mechanic's Tip

Keep your winter emergency kit in a secure but accessible location. Heavy or loose objects should not be left where they could become dangerous during sudden braking or a collision. Check the kit before longer winter journeys and replace used or expired supplies.

9. Pre-winter preparation

When Should You Prepare Your Car for Winter in the UK?

October and November are sensible months for most UK drivers to complete winter maintenance checks. Preparing early gives you time to identify faults, arrange repairs and replace worn components before persistent frost or freezing conditions arrive.

However, winter weather does not arrive on the same date everywhere. Drivers in colder, rural or exposed locations may need to prepare earlier, while sudden frost can affect almost any part of the UK.

What Should You Check Before the First Frost?

2. Coolant Protection

UK MOT and winter safety

Winter Car Checks and the MOT: What Is Actually Tested?

An MOT assesses specified roadworthiness and environmental requirements at the time of the test. It can identify safety-related defects that become particularly important during winter, but it is not a complete maintenance inspection or a guarantee that a vehicle will remain roadworthy until its next test.

Battery health, antifreeze freeze protection and general winter readiness are not routinely established by a standard MOT. These require separate maintenance checks.

Vehicle system MOT relevance Additional winter maintenance

Can Winter-Related Problems Cause an MOT Failure?

Yes. Worn tyres below the legal limit, defective essential lighting, ineffective wipers, unsafe braking components and serious corrosion can result in MOT defects. The classification depends on the relevant inspection criteria and severity of the problem.

The current MOT system uses Minor, Major and Dangerous defect classifications, alongside advisories where appropriate. A Major or Dangerous defect causes the vehicle to fail its MOT. A Dangerous defect identifies a direct and immediate risk to road safety or a serious environmental impact.

Minor Defect

A defect that does not have a significant effect on vehicle safety or the environment, although repair is still needed.

Repair recommended

Major Defect

A defect that may affect vehicle safety, put other road users at risk or have an environmental impact.

MOT failure

Dangerous Defect

A defect presenting a direct and immediate risk to road safety or a serious environmental impact.

Do not drive until repaired

Does a Valid MOT Mean Your Car Is Safe for Winter?

No. A valid MOT certificate does not confirm that every component is free from faults or that the vehicle is fully prepared for freezing weather. The driver remains responsible for keeping the vehicle roadworthy between tests.

A car may pass its MOT but still have a weak battery, insufficient antifreeze protection, unsuitable winter screen wash or poor cabin heating. These problems should be addressed through routine maintenance and targeted inspections.

Important MOT Safety Reminder

Do not continue driving a vehicle with a dangerous defect simply because its previous MOT certificate remains valid. A valid MOT does not make an unsafe vehicle legal or safe to use. Follow the applicable roadworthiness requirements and arrange appropriate repair or recovery.

Mechanic's Tip

Review the advisory items on your previous MOT certificate before winter. Brake pipe corrosion, worn tyres, suspension deterioration and developing structural corrosion may worsen over time. An advisory is an early opportunity to investigate a problem before it becomes a failure.

Frequently Asked Questions

Winter Car Care FAQs

These are common questions UK drivers ask about preparing their cars for cold weather, battery problems, antifreeze, tyres, winter visibility and MOT checks.

When should I prepare my car for winter in the UK?

October and November are sensible months to complete winter checks before regular frost arrives. However, prepare earlier if freezing temperatures are forecast or you regularly travel through colder or exposed areas.

Why does my car battery struggle in cold weather?

Cold temperatures reduce the available starting power of conventional lead-acid batteries while the engine may require more effort to crank. An ageing, discharged or deteriorating battery is more likely to struggle during winter.

How do I know if my car battery needs replacing before winter?

Slow cranking, repeated discharge and unreliable starting are warning signs, but they do not prove that the battery needs replacing. A battery health test and charging-system assessment can help identify the actual cause.

What antifreeze should I use in my car?

Use coolant that meets the exact specification required by your vehicle manufacturer. Coolant types are not universally interchangeable, and colour alone is not a reliable way to identify compatibility.

Does a 50/50 antifreeze mixture protect against freezing?

A 50/50 mixture of suitable ethylene-glycol-based antifreeze and water commonly provides protection to approximately −35°C to −37°C, depending on the product. Always follow the manufacturer's specified concentration and coolant requirements.

Can I use plain water instead of antifreeze in winter?

Plain water does not provide adequate freezing protection and can contribute to corrosion and cooling-system damage. Use the correct coolant mixture specified by the vehicle manufacturer.

What is the legal minimum tyre tread depth in the UK?

For most cars, the legal minimum is 1.6 mm across the central three-quarters of the tread around the entire circumference. Drivers may choose to replace tyres earlier, particularly before winter, because wet-weather performance can deteriorate as tread wears.

Do I legally need winter tyres in the UK?

Winter tyres are not generally compulsory for ordinary car use in the UK. However, suitable winter or all-season tyres may offer advantages in cold, wet, snowy or icy conditions, depending on their design and the circumstances.

Why does my diesel car struggle to start on cold mornings?

Possible causes include a weak battery, glow plug system faults, poor starter performance, fuel delivery problems or other engine-management faults. Testing is needed to identify the cause rather than replacing components based on symptoms alone.

What strength screen wash should I use in winter?

Choose a winter-rated screen wash with freeze protection suitable for the temperatures you expect. Products may offer protection such as −5°C, −10°C or −20°C, but the actual protection depends on the formulation and dilution ratio.

Can I pour boiling water on a frozen windscreen?

No. Sudden temperature changes may crack the glass, particularly where chips or existing damage are present. Use an appropriate ice scraper, automotive de-icer and the vehicle's defrosting system instead.

Why do my car windows keep misting up inside?

Interior condensation is commonly caused by moisture in the cabin meeting cold glass. Wet carpets, damp clothing, air recirculation, water leaks or poor ventilation can make the problem worse.

Can road salt damage my car?

Yes. Road salt and moisture can accelerate corrosion of exposed metal components, including brake pipes, suspension parts, subframes and bodywork. Regular cleaning and appropriate inspections can help reduce deterioration.

Does an MOT check battery health and antifreeze strength?

No. A standard MOT does not routinely measure battery starting capacity or coolant freeze protection. These are separate maintenance checks, even though an MOT assesses other winter-relevant safety items such as tyres, lights, brakes and wipers.

How much does a winter car health check cost in the UK?

Some garages offer basic winter checks free of charge, while others may charge approximately £20 to £60 or more depending on what is included. Confirm whether battery testing, coolant protection measurements and detailed inspections are part of the service.

What should I keep in my car during winter?

Useful supplies include an ice scraper, de-icer, warm clothing, a blanket, torch, charged phone, power bank, water, snacks and breakdown contact details. Adapt your kit to the weather, journey and personal needs.

Final Thoughts

Prepare Your Car Before Winter Problems Become Breakdowns

Winter car care is about preventing avoidable breakdowns and keeping your vehicle safe in difficult conditions. A healthy battery, correctly protected cooling system, suitable tyres and effective visibility equipment can make a substantial difference when temperatures fall.

The best approach is to check the vehicle before cold weather arrives, investigate developing faults early and prioritise safety-critical repairs. A valid MOT is important, but it does not replace routine maintenance or confirm that the car is fully prepared for winter.
