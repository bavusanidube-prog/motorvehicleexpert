Quick Answer

What should you do when a dashboard warning light appears?

First identify the warning symbol and colour. Then check whether the light is steady or flashing and whether the car has developed another symptom such as loss of power, overheating, smoke, unusual noise, poor braking or heavy steering.

Step 1

Identify the colour

Red warnings generally require faster action. Amber warnings usually indicate a fault that needs diagnosis. Green and blue lights are commonly information indicators.

Step 2

Check how the car feels

Loss of braking, heavy steering, overheating, rough running, smoke or significant power loss can make the warning much more urgent than the dashboard symbol alone suggests.

Step 3

Diagnose the affected system

Use the warning as a starting point. Fault codes, inspection and further testing may still be needed before the actual component or root cause can be confirmed.

Stop driving when the warning indicates an immediate safety or engine-damage risk

A red oil-pressure warning, severe overheating, serious brake warning, major steering fault, strong fuel leak or another condition that makes the car unsafe should not be treated as something to monitor until later.

Warning Light Colours

What do dashboard warning light colours mean?

Warning-light colour is a useful first indication of urgency, but it should never be considered on its own. The meaning of the symbol, whether it is flashing and how the vehicle behaves are equally important.

Urgent

Red warning light

Red commonly indicates a serious condition or something requiring immediate driver attention. Oil pressure, braking, overheating and some charging warnings can belong in this category.

Typical Priority: High
Check soon

Amber or yellow warning light

Amber usually indicates a fault or condition that requires diagnosis. The vehicle may continue to operate, but the warning should not be ignored simply because the car still feels normal.

Typical Priority: Medium
Information

Green or blue dashboard light

Green and blue symbols commonly indicate that a system is active, such as dipped headlights, indicators or main beam, rather than warning of a fault.

Typical Priority: Information
Flashing usually matters more than colour alone

A flashing engine-management light, for example, can indicate an active misfire that risks catalytic-converter damage. Always consider whether a warning is steady or flashing and whether the vehicle has developed another symptom.

Colour

Red generally needs faster attention than amber, while green and blue are commonly informational.

Behaviour

A flashing light, multiple lights appearing together or a warning that changes while driving can provide extra diagnostic information.

Symptoms

Poor braking, overheating, smoke, heavy steering, misfiring or major power loss can make any warning more urgent.

Diagnostic Process

How should a dashboard warning light be diagnosed?

A proper warning-light diagnosis should move from evidence to testing. The symbol tells you which system or condition the vehicle has detected, but the root cause still needs to be confirmed before parts are replaced.

1. Identify the exact warning

Confirm the symbol, colour and whether it is steady, flashing or appears only under specific conditions.

2. Check for safety symptoms

Before deeper diagnosis, check for poor braking, overheating, oil-pressure concerns, fuel leakage, heavy steering or anything else that could make further driving unsafe.

3. Record other symptoms

Note power loss, rough running, smoke, vibration, poor starting, noise and whether the fault is intermittent.

4. Read diagnostic trouble codes

Retrieve stored and pending codes where relevant. Record them before clearing anything because they can provide useful diagnostic evidence.

5. Inspect the affected system

Check wiring, connectors, fluid levels, hoses, mechanical condition and obvious damage related to the warning and symptom.

6. Use live data or measurements

Compare sensor information, voltages, pressure, temperature, wheel-speed data or other system values with what should be happening.

7. Test the suspected cause

Prove whether the component, wiring, mechanical system or operating condition is actually responsible rather than replacing the first part associated with the code.

8. Repair the confirmed fault

Repair wiring, correct fluid or mechanical problems, service the affected system or replace the failed component only after the diagnosis supports it.

9. Verify the repair

Recheck warning status, fault codes and system operation, then confirm that the original symptom no longer appears under the same conditions.

Do not diagnose by code description alone

A code mentioning an oxygen sensor, wheel-speed sensor or another monitored component does not automatically prove that component has failed. Wiring, supply voltage, contamination, air leaks, mechanical faults and incorrect system operation can all produce misleading-looking readings.

Safe-To-Drive Guidance

Is it safe to keep driving with a warning light on?

Whether you can continue driving depends on the warning itself, the affected system and how the vehicle is behaving. A low-priority reminder is very different from a red oil pressure warning, severe overheating or a braking fault.

Use the severity guide below as a practical framework. It is not a substitute for inspection when the warning relates to a safety-critical system or the car has developed a serious new symptom.

Lower urgency

Usually safe to monitor briefly

Examples may include a service reminder or another non-safety message where the vehicle operates normally and no fault symptoms are present.

Typical Risk: Lower
Medium urgency

Drive carefully and diagnose soon

A steady amber engine light, ABS warning, TPMS warning or charging concern may still allow limited driving, but the underlying fault should be investigated.

Typical Risk: Medium
High urgency

Stop safely or avoid further driving

Red oil-pressure warnings, serious brake faults, severe overheating, strong fuel leaks and major steering-assistance loss can create immediate safety or engine-damage risks.

Typical Risk: High

Warning Lights & MOT

Can dashboard warning lights fail an MOT?

Yes. Some warning lights can affect the MOT when they show a malfunction in a system included in the inspection. Engine management, ABS, airbags, electronic stability control and tyre-pressure monitoring can all be relevant depending on the vehicle and applicable test requirements.

Turning a warning light off without fixing the fault is not the same as repairing the vehicle. If the control unit detects the problem again, the warning is likely to return.

Used Car Buying

Should you buy a used car with a warning light on?

An unexplained warning light should be treated as a buying risk rather than a minor inconvenience. Some faults are inexpensive to repair, but others can involve ABS modules, airbags, diesel emissions systems, charging faults, engine-management problems or major mechanical issues.

The key question is not whether the seller says the warning is “just a sensor”. The important question is whether the fault has been properly diagnosed and whether there is evidence showing what actually needs repair.

Lower buying risk

Fault already diagnosed

The seller provides a recent diagnostic report, supporting repair quotation and clear evidence that the cause has been identified.

Buying Risk: Lower
Medium buying risk

Intermittent or unexplained warning

The warning appears occasionally, the seller has no diagnostic evidence or the light returns after being cleared.

Buying Risk: Medium
High buying risk

Safety or engine warning currently illuminated

Airbag, ABS, brake, oil-pressure, overheating, engine, DPF or other major warning lights can hide expensive or safety-critical repair requirements.

Frequently Asked Questions

Car Warning Lights FAQs

These questions cover the most common concerns UK drivers have about red, amber and flashing dashboard warnings, safe-to-drive decisions, MOT implications and diagnostic next steps.

What should I do if a red warning light comes on?

A red warning light usually needs urgent attention. Stop safely if the warning relates to oil pressure, brakes, overheating, charging, steering or if the car feels unsafe.

Can I drive with an amber warning light?

An amber warning light usually means a fault needs attention soon. Careful driving may be possible if the car feels normal, but diagnosis should not be delayed.

Can dashboard warning lights fail an MOT?

Yes. Warning lights for systems such as ABS, airbag, engine management, TPMS, ESC or braking faults can affect an MOT result.

Is a flashing warning light serious?

A flashing warning light is usually more urgent than a steady one. A flashing engine management light, for example, can indicate a misfire or emissions damage risk.

Does a warning light always mean an expensive repair?

No. Some warning lights are caused by relatively simple faults, while others indicate more serious problems. The warning identifies a system or condition that requires investigation rather than proving the final repair cost.

What does an amber warning light mean?

An amber or yellow warning usually means the vehicle has detected a fault or condition that needs attention. The car may still drive normally, but the warning should be diagnosed rather than ignored.

What does a green or blue dashboard light mean?

Green and blue dashboard lights are often information indicators showing that a system such as the exterior lights or main beam is operating rather than indicating a fault.

What should I do if the engine management light comes on?

Check whether the engine light is steady or flashing and whether the vehicle has lost power, started misfiring or developed another symptom. A steady light normally requires diagnosis soon, while a flashing light can indicate a more urgent active fault.

Can I drive with the battery warning light on?

A battery warning light usually indicates a charging-system problem rather than simply a weak battery. The vehicle may continue running temporarily, but it can eventually stop once stored battery energy is depleted.

Should I drive with the oil pressure warning light on?

A genuine oil pressure warning should be treated as urgent because insufficient oil pressure can damage the engine very quickly. Stop safely and investigate the cause before continuing normal driving.

What does an ABS warning light mean?

An ABS warning indicates that the anti-lock braking system has detected a fault. Normal braking may remain, but anti-lock assistance can be unavailable and the warning can have MOT implications.

What does an airbag warning light mean?

An airbag warning light indicates a fault in the supplementary restraint system. Airbags or seat-belt pretensioners may not operate as intended, and the warning can affect an MOT.

Why is my tyre pressure warning light on?

The tyre pressure warning can appear because one or more tyres are underinflated, there is a puncture, temperatures have changed significantly or the TPMS itself has a fault. Check actual tyre pressures before resetting the system.

What does the traction control warning light mean?

A traction control light may flash normally while the system is actively controlling wheel slip. If it stays illuminated, the traction or stability-control system may have detected a fault.

What does a DPF warning light mean?

A DPF warning usually means the diesel particulate filter is becoming restricted or regeneration has not completed successfully. Continued short journeys or an underlying engine fault can make the condition worse.

Can I clear a dashboard warning light myself?

Some warnings can be reset after the underlying condition has been corrected, but clearing a warning or fault code without fixing the cause does not repair the vehicle and the warning may return.

Should I buy a used car with a warning light on?

A used car with an unexplained warning light should be treated cautiously. Diagnose the fault before buying because the cause may range from a minor sensor issue to an expensive emissions, braking, airbag or engine-management repair.
