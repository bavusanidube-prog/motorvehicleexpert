Quick Answer

What Are MOT Advisories?

An MOT advisory is information recorded by the tester about a condition that could become more serious in the future or an item that is near, but has not yet reached, a level that justifies MOT rejection. The vehicle can still pass its MOT with advisories, but the advisory should be monitored and repaired when necessary.

This is different from a Minor defect. Minor, Major and Dangerous are defined MOT defect categories. A Minor defect can still result in a pass, while Major and Dangerous defects result in failure. An advisory is best understood as additional information about developing condition rather than a separate level in a mandatory failure sequence.

Advisory

The item could become more serious in future. Monitor its condition and repair it if necessary.

Current Condition

The important question after the MOT is whether the component remains serviceable, legal and safe as mileage and time increase.

Repair Priority

Brakes, tyres, steering, suspension and structural components normally deserve more attention than low-consequence maintenance items because deterioration can affect roadworthiness.

An advisory is not the same as a failed MOT

A passing MOT can contain advisories and Minor defects. Major and Dangerous defects cause an MOT failure. The wording and current physical condition of the component therefore matter more than simply seeing the word “advisory” on the certificate.

The mechanic-style way to read an advisory

1. Identify the component

Is the advisory about a tyre, brake, suspension component, structural area, fluid leak, exhaust or another system?

2. Assess the consequence

Ask what could happen if the condition gets worse — reduced braking, loss of grip, instability, structural weakness, leakage or simply increased repair cost.

3. Measure the condition

Tread depth, remaining brake material, component play, corrosion extent and leak severity give more useful information than guessing from the advisory wording alone.

4. Decide the action

Inspect urgently, plan a repair or monitor with evidence according to safety significance, remaining serviceable condition and deterioration rate.

The most useful question is not “How old is the advisory?”

Ask how much serviceable condition remains, how quickly the component is deteriorating and what the consequence would be if it worsened. Two cars with similar advisory wording can require very different repair decisions.

Advisory Meaning

What Does an MOT Advisory Actually Mean?

An MOT advisory is additional information recorded by the tester about a component or condition that does not currently justify an MOT rejection but is worth bringing to the vehicle owner's attention. In practical terms, it usually means something is worn, deteriorating, corroded, damaged, leaking or approaching a condition where further deterioration may become important.

The important point is that an advisory describes the condition found during the MOT inspection. It does not provide a guaranteed remaining lifespan. A tyre, brake pipe, suspension joint or corroded structural area can continue deteriorating immediately after the vehicle leaves the test station.

It passed today

The condition did not justify rejection at the time it was inspected. That does not mean the component is new, perfect or guaranteed to remain acceptable until the next test.

It deserves attention

The tester has identified something worth monitoring. Depending on the component, that may mean checking it periodically, measuring wear or arranging a repair before deterioration continues.

It can deteriorate

Wear, corrosion, leakage and mechanical play do not stop because the MOT has finished. An advised condition can later become a defined MOT defect or make the vehicle unroadworthy.

Mechanic insight: read the component, not just the word “advisory”

A lightly deteriorated exhaust and an advised brake pipe may both appear under advisories, but their possible consequences are very different. The system affected, current severity and likely deterioration matter much more than treating every advisory as equal.

Why some advisories matter more than others

A useful way to prioritise an advisory is to ask what would happen if that condition became worse. Deterioration affecting braking, tyre grip, steering, suspension control or structural strength can have a direct road-safety consequence. Other conditions may mainly create noise, inconvenience, emissions problems or additional repair expense.

Safety consequence

Brake pipes, tyres, braking components, steering, suspension joints and important structural areas deserve closer attention because failure can affect the driver's ability to control or stop the vehicle.

Maintenance consequence

Oil seepage, early exhaust deterioration and some lower-risk wear conditions may allow more planning time, but they should still be monitored because a small problem can become a larger repair.

Advisories are useful maintenance evidence

Keep previous MOT results and compare them. If the same advisory repeatedly appears over several tests, investigate whether the condition has genuinely remained stable or whether maintenance has simply been postponed.

MOT Defect Categories

Advisory vs Minor, Major and Dangerous MOT Defects

One of the most important distinctions in an MOT result is that advisory is not another step in the official Minor, Major and Dangerous defect hierarchy. The categories describe different outcomes and must not be treated as interchangeable.

Information

Advisory

A condition that is worth bringing to the owner's attention but is not sufficiently deteriorated to justify rejection at the time of the test.

Can still pass
Defined Defect

Minor

A defined MOT defect with no significant effect on the safety of the vehicle or impact on the environment. It should still be repaired as soon as possible.

MOT pass
Defined Defect

Major

A defect that may affect the safety of the vehicle, put other road users at risk or have an environmental impact. A Major defect causes the MOT to fail.

MOT failure
Highest Severity

Dangerous

A defect that represents a direct and immediate risk to road safety or has a serious environmental impact. The vehicle should not be driven until the defect has been repaired.

Do not drive
Do not call something a “Dangerous advisory”

Dangerous is an official defect category, not a stronger form of advisory. A component that was previously advised can later deteriorate until it meets Dangerous criteria, but at that point it is recorded as a Dangerous defect rather than a Dangerous advisory.

Does an advisory have to become Minor before it becomes Major?

No. Vehicle deterioration does not have to follow a fixed Advisory → Minor → Major → Dangerous sequence. The category recorded at a future MOT depends on the condition actually found and the applicable defect wording at that time.

For example, a component could be advised while still serviceable and later deteriorate enough to meet a Major criterion. A sudden breakage, leakage or severe deterioration could also create a much more serious condition between MOT tests.

Advisory today

Condition does not currently justify rejection but is worth monitoring.

Condition worsens

Mileage, corrosion, impact damage, heat, contamination or normal wear changes the physical condition.

Reassessed later

The component is judged against the defect criteria that apply to the condition found during the later inspection.

A pass is a test result, not a 12-month condition guarantee

The MOT confirms that the vehicle met the relevant test requirements when it was examined. Vehicle owners remain responsible for keeping the vehicle roadworthy between tests.

Tester Interpretation

How MOT Testers Record Advisories

MOT testers do not simply invent a severity description for every fault. Testable components are assessed against the current MOT inspection manual and the defects available within the MOT testing system. Where different defect categories exist for the same component, the tester must select the category that reflects the nature and severity of the condition found.

Where an item is not sufficiently deteriorated to justify rejection, an advisory option may be available. DVSA guidance also identifies advising items close to, but not yet at, the point of test failure as good practice.

1. Inspect

The tester examines the component using the inspection method and limitations applicable to the MOT test.

2. Compare

The condition is compared with the relevant defect wording and severity criteria in the inspection manual.

3. Categorise

Where a defined defect applies, the correct Minor, Major or Dangerous category is selected according to severity.

4. Advise where appropriate

If deterioration does not justify rejection but is worth recording for the vehicle presenter, an advisory may be used.

Tester judgement still matters

The manual gives defect wording and categories, but many inspections also require the tester's knowledge, experience and judgement when deciding how severe a condition is. This is one reason two visually similar components can receive different results when their actual condition differs.

What an MOT tester does not normally determine

Exact remaining mileage

An MOT advisory does not normally tell you that a component has exactly 500, 1,000 or 5,000 miles of life remaining.

Full workshop diagnosis

An MOT is a statutory inspection, not a complete dismantling or diagnostic investigation of every system on the vehicle.

Future failure date

The tester records the condition seen at the inspection. They cannot guarantee the date or mileage at which deterioration will cross a future MOT threshold.

Do not translate advisory wording into a guaranteed lifespan

Statements such as “it passed so it will last another year” or “an advisory always gives you several thousand miles” are unsafe assumptions. Remaining life depends on the component, severity, vehicle use, environment and deterioration rate.

Repair Priority

How Should You Prioritise MOT Advisories?

There is no reliable way to prioritise every advisory simply by counting how many are listed on the certificate. One serious developing brake or structural condition may deserve more attention than several low-consequence maintenance advisories.

A better method is to combine safety consequence, current severity, deterioration rate, symptoms and repair consequence.

Priority 1

Safety-critical

Brakes, brake pipes, tyres, steering, important suspension components and structural areas move to the top of the list because deterioration can affect control of the vehicle.

Inspect first
Priority 2

Rapidly deteriorating

A component that has visibly worsened, developed leakage, increased play, lost material or started producing symptoms deserves attention even if it was only advisory at the MOT.

Repair soon
Priority 3

Stable maintenance item

Some lower-consequence advisories may be suitable for planned maintenance if the condition remains legal, serviceable and stable.

Monitor properly

Five questions to ask about every advisory

1. What system is affected?

Brakes, tyres, steering, suspension and structural items deserve different treatment from convenience or cosmetic issues.

2. What happens if it fails?

Think about the consequence: loss of braking pressure, reduced grip, instability, fumes, fluid loss or simply a maintenance repair becoming more expensive.

3. Is it getting worse?

Compare the current condition with previous inspections, MOT history, measurements and photographs where available.

4. Are symptoms present?

Grinding, pulling, vibration, knocking, fluid loss, exhaust fumes or unstable handling can make an advised condition more urgent.

5. What will delay cost?

Waiting can turn a routine wear-item replacement into secondary damage, welding work, breakdown or a future MOT failure.

Then choose the action

Decide between immediate inspection, planned repair or documented monitoring rather than simply waiting until the next MOT.

Symptoms override the convenience of waiting

If an advised vehicle develops weak braking, a brake-fluid leak, unstable steering, serious tyre damage, severe suspension movement, structural deterioration, significant fluid loss or exhaust fumes entering the cabin, reassess the vehicle immediately rather than relying on the earlier MOT result.

Repair order matters when money is limited

If several advisories are present, start with conditions that can affect roadworthiness or create expensive secondary damage. Cosmetic improvement should not take priority over braking, tyres, steering, suspension or structural integrity.

These are the systems where the condition itself should be assessed, rather than deciding that the vehicle is safe simply because the MOT certificate shows a pass.

Brakes, tyres, steering, suspension and structure are directly connected to the vehicle's ability to stop, steer, maintain road contact and remain structurally sound. Deterioration in these areas can therefore change the roadworthiness of the vehicle even before another MOT becomes due.

Brake Pipe Advisories

Corroded Brake Pipe MOT Advisories

Brake pipe corrosion deserves a high position on any MOT advisory repair list because rigid hydraulic pipes carry the pressure used to operate the brakes. Surface deterioration does not automatically mean a pipe has failed the MOT, but increasing corrosion can eventually reduce the pipe wall thickness enough to become unacceptable.

The correct assessment depends on the actual condition of the pipe, not simply whether rust is visible. MOT guidance requires testers to consider the extent of corrosion, damage, leakage, support and the risk of failure.

Advisory Stage

Developing Corrosion

A pipe may be advised where corrosion or deterioration is worth recording but has not reached the level required for rejection.

Inspect and monitor
MOT Major

Excessive Corrosion

A rigid brake pipe that is damaged or excessively corroded is a Major defect. An inadequately supported pipe can also become Major if it is likely to become detached or damaged.

MOT failure
MOT Dangerous

Imminent Failure or Leak

A rigid brake pipe at imminent risk of failure or fracture is Dangerous. Leakage from a hydraulic brake pipe or connection is also a Dangerous defect.

Do not drive
Current MOT corrosion threshold

DVSA guidance states that chafing, corrosion or damage reducing the wall thickness of a typical rigid hydraulic brake pipe by around one-third justifies rejection. Because this can be difficult to determine accurately during an MOT inspection, testers are told to give the benefit of the doubt where they are unsure whether the pipe is sufficiently deteriorated.

What should be checked after a brake pipe advisory?

Corrosion Depth

Distinguish light surface oxidation from deeper pitting, scaling or deterioration that has reduced the pipe's remaining strength.

Pipe Routing

Check clips, supports, bends and areas where the pipe may rub against the body, suspension or other components.

Connections & Leaks

Inspect unions and surrounding pipework for dampness or fluid leakage. Hydraulic leakage is substantially more serious than an early corrosion advisory.

Never judge brake pipe condition from MOT history alone

Corrosion can continue beneath dirt, coatings and retaining clips. If a brake pipe has been advised repeatedly, its present physical condition should be inspected rather than assuming the previous pass still describes it accurately.

Tyre Advisories

Tyre Wear MOT Advisories

Tyre advisories should be treated as both an MOT issue and a roadworthiness issue because tyres provide the vehicle's contact with the road. Tread depth can continue reducing quickly after an MOT, particularly on vehicles covering high mileage or suffering from alignment, suspension or inflation problems.

An advisory does not mean the tyre is already illegal. It means the condition recorded during the inspection deserves attention before further wear or damage changes the situation.

Monitor

Tread Approaching Limit

Measure the tread rather than guessing visually. A tyre that was above the threshold at the MOT may reach it before the next annual inspection.

Diagnose

Uneven Wear

Inner-edge or outer-edge wear can indicate alignment, suspension, pressure or geometry problems. Replacing the tyre without identifying the cause may allow the new tyre to wear in the same way.

Inspect

Damage & Deterioration

Tread depth is only part of tyre condition. Cuts, bulges, exposed cords, structural damage and incorrect fitment can be more important than the amount of tread remaining.

UK tread-depth threshold

For most cars and light vans, the primary tread grooves must be at least 1.6 mm deep across the central three-quarters of the breadth of the tread and around the entire outer circumference. Different requirements apply to certain vehicle classes, so this guide is focused on ordinary UK cars and light vehicles.

Why a tyre advisory can become urgent

Mileage

A high-mileage vehicle can consume the remaining tread far sooner than a lightly used vehicle.

Geometry

Toe or camber problems, worn suspension parts and incorrect pressure can accelerate localised wear.

Damage

A pothole strike, kerb impact or developing sidewall defect can change tyre condition immediately after an MOT.

Mechanic check

Measure several positions across and around the tyre. Do not rely only on the most visible part of the tread, particularly where an advisory has already identified wear.

Brake Wear Advisories

Brake Pad and Brake Disc MOT Advisories

Brake pads and discs are normal wear components, but an advisory should not be translated into a fixed number of remaining miles. Wear rate depends on vehicle weight, driving conditions, brake design, corrosion, caliper operation and how much friction material remains.

Brake Pads

Pad Wear Advisory

An advised pad may still have usable friction material, but its thickness should be checked and compared across the axle. Uneven wear can point towards a sticking caliper, slider or piston rather than simple normal wear.

Brake Discs

Disc Wear or Corrosion Advisory

Discs can develop scoring, corrosion, pitting, thickness wear or uneven friction surfaces. The correct repair decision depends on actual disc condition rather than the presence of an advisory alone.

Wear Indicator

Major

A brake lining or pad worn down to its wear indicator is a Major defect.

Below 1.5 mm

Dangerous

A brake lining or pad worn below 1.5 mm is categorised as Dangerous.

Contaminated

Major

Brake lining or pad contamination with oil, grease or similar material is a Major defect.

Brake wear warning light distinction

The current MOT manual specifically states that an illuminated brake wear indicator is not itself a reason for MOT failure. The physical condition of the pads is what must be assessed against the applicable criteria.

Exact MOT distinctions for brake discs

Significantly Worn

Major

A brake disc or drum that is significantly and obviously worn is a Major defect.

Insecure or Fractured

Dangerous

A disc or drum that is insecure, fractured or otherwise likely to fail is Dangerous.

Contamination

Major

A brake disc or drum contaminated with oil, grease or similar material is a Major defect.

Important disc-thickness distinction

DVSA guidance states that a brake disc or drum must be significantly worn before it should be rejected. Being below the manufacturer's recommended wear limit is not, by itself, an MOT reason for rejection. Manufacturer service limits remain important for maintenance and repair decisions, but they must not be described as an automatic MOT failure threshold.

Diagnose pads and discs as an assembly

If pads are wearing unevenly, discs are badly scored or one wheel runs hotter than the other, inspect caliper movement and hydraulic operation rather than automatically replacing only the friction parts.

Suspension Advisories

Suspension and Shock Absorber MOT Advisories

Suspension advisories can cover bushes, joints, arms, anti-roll-bar components, springs, subframes, shock absorbers and related mountings. Their importance depends on which component is affected, how much deterioration exists and whether vehicle control is beginning to change.

Bushes & Joints

Some movement is normal in compliant rubber bushes. The issue is whether wear has become excessive or the bonding and flexible material have seriously deteriorated.

Arms & Links

Inspect for corrosion, distortion, insecure attachment, cracking and excessive play, particularly on highly stressed suspension components.

Shock Absorbers

Assess security, leakage, bushes and damping effectiveness rather than assuming any visible dampness automatically means MOT failure.

Shock absorber MOT distinctions

Not Rejection

Slight Seepage

Slight fluid seepage forming a film on a shock absorber is specifically stated in the MOT manual as not a reason for rejection.

Major

Severe Leakage

A shock absorber damaged to the extent that it does not function, or showing signs of severe leakage, is a Major defect.

Major

Negligible Damping

A shock absorber with negligible damping effect is a Major defect even if leakage is not the main visible symptom.

Missing or insecure shock absorbers are different defects

A shock absorber insecurely attached to the chassis or axle is Major. A shock absorber that is missing or likely to become detached is Dangerous. A missing shock absorber is rejected on this basis where shock absorbers were fitted as standard.

Suspension wear thresholds

Excessively Worn Joint

Major

An excessively worn suspension pin, bush, joint or bearing is a Major defect.

Likely to Detach

Dangerous

A suspension pin, bush, joint or bearing likely to become detached is Dangerous.

Component Failure Risk

Dangerous

A suspension arm or related component that is fractured or likely to fail can meet Dangerous criteria.

Symptoms that increase urgency

Knocking, unstable handling, steering changes, abnormal body movement, uneven tyre wear or increasing play should prompt inspection even where the earlier MOT result contained only an advisory.

Corrosion Advisories

Structural Corrosion MOT Advisories

Corrosion is one of the easiest MOT subjects to misunderstand because the visible size of the rusty area does not necessarily determine its seriousness. The important questions are where the corrosion is located, what function the affected structure performs and how much strength or continuity has been lost.

A relatively small amount of corrosion in an important load-bearing area can matter more than extensive cosmetic rust on a non-structural panel.

Location

Corrosion close to brake, steering, suspension or other safety-related mountings deserves particular attention.

Remaining Strength

Flaking, perforation, severe thinning and loss of structural continuity are more important than surface colour alone.

Vehicle Construction

The same body panel can have different structural importance on different vehicle designs, so corrosion must be judged in the context of how the vehicle is constructed.

The 30 cm prescribed-area principle

The MOT inspection includes important load-bearing or supporting structure around mountings for testable components. For relevant prescribed areas, this includes load-bearing or supporting structure within 30 cm of the component mounting location.

Major and Dangerous structural distinctions

Major

Rigidity Significantly Reduced

General vehicle structure corroded enough for the rigidity of the assembly to be significantly reduced is a Major defect. Similar Major criteria apply where strength in relevant prescribed areas is significantly reduced.

Dangerous

Control Is Threatened

Structural deterioration can become Dangerous where the weakening is severe enough that steering, braking or control of the vehicle is likely to be adversely affected, depending on the affected area.

Surface rust and structural corrosion are not the same thing

Do not assume every rusty sill or subframe automatically fails an MOT. Equally, do not dismiss corrosion because the visible area looks small. Structural significance, depth, location and remaining strength determine the real risk.

Used-car warning

Repeated structural corrosion advisories deserve careful inspection before buying. Establish whether deterioration has progressed, whether previous welding has been completed correctly and whether further repair is likely.

Fluid Leak Advisories

Oil Leak MOT Advisories

An oil leak advisory can describe anything from developing seepage that needs monitoring to a condition that may become environmentally significant or create a safety concern. The useful diagnostic question is not simply whether oil is visible, but where it is coming from, how quickly it is escaping and where the fluid is travelling.

Seepage

Dampness around a gasket or seal should be cleaned, traced and monitored so that an increasing leak can be identified early.

Active Leak

Fresh drops, increasing oil consumption or oil reaching the undertray and underside justify locating the source and planning repair.

Safety Consequence

Oil reaching hot exhaust components, braking surfaces or the road can create consequences beyond the original gasket or seal failure.

Current MOT fluid-leak threshold

For vehicles subject to the fluid-leak inspection, DVSA guidance states that a vehicle should fail where a relevant fluid leak creates a pool on the floor within five minutes that is more than 75 mm in diameter, or where multiple leaks collectively escape at the same rate.

Major

Excessive Fluid Leak

Fluid leaking excessively and likely to harm the environment or pose a safety risk to other road users is a Major defect.

Dangerous

Continuous Serious Leak

Fluid leaking continuously and likely to pose a serious risk to road safety is Dangerous.

Not every visible vehicle fluid is assessed under this MOT failure rule

The MOT manual specifically states that leaks such as engine coolant, screen wash and fluid required for Selective Catalyst Reduction are not reasons for rejection under the general fluid leak section. Different components can have their own test requirements elsewhere in the manual.

Trace before replacing parts

Oil can travel a considerable distance from its original source. Clean the affected area and identify the highest fresh point of leakage before assuming that the lowest oily component is the failed part.

Exhaust Advisories

Exhaust Corrosion MOT Advisories

Exhaust systems commonly develop surface corrosion because they are exposed to water, road salt, heat cycles and condensation. Corrosion itself does not automatically mean the exhaust should fail the MOT. The important issues are whether deterioration has affected security, leakage, noise, fumes or emissions.

Surface Corrosion

External rust may remain serviceable where the metal retains sufficient integrity and the exhaust remains secure and gas-tight enough for the test.

Developing Weakness

Flaking seams, thinning metal, corroded brackets and deteriorated joints can progress into leakage or insecurity.

Functional Failure

Once corrosion creates a major leak, insecurity, excessive noise or harmful fumes, the MOT decision is based on that resulting defect rather than rust alone.

Exact MOT exhaust distinctions

Major

Major Leak or Insecure

An exhaust system with a major leak or that is insecure is a Major defect.

Major

Fumes Entering Cabin

Exhaust fumes entering the passenger cabin are categorised as a Major defect.

Dangerous

Fumes Endangering Health

Where exhaust fumes cause a danger to the health of people on board, the defect is Dangerous.

Excessive Noise

Major

Exhaust noise unreasonably above the level expected from a similar vehicle with a standard silencer in average condition can result in a Major defect.

Noise System Insecure

Major

An insecure part of the noise-suppression system is Major.

Likely to Detach

Dangerous

A part of the noise-suppression system likely to become detached is Dangerous.

Exhaust leaks can also affect the emissions test

For relevant vehicles, an induction or exhaust leak that could affect emissions levels is a Major defect. A badly leaking exhaust can also make emissions readings unreliable and may need repair before meaningful emissions results can be obtained.

Exhaust corrosion is not automatically an MOT failure

This distinction matters. Rust or surface corrosion should not be described as an automatic failure. The MOT outcome depends on the resulting condition — particularly leakage, security, excessive noise, fumes, emissions impact or a component likely to detach.

Inspect the complete exhaust, not only the rusty silencer

Check joints, flexi sections, clamps, hangers, mounting rubbers, heat shields and pipework. A visually rusty rear box may remain serviceable while a less obvious joint or flexi section is already leaking.

Roadworthiness

Does a Current MOT Mean the Car Is Roadworthy?

No. A current MOT certificate does not guarantee that a vehicle will remain roadworthy until the certificate expires. The MOT records the condition found during the inspection, but tyres continue wearing, brake components deteriorate, corrosion progresses and mechanical faults can develop after the test.

The driver remains responsible for making sure the vehicle is safe to use whenever it is driven. This distinction is especially important where an MOT result already contains advisories involving tyres, brakes, steering, suspension, structural areas or other safety-critical systems.

MOT Result

Condition At Test

The MOT result records whether the vehicle met the relevant test requirements when it was examined.

Driver Responsibility

Condition Today

The driver must still make sure tyres, brakes, lights, steering and the rest of the vehicle remain safe and legal between MOT tests.

Advisory

Developing Condition

An advisory gives you useful warning that a component deserves monitoring because its condition may change before the next MOT.

A valid MOT does not override a dangerous vehicle condition

If a tyre becomes illegal, a brake pipe starts leaking, suspension becomes unsafe or another defect makes the vehicle unroadworthy, the remaining time on the MOT certificate does not make that condition acceptable.

Signs that an advisory needs reassessing

New Symptoms

Grinding, knocking, pulling, vibration, excessive bouncing, warning lights, fluid loss or exhaust fumes mean the condition should be reassessed rather than left until the next test.

Visible Deterioration

Reducing tyre tread, increasing corrosion, worsening leakage or greater mechanical play shows that the component is no longer in exactly the condition recorded at the MOT.

Change In Vehicle Behaviour

Poor braking, unstable handling, steering changes or unusual movement can indicate that a previously developing condition is now affecting the vehicle in use.

Use the MOT as a snapshot, not a maintenance schedule

A good maintenance plan responds to condition and symptoms throughout the year. Waiting for the next MOT to identify every problem can allow inexpensive developing faults to become larger repairs.

Repair Decisions

When Should You Repair an MOT Advisory?

There is no single repair deadline that applies to every MOT advisory. The correct timing depends on the component, present severity, rate of deterioration, annual mileage, symptoms and the consequence if the component fails.

Instead of asking whether an advisory can be left until the next MOT, divide the work into three practical categories: inspect urgently, plan the repair or monitor with evidence.

Inspect Urgently

Safety-Critical Condition

Brake pipe corrosion, rapidly reducing tyre tread, significant brake wear, worsening suspension play or important structural corrosion deserves early inspection.

Highest priority
Plan Repair

Developing Wear

Brake discs, pads, suspension bushes, exhaust deterioration, shock absorber wear and manageable leaks can often be repaired in a planned way before they become failures.

Budget and repair
Monitor

Stable Lower-Risk Condition

Some slowly changing conditions may be monitored if they remain serviceable and roadworthy, but monitoring should involve actual checks rather than simply ignoring the advisory.

Recheck condition

  • ✓ Look for new fluid spots below the vehicle.
  • ✓ Listen for changing exhaust noise.
  • ✓ Watch for new braking or suspension symptoms.

  • ✓ Brake pipe corrosion and hydraulic leaks.

Do not use the MOT advisory as the diagnosis

The advisory identifies a condition worth attention. Before expensive parts are replaced, confirm the actual failed or worn component. Suspension knocks, uneven brake wear and oil leaks in particular can originate from several different sources.

Future MOT Risk

Will an MOT Advisory Fail the Next MOT?

It can, but there is no rule saying that every advisory automatically becomes a failure at the next test. The future result depends on how the condition changes and whether it meets the relevant defect criteria when the vehicle is examined again.

Some conditions can remain stable for a considerable period. Others can cross a failure threshold quickly because of mileage, corrosion, mechanical wear or an unexpected component failure.

Higher Risk

Wear Near a Threshold

Tyres with little remaining tread and heavily worn brake friction components have less remaining margin before further wear becomes important.

Higher Risk

Progressive Corrosion

Brake pipes, structural areas and exhaust components can continue deteriorating between tests, particularly through wet and salted winter conditions.

Variable Risk

Mechanical Wear

Bushes, joints and shock absorbers can remain serviceable for a period or deteriorate more quickly depending on road conditions, mileage and component quality.

Signs the next MOT risk is increasing

Repeated Advisory

The same condition appearing on consecutive MOTs suggests that repair has been deferred or deterioration has remained present.

Stronger Wording

Increasing severity in successive MOT descriptions can indicate that the physical condition is getting worse.

Symptoms Appear

Noise, vibration, leakage, handling changes or other symptoms provide additional evidence that the condition deserves reassessment.

Do not repair only for the test

The objective should be to keep the vehicle roadworthy throughout the year. Repairing developing safety faults before the MOT is often better than waiting until the vehicle crosses a formal rejection threshold.

Driving & Legal Position

Can You Drive With an MOT Advisory?

An advisory does not by itself prohibit the vehicle from being driven. A vehicle can pass its MOT while advisories are recorded. However, that does not remove the driver's responsibility to make sure the vehicle remains roadworthy.

The practical question is therefore not simply “Does the certificate say pass?” but “Is the vehicle still safe and legal in its current condition?”

Advisory Only

Driving may be possible where the vehicle remains roadworthy and no separate legal defect has developed.

Condition Worsens

Reassess the vehicle if tread falls, leakage increases, suspension play becomes excessive or another developing fault changes materially.

Unsafe Condition

Do not rely on the MOT certificate where the current physical condition makes the vehicle unsafe or otherwise unroadworthy.

Failed MOT distinction

If a vehicle later fails an MOT, GOV.UK states that it can only be taken away under the normal failed-MOT rules where its existing MOT is still valid and no Dangerous defect has been recorded. Even then, the vehicle must still meet minimum roadworthiness standards.

Dangerous means do not drive until repaired

A Dangerous defect represents a direct and immediate road-safety risk or serious environmental impact. It should not be confused with an advisory simply because the same component may have been advised on an earlier test.

Insurance

Do MOT Advisories Affect Car Insurance?

An MOT advisory does not automatically invalidate a car insurance policy. Advisories are common on vehicles that have passed their MOT, and the existence of an advisory is not the same thing as the vehicle being uninsured.

The more important issues are whether the vehicle remains roadworthy, what the insurer has asked you to disclose, whether those questions were answered accurately and whether the condition of the vehicle is relevant to a later accident or claim.

Advisory Record

An advisory alone should not be described as automatically cancelling or invalidating insurance.

Insurer Questions

Answer questions accurately when arranging, renewing or varying cover. Consumer insurance law requires reasonable care not to make a misrepresentation.

Roadworthiness

Motor policies commonly contain requirements relating to the roadworthy condition of the vehicle, making unresolved safety-critical defects more important than the word “advisory” itself.

Claim decisions depend on the circumstances

Where vehicle condition becomes relevant to an insurance dispute, issues can include the policy wording, the actual condition of the vehicle, information given to the insurer and whether the defect contributed to the loss. It is therefore too broad to say that every advisory either invalidates insurance or can always be ignored.

Advisory does not mean modification

An MOT advisory describes vehicle condition. It is not the same as modifying the vehicle. Modification-disclosure requirements are a separate insurance question and depend on the changes made and the insurer's terms.

Used Car Buying

Should You Buy a Used Car With MOT Advisories?

A car with MOT advisories is not automatically a bad purchase. Normal wear items appear on many perfectly serviceable vehicles. The better question is whether the advisories reveal predictable maintenance, unresolved neglect or potentially expensive structural and safety work.

MOT history becomes particularly useful when several years are reviewed together. It can show whether previous owners repaired defects promptly, whether the same problems repeatedly returned and whether deterioration appears to be spreading through several vehicle systems.

Usually Manageable

Normal Wear Items

Tyres, brake pads and other predictable service items may simply represent routine upcoming maintenance if their condition and price are understood.

Negotiate

Repairable Developing Faults

Brake discs, suspension wear, exhaust deterioration, shock absorbers and known oil leaks can justify negotiating the price when repair evidence is absent.

High Buying Risk

Corrosion & Brake Pipes

Structural corrosion and extensive brake pipe corrosion deserve closer investigation because repair scope can increase considerably once the vehicle is inspected properly.

How to interpret repeating advisories

One Appearance

May represent ordinary developing wear that was subsequently repaired or has remained serviceable.

Repeated Every Year

Can indicate maintenance has been deferred or that the owner has repeatedly chosen not to repair the developing condition.

Advisory Then Failure

Shows that the condition eventually deteriorated far enough to meet a rejection criterion and is useful evidence when assessing the vehicle's maintenance history.

Fresh MOT does not mean zero upcoming repair cost

A freshly tested used car can still contain several advisories. Add the likely repair cost of those items to the purchase price before deciding whether the vehicle represents good value.

Best buying approach

Ask the seller which advisories have been repaired and request invoices where possible. For structural corrosion, brake pipes, substantial suspension wear or unexplained leaks, an independent pre-purchase inspection can be worth far more than the inspection fee.

Frequently Asked Questions

Most Common MOT Advisory FAQs

These answers cover the most common questions UK drivers ask about MOT advisories, repair priority, roadworthiness, future failure risk, insurance and used-car buying.

What does an MOT advisory mean?

An MOT advisory is information recorded by the tester about an item that could become more serious in the future or is near a point where deterioration may justify rejection. The vehicle can still pass with advisories, but the condition should be monitored and repaired if necessary.

Does an MOT advisory mean the car has failed?

No. An advisory itself does not cause an MOT failure. A vehicle can pass with advisory items. Major and Dangerous defects cause an MOT failure, while Minor defects can also be recorded on a vehicle that passes.

What is the difference between an advisory and a Minor MOT defect?

An advisory warns about an item that could become more serious and should be monitored or repaired if necessary. A Minor defect is a defined defect with no significant effect on vehicle safety or the environment and should be repaired as soon as possible. Both can appear on a passing MOT result.

Can an MOT advisory become a Major defect?

Yes. If the condition deteriorates enough to meet a Major defect criterion at a later MOT, the vehicle will fail. Deterioration does not follow a fixed timetable, so the actual component condition matters more than the age of the advisory.

Can an MOT advisory become Dangerous?

A condition that was previously advised can deteriorate until it becomes dangerous, although it does not have to pass through every MOT category first. Dangerous defects represent a direct and immediate road-safety risk or serious environmental impact and the vehicle should not be driven until repaired.

What are the most important MOT advisories to check?

Advisories involving brakes, brake pipes, tyres, steering, suspension, structural corrosion and other safety-critical components deserve particular attention because deterioration can affect stopping, grip, stability, steering control or structural strength.

Is a corroded brake pipe advisory serious?

It can be. Brake pipes carry hydraulic pressure, so corrosion should be assessed for depth, location and remaining integrity. A brake pipe that becomes excessively corroded can meet Major defect criteria, while an imminent risk of failure or fracture can be Dangerous.

Is a tyre wear advisory serious?

Tyre wear should be monitored carefully because tread continues to reduce after the MOT. For most cars and light vans the legal minimum is 1.6 mm across the central three-quarters of the tread and around the entire circumference, but damage and uneven wear also need attention.

Can brake pad or brake disc advisories become MOT failures?

Yes. Brake pads and discs can deteriorate from an advised condition into defined MOT defects. The correct decision depends on remaining friction material, disc condition, security, contamination and whether the component has deteriorated to the relevant Major or Dangerous threshold.

Is a suspension advisory safe to drive with?

There is no universal answer because suspension advisories cover different components and degrees of deterioration. Increasing play, instability, abnormal tyre wear, knocking or damaged components should be inspected promptly rather than relying on the advisory wording alone.

Does a shock absorber advisory always mean it is leaking badly?

No. Slight fluid seepage that forms a thin film on a shock absorber is not itself an MOT rejection. Severe leakage, negligible damping, insecurity or a shock absorber that is missing or likely to detach can meet failure criteria depending on the exact condition.

Is structural corrosion advisory wording serious?

It can be. Corrosion risk depends on its extent and on the function of the affected area. A relatively small amount of corrosion can be important if it weakens a load-bearing or safety-related part of the vehicle structure.

Can an oil leak advisory become an MOT failure?

Yes, depending on how the leak develops and the applicable MOT criteria. An advised leak should also be assessed for fluid loss, contamination of other components, environmental impact and whether oil is reaching hot or safety-critical parts.

Can exhaust corrosion become an MOT failure?

Yes, but corrosion itself is not automatically a failure. Deterioration can become an MOT problem if the exhaust develops a major leak, becomes insecure, produces excessive noise, allows fumes into the cabin or affects the emissions test.

How long can you drive with an MOT advisory?

There is no universal number of days or miles. Repair urgency depends on the exact component, current condition, rate of deterioration, vehicle use and whether the vehicle remains roadworthy. Safety-critical or rapidly worsening conditions should be inspected sooner.

Do MOT advisories affect car insurance?

An MOT advisory does not automatically invalidate car insurance. Drivers should keep the vehicle roadworthy, answer insurer questions accurately and follow their policy terms. The relevance of a defect can also depend on the circumstances of an accident or claim.

Should you buy a used car with MOT advisories?

A used car with advisories can still be a reasonable purchase. Check the exact advisory wording, whether the same problems repeat in the MOT history, whether repairs are documented, the vehicle's current condition and whether likely repair costs are reflected in the price.

What should you do after receiving an MOT advisory?

Read the exact wording, identify the component, assess its current condition and safety significance, check for symptoms and decide whether it needs immediate inspection, planned repair or documented monitoring. Do not assume the advisory will remain acceptable until the next MOT.

Final Thoughts

Treat MOT Advisories as an Early Maintenance Warning

MOT advisories are most useful when they are treated as information, not ignored because the vehicle passed and not exaggerated into an automatic failure. They provide an opportunity to identify wear, corrosion and deterioration before the condition becomes more serious.

The best approach is to consider the system involved, inspect the current physical condition, identify any symptoms and prioritise work according to safety and deterioration rate. Brakes, tyres, steering, suspension and structural areas deserve particular attention because they can directly affect roadworthiness.

For used-car buyers, MOT advisories also provide valuable historical evidence. Repeated advisories, unresolved corrosion and clusters of suspension or braking problems can reveal maintenance patterns that are not obvious from the vehicle's appearance alone.

The aim is prevention

Finding and repairing a developing defect while it is still an advisory can reduce the risk of breakdown, secondary damage, unexpected MOT failure and larger repair bills later.
