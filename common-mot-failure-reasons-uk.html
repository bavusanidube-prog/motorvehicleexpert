Quick Answer

What Are The Most Common MOT Failure Reasons?

High MOT failure risk?

Warning lights, tyre damage, brake noise, heavy smoke, suspension knocking, poor braking performance or serious rust should be checked before the MOT test.

The most common MOT failure reasons in the UK are worn tyres, faulty lights, brake problems, suspension wear, rust, emissions faults, warning lights, steering problems and visibility issues.

Many MOT failures can be avoided by checking the car properly before the test. Look for worn tyres, faulty bulbs, damaged wipers, dashboard warning lights, brake noise, knocking suspension, fluid leaks, heavy exhaust smoke and visible corrosion.

Major Defect

MOT failure

A major defect means the vehicle does not meet the MOT standard and must be repaired before a pass certificate can be issued.

Dangerous Defect

Do not drive

A dangerous defect means the car is unsafe to drive. Repairs should be completed before the vehicle is used again.

Advisory Item

Future risk

An advisory is not a failure, but it warns that a component is worn, deteriorating or likely to need attention later.

Mechanic-style advice

If your previous MOT included brake, tyre, suspension, corrosion or emissions advisories, inspect those areas before the next test. Repeated advisories often turn into future MOT failures if ignored.

Important: If your car fails MOT with a dangerous defect, do not keep driving it. Dangerous defects should be repaired before the vehicle is used again.

Legal & Driving

MOT Legal Rules After Failure or Expiry

An MOT failure is more than a repair issue—it can affect whether your vehicle can legally be driven, where it may be parked, your insurance cover, road tax, police enforcement and your responsibilities as the registered keeper. The guides below explain the legal rules every UK driver should understand after an MOT failure or expiry.

Important legal reminder

A failed MOT does not automatically make a vehicle illegal to own, but a vehicle with a dangerous defect should not be driven on the road. Driving an unsafe vehicle can lead to prosecution, invalidate insurance in some circumstances and place other road users at risk.

Understanding MOT Failures

Why Do Cars Commonly Fail MOT Tests?

Most MOT failures are not caused by sudden mechanical breakdowns. Instead, they develop gradually through normal wear and tear, ignored MOT advisories, poor maintenance or damage caused by everyday driving. Understanding why vehicles fail MOT tests helps drivers identify problems earlier, reduce repair costs and improve the chances of passing the next inspection.

Mechanic's insight

Many vehicles fail the MOT because the same advisory appears year after year without being repaired. Worn brake pads become brake failures, tyre advisories become illegal tyres, while minor corrosion can eventually become structural rust that results in an MOT failure.

Normal Wear & Tear

Most common cause

Brake pads, brake discs, tyres, suspension components, wiper blades and light bulbs gradually wear during normal driving and eventually fall below MOT standards.

Ignored MOT Advisories

Future failure risk

Many MOT failures begin as advisories. Repeated advisories for brakes, tyres, suspension or corrosion should always be repaired before the next MOT test.

Learn about MOT advisories →

Pothole & Kerb Damage

Common UK problem

Potholes and kerb impacts can damage tyres, alloy wheels, steering components, suspension parts and wheel alignment, increasing the likelihood of an MOT failure.

Electrical & Warning Light Faults

Increasingly common

Dashboard warning lights, ABS faults, traction control issues, electronic parking brake faults, faulty sensors and damaged wiring can all contribute to MOT failures.

Rust & Structural Corrosion

Safety critical

Corrosion affecting chassis rails, suspension mounting points, brake pipes or seat belt anchorages is one of the most serious MOT failure categories on older vehicles.

Brake pipe corrosion guide →

Poor Maintenance

Preventable

Delayed servicing, neglected repairs and ignoring unusual noises or warning lights often allow small faults to develop into expensive MOT failures.

Reduce your chances of failing

A simple pre-MOT inspection can identify many common faults before the test. Check tyres, brakes, lights, windscreen wipers, fluid levels, dashboard warning lights and any previous MOT advisories. Catching problems early is usually far cheaper than repairing major MOT failures after the inspection.

Dashboard Warning Lights

Can Dashboard Warning Lights Cause an MOT Failure?

Modern vehicles constantly monitor braking, steering, stability, emissions and occupant safety systems. During the MOT inspection, certain warning lights are checked because they indicate faults affecting roadworthiness or emissions compliance. Not every illuminated warning light automatically results in an MOT failure, but many safety-related systems do.

Quick MOT Rule

If a warning light indicates a fault with a mandatory safety system or emissions control system, the vehicle may fail its MOT. Always diagnose warning lights before booking the test rather than assuming they can be ignored.

Used Car Buying

Why MOT History Matters When Buying a Used Car

An MOT certificate only shows whether a vehicle passed or failed on the day of the inspection. The complete MOT history tells a much bigger story, revealing how well the vehicle has been maintained, whether faults have been repaired properly and if the same problems continue to appear year after year. Reviewing this history before buying a used car can help you avoid expensive repairs and identify vehicles that may have been poorly maintained.

Buying tip

A vehicle with one or two minor advisories is not necessarily a poor purchase. However, repeated advisories for brakes, tyres, suspension, corrosion, steering or fluid leaks often suggest maintenance has been delayed and further repairs may soon be required.

Professional Advice

Mechanic's Advice Before Taking Your Car for an MOT

If your car already feels wrong before the MOT, there is usually a reason. Grinding brakes, knocking suspension, steering vibration, overheating, smoke, strong exhaust smells or dashboard warning lights should not be ignored. These symptoms often point to faults that may become MOT failures if left unchecked.

The best time to inspect the vehicle is one or two weeks before the MOT, not on the morning of the test. This gives you time to repair simple faults, investigate previous advisories and avoid last-minute garage pressure.

Listen To The Car

Early warning

Grinding brakes, suspension knocks, steering vibration, exhaust noise and warning lights are often early signs of MOT failure risks.

Repair Small Faults Early

Best practice

Bulbs, wipers, washer fluid, number plates and tyre pressure are simple checks that can prevent avoidable MOT failures.

Mechanic tip

A fresh MOT pass does not mean a car is perfect. It only means the vehicle met the minimum legal roadworthiness standard at the time of inspection. Always read the advisory section carefully and repair repeated advisories before they become expensive failures.

FAQs

Frequently Asked Questions About MOT Failures

What are the most common MOT failures?

Tyres, brakes, lights, suspension, rust, warning lights, emissions faults and visibility problems are among the most common MOT failures.

Can warning lights fail an MOT?

Yes. ABS, airbag, brake, traction control, ESC, TPMS and engine management warning lights may affect MOT results depending on the vehicle and the fault.

Can I avoid an MOT failure?

Many MOT failures can be avoided by checking lights, tyres, wipers, washers, warning lights, number plates, previous advisories and obvious damage before the test.

Do MOT advisories matter?

Yes. Advisories often become future MOT failures if ignored for another year, especially advisories for brakes, tyres, suspension, corrosion or leaks.

What is the easiest MOT failure to avoid?

Faulty bulbs, empty washer fluid, damaged wipers, unreadable number plates and tyre pressure issues are among the easiest MOT failure risks to check before the test.

Can potholes cause MOT failures?

Yes. Potholes can damage tyres, wheels, steering, suspension and alignment. These issues can later appear during an MOT inspection.
