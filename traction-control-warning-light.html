Dashboard Warning Light Guide

Traction Control Warning Light Explained

The traction control warning light is one of the most misunderstood dashboard symbols. Sometimes it flashes briefly because the system is actively preventing wheel spin, while in other situations it stays on continuously to warn that a fault has been detected. Knowing the difference can help you decide whether the vehicle is safe to drive or needs immediate diagnosis.

This complete UK guide explains how traction control works, why the warning light comes on, how it relates to the ABS, electronic stability control and braking system, the most common faults, repair costs, MOT implications and whether you should continue driving when the warning appears.

Quick Answer

What Does The Traction Control Warning Light Mean?

The traction control warning light tells you that the vehicle's traction control or electronic stability system is either actively helping maintain grip or has detected a fault. A flashing light usually indicates normal operation while the system is reducing wheel spin. A warning light that stays on continuously normally means the traction control system has been disabled because a fault has been detected.

Modern traction control systems constantly communicate with the anti-lock braking system (ABS), wheel speed sensors, engine management system and the vehicle's electronic braking controls. Because these systems share information, one fault can often trigger several dashboard warning lights at the same time. It is therefore common to see the traction control light appear together with the ABS warning light or, on many modern vehicles, an Electronic Parking Brake Fault message.

Although traction control is primarily designed to improve grip during acceleration, it also works closely with the braking system by applying individual brakes when wheel slip is detected. This means braking faults, hydraulic system problems or ABS sensor issues can sometimes trigger traction control warnings even when the tyres themselves are in good condition.

Important

A traction control warning should never be dismissed without investigation. While some causes are relatively inexpensive, such as a faulty wheel speed sensor, others may involve the ABS hydraulic unit, steering angle sensor, electronic parking brake or stability control system. Understanding the warning early can prevent larger repair bills and improve vehicle safety.

How It Works

How Traction Control Works

Traction control is designed to stop the driven wheels from spinning when the vehicle accelerates on a slippery or low-grip surface. It does this by monitoring wheel speed and comparing how quickly each wheel is rotating. If one wheel begins to spin faster than the others, the system can reduce engine power, apply braking force to an individual wheel or use both methods together to help the vehicle regain grip.

On most modern vehicles, traction control works closely with the ABS system, wheel speed sensors, the engine management system and electronic stability control. Because these systems share sensor data, a fault in one area can trigger several warning lights at the same time. This is why a traction control warning light often appears together with ABS, brake or electronic parking brake warnings.

Simple explanation

ABS helps prevent the wheels from locking during braking. Traction control helps prevent the driven wheels from spinning during acceleration. Both systems often use the same wheel speed sensor information, which is why faults can overlap.

Wheel Speed Sensors

Main input

Wheel speed sensors tell the control module how fast each wheel is rotating. If one wheel spins faster than expected, the system can detect loss of grip.

Engine Torque Reduction

Power control

The system may reduce engine power briefly to stop wheel spin and help the tyres regain traction.

Individual Brake Control

Brake intervention

Some systems apply braking force to a slipping wheel. This is why traction control is closely connected to the hydraulic braking system and ABS.

Common Causes

Common Causes Of A Traction Control Warning Light

A traction control warning light can be caused by anything that prevents the system from accurately monitoring wheel speed, vehicle movement or braking behaviour. Some causes are simple, while others involve the ABS, electronic braking system, steering angle sensor or stability control module.

The most important point is that traction control warnings should not be diagnosed by guessing. A proper scan tool should be used to read the stored fault codes, confirm which system is affected and avoid replacing parts unnecessarily.

Wheel Speed Sensor Fault

Very common

A faulty wheel speed sensor is one of the most common causes of traction control and ABS warning lights. The system cannot control wheel slip accurately if it does not know how fast each wheel is rotating.

ABS System Fault

Diagnose promptly

Because traction control often uses ABS sensor data and braking intervention, an ABS fault can also disable traction control. Learn how the system works in our ABS Explained guide.

Steering Angle Sensor

Stability control

The steering angle sensor helps the stability system understand where the driver intends the vehicle to go. If the reading is incorrect, traction or stability warnings may appear.

Low Battery Voltage

Electrical issue

Low voltage after a weak battery, jump start or charging fault can confuse electronic control modules and trigger temporary traction control warnings.

Tyre Size Or Grip Difference

Check tyres

Different tyre sizes, uneven tread depth or mismatched tyres can cause the system to detect unusual wheel speed differences.

Electronic Parking Brake Fault

Related system

Modern electronic parking brakes often communicate with ABS and stability control systems. A parking brake fault can sometimes trigger related traction or stability warnings. Learn more in our Electronic Parking Brake Fault guide.

Brake Switch Fault

Signal problem

A faulty brake pedal switch can send incorrect braking signals to the traction control, ABS or stability control system.

Damaged Wiring

Intermittent fault

Broken, corroded or stretched wiring near the wheels can interrupt wheel speed sensor signals and cause intermittent warning lights.

Brake Overheating

Safety concern

Severe brake overheating can affect braking behaviour and may appear alongside other warning symptoms. If you notice burning smells, smoke or one hot wheel, read our Brake Overheating Explained guide.

Do not ignore combined warnings

If the traction control light appears together with the ABS light, brake warning light or electronic parking brake fault message, the vehicle should be diagnosed promptly. These systems are connected and may affect braking stability, grip control and driver assistance functions.

Driving Advice

Can You Drive With The Traction Control Warning Light On?

Whether it is safe to continue driving depends on why the traction control warning light has appeared. A flashing warning light during acceleration on a wet or slippery road is usually normal because the system is actively preventing wheel spin. Once grip returns, the warning light normally stops flashing.

A traction control warning light that stays on continuously is different. It usually means the system has detected a fault and traction control may no longer be available when you need it most. Normal braking often remains available, but the vehicle may no longer be able to automatically reduce wheel spin or assist with stability during poor road conditions.

If the warning light appears together with the ABS warning light, a brake warning light or an Electronic Parking Brake Fault message, you should arrange diagnosis as soon as possible because these systems share information and often rely on the same sensors and electronic control modules.

Important safety advice

A traction control fault rarely causes the vehicle to stop immediately, but it can reduce grip assistance during acceleration, cornering and slippery conditions. Extra care should always be taken in heavy rain, snow, ice or loose gravel until the fault has been diagnosed.

Normal Operation

Why Does The Traction Control Light Flash?

Many drivers become concerned when they see the traction control warning light flashing, but this is often a sign that the system is doing exactly what it was designed to do. The flashing light simply indicates that the vehicle has detected wheel slip and is actively trying to restore traction.

Typical situations include accelerating quickly on wet roads, pulling away on snow or ice, driving across loose gravel, climbing steep hills or exiting muddy ground. During these situations the traction control system may briefly reduce engine power or apply braking force to an individual wheel until grip returns.

Heavy Rain

Normal

Reduced tyre grip during wet weather frequently activates traction control for a short period.

Snow Or Ice

Expected

Traction control often intervenes repeatedly on slippery winter roads to help prevent excessive wheel spin.

Loose Gravel

Temporary

Loose road surfaces naturally reduce grip and can cause the traction control light to flash while accelerating.

Good news

If the warning light only flashes briefly and then disappears once grip returns, the traction control system is usually operating normally and no repair is required.

Fault Diagnosis

Why Does The Traction Control Warning Light Stay On?

A traction control light that remains illuminated after the engine has started usually indicates that the system has detected a stored fault. Unlike a flashing warning light, a constant warning generally means the electronic control module has disabled part or all of the traction control system until repairs have been completed.

The fault may be something relatively simple, such as a damaged wheel speed sensor, but it may also involve the ABS hydraulic control unit, steering angle sensor, stability control module or another electronic braking component.

Do not ignore permanent warnings

Although the vehicle may continue to drive normally, the electronic systems designed to improve stability during emergency situations may no longer operate correctly. Diagnostic fault codes should always be read before replacing parts.

The most common cause of a permanent traction control warning light.

ABS Module

ABS & Stability Control

Why The Traction Control And ABS Warning Lights Come On Together

One of the most common questions drivers ask is why the traction control warning light appears at the same time as the ABS warning light. The answer is straightforward: both systems rely on many of the same components. Modern traction control uses the wheel speed sensors, ABS hydraulic unit and electronic brake control module to monitor grip and, when necessary, apply braking force to individual wheels.

If the ABS system loses accurate wheel speed information, the traction control system can no longer determine whether a wheel is slipping or rotating normally. Rather than operate with unreliable data, the vehicle disables the traction control system and illuminates the warning light. This protective strategy prevents incorrect braking intervention while alerting the driver that electronic assistance has been reduced.

Because these systems work so closely together, diagnosing one warning light without checking the other often leads to unnecessary parts replacement. Before replacing a wheel speed sensor, ABS module or steering angle sensor, the vehicle should always be scanned using professional diagnostic equipment capable of reading manufacturer-specific fault codes.

System relationship

Traction control does not work independently. It communicates continuously with the ABS system, the vehicle's electronic stability control, engine management system and hydraulic braking components to improve safety whenever tyre grip is reduced.

ABS Wheel Speed Sensors

Most common fault

A damaged wheel speed sensor can disable both ABS and traction control because accurate wheel speed information is no longer available.

ABS Hydraulic Unit

Professional diagnosis

Internal faults within the ABS hydraulic control unit may trigger multiple electronic braking warnings and usually require diagnostic testing before replacement.

Shared Electronics

Connected systems

ABS, traction control, electronic stability control and the electronic parking brake often share the same control network, allowing faults to affect several systems simultaneously.

Diagnosis

How Mechanics Diagnose A Traction Control Warning Light

Professional diagnosis is far more reliable than replacing parts based on guesswork. Although wheel speed sensors are common causes of traction control faults, experienced technicians begin by reading stored fault codes, checking live wheel speed data and inspecting the braking system before recommending repairs.

Modern diagnostic equipment can identify whether the warning is being caused by an ABS sensor, steering angle sensor, electronic parking brake, brake switch, damaged wiring or another control module. This saves time, avoids unnecessary expense and ensures the correct repair is carried out first time.

Do not replace parts blindly

Many traction control faults share similar symptoms. Replacing a wheel speed sensor simply because it is the most common failure can become expensive if the actual fault is damaged wiring, a poor electrical connection or an ABS control module problem.

Diagnostic Scan

First step

Read manufacturer-specific fault codes before carrying out any repairs.

Live Data Checks

Compare sensors

Monitor live wheel speed readings to identify sensors producing incorrect values while driving.

Visual Inspection

Check wiring

Inspect wheel speed sensor wiring, connectors, reluctor rings and suspension components for physical damage.

Brake System Inspection

Safety check

Inspect the braking system, including brake discs, brake calipers and brake fluid, because braking faults can influence electronic safety systems.

Road Test

Confirm repair

After repairs, a controlled road test confirms that the traction control system activates normally and no warning lights return.

Final Verification

Complete

Clear fault codes, recheck live data and verify that ABS, traction control and stability control all operate correctly.

MOT & Roadworthiness

Will A Traction Control Warning Light Fail An MOT?

Whether a traction control warning light results in an MOT failure depends on the vehicle, the systems fitted and the fault that has been detected. Modern vehicles equipped with Electronic Stability Control (ESC) are subject to specific MOT inspection requirements, meaning certain stability or braking system faults may result in an MOT failure.

Even where the warning itself does not automatically fail the MOT, it should never be ignored. The warning may indicate faults affecting the ABS, braking system or electronic stability systems that are important for vehicle safety.

MOT advice

If your traction control warning light remains illuminated before an MOT test, arrange diagnosis first. Repairing the fault beforehand is usually quicker and cheaper than dealing with a failed MOT and repeat test.

Buying Advice

Should You Buy A Used Car With The Traction Control Warning Light On?

Buying a used car with the traction control warning light illuminated is always a calculated risk. In some cases the fault is nothing more than a failed wheel speed sensor, but it can also indicate expensive ABS faults, electronic braking problems or stability control issues that require specialist diagnosis.

Before agreeing to buy, ask the seller to provide a diagnostic report explaining the warning. If this cannot be produced, budget for professional diagnosis before assuming the repair will be inexpensive.

Simple Sensor Fault

Often acceptable

Minor sensor faults are common and are usually among the least expensive traction control repairs.

No Diagnostic History

Mechanic Tips

Mechanic Tips For Traction Control Warning Lights

Traction control faults are easy to misunderstand because the warning light does not always mean the traction control unit itself has failed. In many cases, the real cause is a shared sensor, low voltage, damaged wiring, ABS fault or braking system issue. A careful diagnostic process saves money and prevents unnecessary parts replacement.

Scan Before Replacing Parts

Best practice

Always read the stored fault codes before replacing wheel speed sensors, ABS components or electronic brake parts.

Check Tyres First

Simple check

Mismatched tyre sizes, uneven tread depths or incorrect tyre pressures can confuse traction control readings.

Inspect Brake Warnings Together

Connected systems

Traction control, ABS, electronic parking brake and brake warning lights often share fault information, so they should be diagnosed together.

Do Not Ignore Heat Symptoms

Safety concern

Burning smells, smoke from a wheel or one wheel much hotter than the others may indicate a braking fault rather than a simple electronic issue. Learn more in our Brake Overheating Explained guide.

Check Brake Condition

Full inspection

If electronic warnings appear with poor brake feel, vibration or pulling, inspect the brake pads, discs and calipers before assuming the fault is only electrical.

Verify The Repair

Final step

After repairs, clear the fault codes and road test the vehicle to confirm that the traction control, ABS and stability systems work correctly.

Frequently Asked Questions

Traction Control Warning Light FAQs

Can I drive with the traction control warning light on?

If the light is on by itself, normal braking may still work but traction control or stability control may be disabled. Drive carefully and arrange diagnosis. If it appears with ABS, brake or electronic parking brake warnings, have the vehicle inspected as soon as possible.

What causes the traction control warning light to come on?

Common causes include faulty wheel speed sensors, ABS faults, steering angle sensor issues, low battery voltage, tyre size differences, damaged wiring, brake switch faults, electronic parking brake faults and stability control errors.

Why does the traction control light flash while driving?

A flashing traction control light usually means the system is actively working because one or more wheels are losing grip. This commonly happens on wet roads, snow, ice, loose gravel or mud.

Why does the traction control light stay on permanently?

A traction control light that stays on usually means the system has detected a fault or has been switched off. The vehicle should be scanned for fault codes before replacing parts.

Is traction control connected to ABS?

Yes. Traction control commonly uses ABS wheel speed sensors and electronic braking data to detect wheel slip, which is why traction control and ABS warning lights often appear together.

Can an electronic parking brake fault cause traction control warnings?

Yes. On many modern vehicles, electronic parking brake, ABS, stability control and traction control systems share data. A fault in one system can sometimes trigger related warning lights.

Can brake problems cause traction control warning lights?

Some braking faults can contribute to traction control or stability warnings, especially if they affect wheel speed sensor readings, brake switch signals, ABS operation or electronic brake control modules.

Is the traction control warning light an MOT failure?

It can be an MOT concern if the warning indicates a fault affecting electronic stability control or related safety systems on a vehicle where those systems are required.

How much does it cost to fix a traction control fault?

Typical UK costs vary. A diagnostic scan may cost around £50 to £120, a wheel speed sensor may cost around £100 to £250, and more complex ABS or electronic brake repairs can cost significantly more.

Should I buy a used car with the traction control light on?

A used car with the traction control warning light on should be diagnosed before purchase. It may be a simple sensor fault, but it can also indicate ABS, electronic braking or stability control problems.
