Quick answer

How much does brake caliper replacement cost in the UK?

Brake caliper replacement in the UK usually costs around £150–£400 per caliper at an independent garage. The final price depends on whether it is a front or rear caliper, whether a new or reconditioned caliper is fitted, the labour rate, brake fluid bleeding, and whether brake pads or discs have also been damaged.

Front brake calipers can cost more on larger or performance vehicles because they often do more braking work and may be larger. Rear calipers can also be expensive if they include a handbrake mechanism or electronic parking brake system. If the caliper has been seized for some time, the repair may also require brake pads, brake discs or brake fluid replacement.

Typical independent garage

£150–£400 per caliper
Common UK range

Covers many standard sliding caliper replacements, including fitting and bleeding where required.

Caliper + pads

£220–£550+
Often needed

Pads may need replacing if they are unevenly worn, overheated or contaminated by brake fluid.

Caliper + discs + pads

£350–£800+
Higher repair risk

A seized caliper can overheat or damage the disc and pads, turning one repair into a larger brake job.

Important safety note:

A seized or leaking brake caliper is not just a cost issue. It can cause brake drag, overheating, fluid loss, pulling to one side, uneven pad wear and reduced braking performance. The fault should be diagnosed before new pads or discs are fitted.

Price factors

What Affects Brake Caliper Replacement Cost?

Brake caliper replacement costs vary because calipers combine hydraulic, mechanical and sometimes electronic functions. The price is affected by the caliper design, the replacement part quality, labour time and any extra damage caused by the faulty caliper.

Front vs rear

Front calipers may be larger, while rear calipers can be more complex if they include parking brake mechanisms.

New vs reconditioned

Reconditioned calipers are often cheaper, while new calipers may offer better availability or warranty.

Sliding vs fixed caliper

Standard sliding calipers are usually cheaper than fixed or multi-piston performance calipers.

Electronic parking brake

Rear calipers linked to electronic parking brake systems may need extra procedures and diagnostic equipment.

Brake fluid bleeding

Caliper replacement opens the hydraulic system, so proper bleeding is required before the vehicle is driven.

Damage to pads and discs

A seized caliper can overheat or wear out pads and discs, increasing the total repair cost.

Quote comparison tip:

Ask whether the quote includes a new or reconditioned caliper, brake fluid bleeding, VAT, labour, brake pads if required, brake disc inspection and a final road test.

New vs reconditioned

New Vs Reconditioned Brake Calipers

Many UK garages offer either new or reconditioned brake calipers. A reconditioned caliper is a used caliper that has been rebuilt with replacement seals, cleaned components and sometimes a new piston or slider hardware. A new caliper is manufactured as a replacement part.

Reconditioned Caliper

Quote comparison

How To Compare Brake Caliper Replacement Quotes

Brake caliper quotes can vary because one garage may quote for the caliper only while another includes pads, discs, brake fluid bleeding, VAT and a full brake inspection. Always compare what is included rather than judging on the headline price alone.

Best value approach:

The best quote is the one that identifies why the caliper failed, explains whether the replacement is new or reconditioned, checks the pads and discs, bleeds the system correctly and confirms the brake operates safely afterwards.

Seized caliper

Seized Brake Caliper: Symptoms, Risks And Repair Cost

A seized brake caliper is one of the most common reasons for caliper replacement. It happens when the piston, sliders or handbrake mechanism sticks and prevents the brake from releasing correctly. This can cause brake drag, overheating, uneven pad wear and damage to the brake disc.

Brake Drag

High risk

The brake remains partly applied, creating heat and resistance while driving.

Hot Wheel

Urgent

One wheel may feel much hotter than the others after driving, often with a burning smell.

Uneven Pad Wear

Common clue

One pad may wear much faster than the other when the caliper or slider is sticking.

Pulling To One Side

Safety concern

A seized caliper can cause the vehicle to pull during braking or even while driving.

Fuel Economy Drops

Hidden cost

Brake drag makes the engine work harder and can reduce fuel economy.

Disc Damage

Cost increase

Overheating can score, warp or damage the brake disc, increasing the repair bill.

Do not ignore a seized caliper.

A seized caliper can quickly destroy brake pads and discs. If one wheel smells hot, the car pulls, or the brakes feel like they are dragging, arrange inspection before regular driving.

Leaking caliper

Leaking Brake Caliper: Why It Is Serious

A leaking brake caliper is a serious hydraulic brake fault. Brake fluid may escape from the piston seal, bleed nipple, hose connection or caliper body. Any brake fluid leak can reduce hydraulic pressure and affect braking performance.

Fluid On Caliper

Critical

Wetness around the caliper may indicate brake fluid escaping from a seal or connection.

Low Brake Fluid

Pressure risk

Falling brake fluid level should always be investigated and not simply topped up.

Soft Brake Pedal

Unsafe symptom

Air or fluid loss in the hydraulic system can cause a soft or sinking pedal.

Contaminated Pads

Extra cost

Brake fluid can contaminate pads, often requiring pad replacement as well as caliper repair.

Brake Warning Light

Do not ignore

Low fluid or hydraulic faults can trigger brake warning lights.

MOT Failure Risk

High

A brake fluid leak is a serious MOT issue and should be repaired immediately.

Hydraulic safety warning:

Do not drive a vehicle with a confirmed brake fluid leak. Brake fluid loss can reduce braking performance and may lead to unsafe pedal feel.

Inspection guide

Brake Caliper Inspection Guide

Brake calipers should be inspected as part of the complete braking system. A technician checks the piston, sliders, seals, bleed nipple, hose connection, brake pads, discs and brake fluid condition before deciding whether the caliper should be repaired or replaced.

1. Check For Leaks

Critical check

Inspect the piston seal, bleed nipple, hose joint and caliper body for wetness.

2. Check Slider Movement

Common fault

Sticky sliders can cause uneven pad wear and brake drag even if the piston is working.

3. Check Piston Movement

Seizure risk

A seized piston may fail to apply or release the brake correctly.

4. Inspect Brake Pads

Wear pattern

Uneven pad wear often confirms caliper slider or piston issues.

5. Inspect Brake Discs

Final confirmation

Workshop method:

MOT advice

Can A Faulty Brake Caliper Fail An MOT?

Yes. A seized, leaking or damaged brake caliper can fail an MOT if it causes brake imbalance, poor braking efficiency, brake drag, fluid leakage or unsafe braking performance. The MOT brake test measures braking force and imbalance, so caliper faults may show up even if the vehicle still stops.

Brake Fluid Leak

Failure risk

Brake fluid leaking from the caliper is a serious safety issue.

Brake Imbalance

Measured fault

A sticking caliper can make one wheel brake harder or weaker than the opposite side.

Brake Drag

Efficiency issue

A seized caliper may cause the brake to bind and affect the MOT brake test.

Damaged Components

Visual check

Broken, insecure or visibly damaged calipers can create MOT concerns.

Brake Warning Light

Dashboard check

Brake warning lights or ABS-related faults can also affect MOT results.

Overheated Brakes

Safety risk

A dragging caliper can overheat discs and pads, reducing braking performance.

MOT planning tip:

If your MOT history shows brake imbalance, binding brakes, worn pads on one side or brake fluid leaks, inspect the calipers before the next MOT.

Driving advice

Can You Drive With A Faulty Brake Caliper?

You should not continue regular driving with a seized or leaking brake caliper. A minor slider issue may be caught early, but a caliper causing overheating, pulling, brake drag or fluid loss should be treated as a safety concern.

Minor Slider Sticking

Inspect soon

May not be immediately dangerous if caught early, but it can quickly wear pads unevenly.

Seized Caliper

Avoid driving

Can overheat brakes and damage discs, pads and wheel components.

Leaking Caliper

Do not drive

Brake fluid loss can reduce hydraulic pressure and create unsafe pedal feel.

Stop driving and arrange inspection if:

One wheel smells hot, the car pulls strongly, the brake pedal feels soft, brake fluid is leaking, the brake warning light appears or the brakes feel like they are dragging.

Repair decision

Can A Brake Caliper Be Repaired Or Should It Be Replaced?

Some brake caliper faults can be repaired, especially seized sliders or minor serviceable hardware. However, a caliper with a leaking piston seal, severe corrosion, cracked body, seized piston or failed handbrake mechanism is often better replaced.

Clean And Lubricate Sliders

Minor fault

Suitable when the slider pins are sticking but the piston and seals are healthy.

Seal Kit Or Rebuild

Specialist repair

May be possible on some calipers but must be done correctly because the system is safety-critical.

Replace Caliper

Most reliable

Usually best when the caliper leaks, the piston is seized, or corrosion is severe.

Practical repair advice:

If the caliper has already damaged pads or discs, replacing only the caliper may not fully fix the braking issue. The full brake corner should be inspected.

Money-saving advice

How To Save Money On Brake Caliper Replacement

Brake caliper repairs can become expensive when the fault damages discs, pads or hydraulic components. The safest way to save money is to diagnose the fault early, compare clear quotes and avoid replacing good parts unnecessarily.

Act Before Disc Damage

A sticking caliper can destroy a brake disc quickly, so early repair can prevent a bigger bill.

Ask About Reconditioned Parts

A quality reconditioned caliper may offer good value if it comes with a suitable warranty.

Compare Full Quotes

Make sure quotes include caliper type, labour, brake bleeding, VAT and road testing.

Check Pads And Discs

Replacing a caliper without checking damaged pads or discs can lead to repeat problems.

Do Not Ignore Brake Drag

Brake drag wastes fuel, overheats parts and can turn a small fault into a full brake repair.

Avoid Unknown Used Calipers

Cheap used calipers may already have worn seals, corrosion or seized pistons.

Best value rule:

The best repair is not always the cheapest caliper. It is the repair that fixes the original fault, restores safe braking and prevents repeat damage to pads, discs and hydraulic components.

Used car buying

Brake Caliper Checks Before Buying A Used Car

A faulty brake caliper can turn a cheap used car into an expensive repair. Before buying, look for brake drag, uneven pad wear, hot wheels, pulling, brake fluid leaks and repeated MOT advisories for binding brakes or brake imbalance.

Hot Wheel After Driving

High risk

One hot wheel may indicate a sticking caliper or brake drag.

Uneven Pad Wear

Inspection clue

Pads worn more on one side can point to a seized slider or piston.

Brake Fluid Leak

Walk-away risk

Wetness around the caliper, wheel or hose needs proper inspection.

Car Pulls Under Braking

Safety concern

Pulling can be caused by uneven braking force from a caliper fault.

Burning Smell

Urgent clue

A hot brake smell after a test drive can indicate brake drag.

MOT History

Check carefully

Look for brake imbalance, binding brakes, worn pads or fluid leak advisories.

Buyer warning:

Do not accept “it just needs pads” if one wheel is hot, the car pulls, or the brakes smell. A seized caliper can damage pads and discs quickly.

Brake Caliper Replacement Cost UK FAQs

How much does brake caliper replacement cost in the UK?

Brake caliper replacement usually costs around £150–£400 per caliper at an independent garage.

Can a seized brake caliper fail an MOT?

Yes. A seized caliper can cause brake imbalance, brake drag or poor braking efficiency, which can fail an MOT.

Can you drive with a seized brake caliper?

Regular driving is not recommended because a seized caliper can overheat the brakes and damage pads and discs.

Is a leaking brake caliper dangerous?

Yes. Brake fluid leaks can reduce hydraulic pressure and affect braking performance.

Should brake pads be replaced with a caliper?

Often yes, especially if the pads are unevenly worn, overheated or contaminated by brake fluid.

Is a new or reconditioned caliper better?

A quality reconditioned caliper can be good value, but a new caliper may be preferred for reliability or warranty.
