Wrong Mileage on MOT History?

Found an incorrect mileage reading on your car's MOT history? A recording mistake can create confusion when selling a vehicle, checking its service history or investigating a possible mileage discrepancy. This complete UK guide explains how to identify genuine errors, correct MOT mileage within or after 28 days, gather evidence for DVSA and protect yourself when buying a used car.

Quick answer

How do you correct wrong mileage on an MOT history?

If the mileage recorded on your MOT history is incorrect, the correction process depends on when the test took place. For a test carried out within the last 28 days, contact the MOT test centre that recorded the mileage. The centre can check the error and follow the appropriate correction process.

If the MOT was more than 28 days ago, use the official GOV.UK service to report the mistake to DVSA. You must provide suitable documentary evidence showing the correct mileage at the time of the original MOT test.

Within 28 days

Contact the garage or MOT test centre that carried out the inspection. Explain which mileage reading is wrong and provide the vehicle registration and test details.

Contact the test centre

More than 28 days

Submit a correction request through the official DVSA service. You will need supporting documents that show the correct mileage close to the original test date.

What evidence do you need for an older mileage error?

For a mileage correction more than 28 days after the test, DVSA requires a qualifying document showing the vehicle registration or VIN, the correct mileage and a date within one day of the MOT test. Examples include a service receipt, invoice, emissions printout, service history document or MOT centre inspection sheet.

A photograph of the dashboard mileage display or the MOT certificate itself is not accepted as supporting evidence for this correction procedure.

MOT record checks

How to check if your MOT mileage history is wrong

Before asking for an MOT mileage correction, check exactly which test contains the suspected mistake. A single incorrect reading can make a vehicle appear to have travelled backwards in mileage, even when the odometer has never been altered.

The official MOT history service shows recorded odometer readings from previous tests where that information is available. Compare the readings in date order, then check them against the vehicle's service records, repair invoices and other dated documents.

1. Open the official MOT history

Visit GOV.UK and enter the vehicle registration. Confirm that the displayed vehicle details match the car you are investigating.

2. Find the incorrect reading

Look through the recorded mileage figures and identify any reading that appears unusually high, unusually low or inconsistent with neighbouring tests.

3. Compare supporting records

Check dated servicing invoices, previous inspection records and other reliable documents to establish what the mileage was around the MOT date.

4. Record the test details

Write down the test date, recorded mileage, suspected correct reading and the evidence available before contacting the test centre or DVSA.

Example of an MOT mileage recording mistake

The following example shows how one incorrectly entered figure can create an apparent mileage discrepancy. These are illustrative figures, not an actual vehicle record.

MOT test Recorded mileage What it suggests
First test 42,150 miles Starting point for comparing the vehicle's mileage history.
Second test 148,900 miles A large increase that should be checked against other records.
Third test 56,700 miles Appears to show a substantial mileage reduction.
Possible explanation 48,900 miles The second reading may contain an extra digit, but supporting evidence is needed before treating it as an error.
Do not assume which reading is incorrect

An unusual mileage sequence does not prove that the highest figure was entered wrongly. The lower reading could be inaccurate, the odometer could have been replaced or the vehicle could have a genuine unexplained mileage reduction. Establish the facts before requesting a correction.

Why checking the complete sequence matters

Looking at only two MOT readings can produce a misleading conclusion. A vehicle may have several consistent mileage entries before and after one unusual test. That pattern can help identify which record needs further investigation, although it does not replace the documentary evidence required by DVSA.

Common causes

Why incorrect mileage appears on MOT history

Incorrect MOT mileage can have several explanations. Some are simple recording mistakes made during the test, while others involve the vehicle's odometer, previous repairs or incomplete history. Understanding the possible cause helps determine whether a record correction is appropriate.

Typing error

A digit may be added, omitted or entered in the wrong position when the odometer reading is recorded during the MOT.

Possible recording mistake

Digits entered in the wrong order

Two numbers may be reversed. For example, 64,320 miles could be recorded as 63,420 miles. Dated evidence is needed to establish the correct reading.

Compare original documents

Miles and kilometres confusion

Some imported vehicles or replacement instrument clusters display kilometres instead of miles. The unit shown on the original MOT record must be checked before assuming an error.

Check recorded units

Odometer replacement

Replacing a faulty instrument cluster may change the displayed mileage if the replacement unit is not correctly configured or documented.

Check repair history

Odometer rollover

Some older odometers return to zero after reaching their maximum displayed value. This can create an apparent reduction without deliberate mileage alteration.

Check vehicle age and equipment

Genuine mileage discrepancy

A reduction may reflect an unexplained change to the odometer reading. This requires careful investigation rather than automatically requesting that an MOT entry be changed.

Investigate before buying

Can an MOT tester record the wrong mileage?

Yes. MOT mileage is recorded as part of the test process, and mistakes can occur when the displayed odometer reading is entered. The official correction procedure exists to deal with errors in recorded MOT information.

However, an MOT tester is not responsible for independently certifying that a vehicle's odometer reflects its complete lifetime distance. A correctly recorded display reading may still be inconsistent with the vehicle's actual history.

Recording error versus odometer problem

If the MOT tester entered a different number from the odometer display, that may be a correctable recording error. If the tester accurately recorded a faulty or altered odometer display, the problem is different. Do not assume DVSA will change a historically accurate test entry simply because the displayed mileage was misleading.

Mileage investigation

How to tell if an MOT mileage discrepancy is a genuine error

A mileage discrepancy is a warning that the vehicle's history needs checking. It is not a diagnosis on its own. Before reporting an MOT record mistake, compare independent evidence and establish whether the recorded reading was incorrect at the time of the test.

1. Check neighbouring MOT readings

Compare tests before and after the suspicious entry. Look for a consistent mileage progression and identify where the pattern changes.

2. Review service invoices

Look for dated workshop invoices showing the vehicle registration or VIN and recorded mileage. These may help establish the odometer reading around the test date.

3. Check instrument repairs

Find out whether the instrument cluster or odometer was replaced. Ask for repair invoices and any documented mileage adjustment.

4. Identify the actual error

Determine whether the MOT entry was recorded incorrectly or whether the vehicle itself had an unexplained odometer reading at the time.

Evidence that supports a possible recording mistake

Signs consistent with a recording error

  • ✓ One reading differs sharply from surrounding tests
  • ✓ A dated workshop document supports a different reading
  • ✓ The error resembles an extra digit or transposition
  • ✓ Other records show a consistent mileage progression

Signs requiring further investigation

  • ✓ Several readings show unexplained reductions
  • ✓ Service records contradict the advertised mileage
  • ✓ An instrument replacement has no supporting paperwork
  • ✓ The seller cannot explain the discrepancy

Does an incorrect MOT mileage reading prove a car was clocked?

No. An incorrect reading does not automatically prove that the vehicle's odometer has been deliberately altered. Recording mistakes and legitimate instrument changes can create unusual mileage patterns.

Equally, a plausible explanation should not be accepted without evidence. A vehicle showing unexplained mileage reductions deserves further checks before money changes hands.

Used car buyer warning

If you are considering buying a vehicle with inconsistent mileage, ask the seller for dated supporting documents. Do not rely on a verbal explanation or a promise that the MOT history will be corrected later.

What should you do once you identify the wrong entry?

Record the exact MOT test date, the mileage currently displayed in the official history and the reading you believe should have been entered. Keep copies of any supporting documents and check how much time has passed since the test.

The next step depends on whether the test took place within the last 28 days or more than 28 days ago. The official correction routes are explained in the following sections.

Recent MOT mileage error

How to correct MOT mileage within 28 days

If the incorrect mileage was recorded during an MOT test in the last 28 days, contact the MOT test centre that carried out the test. Explain that the recorded odometer reading appears incorrect and ask the centre to investigate the original entry.

The official GOV.UK MOT record correction service also provides guidance for reporting mileage mistakes made within the last 28 days. This is different from the evidence-based DVSA application used for older mileage errors.

1. Check the test date

Open the official MOT history and confirm the exact date of the test containing the incorrect mileage. Check that the test was carried out within the last 28 days.

2. Contact the test centre

Find the garage or MOT centre responsible for the test. Explain which mileage figure appears wrong and provide the vehicle registration.

3. Explain the discrepancy

Give the recorded reading, the reading you believe is correct and any available documents supporting your explanation.

4. Check the corrected record

Follow the centre's instructions and check the official MOT history after the correction has been processed.

What information should you give the MOT centre?

Providing clear information helps the test centre identify the correct test and understand the suspected mistake. Have the following details ready:

Vehicle and MOT details

  • ✓ Vehicle registration number
  • ✓ MOT test date
  • ✓ MOT test number, if available
  • ✓ Mileage currently recorded

Information about the error

  • ✓ Mileage believed to be correct
  • ✓ Explanation of the discrepancy
  • ✓ Available supporting documents
  • ✓ Contact details for follow-up
Act promptly after finding the mistake

If you discover a mileage error shortly after the MOT, contact the test centre without delay. Do not assume that the mistake will automatically disappear from the online record.

What if the MOT centre cannot correct the mileage?

Ask the centre to explain the next step. You can use the official GOV.UK MOT record mistake service to find the appropriate reporting route for a recent mileage error. Do not attempt to change the vehicle's odometer to make it match the incorrect MOT entry.

Older MOT mileage errors

How to correct MOT mileage after 28 days

If the MOT test took place more than 28 days ago, the official route is to submit a mileage correction request through the GOV.UK MOT record mistake service.

The Driver and Vehicle Standards Agency (DVSA) requires supporting evidence showing the correct mileage at the time of the original test. A statement that the recorded mileage looks wrong is not enough on its own.

1. Identify the incorrect test

Find the original MOT date, test number and mileage entry using the official MOT history service.

2. Gather qualifying evidence

Locate a suitable document showing the correct mileage, the vehicle registration or VIN and a date within one day of the test.

3. Complete the online form

Enter the required personal, vehicle and MOT details into the official DVSA correction form.

4. Upload and submit

Upload the supporting document, submit the request and retain any confirmation or reference provided by the service.

What details does the DVSA correction form require?

According to GOV.UK, the application requires personal contact information, vehicle identification details and relevant MOT information.

Your details

Your name, driving licence number, address, telephone number and email address.

Vehicle details

Vehicle registration, VIN, make and model.

MOT details

The relevant MOT test date and test number, together with evidence supporting the mileage correction.

DVSA must assess the evidence

Submitting a correction request does not guarantee that the mileage entry will be changed. DVSA assesses the supporting information before deciding whether a correction can be made.

Where do you submit the application?

Use the official government correction service. It explains the reporting process and provides access to the online application.

Supporting documents

What evidence does DVSA accept for an MOT mileage correction?

For a mileage correction more than 28 days after the MOT, DVSA requires documentary evidence showing the correct mileage at the time of the test.

GOV.UK identifies several types of documents that may be used. Whatever document you provide, it must contain the required vehicle, mileage and date information.

Documents accepted by the official correction service

Document What it may establish Important requirement
Garage invoice Mileage recorded during maintenance or repairs. Must identify the vehicle, mileage and qualifying date.
Service receipt Odometer reading documented during a service. Must contain the required vehicle details and date.
Vehicle service history A dated record of mileage at a previous service. The relevant entry must meet DVSA's date requirement.
Emissions printout A recorded mileage reading associated with an emissions inspection. Must include the necessary vehicle, mileage and date information.
MOT centre job card Mileage documented on the centre's workshop paperwork. Must satisfy the official identification and timing requirements.
Inspection sheet Mileage recorded during an inspection at the MOT centre. Must show the relevant vehicle and qualifying date.

The three essential evidence requirements

1. Vehicle identification

The document must show the vehicle registration number or vehicle identification number (VIN).

2. Recorded mileage

The document must contain the mileage reading supporting the correction.

3. Correct date

The document must be dated within one day of the original MOT test.

Evidence DVSA does not accept

GOV.UK specifically states that the following cannot be used as evidence for an older mileage correction:

MOT certificate

An MOT certificate is not accepted as the supporting document for correcting the mileage recorded on that test.

Dashboard photograph

A photograph showing the mileage on the vehicle's dashboard is not accepted as correction evidence.

Check the date before uploading evidence

A service invoice from several weeks before or after the MOT may help you understand the vehicle's history, but it does not meet the official requirement for a document dated within one day of the test.

Example of suitable evidence

Suppose an MOT took place on 10 March and recorded 87,450 miles. A garage inspection sheet dated 10 March shows the same vehicle's registration and a mileage reading of 67,450 miles.

That inspection sheet contains the type of information DVSA requires for assessing a possible recording error. DVSA must still review the application and decide whether to correct the record.

Keep the original documents

Upload clear copies of the requested evidence and retain the original paperwork. Avoid editing or altering the mileage, dates or vehicle details on supporting documents.

Missing paperwork

Can you correct MOT mileage without supporting evidence?

If the MOT took place more than 28 days ago and you cannot provide a qualifying document, you may not be able to obtain the mileage correction.

The official process requires documentary evidence showing the correct mileage at the time of the test. A convincing explanation or a later odometer reading does not replace those requirements.

Contact the original garage

Ask whether the garage still holds an invoice, inspection sheet or job card from the relevant date.

Check your service records

Look through paper records, emails and digital service documents for an eligible dated entry.

Ask DVSA for guidance

If you cannot locate qualifying evidence, use the official correction service for guidance rather than submitting altered or misleading paperwork.

What if the garage has closed?

A closed MOT centre may make older records harder to obtain. Check whether you already have a service invoice or another eligible document from the relevant date.

Do not assume DVSA can reconstruct the correct mileage solely from later MOT tests. The official evidence requirements still apply.

Preserve the explanation even if the record remains unchanged

Keep copies of the available service history, repair records and correspondence. These may help explain the discrepancy to a future buyer, even if DVSA cannot amend the MOT entry.

DVSA processing times

How long does an MOT mileage correction take?

GOV.UK states that applicants using the official MOT record mistake service will usually receive a reply within eight weeks. Some applications may take longer.

This is a typical response period, not a guarantee that the mileage will be corrected within eight weeks. DVSA must assess the information and supporting evidence provided.

Recent mileage mistake

Contact the original MOT test centre for the correction route applying within 28 days.

Older mileage mistake

Submit the DVSA application with qualifying evidence. GOV.UK gives a usual reply period of up to eight weeks.

After the decision

Check the official MOT history to see whether the entry has been corrected. Keep the DVSA correspondence.

Can you speed up an MOT mileage correction?

There is no guaranteed fast-track correction route described on the official GOV.UK service page.

The most useful preparation is to identify the exact incorrect test, provide the required vehicle details and submit qualifying evidence in a clear and readable form.

Selling your car while a correction is pending

Do not tell a buyer that the MOT mileage has been corrected until the official record confirms it. Explain the discrepancy accurately and retain evidence of any correction request.

Where can you check the result?

Use the official GOV.UK MOT history service to check the recorded mileage after DVSA has responded.

Odometer history

Can an odometer replacement cause an MOT mileage discrepancy?

Yes. An instrument cluster or odometer replacement can create an apparent mileage reduction if the replacement display shows a different reading. This does not automatically mean the vehicle has been clocked, but the change should be supported by reliable documentation.

It is important to distinguish the distance displayed on the dashboard from the vehicle's actual accumulated mileage. A replacement instrument may not display the full distance travelled by the vehicle.

Replacement instrument cluster

A faulty dashboard or odometer may have been replaced with another unit. The replacement display may show a different mileage reading.

Check repair documents

Incorrect instrument configuration

A replacement instrument may require vehicle-specific programming or configuration. An incorrect setup can create confusion about the displayed mileage.

Seek specialist verification

Older odometer rollover

Some older mechanical odometers have a limited number of digits. When their maximum displayed value is reached, the reading may return to zero.

Check vehicle history

What records should you keep after an odometer replacement?

If an instrument cluster or odometer has been replaced, retain documentation showing the reason for replacement, the date, the mileage displayed before the work and the reading displayed by the replacement unit.

  • ✓ Original repair invoice identifying the vehicle
  • ✓ Date of the instrument replacement
  • ✓ Mileage recorded before replacement, if known
  • ✓ Mileage displayed by the replacement unit
  • ✓ Details of any authorised instrument configuration
Important: an accurate MOT entry may not be a recording error

If an MOT tester correctly recorded the mileage displayed on a replacement odometer, that entry is not necessarily a mistake in the MOT record. Do not assume DVSA will replace it with the vehicle's estimated lifetime mileage.

The official correction procedure is intended for incorrect recorded information. Explain any instrument replacement separately and preserve the supporting documentation.

Mileage discrepancy investigation

Wrong MOT mileage or a clocked car? How to recognise the difference

An MOT mileage discrepancy can be caused by a recording mistake, an odometer replacement or a genuine unexplained change in the vehicle's displayed mileage.

Clocking generally refers to altering a vehicle's recorded mileage so that it appears to have travelled a shorter distance than it actually has. However, a suspicious MOT reading alone does not establish that clocking has occurred.

Signs consistent with a recording mistake

  • ✓ One unusual reading among otherwise consistent MOT mileage entries
  • ✓ An obvious extra digit or transposed number
  • ✓ Independent dated records support a different reading
  • ✓ The original test centre can confirm a recording mistake

Signs requiring further investigation

  • ✓ Several unexplained mileage reductions
  • ✓ Service invoices showing higher mileage than the current odometer
  • ✓ Undocumented instrument cluster replacement
  • ✓ Conflicting mileage information in sales adverts and vehicle documents

Does a mileage discrepancy make a car unsafe?

A mileage discrepancy does not automatically mean a vehicle is mechanically unsafe. However, an inaccurate understanding of its mileage can make it harder to assess maintenance requirements and component wear.

If the true distance travelled is uncertain, consider a professional vehicle inspection and review the service history carefully. Do not use a recent MOT pass as proof that the mileage is genuine or that the vehicle has no mechanical faults.

Do not alter an odometer to match an MOT record

If the official MOT history contains an incorrect entry, use the appropriate correction process. Changing the vehicle's displayed mileage to match an inaccurate historical record is not a substitute for correcting the MOT information.

Used car buying checks

Should you buy a used car with incorrect MOT mileage?

A car with an incorrect MOT mileage entry is not automatically a bad purchase. However, an unexplained discrepancy increases uncertainty about its history and should be investigated before agreeing to buy.

The key question is whether the seller can provide a credible explanation supported by dated documents. A promise to correct the mileage after the sale is not equivalent to an approved correction.

Check the MOT sequence

Compare all available mileage readings, not just the latest test. Identify where the discrepancy first appears.

Review the evidence

Request supporting documents

Ask for service invoices, instrument replacement records and any DVSA correspondence explaining the incorrect reading.

Verify the explanation

Walk away if concerns remain

If the seller cannot explain a substantial discrepancy or the evidence conflicts, consider another vehicle.

Avoid unexplained risk

Questions to ask the seller about wrong MOT mileage

  • ✓ Which MOT test contains the suspected error?
  • ✓ What was the correct mileage at that time?
  • ✓ Is there a dated document supporting that reading?
  • ✓ Has the instrument cluster ever been replaced?
  • ✓ Has DVSA already approved a correction?
  • ✓ Does the advertised mileage agree with the available service history?

Should you accept a pending DVSA correction?

A pending correction request is not confirmation that DVSA will amend the mileage record. If you decide to proceed with a purchase, understand the remaining uncertainty and obtain the seller's explanation and relevant documents in writing.

A valid MOT is not a mileage guarantee

An MOT test checks specified vehicle safety and environmental requirements at the time of testing. It does not guarantee that the odometer shows the vehicle's complete lifetime mileage.

Selling a vehicle

Can you sell a car with wrong mileage on its MOT history?

An incorrect MOT mileage entry does not automatically prevent a vehicle from being sold. However, sellers should not misrepresent the mileage or conceal a known discrepancy.

If you have identified a genuine recording mistake, start the appropriate correction process before advertising the vehicle where practical.

Explain the discrepancy

Tell prospective buyers which MOT reading is disputed and why you believe it is incorrect.

Provide supporting records

Make relevant service invoices, repair records and DVSA correspondence available for inspection.

Describe the mileage accurately

Avoid stating that the mileage is verified if the available records do not support that claim.

Should you correct the mileage before advertising?

Where possible, resolving an identifiable MOT recording error before selling can reduce uncertainty for prospective buyers. Keep confirmation of any correction.

If DVSA has not yet made a decision, describe the situation accurately rather than claiming the official record has already been amended.

Keep a written record

Retain the evidence supporting your explanation and any correspondence with the test centre or DVSA. Accurate written disclosure is particularly important when the recorded mileage remains unresolved.

Wrong MOT Mileage FAQs

These are common questions UK drivers ask when an incorrect mileage reading appears in their vehicle's MOT history. The answers explain the official correction process, evidence requirements and what to do when a discrepancy cannot be resolved immediately.

Can you correct wrong mileage on an MOT history?

Yes. Incorrect MOT mileage can be corrected through the official process. For a test carried out within the last 28 days, contact the MOT test centre. For an older test, use the DVSA correction service and provide qualifying documentary evidence.

How do I correct MOT mileage within 28 days?

GOV.UK says applicants will usually receive a reply within eight weeks, although some requests may take longer. This is not a guarantee that the record will be changed within eight weeks.

Does wrong MOT mileage mean a car has been clocked?

No. Incorrect MOT mileage does not automatically prove clocking. A typing mistake, odometer replacement or other recording issue may explain the discrepancy. Unexplained mileage reductions should still be investigated using independent records.

Can an odometer replacement cause incorrect MOT mileage?

Yes. A replacement instrument cluster may display a different mileage reading. However, if the MOT tester accurately recorded the displayed reading, the MOT entry is not necessarily a recording error. Keep the replacement documentation and seek appropriate guidance.

Can I sell a car with incorrect mileage on its MOT history?

An incorrect MOT mileage entry does not automatically prevent a vehicle from being sold. However, sellers should describe the mileage accurately, disclose known discrepancies and avoid claiming a correction has been completed before the official record confirms it.

Should I buy a used car with a mileage discrepancy?

Investigate the discrepancy before buying. Compare MOT readings, service invoices and instrument replacement records. If the seller cannot provide a credible explanation supported by evidence, consider another vehicle.

Where can I report incorrect MOT mileage?

Use the official GOV.UK Report a Mistake on Your MOT Record service for the applicable correction process in England, Scotland and Wales. MOT records from Northern Ireland are handled separately through the DVA.

Final Thoughts

What to do when your MOT history shows the wrong mileage

Finding an incorrect mileage reading on an MOT history can be concerning, particularly when buying or selling a vehicle. However, an unusual reading does not automatically indicate that the car has been clocked or that its history has been deliberately altered.

The first step is to identify the disputed test and compare the recorded mileage with independent documents. Once you have established a likely recording mistake, follow the official correction procedure.

Check the date

For a test within the last 28 days, contact the original MOT test centre.

Gather the evidence

For older errors, obtain a qualifying document showing the vehicle, mileage and required date.

Confirm the result

Keep correspondence and check the official MOT history after a correction decision.

Do not confuse a mileage discrepancy with proof of clocking

A suspicious reading deserves investigation, but it is not proof of deliberate odometer alteration. Use dated evidence and consider professional advice where the vehicle's true mileage remains uncertain.
