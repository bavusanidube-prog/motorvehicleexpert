Vehicle Records Explained

How MOT history works when a vehicle changes registration number

MOT testing records contain information about the vehicle tested, the test date, its result and the recorded mileage. They may also include defects and advisory items.

The official MOT history service allows drivers to search using a vehicle registration number. Vehicle identification details are important when investigating whether a particular result belongs to the correct vehicle.

A registration number is a visible identifier assigned to a vehicle, but it can change through the private registration transfer process.

Registration number

The number plate is used to search official vehicle and MOT services. It can change when a private registration is transferred.

Vehicle identification number

The VIN identifies the underlying vehicle and is important when checking whether a test record was associated with the correct car.

MOT test record

The record documents a particular test result, including its date, mileage and recorded defects.

DVLA & DVSA Explained

DVLA or DVSA: who should you contact about missing MOT history?

DVLA and DVSA are different government agencies with different responsibilities. Understanding which records each organisation handles can help you choose the appropriate route for your problem.

The Driver and Vehicle Licensing Agency (DVLA) handles vehicle registration and licensing information, including private registration number transfers.

The Driver and Vehicle Standards Agency (DVSA) is responsible for MOT testing oversight in Great Britain and the associated MOT testing records.

DVLA — Vehicle registration

DVLA handles vehicle registration records, registration number assignments and private plate transfers.

  • ✓ Private number plate transfers.
  • ✓ Vehicle registration details.
  • ✓ Registration documents.
  • ✓ Changes to registration records.

DVSA — MOT testing records

DVSA oversees MOT testing in England, Scotland and Wales and provides routes for reporting mistakes in MOT records.

  • ✓ MOT test records.
  • ✓ Missing MOT test results.
  • ✓ Incorrect recorded test details.
  • ✓ MOT record correction guidance.

UK Driving Rules

Can you drive if your MOT history is missing after a number plate change?

Missing historical MOT records do not automatically make a vehicle illegal to drive. The important questions are whether the vehicle currently requires an MOT, whether it has a valid MOT and whether it is roadworthy.

For vehicles that require an MOT, driving without a valid certificate is generally prohibited, subject to limited exceptions such as travelling to a pre-arranged MOT test.

Even where an exception applies, the vehicle must still be roadworthy. A valid MOT does not excuse driving a dangerous or unroadworthy car.

Frequently Asked Questions

MOT history after number plate change FAQs

These answers explain common MOT record problems following a private registration change, including missing results, old number plates, vehicle identity and MOT validity.

1. Does changing a number plate remove MOT history?

No. Changing a registration number does not make the vehicle a different car or automatically erase its previous MOT tests. If the history appears incomplete, check the current registration, confirm the vehicle details and investigate any missing records.

2. Why is my MOT history missing after a private plate transfer?

You may be searching using an old registration number, the vehicle details may not match what you expected, or an individual MOT result may be missing. Confirm the current registration and check the official GOV.UK MOT history service first.

3. Can I check MOT history using my old registration number?

You can try searching using the previous registration, but check the returned vehicle details carefully. Registration numbers can be transferred, so an old plate may no longer identify your vehicle.

4. Will my old MOT history transfer to my new private plate?

Previous MOT tests remain part of the vehicle's history when its registration changes. If expected results do not appear under the current plate, verify the vehicle identity and follow official MOT record guidance.

5. Does a private number plate change cancel my MOT?

No. Changing a private registration number does not, by itself, cancel a valid MOT. You should still check the current MOT status and expiry date using the official GOV.UK service.

6. How long does MOT history take to update after a plate change?

There is no single guaranteed update time that should be assumed for every registration or MOT record issue. Confirm that the private plate transfer has been completed and check the official vehicle details. If records remain incorrect, follow the relevant guidance.

7. Who fixes missing MOT history, DVLA or DVSA?

DVLA handles vehicle registration information and private plate transfers. DVSA oversees MOT testing records in Great Britain. If the registration itself is wrong, check DVLA guidance. If an MOT test record is missing or incorrect, follow the official MOT record correction process.

8. What if my old number plate shows another vehicle?

Do not assume the returned MOT history belongs to your car. A registration number can be associated with another vehicle following a transfer. Use the current registration and verify the vehicle identity before relying on the results.

9. What if my latest MOT pass is not showing online?

Check the official MOT history and current MOT status separately. If the latest successful test is missing, contact the test centre to confirm the test details and follow GOV.UK record correction guidance if necessary.

10. Can I drive if my MOT history is missing?

Missing older MOT results do not automatically make a vehicle illegal to drive. What matters is whether the vehicle requires an MOT, has a valid MOT where required and is roadworthy. If current MOT validity cannot be established, seek clarification before ordinary road use.

11. Can I correct MOT history after changing my number plate?

Genuine MOT record errors can be investigated through the appropriate official process. The route depends on whether the problem involves a missing test, incorrect registration, wrong VIN, mileage or another recorded detail.

12. Should I buy a used car with missing MOT history?

Do not automatically reject a car because it previously carried a private number plate. However, investigate unexplained gaps, check the VIN and V5C, compare mileage and service records, and confirm the current MOT. Avoid completing the purchase if serious identity or history concerns remain unresolved.

Key Takeaways

What to remember when MOT history disappears after a plate change

A private number plate change does not automatically delete a vehicle's MOT history or cancel a valid MOT certificate. However, incomplete online records should still be investigated carefully.

What you should do

  • ✓ Confirm the current registration.
  • ✓ Check official MOT history.
  • ✓ Verify the vehicle identity.
  • ✓ Check current MOT status separately.
  • ✓ Investigate missing test records.
  • ✓ Follow official correction guidance.

What you should avoid

  • ✓ Assuming old plates always identify the same vehicle.
  • ✓ Treating missing history as proof that the MOT has expired.
  • ✓ Relying only on third-party checkers.
  • ✓ Ignoring mismatched VIN information.
  • ✓ Driving when MOT validity is uncertain.
  • ✓ Buying a vehicle with unexplained identity concerns.

Final advice for UK drivers

The most useful first step is to check the current registration using the official GOV.UK MOT history and MOT status services. If the vehicle details are correct but previous test results are missing, identify the exact discrepancy and follow the official reporting process.

When buying a used car, remember that a private registration is not automatically suspicious. The important issue is whether the vehicle's identity, recorded mileage, MOT history and supporting documents can be verified.

Motor Vehicle Expert recommendation

Always establish the correct vehicle identity before interpreting MOT history. A reliable decision depends on the underlying vehicle and its records, not simply the number plate currently displayed.
