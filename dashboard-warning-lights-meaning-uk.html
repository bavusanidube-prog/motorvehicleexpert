UK Car Dashboard Warning Lights – Full Meanings, Safety & MOT Rules

Dashboard warning lights can mean anything from a normal system being switched on to an engine, braking or electrical fault that needs immediate attention. This complete UK guide explains what the main red, amber, green and blue lights mean, whether it is safe to keep driving, simple checks you can make, typical repair costs and how each warning relates to the MOT.

Quick answer

What Should You Do When a Dashboard Warning Light Comes On?

Start with the colour, but do not stop there. Look at whether the light is steady or flashing, what symbol is showing, whether a dashboard message appears and what the car is doing at the same time. A warning light is evidence that helps you decide the next check; it is not always the complete diagnosis.

Red warnings usually need the fastest response, amber warnings normally mean a system needs attention, and green or blue lights are commonly status indicators. However, a flashing light, loss of braking, heavy steering, overheating, misfiring, smoke or sudden power loss can make the situation much more urgent regardless of colour.

🟥 Red

Stop / Investigate
High Safety Priority

Red lights can indicate problems involving oil pressure, overheating, braking, charging or other systems that may become unsafe or cause expensive damage if ignored.

🟠 Amber / Yellow

Diagnose Soon
Fault or Attention Needed

Amber often means the car has detected a fault or a system needs attention. Short cautious driving may be possible for some warnings if the vehicle otherwise behaves normally.

🟢 Green / 🔵 Blue

Usually Information
System Operating

These colours commonly confirm that headlights, cruise control, indicators, main beam or another normal function is active.

⚡ Flashing

Check Immediately
Behaviour Changes Urgency

A flashing warning can mean something different from the same symbol displayed steadily. A flashing engine-management light, for example, can indicate an active severe misfire.

❌ Stop driving when the warning is matched by a serious symptom

Stop somewhere safe if you have an oil-pressure warning, overheating, major brake-performance change, very heavy steering, severe engine misfire, smoke, burning smell, rapidly falling power or a dashboard instruction telling you to stop the vehicle.

✅ A warning light does not always mean “replace the part shown”

An ABS light does not automatically mean the ABS pump has failed. A battery light does not automatically mean the battery is bad. An engine-management light does not automatically identify a sensor to replace. Use the warning together with symptoms and testing before buying parts.

Colour guide

What Do Dashboard Warning-Light Colours Mean?

Manufacturers use colour to help drivers judge priority, but there is no rule saying every red symbol means exactly the same thing or every amber symbol is safe to ignore. Treat colour as the first sorting step, then use the actual symbol, vehicle message and symptoms to decide what to do.

🟥 Red = Urgent

Often indicates a safety-critical fault, risk of mechanical damage or a condition that requires the driver to stop and investigate.

🟠 Amber = Attention

Usually means a monitored system has detected a problem or needs attention. Some amber faults allow cautious driving; others become urgent when symptoms appear.

🟢 Green = Active

Commonly confirms that a normal system such as exterior lighting, indicators or cruise control is switched on.

🔵 Blue = Active

Most commonly associated with main/high beam or another status indication rather than a mechanical failure.

⚪ White / Grey

Often used for information, driver-assistance status or a feature that is available but not currently active.

⚡ Flashing

Flashing can change the meaning or urgency completely. Always check the owner's handbook and accompanying dashboard message.

MOT result is based on the tested system — not the colour

The DVSA inspection manual has separate requirements for systems such as ABS, SRS, power steering, TPMS and the engine malfunction indicator lamp. A warning's colour alone does not decide whether the vehicle passes or fails.

🟥 Red warning lights

Red Dashboard Warning Lights: Stop, Check or Recover?

Red warning lights usually deserve the fastest response because they can relate to engine damage, braking safety, overheating, charging failure or another fault that may become serious quickly.

That does not mean every red symbol automatically fails an MOT or that every red light means the engine must be switched off instantly. The correct decision depends on the system involved, whether the warning is steady or flashing and what the car is doing at the same time.

🟥 Critical engine warning

Flashing Engine Management Light

A flashing engine-management light is more urgent than a steady amber EML. It commonly means the ECU has detected a severe combustion or misfire problem capable of overheating the catalytic converter.

Meaning

Active Misfire / Combustion Fault

Spark, injector, compression, fuelling or other combustion problems can leave unburned fuel entering the exhaust.

Safe to Drive?

❌ Usually No
Stop Safely

If the engine is shaking, losing power, smelling strongly of fuel or the warning continues flashing, stop and arrange diagnosis or recovery.

Quick Checks

Observe, Do Not Guess

Note whether the engine is rough, which conditions trigger the warning and whether other lights appear. Avoid repeatedly restarting and revving a badly misfiring engine.

MOT Status

FAIL — Major Where MIL Inspection Applies
Engine MIL Malfunction

For vehicles within the DVSA MIL inspection requirements, an engine malfunction indicator lamp that is inoperative or indicating a malfunction is a Major defect.

Typical UK Repair Cost

£40–£1,500+
Cause Dependent

Diagnostic scanning may cost around £40–£100. Ignition coils or plugs can be relatively modest, while injector, catalyst or mechanical-engine faults can cost several hundred pounds or more.

Do not drive through a severe misfire

A relatively modest ignition fault can become a much more expensive catalytic-converter repair if the engine is driven while unburned fuel is entering the exhaust.

Flashing Then Stops?

Intermittent flashing still matters because the fault may only occur under load, acceleration or specific engine conditions.

🟥 Engine lubrication

Oil Pressure Warning Light

A red oil-can warning usually means the engine may not have enough oil pressure to lubricate itself correctly. It is not the same thing as an oil-service reminder.

Meaning

Lubrication Pressure Problem

Possible causes include low oil level, a faulty pressure sensor, blocked oil pickup, worn oil pump or internal engine wear.

Safe to Drive?

❌ No
Stop the Engine

Continuing to run the engine with genuinely low oil pressure can damage bearings, camshafts, turbochargers and other lubricated components very quickly.

Quick DIY Checks

Check Level When Safe

Park on level ground, switch off, allow the oil to settle and check the dipstick where fitted. If low, use the correct oil. Do not keep driving if the warning remains after the level is corrected.

MOT Status

Not a Blanket Warning-Light FAIL
Underlying Defect May Matter

The red oil-pressure warning itself is not listed in the current car MOT manual as a universal standalone warning-lamp failure in the same way as an applicable engine MIL, ABS or SRS warning. Separate defects such as serious fluid leakage may still affect the MOT.

Typical UK Repair Cost

£10–£1,500+
Huge Cost Range

Correct oil may cost only £10–£40. A pressure switch can be around £60–£180 fitted, while oil-pump or internal-engine repairs can cost hundreds or thousands.

Correct oil level does not prove correct oil pressure

If the dipstick shows the correct level but the pressure warning remains illuminated, do not assume the sensor is faulty. Actual oil pressure should be tested before the engine is used further.

🟥 Cooling system

Coolant Temperature / Overheating Warning Light

A red coolant-temperature warning means the engine or cooling system is operating too hot. Some vehicles use a thermometer symbol, others display a red temperature message or tell the driver to stop the vehicle.

Meaning

Engine Temperature Too High

Low coolant, a leak, thermostat fault, failed water pump, radiator problem, fan fault or internal engine problem can all cause overheating.

Safe to Drive?

❌ No
Stop and Cool Down

Continued overheating can damage the cylinder head, head gasket, turbocharger and engine.

Quick DIY Checks

Only When Fully Cold

Allow the vehicle to cool completely before checking coolant. Look underneath for leakage and check the expansion-tank level only when safe.

MOT Status

Not Automatically a Dashboard-Light FAIL
Vehicle Condition Still Matters

The temperature warning itself is not treated as one universal dashboard-light MOT defect. However, a vehicle that is unsafe to test or has another testable defect can still fail or be refused testing as appropriate.

Typical UK Repair Cost

£80–£2,000+
Overheating Can Escalate

A hose may cost around £80–£200, thermostat £120–£350, water pump £250–£650, radiator £250–£700, while head-gasket or cylinder-head work can exceed £800–£2,000.

Never remove a hot coolant cap

A hot cooling system is pressurised and can release boiling coolant or steam. Allow the engine to cool fully before opening the reservoir or radiator cap.

🟥 Brake system

Brake System Warning Light

A red brake warning can mean different things depending on the car. It may simply show that the parking brake is applied, but it can also indicate low brake fluid, a hydraulic problem or another braking fault.

Meaning

Parking Brake / Fluid / Brake Fault

The same warning lamp can sometimes serve more than one braking function, so check the dashboard message and vehicle handbook.

Safe to Drive?

⚠️ / ❌ Depends on Pedal & Braking
Safety-Critical

If the warning stays on with the parking brake released, braking feels different, the pedal is soft or fluid is low, stop and investigate rather than continuing normally.

Quick DIY Checks

Parking Brake & Visible Fluid Level

Confirm the parking brake is fully released. If the brake-fluid reservoir is transparent and safely visible, check whether the level is near the minimum mark without removing the cap.

MOT Status

Warning Lamp: Usually Minor; Serious Brake Faults Can FAIL
Exact Defect Matters

Under the current DVSA manual, an illuminated or defective brake-fluid warning light is a Minor defect. Brake fluid significantly below minimum is Major, fluid not visible is Dangerous, and serious hydraulic or braking faults can separately cause Major or Dangerous failures.

Typical UK Repair Cost

£60–£1,000+
Cause Dependent

Brake-fluid replacement may cost £60–£120, pads around £100–£220 per axle, while hydraulic, caliper, master-cylinder or ABS-related repairs can run into several hundred pounds.

Brake-fluid warning and ABS warning are not the same MOT rule

The brake-fluid warning lamp itself can be Minor, while an ABS warning device showing a system malfunction is a Major defect. Always identify which symbol is actually illuminated.

🟥 Charging system

Battery / Charging Warning Light

The red battery symbol usually means the charging system is not maintaining the vehicle's electrical supply correctly. It does not automatically mean the battery itself needs replacing.

Meaning

Charging System Fault

Possible causes include an alternator fault, belt or tensioner problem, charging cable, poor connection, voltage regulator or battery-management issue.

Safe to Drive?

⚠️ Only to Reach Safety
Vehicle May Stop

Once the battery's stored energy is exhausted, the engine or EV low-voltage systems may shut down and the vehicle may not restart.

Quick DIY Checks

Reduce Electrical Load

Switch off heated screens, seat heaters and other unnecessary loads. Note dimming lights, steering changes or multiple warning lamps. Do not touch belts or charging components with the engine running.

MOT Status

Not an Automatic Standalone Warning-Light FAIL
Underlying Electrical Defects May Matter

The battery/charging warning itself is not listed as one universal standalone MOT failure. The MOT does inspect relevant batteries, wiring and electrical equipment, and other faults can cause separate defects.

Typical UK Repair Cost

£90–£700+
Test Before Replacing

A battery may cost roughly £90–£300, while alternator replacement is often around £250–£700 or more. Wiring, belt and charging diagnosis can add further cost.

Battery light + heavy steering + rising temperature can indicate a belt problem

On some engines one auxiliary belt can drive the alternator and other components. If the charging warning appears with sudden heavy steering, overheating or unusual belt noise, stop rather than assuming it is only a battery fault.

🟥 Restraint system

Airbag / SRS Warning Light

The SRS warning relates to the vehicle's supplementary restraint system. Depending on the car, this can include airbags, crash sensors, seat-belt pretensioners, occupancy sensors, clocksprings and associated wiring.

Meaning

Restraint System Malfunction

A fault has been detected that may prevent some restraint components from operating correctly in a collision.

Safe to Drive?

⚠️ Vehicle May Drive Normally
Crash Protection May Be Reduced

The fault may not affect steering or engine operation, but you should not assume all airbags and pretensioners remain available.

Quick DIY Checks

Visual Check Only

Look for obvious objects disturbing wiring beneath the seats, but do not probe, unplug or test airbag connectors with ordinary electrical equipment.

MOT Status

FAIL — Major
SRS MIL Malfunction

The current DVSA manual states that an SRS malfunction indicator lamp indicating a system malfunction is a Major defect. A lamp showing only that the passenger airbag has intentionally been switched off is not treated as that defect.

Typical UK Repair Cost

£50–£1,000+
Diagnose Before Parts

Diagnosis may be around £50–£120. Wiring or connector repairs can be relatively modest, while clocksprings, modules, sensors or airbag replacement can cost several hundred pounds or more.

Do not test airbag circuits like ordinary wiring

Airbag and pretensioner circuits contain pyrotechnic components. Diagnosis should follow the manufacturer's procedure using appropriate equipment.

Passenger airbag switched off is different

DVSA specifically states that a passenger airbag intentionally switched off is not itself a defect. The failure applies when the SRS malfunction indicator is actually indicating a system fault.

🟠 Amber / yellow warning lights

Amber Dashboard Warning Lights: Can You Keep Driving?

Amber or yellow warning lights normally mean a system needs attention, but they are not all equally urgent. Some allow cautious driving to a garage if the car feels completely normal; others become more serious when they appear with power loss, heavy steering, poor braking, overheating or additional warnings.

The safest approach is symptom-first: identify the symbol, read any dashboard message, note whether the light is steady or flashing and pay attention to how the vehicle is actually behaving.

Solid Engine Management Light

⚠️ Diagnose Soon
Engine / Emissions Fault

The ECU has detected a monitored fault. The car can sometimes drive normally even though the fault is MOT-relevant.

ABS

⚠️ Cautious Driving Only
Anti-Lock Function May Be Lost

Conventional braking may remain, but ABS intervention can be unavailable when you need it most.

TPMS

⚠️ Check Tyres First
Pressure or System Fault

It may simply mean a tyre is low, or the pressure-monitoring system itself may have malfunctioned.

DPF

⚠️ Act Before It Escalates
Soot / Regeneration Issue

A diesel particulate filter may be becoming overloaded or a fault may be preventing regeneration.

EPS / Power Steering

⚠️ / ❌ Depends on Steering
Steering Assistance Fault

If steering suddenly becomes very heavy or inconsistent, stop rather than continuing normally.

AdBlue / SCR

⚠️ Refill or Diagnose
Emissions System

A low-level warning is different from an SCR-system malfunction, so read the accompanying message carefully.

🟠 Common amber warnings

Engine Management, ABS and TPMS Warning Lights

Solid Engine Management Light

A steady engine-management light means the ECU has detected a monitored engine or emissions-system fault. The car may feel completely normal, which is why this light is often ignored for too long.

Meaning

Engine / Emissions Fault

Possible causes include oxygen sensors, misfires, air leaks, airflow sensors, catalyst efficiency, EGR, DPF, EVAP, injectors, boost faults or wiring problems.

Safe to Drive?

⚠️ Sometimes
Only if Vehicle Feels Normal

Short cautious driving may be reasonable if the light is steady, the engine is smooth and there is no smoke, overheating, severe power loss or unusual noise.

Quick Checks

Scan Before Replacing Parts

Note any symptoms and arrange a diagnostic scan. Do not assume a code naming a sensor proves that sensor itself has failed.

MOT Status

FAIL — Major Where MIL Inspection Applies
Applicable Vehicle Required to Pass

Where the engine malfunction indicator lamp is within the vehicle's MOT inspection requirements, an inoperative MIL or one indicating a malfunction is a Major defect.

Typical UK Repair Cost

£40–£1,500+
Diagnosis First

A scan may be around £40–£100, while sensor, ignition, injector, catalyst, DPF or wiring repairs can range from modest to very expensive.

ABS Warning Light

The ABS warning means the anti-lock braking system has detected a malfunction. Basic hydraulic braking may still operate, but the system designed to prevent wheel lock during heavy braking may not be available.

Meaning

Anti-Lock Braking Fault

Common causes include wheel-speed sensors, damaged sensor rings, wiring faults, wheel bearings, low voltage or ABS hydraulic / control-unit problems.

Safe to Drive?

⚠️ Cautiously
Only if Normal Braking Remains

If braking feels normal and no red brake warning is present, cautious travel for diagnosis may be possible. Stop if pedal feel, braking balance or stopping performance changes.

Quick Checks

Restart and Inspect

Restart once and note whether the light returns. Check for obvious tyre or wheel damage following recent repair or a hard impact, but proper fault-code diagnosis is normally required.

MOT Status

FAIL — Major
ABS System Malfunction

Under the current DVSA manual, an ABS warning device showing a system malfunction is a Major defect. An ABS warning device that does not work is also Major.

Typical UK Repair Cost

£80–£1,200+
Sensor to Module Range

A wheel-speed sensor may cost roughly £80–£220, while bearing, wiring, hydraulic-unit or ABS-module repairs can cost several hundred pounds or more.

ABS warning + red brake warning is more serious

Two braking warnings together can indicate more than the loss of anti-lock assistance. If braking performance or pedal feel is abnormal, stop and arrange inspection.

TPMS / Tyre Pressure Warning Light

A tyre-pressure warning can mean one or more tyres are below their expected pressure, but a flashing or persistent lamp can also indicate a fault within the tyre-pressure monitoring system itself.

Meaning

Low Pressure or TPMS Fault

Pressure can drop because of temperature change, a slow puncture, leaking valve, wheel damage or normal air loss. Sensor batteries and system faults can also trigger warnings.

Safe to Drive?

⚠️ Check Tyres First
❌ Do Not Drive on Flat Tyre

Stop somewhere safe and inspect the tyres. Do not continue on a visibly flat, damaged or rapidly deflating tyre.

Quick DIY Checks

Measure All Tyres

Check pressures using an accurate gauge and adjust them to the vehicle manufacturer's recommended cold pressures. Inspect for nails, sidewall damage and obvious leakage.

MOT Status

Depends on What the Warning Means
Applicable M1 Vehicles From 1 January 2012

TPMS inspection applies to applicable M1 vehicles first used on or after 1 January 2012. A malfunctioning or obviously inoperative TPMS is a Major defect. An obviously underinflated tyre is a Minor defect. If the tester cannot tell whether the lamp means low pressure or a system malfunction, advisory information should be recorded.

Typical UK Repair Cost

£0–£250+
Often Lower Cost

Correcting pressure may cost nothing. Puncture repair may be around £20–£40, a tyre roughly £60–£250+, and a replacement TPMS sensor commonly around £50–£120 each.

Do not reset TPMS until the tyre pressure is correct

Resetting a warning without checking the tyres can hide the real reason the system alerted you. Confirm tyre condition and pressure first.

🟠 Diesel & steering warnings

DPF, Power-Steering and AdBlue Warning Lights

DPF Warning Light

A DPF warning means the diesel particulate filter or its monitoring system needs attention. The filter traps soot from diesel exhaust and periodically burns that soot away through regeneration.

Meaning

Soot Loading / Regeneration Problem

Short journeys, failed regeneration, pressure-sensor faults, temperature-sensor problems, EGR faults, injector problems or another engine fault can all contribute.

Safe to Drive?

⚠️ Often Initially
Follow Manufacturer Guidance

If the warning is the only symptom and the handbook permits it, a suitable drive may allow regeneration. Limp mode, multiple warnings or severe performance loss needs diagnosis instead.

Quick Checks

Read the Message

Check the handbook for the vehicle-specific regeneration procedure. Do not repeatedly attempt forced regeneration where another engine fault is preventing the process.

MOT Status

Warning Alone Is Not the Whole Test
DPF / Emissions Defects Can FAIL

The MOT checks emissions-control equipment and diesel smoke. A manufacturer's emissions-control device that is missing, obviously modified or obviously defective is a Major defect. Evidence that a DPF has been tampered with can also fail the test.

Typical UK Repair Cost

£80–£2,000+
Diagnose Root Cause

Forced regeneration may cost around £80–£180, cleaning £150–£350, sensors £100–£300, while replacement filters can range from roughly £500 to £2,000 or more.

A blocked DPF may be the result, not the root fault

Injector problems, thermostat faults, EGR issues, failed sensors and repeated short journeys can all prevent regeneration or produce excessive soot. Find the cause before replacing the filter.

EPS / Power-Steering Warning Light

The steering warning means the power-assistance system has detected a fault. Depending on the vehicle, assistance may be electric, electro-hydraulic or conventional hydraulic.

Meaning

Steering Assistance Fault

Possible causes include EPS motors, torque or position sensors, steering racks, wiring, low voltage, hydraulic faults or control-unit issues.

Safe to Drive?

⚠️ / ❌ Depends on Steering
Stop if Steering Becomes Unsafe

If steering is suddenly very heavy, jerky, inconsistent or hard to control, stop rather than attempting to continue the journey.

Quick Checks

Restart Once / Check Voltage Clues

Restart once and note whether battery or charging warnings are also present. Low system voltage can trigger EPS faults on some vehicles.

MOT Status

FAIL — Major
Dangerous if Steering Adversely Affected

The current MOT manual classifies a power-steering system malfunction or inoperative power assistance as a Major defect. If steering is adversely affected, relevant defects can become Dangerous.

Typical UK Repair Cost

£50–£1,500+
Wide Repair Range

Diagnosis may be £50–£120, wiring or sensors £100–£400, while an EPS motor, column or steering rack can cost £500–£1,500 or more.

Heavy steering after battery or alternator trouble can be connected

Electric power steering depends on a healthy low-voltage supply. A charging-system problem can sometimes trigger steering warnings alongside battery and other electrical faults.

AdBlue / SCR Warning Light

AdBlue is used by many diesel SCR systems to reduce nitrogen-oxide emissions. A dashboard message may simply mean the fluid level is low, or it may indicate an SCR-system malfunction.

Meaning

Low AdBlue or SCR Fault

Level warnings are different from faults involving pumps, injectors, heaters, NOx sensors, wiring or SCR-control components.

Safe to Drive?

⚠️ Usually Initially
Do Not Ignore Countdown

A simple low-level warning normally gives time to refill, but many vehicles eventually prevent restarting if the issue is not resolved.

Quick Checks

Refill Only if Level Warning

Use the correct AdBlue and follow the manufacturer's minimum refill requirement. If the warning remains after a proper refill, diagnose the SCR system.

MOT Status

Depends on the Underlying Emissions Fault
SCR Equipment Is Test-Relevant

A low-level message alone is not one universal standalone MOT warning-lamp failure. However, emissions-control equipment that is missing, obviously modified or obviously defective can be a Major defect, and the vehicle must still meet its applicable emissions requirements.

Typical UK Repair Cost

£10–£1,500+
Level vs System Fault

AdBlue itself may cost only £10–£30. Sensors or injectors can cost £150–£500, while pumps, tanks and complex SCR repairs may reach £400–£1,500 or more.

Read the wording as well as the symbol

“AdBlue low — 1,000 miles remaining” is a different problem from “AdBlue system fault — no restart in 500 miles”. The dashboard message can tell you whether the priority is simply topping up or diagnosing the emissions system.

🟢 Green / 🔵 blue

Green and Blue Dashboard Lights: Usually Not Faults

Green and blue dashboard symbols usually tell you that a function is switched on or available rather than warning that a component has failed.

🔵 Main Beam

✅ Normal

Confirms the main/high-beam headlamps are switched on.

🟢 Dipped / Exterior Lights

✅ Normal

Shows that the vehicle's exterior lighting is active.

🟢 Indicators

✅ Normal

Flashing green arrows confirm direction indicators or hazards are operating.

🟢 Cruise Control

✅ Usually Normal

Commonly confirms cruise control is enabled or actively holding speed.

🟢 Front Fog Lamps

✅ Normal

Confirms the front fog lamps are switched on where fitted.

🟢 Start-Stop

✅ Usually Normal

Often indicates that automatic engine stop-start is available or currently active.

🟢 EV READY

✅ Normal

On hybrids and EVs, READY often means the vehicle can move even though there is no engine sound.

🟢 Driver Assistance

✅ Usually Active

Lane-assistance, adaptive cruise and other driver aids often use green when actively functioning.

⚪ White / Grey Status

✅ Information

Often means a function is available, selected or waiting to activate.

MOT status: normally PASS as an indication — but the equipment itself must work where tested

A green or blue dashboard indication is not normally a fault in itself. However, the MOT still checks applicable equipment such as required lamps and the main-beam tell-tale where relevant.

A status lamp behaving strangely can still be a clue

An indicator arrow flashing unusually fast may suggest an exterior lamp fault, and a lighting tell-tale that does not behave normally can point towards a switch, lamp or electrical problem.

⚡ Hybrid, EV & unusual warnings

Flashing, Master Warning, Immobiliser and Hybrid / EV Lights

Modern dashboards can show symbols that do not fit neatly into the traditional red-and-amber categories. Hybrid and electric vehicles also add traction-battery, charging, regenerative-braking and high-voltage warnings.

⚡ Flashing Warning

Check Immediately
Flashing Changes Meaning

Always find out why the light is flashing. A flashing EML can be urgent, while a flashing security LED after locking the car may be completely normal.

⚠️ Master Warning Triangle

Read the Message
Secondary Warning

A triangle with an exclamation mark often tells you to look at a separate dashboard message explaining the actual problem.

🔐 Immobiliser / Security

Depends on Behaviour
Often Normal When Parked

Never carry out DIY work on orange high-voltage cables or traction-battery components

EV READY does not mean the vehicle is switched off

An electric or hybrid vehicle can be ready to move without engine noise. Always confirm the READY indication and vehicle state before working around or leaving the car.

MOT treatment still depends on the specific testable system

There is no separate rule saying every hybrid or EV dashboard warning automatically fails. The tester applies the current defect criteria to the system involved, just as with petrol and diesel vehicles.

📜 Official UK MOT rules

Which Dashboard Warning Lights Fail an MOT in the UK?

There is no single UK rule saying that every illuminated dashboard warning light automatically fails an MOT. The MOT tester applies the defect criteria for the specific system involved. That is why an ABS malfunction light can be a Major defect while a battery-charging warning is not automatically a standalone warning-lamp failure.

The current DVSA inspection manual for cars and passenger vehicles is updated to 1 June 2026. It uses the defect categories Minor, Major and Dangerous. Advisory information can also be recorded where something deserves attention but is not a testable defect at that level.

✅ PASS

No Relevant Defect
Vehicle Meets Test Standard

A normal green or blue status lamp is not a fault simply because it is illuminated. The equipment itself must still meet any applicable MOT requirements.

⚠️ MINOR

MOT Still Passes
Repair / Monitor

A Minor defect does not cause an MOT failure, but it is recorded and should not be ignored. Some brake-fluid and tyre-pressure conditions fall into this category.

❌ MAJOR

MOT FAIL
Repair Required

Major defects include several testable warning-system malfunctions such as ABS, SRS and applicable engine MIL faults.

❌ DANGEROUS

MOT FAIL
Do Not Drive

Dangerous is used where a defect represents an immediate safety risk or seriously affects vehicle control or road safety.

“Advisory” is not the same thing as a Major MOT failure

Testers can record advisory information for developing conditions or matters that deserve monitoring. A vehicle can pass with advisory information, but repeated advisories can become test failures as wear or deterioration progresses.

Symptom-first diagnosis

What Should You Check When a Dashboard Warning Light Appears?

The quickest way to waste money is to replace the component that seems to match the dashboard icon. A warning lamp identifies a system or condition that needs attention; diagnosis establishes the actual cause.

Use the warning light together with what the car is doing. A battery warning plus heavy steering tells you more than the battery symbol alone. An EML plus severe shaking tells you more than a stored fault code alone.

1. Identify the Symbol

Confirm whether the lamp represents the engine, ABS, tyres, battery, oil, coolant, SRS, steering or another system.

2. Check the Colour

Red generally means greater urgency, amber normally requires diagnosis and green or blue often indicates normal operation.

3. Steady or Flashing?

Flashing can change the meaning significantly. A flashing engine lamp, for example, can indicate an active severe misfire.

4. Read the Dashboard Message

Modern vehicles often explain whether the issue is low fluid, reduced power, a system malfunction or an instruction to stop.

5. Check What Changed

Did the steering become heavy? Did the engine start shaking? Did braking change? Did temperature rise? Those symptoms set the urgency.

6. Decide Whether to Stop

Oil-pressure loss, overheating, severe brake faults, dangerous steering changes and strong misfires are reasons to prioritise safety over completing the journey.

7. Make Only Safe Basic Checks

Check tyre pressures, cold fluid levels and obvious loose connections where appropriate. Never open a hot cooling system or work on live high-voltage components.

8. Scan Electronic Systems

Engine, ABS, SRS, steering and many hybrid/EV faults require diagnostic trouble codes and system data before the cause can be narrowed down.

9. Record Codes Before Clearing

Save current, pending and stored codes plus freeze-frame information where available before deleting anything.

10. Test the System

Electrical supply, wiring, live data, pressure, temperature, mechanical condition or another targeted test may be required.

11. Repair the Proven Cause

Replace a sensor, battery, pump, module or mechanical component only after testing supports the repair.

12. Confirm the Warning Stays Off

Road test where appropriate, rescan and confirm that the system has returned to normal operation before assuming the repair is complete.

Never clear warning lights simply to make the dashboard look clean

Erasing a code does not repair a failing ABS sensor, misfire, charging fault, airbag circuit, DPF problem or steering defect. If the underlying fault remains, the warning can return.

Several warning lights at once can share one cause

Low charging voltage, for example, can produce battery, steering, ABS and stability warnings together. Diagnose what all the symptoms have in common before replacing several unrelated control units.

Pre-MOT warning-light check

What Should You Do About Dashboard Warning Lights Before an MOT?

Do not wait until the morning of the MOT to investigate a warning light. Some faults need diagnosis, parts and a road test before you can be confident that the warning will stay off.

  • 1 Switch the ignition on and observe the self-check. Note which warning lamps illuminate before the engine starts.
  • 2 Start the vehicle. Check which lights go out and which remain illuminated.
  • 3 Identify every remaining warning. Do not assume several lamps are one fault.
  • 4 Read dashboard messages. Record the wording before switching the car off.
  • 5 Check tyre pressures. Correct genuine low-pressure warnings before resetting TPMS.
  • 6 Check safe fluid levels. Inspect oil, coolant and brake fluid only using the correct procedure and never open a hot cooling system.
  • 7 Scan testable electronic systems. Engine, ABS, SRS and steering faults normally need fault-code diagnosis rather than guesswork.
  • 8 Repair the actual cause. Do not remove bulbs, mask warnings or clear codes only to obtain a temporary clean dashboard.
  • 9 Road test where safe. Confirm the repaired system behaves normally under the conditions that previously triggered the warning.
  • 10 Rescan before test day. Check whether current or pending faults have returned.
  • 11 Check exterior lamps too. A clean dashboard does not mean the vehicle's headlights, indicators, brake lights and rear lights all meet MOT standards.
  • 12 Leave time before the MOT. Investigate warnings several days beforehand where possible so parts and further diagnosis can be completed properly.
Never remove or cover a warning lamp to try to pass the MOT

Several systems require the warning device itself to operate correctly. For example, an ABS warning device not working is a Major defect. Hiding the lamp can therefore create another failure rather than solve the original fault.

Fast pre-MOT decision

If an ABS, SRS, EPS or applicable engine MIL remains illuminated, diagnose it before presenting the vehicle. If the warning relates to oil pressure, overheating, braking performance or unsafe steering, deal with the safety problem before worrying about the MOT itself.

❓ Frequently Asked Questions

Dashboard Warning Light FAQs

These are the most common questions UK drivers ask about dashboard warning lights, driving safety, MOT failures, fault codes and what to do before test day.

Can I pass an MOT with a dashboard warning light on?

Sometimes. There is no blanket rule that every warning light causes an MOT failure. The result depends on the system involved. An applicable engine MIL, ABS malfunction warning, SRS malfunction warning, EPS fault or applicable TPMS malfunction can cause a Major defect, while many normal green and blue status lights are not faults.

What does a red dashboard warning light mean?

Red usually means urgent attention is required. Oil-pressure, overheating and serious brake warnings should be treated particularly seriously because continued driving can cause damage or create a safety risk.

Can I drive with an amber warning light?

Sometimes, if the warning is steady and the vehicle feels completely normal. Stop instead if the warning is accompanied by heavy steering, braking problems, severe power loss, overheating, smoke, misfire or another serious symptom.

What does a flashing engine management light mean?

A flashing engine-management light commonly indicates a severe combustion or misfire fault. Continued driving can send unburned fuel into the exhaust and damage the catalytic converter, so the vehicle should be stopped and diagnosed promptly.

Will a solid engine management light fail an MOT?

Yes where the vehicle is within the DVSA engine-MIL inspection requirements. An applicable MIL that is inoperative or indicating a malfunction is classed as a Major defect.

Will an ABS warning light fail an MOT?

Yes. An ABS warning device showing a system malfunction is a Major defect. An ABS warning device that does not operate correctly is also a Major defect.

Will an airbag warning light fail an MOT?

Yes when the SRS malfunction indicator is showing a system malfunction. That is a Major defect. A lamp simply indicating that the passenger airbag has intentionally been switched off is not the same defect.

Will a tyre-pressure warning light fail an MOT?

It depends on what the lamp is indicating. On applicable M1 vehicles first used on or after 1 January 2012, a malfunctioning or obviously inoperative TPMS is a Major defect. A tyre that is simply obviously underinflated is classed as a Minor defect.

Does a battery warning light mean the battery is bad?

Not necessarily. The battery symbol normally indicates a charging problem. Possible causes include the alternator, drive belt, charging cable, voltage regulator, poor electrical connections or the battery itself.

Can I drive with the battery warning light on?

Only cautiously to reach a safe place if the vehicle remains normal. If the charging system has failed, the vehicle may stop once the battery is discharged. Stop sooner if heavy steering, overheating or multiple electrical warnings appear.

What should I do when the oil-pressure warning comes on?

Stop the engine as soon as safely possible. Once the vehicle is level and the oil has settled, check the oil level where a dipstick is fitted. Do not continue running the engine if the warning remains on after the oil level is confirmed correct.

Can I drive with the coolant-temperature warning light on?

No. Stop somewhere safe and allow the engine to cool fully. Continued overheating can damage the cylinder head, head gasket and other engine components. Never remove a hot pressurised coolant cap.

Does a DPF warning light automatically fail an MOT?

The DPF warning lamp by itself is not the complete MOT test. However, emissions-control equipment that is missing, obviously modified or obviously defective is a Major defect, and evidence of DPF tampering can also cause failure.

Does an EPS or power-steering warning light fail an MOT?

A malfunctioning power-steering system or inoperative power assistance can be a Major defect. Steering defects can become Dangerous where vehicle control is adversely affected.

Are green and blue dashboard lights faults?

Usually not. Green and blue symbols commonly confirm that a normal function such as exterior lighting, main beam, indicators or cruise control is active. The underlying equipment must still work correctly where the MOT tests it.

Should all warning lights come on when I switch the ignition on?

Many warning lamps illuminate briefly as part of a self-check and then go out when the system is confirmed healthy. The exact sequence varies by vehicle. Some testable systems can fail the MOT if their required warning device is inoperative.

Can I clear a warning light before an MOT?

Clear diagnostic codes only after the underlying fault has been diagnosed and repaired. Erasing a code does not fix a misfire, ABS sensor, airbag circuit, charging fault, DPF problem or steering malfunction, and the warning can return.

How much does it cost to fix a dashboard warning light?

The cost depends entirely on the cause. Diagnosis may start around £40 to £100, while relatively simple sensor or wiring repairs may cost a few hundred pounds. ABS modules, DPFs, steering racks, catalysts and complex hybrid or EV faults can cost £1,000 or more.
