Quick answer

Will a Tail Light Fail an MOT?

Yes. A required tail light can fail an MOT if it is missing, inoperative, insecure, has a defective lens or does not produce the required light correctly. Rear position lamps must emit a steady red light, and current MOT guidance requires testers to check the presence, condition, security and function of required lamps.

The important point is that “the bulb comes on” is not the only test. Damaged or repaired lamps are also assessed for colour, light output and durability. A lamp that is extremely dim, flickers, changes behaviour when another rear lamp operates or has serious lens damage can therefore need attention even though some light is still visible.

Quick pre-MOT check

Switch on the sidelights or headlights, stand behind the vehicle and compare both rear position lamps. UK MOT rules

What Are the MOT Rules for Tail Lights?

Rear position lamps are part of the vehicle lighting inspection. The tester checks whether the required lamps are present, secure, in acceptable condition and operating correctly when the vehicle lighting is switched on.

For the normal rear position-light function, the light shown to the rear should be steady red. The tester is not simply checking whether something inside the lamp glows. Lens condition, emitted colour, security and the effectiveness of the light can all matter.

This is particularly important with damaged or repaired lamps. A bulb or LED may still illuminate through a cracked lens, temporary repair or damaged housing, but the complete lamp still needs to perform its required function correctly.

Tester inspection

What Does an MOT Tester Check on Tail Lights?

The tester works through the required rear-light function rather than trying to diagnose every electrical fault. If the lamp does not perform correctly, the cause could later prove to be a bulb, poor earth, wiring fault, damaged lens, water ingress or internal LED failure.

1. Is the Lamp Present?

Required rear position lamps should be fitted where the vehicle is required to have them.

2. Does It Illuminate?

The tester switches on the relevant lighting and confirms that the required rear position lamp operates.

3. Is the Colour Correct?

The normal rear position-light function should show steady red light to the rear.

4. Is the Lens Acceptable?

Cracks, missing material or serious deterioration can affect the condition and performance of the lamp.

Cracked Lens MOT Guide →

5. Is the Lamp Secure?

The assembly should not be loose because of broken brackets, damaged mountings or poor previous repairs.

6. Is the Output Effective?

A lamp that is obviously weak, heavily obscured or partly failed deserves investigation even where it is not completely dark.

Dim Tail Light MOT Guide →

7. Does It Flicker?

Intermittent operation can reveal a bulb-holder, connector, earth or wiring problem.

8. Has It Been Repaired?

Tape, adhesive or another repair does not automatically make a lamp acceptable. The completed repair still needs to leave the lamp effective and secure.

9. Are Other Functions Affected?

A combined rear cluster may also contain stop lamps, indicators, reversing lamps and fog lamps. Electrical or physical damage can affect more than one function.

Check the whole rear cluster before the MOT

Tail lights, brake lights, indicators, reversing lamps and rear fog lamps are separate functions even when they share one housing. Do not stop the inspection after confirming the tail light itself comes on.

Weak and unstable output

Why Is a Tail Light Dim or Flickering?

A dim or flickering tail light usually points to either a weak light source, excessive electrical resistance or an unstable connection. The lamp may still illuminate, but that does not mean the circuit is healthy.

Start by comparing the affected side with the matching lamp on the opposite side. Then watch what happens when the brake lights, indicators and other rear-light functions are operated.

One Side Is Dimmer

A clear side-to-side difference often points towards a local bulb, holder, connector, earth or LED fault rather than a vehicle-wide charging problem.

Electrical diagnosis

Common Electrical Causes of Tail Light Problems

Tail-light faults are often caused by simple electrical problems rather than the lamp housing itself. A methodical diagnosis should check the light source, power supply, earth path, connector condition and wiring before expensive parts are replaced.

Blown Bulb

On conventional lamps, a failed filament remains one of the simplest and most common causes of a dead tail light.

Incorrect Bulb

The wrong wattage, base or filament arrangement can create poor output or unusual interaction between tail and brake functions.

Corroded Bulb Holder

Oxidation increases electrical resistance and can cause dim, intermittent or completely failed illumination.

Poor Earth Connection

A weak earth is a classic cause of dim lamps and strange interaction between rear-light functions.

Common Rear-Light Fault

Damaged Connector

Loose, corroded or overheated terminals can create voltage drop even when the connector still appears to be attached.

Broken Wiring

Tailgate looms, boot-lid wiring, previous repairs and towbar wiring are all common places for damage to develop.

Fuse or Supply Fault

Some lighting circuits use shared fuses or electronic control rather than a simple one-fuse-per-lamp arrangement.

Body Control Module Issue

On modern vehicles, exterior lighting may be controlled or monitored by a body control module, so diagnosis can extend beyond bulbs and wiring.

Water-Related Corrosion

Moisture can damage contacts and circuits even after the lamp itself has dried.

Water Ingress Diagnosis →
Test the circuit under load

A connector can show voltage when disconnected and still fail once the lamp draws current. Voltage-drop testing across the power and earth paths while the lamp is operating is often more useful than checking for voltage with no load.

Modern LED rear lamps

Can LED Tail Lights Fail an MOT?

Yes. LED tail lights still have to perform the required lighting function correctly. The difference is that the light source is often built into the complete lamp rather than being a simple replaceable bulb.

That makes diagnosis more important because a partially failed LED lamp can look different from a conventional blown bulb. Several LEDs may stop working while the remaining section continues to glow.

Partial LED Failure

Some sections can fail while the rest of the lamp remains lit, producing noticeably reduced output.

Internal Driver Fault

LED assemblies may contain internal control electronics that regulate or drive the individual light sources.

Water-Damaged Electronics

Moisture inside a sealed LED assembly can cause expensive internal damage.

Entire Assembly May Be Required

Many manufacturers do not supply individual internal LEDs, so complete lamp replacement may be necessary.

Higher Repair Cost

Vehicle-Side Fault Can Mimic LED Failure

Poor power supply, earth or connector condition can make an LED unit appear faulty even when the assembly itself is healthy.

Warning Message May Appear

Some vehicles monitor lamp current and can display exterior-light warnings when part of an LED assembly or circuit fails.

Do not condemn an expensive LED lamp without testing its supply

Confirm power, earth and connector integrity before replacing the complete unit. A vehicle-side circuit fault can imitate internal LED failure.

Mechanic diagnostic process

How to Diagnose a Tail Light Fault Step by Step

The most efficient diagnosis starts with the visible symptom and moves towards electrical testing only where necessary. This avoids replacing bulbs, lamps or wiring at random.

1. Identify the Failed Function

Confirm whether the problem affects the tail light itself or a brake, indicator, reverse or fog function in the same cluster.

2. Compare Left and Right

Check brightness, colour and stability against the matching lamp on the opposite side.

3. Inspect the Lens and Housing

Look for cracks, missing cover material, water ingress, loose mountings and impact damage.

4. Check the Bulb or LED Pattern

Inspect conventional bulbs for damage and LED lamps for missing or weak sections.

5. Inspect the Holder and Connector

Look for corrosion, heat damage, loose pins or moisture around the electrical connection.

6. Check the Power Supply

Confirm the lamp receives the correct supply when the circuit is switched on and loaded.

7. Check the Earth Path

Use voltage-drop testing if the lamp is weak or other rear lights interact with it.

8. Operate Other Rear Lights

Apply the brakes and indicators and watch for cross-feeding, pulsing or brightness changes.

9. Confirm the Repair

Recheck all rear-light functions after repair rather than stopping as soon as the original symptom disappears.

Diagnostic order matters

Function → visual condition → bulb or LED source → connector → power → earth → internal lamp fault is usually a more efficient route than replacing components until the problem disappears.

Pre-MOT checklist

How to Check Your Tail Lights Before an MOT

A complete rear-light check only takes a few minutes and can prevent a simple lighting defect from becoming an avoidable MOT failure.

1. Switch on the Position Lamps

Turn on the vehicle lighting and check that both rear position lamps illuminate.

2. Compare Brightness

Stand several metres behind the car and compare both sides for obvious differences.

3. Confirm the Colour

Make sure the rear position-light output is steady red.

4. Inspect the Lenses

Look for cracks, missing material, badly faded plastic and previous repairs.

5. Check for Water

Look for persistent droplets, standing water or corrosion inside the lamp.

6. Check Lamp Security

Confirm the housing is not loose because of damaged brackets or mounting points.

7. Operate Brake Lights

Ask someone to press the brake pedal while you check both sides and watch for abnormal interaction.

8. Operate Indicators

Check the indicators and make sure the tail-light circuit does not pulse or dim with them.

9. Check Reverse and Fog Lamps

Inspect the other functions contained in the same rear lamp assembly where applicable.

10. Recheck Warning Messages

Investigate any exterior-light warning instead of assuming it is unrelated because the lamp appears to work.

Do not wait for the lamp to fail completely

A cracked lens, obvious dimness, flickering, standing water, loose housing or failed LED section deserves diagnosis before the test.

Rear light functions

Tail Lights vs Brake Lights: What Is the Difference?

Drivers often use “rear light” to describe several different functions, but tail lights and brake lights are not the same thing. Understanding the difference helps when checking the vehicle before an MOT.

Tail Lights

Tail lights are rear position lamps. They normally illuminate when the sidelights or headlights are switched on and remain steadily lit to make the vehicle visible from behind.

  • ✓ Operate with vehicle lighting
  • ✓ Show red light to the rear
  • ✓ Stay steadily illuminated

Brake Lights

Brake lights, or stop lamps, illuminate when the brake pedal is pressed and are normally brighter than the tail-light function.

  • ✓ Operate with brake-pedal input
  • ✓ Warn following traffic
  • ✓ Separate MOT lighting function
Brake Light MOT Guide →
One bulb can sometimes perform both functions

Some conventional rear lamps use a dual-filament bulb for both tail and stop functions. One filament can fail while the other still works, so do not assume the complete bulb is healthy because one function remains illuminated.

Check every function in the cluster

Tail lights, brake lights, indicators, reversing lamps and rear fog lamps are separate functions even when they share the same physical housing.

Frequently Asked Questions

Tail Light MOT FAQs

These are the most common questions UK drivers ask about tail lights, cracked lenses, broken covers, water ingress, dim output, LEDs, electrical faults and MOT inspections.

Can tail lights fail an MOT?

Yes. A required tail light can fail an MOT if it is missing, inoperative, insecure, has a defective lens, shows the wrong colour or does not produce the required light correctly.

Will one tail light out fail an MOT?

Yes. If a required rear position lamp does not operate correctly, it can result in an MOT failure.

What colour should tail lights be for an MOT?

Rear position lamps should emit steady red light to the rear. Missing coloured lens material or an unsuitable repair can allow the wrong colour to show.

Can a dim tail light fail an MOT?

Yes, if the required lamp's visible output is significantly reduced or it is otherwise not performing correctly. Poor earths, corrosion, voltage drop and partial LED failure are common causes.

Can a tail light fail even if it still comes on?

Yes. A lamp can illuminate but still have a problem with light output, colour, security, lens condition or intermittent operation.

Can a cracked tail light fail an MOT?

Yes. A defective rear-light lens can become an MOT issue, particularly where the damage affects the lamp's condition, colour, output, security or ability to perform its required function.

Will a broken tail light cover fail an MOT?

It can. Missing or badly damaged cover material can affect lamp condition, emitted colour, security and weather protection.

Will water in a tail light fail an MOT?

Water or condensation does not automatically create the same MOT result in every case. It becomes more serious when it causes a defective lens, corrosion, reduced output, electrical failure or another relevant lamp defect.

Is condensation inside a tail light an MOT failure?

Light temporary misting is different from significant water ingress. Persistent droplets or standing water should be investigated because they can damage the lamp and electrical connections.

Can a poor earth cause a tail light MOT failure?

Yes. A poor earth can make the lamp dim, intermittent or cause other rear-light functions to behave abnormally. If the required lamp no longer operates correctly, the MOT result can be affected.

Why does my tail light dim when I press the brake?

This commonly points to a shared-earth or high-resistance connection. The brake-light circuit can interfere with the tail-light circuit when the return path is poor.

Why does my indicator make another rear light flash?

A poor shared earth, corroded connector or damaged wiring can allow current to cross-feed through another rear-light circuit.

Can LED tail lights fail an MOT?

Yes. LED tail lights still have to perform their required function correctly. Partial LED failure can leave the lamp illuminated but noticeably weaker than intended.

Does a partially working LED tail light fail an MOT?

It can. The important issue is whether enough of the required lamp has failed for its output or operation to become inadequate.

Can tape over a cracked tail light pass an MOT?

Tape does not automatically make a damaged lamp acceptable. The repair still needs to leave the lamp secure, durable, correctly coloured and capable of providing effective light output.

How much does a tail light repair cost in the UK?

A conventional replacement bulb can cost only a few pounds, while wiring repairs or a complete integrated LED lamp can cost several hundred pounds. Diagnosis should identify the cause before expensive parts are replaced.

Should I replace the whole tail light if it is faulty?

Not automatically. Bulbs, holders, connectors, earth faults and wiring can often be repaired separately. Complete replacement is more likely where the housing, lens or integrated LED electronics are seriously damaged.

Should I buy a used car with a tail light fault?

It depends on the cause. A failed bulb can be inexpensive, while standing water, corrosion, partial LED failure, broken mountings or accident damage can indicate a much larger repair.
