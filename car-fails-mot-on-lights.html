Car Fails MOT On Lights UK

If your car fails its MOT on lights, the fault may be as simple as a blown bulb, but it can also involve poor headlight aim, cracked lenses, wrong colour output, water ingress, loose lamp units, LED module faults, fuse issues, bad earth points or wiring problems.

This upgraded guide explains the most common lighting MOT failures, what to fix first, when it is more than just a bulb, likely repair costs and how to prepare for the retest.

Quick answer

Why did my car fail its MOT on lights?

A car can fail its MOT if required lights do not work, are insecure, show the wrong colour, are badly damaged, dazzle other road users, have poor beam aim, or do not illuminate the correct area.

Many lighting failures are easy to fix, especially blown bulbs, number plate lights, brake lights and indicators. But modern LED, xenon or adaptive light faults can be more expensive if the problem is inside the lamp unit, ballast, control module or wiring.

The first step is to read the MOT failure sheet carefully. It should tell you which light failed and whether the issue is operation, aim, lens condition, colour, security or wiring.

Best answer

Is a lighting MOT failure usually expensive?

Often no. A simple bulb, fuse, number plate lamp or headlight adjustment can be a low-cost repair. The cost rises when the lamp unit is damaged, filled with water, has wiring faults, or uses LED, xenon or adaptive lighting.

If a new bulb does not solve the issue, do not keep replacing bulbs blindly. Check the bulb holder, fuse, earth point, wiring, moisture inside the lamp and whether the control module is working.

Lighting failures are among the easiest MOT failures to prevent with a five-minute walkaround before test day.

Common failures

Common Reasons a Car Fails MOT On Lights

Lighting failures are common because many drivers do not check every exterior lamp before the MOT.

1

Blown bulbs

A failed headlight, brake light, indicator, sidelight, tail light or number plate light can fail. Always replace bulbs with the correct type and colour.

2

Headlight beam aim

Headlights can fail if the beam is too high, too low, uneven, dazzling or has an incorrect pattern after bulb replacement, accident repair or adjustment.

3

Cracked or damaged lenses

A cracked lens may fail if it affects light output, colour, security, water resistance or visibility.

4

Wrong colour or poor output

Incorrect bulbs, faded lenses, unsuitable LED conversions and tinted lamps can cause wrong colour or poor brightness.

5

Water ingress

Moisture inside a lamp can corrode contacts, reduce brightness, damage modules and affect beam pattern.

6

Electrical faults

Fuses, relays, wiring, earth points, switches, holders or control modules can stop lamps working even after a bulb is replaced.

What is checked?

Lights Commonly Involved in MOT Failures

The MOT lighting section covers many lamps, reflectors and electrical items, including headlamps, stop lamps, indicators, fog lamps, registration plate lamps, wiring and battery checks.

  • Headlights: dipped beam, main beam, beam aim and beam pattern.
  • Brake lights: rear stop lamps and high-level brake lamp where applicable.
  • Indicators and hazards: operation, flash rate, colour and visibility.
  • Rear position lamps: red rear lights and lens condition.
  • Fog lights: required fog lamp operation, colour and switching.
  • Reverse lights: where applicable, correct operation and white output.
  • Number plate lights: rear registration plate must be illuminated clearly.
  • Sidelights and position lamps: correct operation and visibility.
  • Electrical wiring: visible wiring condition, security and safety.

Electrical diagnosis

When it is not just a bulb

If a new bulb does not fix the problem, the fault may be electrical. This is common when lights work intermittently, flicker, fail repeatedly or several lamps stop working at the same time.

  • Blown fuse or relay fault.
  • Bad earth connection causing dim or strange lamp behaviour.
  • Water inside the lamp holder corroding contacts.
  • Broken wiring in tailgate, bumper or lamp harness.
  • Faulty light switch, stalk or control module.
  • LED driver, xenon ballast or adaptive lamp failure.
Mechanic tip

Clues it is a wiring fault

A wiring or earth fault is more likely if more than one lamp behaves strangely, bulbs fail repeatedly, lights dim when another lamp is switched on, or the fault changes when the boot, tailgate or bumper area is moved.

Do not keep fitting bulbs if they keep blowing. Find the cause before the retest.

Repair cost guide

Likely UK Lighting Repair Costs

Lighting repair cost depends on whether the issue is a simple bulb, awkward access, damaged lamp unit, wiring fault or modern LED/xenon module fault.

Lower cost

Bulb or number plate lamp

Often low cost, although access can be awkward on some vehicles and may require trim removal.

Medium cost

Headlight aim or cracked lens

Headlight alignment is usually modest if adjusters work. Lens or lamp-unit damage can cost more.

Higher cost

LED, xenon or wiring fault

Modern lamp units, ballasts, modules, adaptive lights and wiring diagnosis can become expensive.

Questions to ask the garage

  • Is the failure only a bulb or is there a wiring fault?
  • Is the lamp unit full of water?
  • Does the bulb holder have corrosion?
  • Is the headlight aim adjustable?
  • Does the fault need a lamp unit, ballast or module?
  • Will the light be checked again before retest?
  • Is the replacement bulb the correct type and colour?
Retest steps

What happens after a lighting MOT failure?

Read the MOT failure sheet carefully and identify exactly which lamp or function failed. A failed bulb can often be repaired quickly, but beam alignment, wiring, cracked lenses, water ingress or LED unit faults may need proper diagnosis.

  • Confirm which lamp or function failed.
  • Replace bulbs with the correct type and colour.
  • Check lens, holder, fuse, wiring and earth if the fault remains.
  • Correct headlight aim if the failure mentions beam pattern.
  • Test every exterior light again before the retest.
  • Arrange the MOT retest once the lighting defect is fixed.
Before retest

Quick lighting check

  • Check sidelights, dipped beam and main beam.
  • Check indicators and hazards.
  • Check brake lights with a helper.
  • Check reverse light and rear fog light.
  • Check number plate lamps.
  • Inspect lenses for cracks, water and missing pieces.
  • Compare left and right brightness.
  • Confirm dashboard warning messages are cleared.

Used car buying

Lighting Checks When Buying a Used Car

Lighting faults can reveal more than a failed bulb. Moisture, warning messages, mismatched lamps or poor aim can suggest accident repair, cheap parts or electrical problems.

Acceptable

A simple blown bulb or number plate lamp may be acceptable if the price is fair and no wiring fault is present.

Negotiate

Cracked lenses, water ingress, failed LED units or headlight aim problems should be priced before buying.

Be cautious

Mismatched headlights, repeated lighting failures or adaptive light warnings can point to bigger repair costs.

Used car lighting checklist

  • Test every exterior light during the viewing.
  • Check for moisture inside headlights and rear lamps.
  • Look for mismatched lamp units after accident repairs.
  • Check number plate lights and brake lights with a helper.
  • Check MOT history for repeated lighting failures.
  • Be careful with warning messages for LED or adaptive headlights.

Prevention

How To Avoid Lighting MOT Failures Next Time

Most simple lighting failures can be caught before MOT day.

Do a monthly walkaround

Switch the lights on, walk around the car and check both front and rear lamps.

Use a helper

Ask someone to stand behind the car while you test brake lights and reverse lights.

Fix repeat faults

Repeated bulb failures usually mean moisture, holder corrosion, wiring or voltage problems.

Check headlight aim

Check aim after bulb changes, front-end repairs, suspension work or lamp replacement.

Avoid poor conversions

Unsuitable LED conversions can scatter light, dazzle or create incorrect beam patterns.

Repair water ingress early

Water inside lamps can damage contacts, modules and bulb holders if ignored.

Frequently asked questions

FAQs About Cars Failing MOT On Lights

Straight answers to common UK driver questions about lighting MOT failures.

Can one blown bulb fail MOT?

Yes, if it is a required lamp or affects visibility, signalling or safety.

Can headlight aim fail MOT?

Yes. Incorrect headlight aim or poor beam pattern is a common lighting failure.

Can a cracked light lens fail?

Yes, especially if it affects colour, light output, security or allows water inside.

Are LED lights expensive to fix?

Sometimes. Many modern LED faults require a complete lamp unit rather than a simple bulb.

Can I retest after replacing a bulb?

Yes, once the listed lighting defect is repaired, book or return for the MOT retest.

Can number plate lights fail MOT?

Yes. If the rear number plate is not clearly illuminated, the car can fail.

Can wrong colour bulbs fail MOT?

Yes. Lamps must show the correct colour for their position and function.

What is the best pre-MOT lighting check?

Test every exterior light, inspect lenses and confirm both headlights look even.
