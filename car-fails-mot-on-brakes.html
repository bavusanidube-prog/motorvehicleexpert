Car Fails MOT On Brakes UK

If your car fails its MOT on brakes, the problem may involve worn brake pads, corroded brake discs, brake pipe corrosion, brake fluid leaks, ABS faults, handbrake inefficiency, seized calipers or dangerous brake imbalance.

This guide explains the most common brake MOT failures, safety risks, repair costs, retest requirements and whether the vehicle should still be driven.

Quick answer

Why did my car fail MOT on brakes?

A car can fail its MOT on brakes because the braking system is worn, leaking, imbalanced, insecure, contaminated or not performing strongly enough. Common causes include low brake pads, damaged discs, corroded brake pipes, brake fluid leaks, weak parking brake performance, seized calipers and ABS faults.

Brake failures should be treated seriously. If the defect is marked dangerous, do not drive the car until repaired. If it is marked major, the car still needs repair before it can pass the MOT.

The best next step is to read the MOT failure sheet carefully and ask the garage exactly which brake component failed, whether the defect is major or dangerous, and whether related advisories are likely to become future failures.

Best answer

Is a brake MOT failure serious?

Yes. Brakes are safety-critical because they affect stopping distance, vehicle control, stability and parking safety. A brake fault that feels minor during normal driving can still fail on the MOT brake rollers if braking force is weak or uneven.

Some brake failures are straightforward repairs, such as worn pads or a weak handbrake adjustment. Others can be more serious, such as brake fluid leaks, severe pipe corrosion, seized calipers or ABS system faults.

Do not only ask “how much to pass the MOT?” Ask whether the repair restores proper braking performance and whether the opposite side should also be checked.

Repair cost guide

Typical Brake Repair Costs UK

Brake repair cost depends on vehicle type, parts quality, labour, corrosion, seized fittings and whether both sides on the axle need work.

Lower cost

Brake pads or adjustment

Brake pads, handbrake adjustment or simple sensor faults are often lower-cost compared with hydraulic or ABS module faults.

Medium cost

Discs and pads or caliper

Pads and discs together, seized calipers or parking brake cable repairs are common mid-range repairs.

Higher cost

Brake pipes or ABS module

Brake pipe routing, seized unions, subframe access or ABS modules can push costs higher.

Questions to ask the garage

  • Which exact brake component failed?
  • Is the defect major or dangerous?
  • Is the opposite side also worn or seized?

  • Is there a brake fluid leak or only low fluid level?

Driving advice

Can you still drive after failing MOT on brakes?

If the MOT failure includes a dangerous brake defect, do not drive the vehicle until it has been repaired. Dangerous brake defects can affect stopping distance, control and safety.

If the defect is major rather than dangerous, whether you can drive depends on the vehicle’s MOT status, insurance, tax where required and roadworthiness. The car must still be safe and legal to use on the road.

The safest option after any brake MOT failure is to repair the defect promptly and arrange the retest.

Stop immediately

Do not drive if...

  • The MOT sheet says the brake defect is dangerous.
  • Brake fluid is leaking.
  • The brake pedal sinks, feels soft or loses pressure.
  • The car pulls hard to one side when braking.
  • The brakes grind heavily or feel weak.
  • A wheel is overheating from a binding brake.
  • Brake warning lights suggest a serious system fault.
  • The garage says the car is unsafe to drive.
Pads and discs

Brake pad and disc MOT failures

Brake pads and discs are among the most common brake wear items. A car can still feel acceptable in everyday driving but fail if pads are too low, discs are badly worn, discs are corroded, or braking effort is uneven.

  • Brake pads worn too thin or close to backing plate.
  • Brake discs heavily scored, cracked, worn or badly corroded.
  • Brake components insecure, missing or badly deteriorated.
  • Brake material contaminated with oil, grease or fluid.
  • Braking effort low on one wheel or one axle.

Mechanic tip

Should pads and discs be done together?

Often, yes. If brake discs are worn, scored, corroded or below safe condition, fitting pads only may not solve the problem properly.

New pads need a good disc surface to bed in correctly. If the disc is damaged, braking noise, vibration and poor pad contact can continue.

Hydraulic system

Brake fluid leaks, hoses and brake pipe corrosion

Brake fluid leaks and badly corroded pipes are serious because the braking system relies on hydraulic pressure. If a pipe, hose, caliper or cylinder leaks, the pedal may become soft and braking force may drop.

Severe brake pipe corrosion can fail even before a visible leak appears because the pipe may be weakened.

  • Brake fluid leak near a wheel or under the car.
  • Corroded brake pipes under the vehicle.
  • Cracked, bulging or twisted brake hoses.
  • Low brake fluid warning light.
  • Soft or sinking brake pedal.
Related advisory

Brake pipe corrosion often starts as an advisory

Brake pipe corrosion is one of the most important advisories to act on early. If ignored, it can become a major or dangerous brake failure.

If your current MOT failed on brakes and previous MOTs mention brake pipe corrosion, get the pipes inspected properly before approving only the minimum repair.

Parking brake

Handbrake and parking brake MOT failures

The parking brake must hold the vehicle properly. A car can fail if the handbrake has poor efficiency, excessive travel, seized cables, uneven rear braking or faults with an electronic parking brake system.

  • Handbrake lever comes up too high.
  • Car rolls or does not hold properly on a slope.
  • One rear brake does not apply properly.
  • Parking brake cable is seized, stretched or damaged.
  • Electronic parking brake warning appears.
Repair clues

Why handbrakes fail

Handbrake failure may be caused by stretched cables, seized linkages, worn rear brake shoes, rear caliper issues, incorrect adjustment or electronic parking brake faults.

On some vehicles, the parking brake is linked to rear caliper condition, so a weak handbrake may also reveal a wider rear brake issue.

Retest

What happens next after a brake MOT failure?

  • Read the MOT failure sheet carefully.
  • Check whether defects are major or dangerous.
  • Ask which brake parts caused the failure and why.
  • Repair safety defects before using the car normally.
  • Keep receipts for parts and labour.
  • Book the retest within the relevant retest window.
  • Do not ignore related brake advisories.

Avoid repeat failure

How to avoid brake MOT failures next time

  • Listen for grinding, squealing or scraping.
  • Watch ABS, brake and parking brake warning lights.
  • Check brake feel: spongy pedal, pulling or vibration.
  • Use the handbrake regularly to reduce sticking.
  • Read brake advisories early.
  • Book a brake inspection before MOT if warning signs appear.

Used car buying

Should You Buy a Car That Failed MOT On Brakes?

Brake failures and advisories in MOT history are useful clues when buying a used car. Repeated brake failures can show poor maintenance or upcoming repair costs.

Acceptable

Recent brake repair proved

A previous brake failure may be acceptable if invoices prove the failed parts were repaired properly.

Negotiate

Outstanding brake advisories

Brake disc wear, pad advisories, brake pipe corrosion or weak handbrake issues should reduce the purchase price.

Walk away?

Repeated or dangerous faults

Multiple years of brake failures, dangerous defects, severe corrosion or no repair proof should make you cautious.

Used car brake checklist

  • Check MOT history for brake advisories and repeated failures.
  • Look for brake warning lights during the test drive.
  • Listen for grinding, squealing or scraping.
  • Check whether the car pulls to one side under braking.
  • Ask for invoices if recent brake repairs are claimed.
  • Budget for pads, discs or brake pipe work if advisories are present.

Final decision

Repair, Diagnose or Stop Driving?

Use this final decision guide if you are unsure what to do after a brake MOT failure.

Stop driving

Stop driving if...

  • The defect is dangerous.
  • Brake fluid is leaking.
  • The pedal sinks or feels unsafe.
  • Brakes grind heavily.
  • The car pulls hard under braking.
  • The garage says it is unsafe.
Repair now

Repair now if...

  • Pads or discs failed.
  • ABS or brake warning light is on.
  • The handbrake failed.
  • A caliper is seized.
  • Brake pipes are corroded.
  • The retest is due soon.
Diagnose further

Diagnose further if...

  • One side wears faster.
  • ABS faults return after clearing.
  • Brake imbalance is unexplained.
  • Several warning lights appear.
  • Brakes overheat or smell.
  • New parts did not fix the fault.

Frequently asked questions

FAQs About Cars Failing MOT On Brakes

Straight answers to common UK driver questions about brake MOT failures.

Is a brake MOT failure serious?

Yes. Brake defects affect stopping, stability and safety, so they should be repaired promptly. If the MOT sheet says dangerous, do not drive the car until repaired.

Can worn brake pads fail MOT?

Yes. Brake pads can fail if they are below safe limits, contaminated, missing, insecure or seriously deteriorated.

Can brake discs fail MOT?

Yes. Heavily worn, cracked, damaged, insecure or badly corroded discs can fail.

Can I drive to another garage?

Not if the vehicle has dangerous brake defects. For major defects, only drive if the car is legal, insured and roadworthy.

Can ABS light fail MOT?

Yes. ABS warning faults can cause MOT failure if the system indicates a defect.

Can brake fluid fail MOT?

Yes. Leaks, low fluid, contamination, warning lights or braking system defects can lead to failure.

Can a weak handbrake fail MOT?

Yes. The parking brake must meet required efficiency and operate properly.

What is the best next step?

Repair the listed brake defects, keep invoices, check related advisories and arrange the MOT retest promptly.
