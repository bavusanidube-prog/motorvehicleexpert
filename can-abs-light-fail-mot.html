Quick Answer

Can an ABS Warning Light Fail an MOT?

Yes. If the ABS warning device shows a system malfunction, the defect is classified as Major. An ABS warning device that does not work at all is also a Major defect.

The inspection goes further than the dashboard lamp. Missing or damaged wheel-speed sensors, damaged ABS wiring, other missing or damaged ABS components and an ABS system that has obviously been removed are also specifically listed as Major defects.

ABS MOT failure is not limited to “the light stays on”

MOT Inspection

What Does the MOT Tester Check on the ABS System?

The current ABS inspection contains six specific Major-defect routes. This makes the page much more precise than treating every ABS issue as simply “warning light on”.

Check 1

ABS Warning Device

Not working = Major

The warning device must function correctly rather than being disabled, removed or incapable of showing the system state.

Check 2

ABS Malfunction Warning

System malfunction = Major

Where the warning device shows that the ABS system has detected a malfunction, the defect is Major.

Check 3

Wheel-Speed Sensors

Missing or damaged = Major

The wheel-speed sensors are specifically included in the inspection because ABS depends on accurate individual wheel-speed information.

Check 4

ABS Wiring

Damaged = Major

Visible wiring damage affecting the ABS system is a specific Major defect.

Check 5

Other ABS Components

Missing or damaged = Major

The ABS inspection is not restricted to the warning lamp or wheel-speed sensors. Other relevant components can also result in failure when missing or damaged.

Check 6

ABS System Removed

Obviously removed = Major

An ABS system that has obviously been removed is a Major defect under the inspection rules.

Removal rules matter on newer vehicles

ABS must not be removed or disabled from a vehicle first used on or after 1 January 2010 where ABS was fitted. Not every vehicle first used after that date necessarily had ABS, so the issue is whether a fitted system has obviously been removed.

ABS Light Comes On Then Goes Out — Is That an MOT Failure?

No, not by itself. On many vehicles the ABS warning device illuminates briefly when the ignition is switched on or the engine is started, then goes out once the system completes its normal self-check.

For MOT purposes, the important distinction is between normal warning-device operation and a warning that remains illuminated because the ABS system has detected a malfunction.

Ignition On

Warning Appears Briefly

Initial illumination can show that the warning device itself is capable of operating during the system check.

Self-Check Complete

Warning Goes Out

If the warning extinguishes normally and does not subsequently indicate a malfunction, that behaviour is different from a persistent ABS fault.

Returns After Moving

Dynamic Fault Possible

Some ABS faults only become detectable once the vehicle moves and the control module starts comparing individual wheel-speed signals.

Normal startup illumination is not the same as an ABS malfunction

The MOT concern begins when the warning device fails to operate correctly or indicates that the ABS system itself has a malfunction.

Persistent ABS Warning

ABS Light Stays On — Will It Fail the MOT?

Yes, if the warning device is showing an ABS system malfunction. That condition is classified as a Major defect.

A warning that remains illuminated after the normal startup sequence should be diagnosed before the vehicle is presented for test rather than treated as a cosmetic dashboard issue.

Persistent Warning

ABS Malfunction Indicated

Major

If the warning remains on because the ABS control system has detected a malfunction, the MOT defect is Major.

Warning After Moving

Wheel-Speed Fault Possible

If the light initially goes out but returns after the car begins moving, investigate the wheel-speed inputs and related ABS circuits.

Intermittent Warning

Do Not Ignore It Before Test

A warning that appears only occasionally can still indicate an intermittent sensor, wiring, connector or signal-source fault.

Do not wait for the MOT tester to diagnose the fault

If the ABS warning remains illuminated during normal operation, diagnose the underlying fault before test day so the repair can be confirmed rather than discovered through an avoidable MOT failure.

Warning Device Failure

ABS Warning Light Does Not Come On — Can That Fail the MOT?

Yes. An ABS warning device that is not working is itself a Major MOT defect.

This is one of the most important distinctions on this page. A driver cannot avoid an ABS warning-light failure simply by disabling, removing, covering or otherwise preventing the warning device from operating.

No Startup Light

Warning Device May Not Be Working

If the vehicle is designed to illuminate the ABS warning during startup but the device does not operate, the warning circuit itself may require investigation.

Bulb / Display Disabled

Not a Repair

Preventing the warning from appearing does not restore ABS operation and can create its own MOT failure route because the warning device must work.

Cluster / Circuit Fault

Diagnose the Warning Path

The fault may involve the instrument display, wiring, communication or another part of the warning-device circuit rather than the ABS hydraulic hardware itself.

A warning lamp that never illuminates can be just as important as one that stays on

The MOT requires the ABS warning device to operate correctly. Hiding the malfunction by stopping the lamp from working does not make the vehicle compliant.

Confirm the complete warning sequence

Before test day, check whether the ABS warning appears when expected, extinguishes after the system self-check and remains off unless a genuine malfunction is detected.

Dynamic ABS Fault

ABS Light Comes On While Driving — What Does That Mean for the MOT?

An ABS warning that appears only after the vehicle starts moving can indicate a fault that the control module cannot identify until wheel-speed information becomes available.

For MOT preparation, this is important because the warning may appear normal during startup but still represent a genuine ABS malfunction once the system begins monitoring the wheels dynamically.

One Wheel Signal

Speed Reading Drops Out

A sensor, wiring, encoder or bearing-related fault can produce an implausible wheel-speed signal only after the wheel starts rotating.

Particular Speed

Fault Appears Repeatedly

A warning that returns at a similar speed can provide useful evidence when comparing live wheel-speed data during diagnosis.

Steering / Suspension Movement

Intermittent Wiring Fault

Wheel-arch wiring can lose continuity only when steering or suspension movement flexes a damaged section of the harness.

After Bearing Work

Encoder or Installation Issue

If the warning began after wheel-bearing replacement, inspect the affected corner before assuming an unrelated ABS component has failed.

After Brake Work

Sensor Area May Have Been Disturbed

Work around hubs, discs and wheel bearings can affect nearby ABS wiring, sensor mounting or signal targets.

MOT Preparation

Confirm the Repair While Moving

A repair should be verified under the conditions that previously caused the warning, not only with the vehicle stationary in the workshop.

A clean startup does not always prove the ABS system is fault-free

Some faults need wheel rotation before they can be detected. That is why post-repair verification should include appropriate live-data checks and vehicle movement where safe.

ABS + Stability Warning

ABS and ESC Lights On Together — How Does That Affect the MOT?

ABS and electronic stability control are separate systems for MOT purposes, but they can share wheel-speed sensors, wiring and braking-system data. That means one underlying fault can create two warning systems.

Shared Sensor

One Fault, Two Warnings

A missing or incorrect wheel-speed signal can affect ABS and ESC simultaneously because both systems may depend on the same information.

Separate MOT Sections

ABS and ESC Are Still Assessed Separately

Fixing one shared cause may restore both systems, but each remaining warning or defect still has to satisfy its own MOT inspection criteria.

Diagnostic Order

Look for the Shared Root Cause First

When both warnings appear together, compare wheel-speed data and inspect common wiring and related components before assuming that two control modules have failed independently.

Shared hardware does not mean identical MOT intent

ABS MOT Failure Route

Can a Missing or Damaged ABS Wheel-Speed Sensor Fail the MOT?

Yes. A wheel-speed sensor that is missing or damaged is specifically classified as a Major MOT defect within the ABS inspection.

However, a stored wheel-speed fault does not automatically prove that the sensor itself is defective. The sensor is only one part of the complete wheel-speed signal path.

Explicit MOT Defect

Sensor Missing

Major

If a wheel-speed sensor that should form part of the ABS system is missing, that is a specific ABS failure route.

Explicit MOT Defect

Sensor Damaged

Major

Visible damage affecting an ABS wheel-speed sensor can also result in a Major defect.

Diagnostic Evidence

Compare All Wheel Speeds

Live data can reveal whether one wheel-speed reading drops out, jumps, lags or behaves differently from the other wheels at the same road speed.

Common Misdiagnosis

Sensor Code Means Sensor Failure

The actual problem may involve wiring, a connector, wheel-bearing play, an encoder, reluctor damage or incorrect installation rather than the sensor itself.

Physical Check

Inspect the Complete Wheel Corner

Sensor mounting, cable routing, connector security, bearing condition and the signal target should all be considered before replacement is authorised.

Shared System

ESC May Be Affected Too

The same wheel-speed information can also be used by ESC and traction control, so one wheel fault can create several warning lamps.

Do not fit an ABS sensor simply because the fault code names that wheel

Confirm the actual signal and inspect the sensor circuit and mechanical signal source. A new sensor fitted to damaged wiring or a faulty encoder will leave the underlying ABS problem unresolved.

ABS MOT Failure Route

Can Damaged ABS Wiring Fail an MOT?

Yes. Damaged ABS wiring is specifically classified as a Major defect.

ABS wiring is exposed to heat, vibration, suspension movement, road debris and previous repair work, particularly around the wheel-speed sensor circuits.

Wheel-Arch Harness

Movement Can Damage the Cable

Wiring can rub through, stretch, become trapped or lose its securing clips as the suspension and steering move.

Connector

Corrosion or Poor Contact

Damaged pins, contamination, corrosion or a loose connector can interrupt the wheel-speed signal without the sensor itself being faulty.

Previous Repair

Harness May Have Been Disturbed

Wheel-bearing, brake or suspension work can disturb nearby ABS wiring and cause a warning that was not present before the repair.

Intermittent Fault

May Fail Only While Moving

A damaged conductor can make contact while stationary but open when the steering or suspension moves.

Proper Repair

Secure the Circuit Correctly

The repaired harness should be protected from movement, rubbing and contamination and should restore a stable signal.

Confirmation

Verify After Vehicle Movement

After wiring repair, confirm the ABS warning behaves correctly and the fault does not return during an appropriate road test.

Wiring can be the complete fault

A healthy sensor cannot provide useful wheel-speed information through a damaged circuit. Where the evidence points to wiring, repair the circuit before replacing other ABS components.

ABS MOT Failure Route

Can a Missing or Damaged ABS Component Fail the MOT?

Yes. Other ABS components that are missing or damaged can also be classified as Major defects.

ABS is a complete system rather than one dashboard lamp or one wheel sensor. The control unit, hydraulic components, sensors, wiring and associated parts must work together correctly.

Hydraulic Unit

Controls Brake Pressure During ABS Operation

The hydraulic control unit can modulate brake pressure when wheel lock is detected. A genuine unit fault can therefore create an ABS malfunction.

Electronic Control

Module Failure Is Possible

An ABS control-module fault can occur, but power supply, grounds, wiring and sensor inputs should be checked before condemning the module.

Physical Damage

Inspect Following Impact or Repair

Accident damage or previous work around the braking and suspension systems can affect ABS components or their mounting.

Missing Component

Removal Can Create an MOT Defect

A component that should form part of the fitted ABS system should not be removed simply to avoid repairing the fault.

Warning Device

System Fault May Be the First Clue

A persistent ABS warning can be the first indication of a deeper component problem, but diagnosis must establish the actual failed part.

Before Replacement

Prove the Expensive Component

The more expensive the proposed ABS repair, the stronger the supporting evidence should be from power, ground, wiring, sensor and communication checks.

A control-unit code does not automatically prove the ABS unit has failed

Low voltage, damaged wiring, poor connections and missing sensor data can all cause control units to store faults. Confirm the supply and input conditions before authorising a high-cost ABS module or hydraulic unit.

ABS MOT Failure Route

Can Removing or Disabling ABS Cause an MOT Failure?

Yes. An ABS system that has obviously been removed is classified as a Major defect.

The current inspection rules also state that ABS must not be removed or disabled from a vehicle first used on or after 1 January 2010 where ABS was fitted.

Obviously Removed

Explicit Major Defect

Where the fitted ABS system has clearly been removed, the MOT classification is Major.

Disabled System

Not a Legitimate Repair Strategy

Disabling ABS to avoid correcting a warning or system fault does not restore the braking system to its intended condition.

Warning Lamp Removed

Creates Another MOT Problem

Preventing the ABS warning device from operating correctly can itself produce a Major defect because the warning device must work.

Vehicle Age

1 January 2010 Rule

The removal or disabling restriction specifically applies to a vehicle first used on or after 1 January 2010 where ABS was fitted.

Important Detail

Not Every Post-2010 Vehicle Had ABS

The inspection issue is whether a fitted ABS system has been removed; the date alone does not prove that every vehicle originally had ABS.

Integral Parts

Some Signal Components Form Part of Other Assemblies

ABS sensor rings and similar parts can be integrated into another component such as a hub, brake disc or driveshaft, which is why system configuration should be understood before judging what has been removed.

Do not remove the ABS warning lamp or system to avoid repairing the fault

The ABS warning device not working and the ABS system being obviously removed are both explicit Major-defect routes. Correct the actual fault and restore proper system operation instead.

Diagnostic Cause

Can a Reluctor Ring or Magnetic Encoder Cause an ABS MOT Failure?

It can cause the ABS system to report a malfunction, but this distinction is important: a reluctor or magnetic encoder fault should not be presented as though the MOT manual gives every encoder defect its own separate standalone failure wording.

Instead, the damaged signal source can cause an incorrect wheel-speed reading, which may then lead to the applicable ABS malfunction or component defect being recorded.

Reluctor Ring

Damaged Teeth or Corrosion

A damaged or heavily corroded reluctor can alter the signal seen by the wheel-speed sensor as the wheel rotates.

Magnetic Encoder

Often Integrated Into a Bearing

Some wheel bearings contain a magnetic encoder that provides the ABS sensor with rotational information.

Incorrect Bearing Orientation

New Bearing, No Valid Signal

Where the encoder is only on one side of the bearing, incorrect installation can leave the sensor unable to read the expected signal.

Bearing Play

Sensor Relationship Can Change

Excessive movement can affect the relationship between the sensor and its signal target and should be checked where wheel-speed faults recur.

Live Data

Compare the Suspect Wheel

A distorted or intermittent speed trace can support further inspection of the encoder or reluctor rather than immediate sensor replacement.

Correct MOT Wording

Separate Cause From Defect

The workshop identifies the failed signal source; the MOT records the relevant ABS defect that the condition creates.

This is exactly why diagnosis and MOT wording should remain separate

The tester identifies whether the ABS system meets the inspection standard. The technician then establishes why the system has failed. That prevents diagnostic causes from being incorrectly presented as invented MOT defect categories.

Warning After Recent Work

ABS Light Came On After a Repair — What Should Be Checked Before the MOT?

If the ABS warning appeared immediately after wheel-bearing, brake, suspension, tyre or related wheel work, that timing should influence the diagnostic order.

It does not automatically prove the repair caused the warning, but the parts and wiring that were recently disturbed should be checked before unrelated ABS components are replaced.

Wheel Bearing

Check Encoder Orientation and Sensor Signal

A new bearing can still produce an ABS warning if its encoder is damaged, incorrectly orientated or incompatible with the sensor arrangement.

Brake Work

Inspect Nearby ABS Components

Brake-disc, hub and calliper work takes place close to wheel-speed sensors and wiring, so the affected wheel corner should be inspected carefully.

Suspension Work

Harness May Have Been Stretched or Trapped

ABS sensor wiring routed near struts, hubs and suspension arms can be disturbed during suspension repairs.

Driveshaft Work

Check Reluctor or Signal Source

On designs where the ABS signal source is associated with the driveshaft or CV joint area, recent work there can be relevant to a new wheel-speed fault.

Battery Work

Check Voltage and Stored Faults

Where several electronic warnings appeared after battery trouble, system voltage and stored fault history should be considered before ABS parts are condemned.

MOT Preparation

Confirm the Repair Dynamically

If the original warning appeared only after vehicle movement, the repair should be verified under the same conditions before the car is presented for test.

A warning cleared after repair is not enough by itself

Some ABS faults return only after wheel-speed data becomes available. The system should be checked through startup and suitable vehicle movement before the repair is considered complete.

Recent work is valuable diagnostic evidence

If the ABS light appeared immediately after one front wheel bearing was replaced, begin with that wheel corner, its encoder, sensor and wiring rather than jumping directly to the ABS control unit.

Pre-MOT Checks

What Should You Check Before an MOT if the ABS Light Is On?

The goal is not to make the ABS warning disappear temporarily. The goal is to confirm whether the warning device works correctly, whether the ABS system has a genuine malfunction and what physical or electrical fault must be repaired before the vehicle is presented for test.

Check 1

Observe the Warning Sequence

Check whether the ABS warning illuminates during startup, goes out normally and stays off once the vehicle begins moving.

Check 2

Note Other Warning Lights

ESC, traction-control and brake warnings can indicate a shared wheel-speed or braking-system problem and should be recorded before diagnosis begins.

Check 3

Inspect Tyres and Wheels

Look for obvious tyre damage, incorrect pressure, wheel damage and signs of recent bearing, brake or suspension work around the affected corner.

Check 4

Inspect Visible ABS Wiring

Check wheel-speed sensor cables, connectors and securing points for rubbing, stretching, contamination or obvious physical damage.

Check 5

Consider Recent Repairs

If the ABS warning appeared after wheel-bearing, brake, suspension, driveshaft or battery work, inspect the recently disturbed area first.

Check 6

Scan the ABS Module

Use a diagnostic tool capable of accessing the ABS control system rather than relying only on an engine-code reader.

Do not disable the warning lamp before the MOT

The ABS warning device itself is part of the test. A warning device that does not work correctly can create its own Major defect, even if the underlying ABS malfunction has been hidden from the dashboard.

Workshop Confirmation

How Should an ABS MOT Fault Be Diagnosed?

A proper ABS diagnosis should move from warning behaviour → stored faults → live wheel-speed data → physical inspection → circuit testing → repair → confirmation.

Step 1

Confirm Warning Operation

Establish whether the ABS warning device works during startup and whether it is currently indicating a genuine system malfunction.

Step 2

Read ABS Fault Memory

Record current, stored and intermittent faults before clearing anything so the original failure pattern is not lost.

Step 3

Compare Live Wheel Speeds

Check whether all wheel-speed signals rise smoothly and agree with each other as the vehicle moves.

Step 4

Inspect the Suspect Wheel Corner

Check the sensor, connector, cable routing, bearing condition and signal source such as a reluctor or magnetic encoder.

Step 5

Test Wiring and Supply

Where the evidence points to the circuit, check continuity, power, ground and connector integrity rather than fitting parts blindly.

Step 6

Repair and Recheck Dynamically

Confirm the warning behaves correctly through startup and suitable vehicle movement, especially where the original fault only appeared after the wheels began turning.

A wheel-speed fault code identifies where to investigate, not what to buy

The actual fault may be the sensor, wiring, connector, bearing, encoder, reluctor or installation. The physical cause should be proved before parts are replaced.

Before Test Day

What Should Be Repaired Before Presenting the Car for MOT?

Any confirmed ABS malfunction or qualifying ABS defect should be repaired before the test. The target is a system that completes its normal warning check, operates correctly and does not return a malfunction warning once the vehicle moves.

Warning Device Fault

Restore Correct Lamp Operation

If the ABS warning device does not work, repair the warning circuit or display fault rather than attempting to bypass it.

Sensor Fault

Restore the Wheel-Speed Signal

Repair the proven sensor, wiring, connector, encoder or bearing-related cause and confirm a stable live-data signal afterwards.

Wiring Fault

Repair the Circuit Properly

Damaged ABS wiring is itself an explicit MOT failure route, so repairs should be secure, protected and mechanically sound.

ABS Component Fault

Replace Only the Proven Component

Confirm power, ground, wiring, sensor inputs and communication before authorising expensive ABS control or hydraulic components.

System Removed

Restore the Fitted ABS System

Where ABS has been removed or disabled from a vehicle that should have the fitted system, restore the system rather than trying to disguise its absence.

Final Confirmation

Verify Under Real Operating Conditions

If the original warning appeared after the vehicle began moving, confirm the repair while wheel-speed information is available rather than only with the car stationary.

A repair is complete when the system behaves correctly, not when the dashboard is temporarily clear

Some ABS faults only return after several metres of driving. Confirmation should reproduce the conditions that originally triggered the warning.

Reset vs Repair

Can You Reset the ABS Light Before an MOT?

A diagnostic tool may be used to clear stored information after a repair, but resetting the warning is not itself a repair.

Correct Use

Clear Faults After Repair

Once the actual cause has been corrected, clearing stored diagnostic information can form part of final verification.

Wrong Approach

Clear the Warning and Hope

If the fault remains, the ABS warning may return immediately or only once the vehicle begins moving.

Battery Disconnect

Does Not Fix ABS Hardware

Removing battery power cannot repair damaged wiring, a failed wheel-speed signal, a faulty encoder or a missing ABS component.

Warning Device

Do Not Disable the Lamp

A non-working ABS warning device is itself an MOT defect, so covering, disconnecting or disabling it creates another problem rather than solving the original fault.

Intermittent Fault

May Return Later

Sensor and wiring faults can require wheel movement, vibration or steering input before the module detects them again.

Evidence

Record Before Clearing

Fault status and operating conditions can be useful during diagnosis, especially where the warning is intermittent.

A hidden ABS fault is still an ABS fault

If the ABS system remains malfunctioning, the underlying MOT problem still exists regardless of whether the warning has temporarily disappeared.

After an ABS MOT Failure

What Happens After an ABS MOT Failure?

If the vehicle fails because of an ABS Major defect, the fault must be repaired before the vehicle can obtain a pass.

The exact retest arrangement depends on whether the vehicle remains at the test station for repair or is taken away and returned later.

Vehicle Stays for Repair

Partial Retest Route

Where the vehicle remains at the same test station for repair and is re-examined within the permitted retest period, a partial retest can apply.

Vehicle Taken Away

Return to the Same Test Station

If the vehicle is removed for repair and returned to the same test station within the permitted period, a partial retest can apply and a retest fee may be charged.

Repair Confirmation

Check Before Returning

Confirm that the ABS warning behaves normally and the original dynamic fault does not return before the vehicle is presented again.

Do not assume every ABS repair qualifies for a next-day free retest

Retest arrangements depend on the applicable procedure, repair location and return timing. If the vehicle leaves the test station, confirm the retest arrangement and deadline with the same test centre.

A retest is not a substitute for post-repair diagnosis

If the original ABS malfunction remains, the warning can return once the wheels begin moving and the vehicle can fail again.

Frequently Asked Questions

ABS Warning Light and MOT FAQs

Can an ABS warning light fail an MOT?

Yes. If the ABS warning device shows a system malfunction, the defect is classified as Major under the current MOT inspection manual.

Is an ABS warning light always an MOT failure?

No. Normal startup self-check behaviour is not the same as a system malfunction. The MOT issue is whether the ABS warning device is working correctly and whether it subsequently indicates an ABS fault.

What does the MOT tester check on the ABS system?

The ABS inspection includes the warning device, wheel-speed sensors, ABS wiring, other ABS components and whether the ABS system has obviously been removed.

Can an ABS warning device that does not work fail the MOT?

Yes. An ABS warning device that is not working is classified as a Major MOT defect.

Can an ABS warning light showing a malfunction fail the MOT?

Yes. An ABS warning device showing a system malfunction is classified as a Major defect.

Can a missing or damaged wheel-speed sensor fail the MOT?

Yes. Wheel-speed sensors that are missing or damaged are listed as Major defects within the ABS inspection.

Can damaged ABS wiring fail an MOT?

Yes. Damaged ABS wiring is specifically classified as a Major defect.

Can a missing or damaged ABS component fail the MOT?

Yes. Other ABS components that are missing or damaged can be classified as Major defects where the ABS inspection applies.

Can removing the ABS system fail an MOT?

Yes. An ABS system that has obviously been removed is classified as a Major defect. It is not permissible to remove or disable ABS from a vehicle first used on or after 1 January 2010 where ABS was fitted.

Is it normal for the ABS light to come on and then go out?

Yes. On many vehicles the ABS warning light illuminates briefly during the startup self-check and then goes out. That behaviour is different from a warning device that remains on because the system has detected a malfunction.

What if the ABS light comes on while driving?

If the ABS warning appears while driving and remains illuminated, the system may have detected a fault. The cause should be diagnosed before the MOT rather than assuming the warning will clear permanently.

Can an ABS fault also trigger traction control or ESC warnings?

Yes. ABS, traction control and ESC can share wheel-speed sensors, wiring and braking-system information, so one underlying fault can cause several warning lamps to appear.

Can an ABS light and ESC light fail the MOT together?

Yes. ABS and ESC have separate MOT inspection requirements, and malfunction warnings or qualifying defects in either system can result in Major defects. One shared wheel-speed or wiring fault can sometimes affect both.

Can a reluctor ring or magnetic encoder cause an ABS MOT failure?

A damaged reluctor ring or magnetic encoder can create an incorrect wheel-speed signal and cause the ABS system to report a malfunction. The MOT failure is based on the applicable ABS defect, while diagnosis should identify the actual signal source.

Can I clear the ABS light before the MOT?

Clearing the warning without repairing the underlying ABS fault is not a proper fix. The warning can return and the vehicle can still fail the MOT.

Can low battery voltage cause an ABS warning light?

Low system voltage can contribute to ABS and other electronic warnings on some vehicles. Battery and charging condition should be considered when several warnings appeared together, but the ABS fault memory and live data should still be checked.

Should I repair an ABS fault before the MOT?

Yes. If the ABS warning device is showing a system malfunction or another qualifying ABS defect is present, diagnose and repair the cause before the test and confirm that the warning behaviour is correct afterwards.

What should I check first if the ABS light is on before an MOT?

Confirm the exact warning behaviour, note any ESC or brake warnings, scan the ABS control system, compare wheel-speed data, inspect the relevant sensors and wiring and consider recent wheel, bearing, brake, suspension or battery work before replacing parts.

How Motor Vehicle Expert Approaches ABS and MOT Faults

Motor Vehicle Expert writes ABS and MOT guides around the actual inspection requirement rather than treating every ABS warning as the same problem. The first step is understanding whether the warning device itself works correctly and whether the ABS system is reporting a genuine malfunction.

We then separate the MOT defect from the workshop diagnosis. A Major ABS defect tells you how the vehicle is treated during the test; it does not automatically tell a technician which part should be replaced.

Wheel-speed sensors, wiring, connectors, wheel bearings, reluctor rings, magnetic encoders, power supply, control modules and hydraulic components can all form part of the diagnostic path. A fault code therefore points towards a circuit or condition that requires testing rather than acting as permission to replace the named component immediately.

We also keep closely related warning-light pages deliberately separated. This guide owns the ABS-specific MOT question. The Service ESC pillar owns shared stability-system diagnosis, the ESP child owns safe-driving decisions and the traction-control child owns ESC and stability-control MOT intent.
