UK MOT rear light lens guide

Is a Cracked Tail Light an MOT Failure?

A cracked tail light can fail an MOT, but the result depends on what part of the rear lamp is damaged and how the defect affects the required lighting function. MOT testers consider lens condition, security, emitted colour, light output and whether the damaged lamp remains durable and correctly attached.

Quick answer

Will a Cracked Tail Light Fail an MOT?

Yes, a cracked tail light can fail an MOT. For a required rear position lamp, the current MOT inspection checks the lamp's presence, condition, security and function. A rear position lamp with a lens the tester considers defective can be recorded as a Major defect.

However, the exact result depends on which lighting function is affected and the actual condition of the lamp. Modern rear clusters often combine the tail light, brake light, indicator, reversing light, fog light and reflector inside one housing. The tester therefore assesses the relevant function rather than treating every visible mark on a plastic lens in exactly the same way.

Crack in Rear Position Lamp Lens

If the required rear position-lamp lens is genuinely defective, it can result in an MOT failure even where the light source itself still illuminates.

Light Works but Damage Affects Output

A working bulb does not guarantee a pass. Damage that changes the emitted colour, significantly reduces light output or compromises the lamp can still create an MOT defect.

Superficial Mark or Tiny Surface Crack

The important question is whether the tester judges the lens or lamp to be defective. Condition, security, colour, output and durability are considered together rather than using a simple crack-length measurement.

Tail light still works? Do not assume it will pass.

The MOT does not only check whether light appears when the switch is operated. A cracked rear lamp must also remain secure, produce the required colour and output, and be in acceptable condition.

Mechanic-style pre-MOT advice

If a crack has opened through the lens, a piece is missing, white light is visible through a normally red section, moisture is entering the unit or the lamp housing has become loose, repairing or replacing the lamp before the MOT is the safer option.

UK MOT rules

What Do the MOT Rules Say About a Cracked Tail Light?

For required rear position lamps, the MOT inspection checks whether the lamp is present, secure, in acceptable condition and functioning correctly. The lens is part of that inspection, so damage cannot be judged only by asking whether the bulb or LED still illuminates.

A required rear position lamp with a defective lens can be recorded as a Major defect. The tester also considers whether the lamp remains securely attached, whether it emits the correct colour and whether the light output is affected by damage or an attempted repair.

Lens Condition

A cracked lens is assessed by its actual condition. Splitting, missing material, distortion or damage that compromises the required lamp can result in an MOT defect.

Correct Red Light

A required rear position lamp must emit the required red light. If cracking or missing red lens material allows white or another incorrect colour to show, the damage is more likely to cause a failure.

Security and Function

The lamp must remain secure and function correctly. A cracked housing that moves, separates from the body or no longer supports the lamp properly creates a different problem from a superficial mark.

Combined rear light clusters need careful assessment

A modern rear lamp cluster can contain several different functions behind one outer lens, including the tail light, brake light, indicator, reversing light and rear fog lamp. The tester assesses the relevant required lamp and the way the damage affects that particular function.

Do not rely on the phrase “the crack is only cosmetic”

Whether damage is acceptable is determined by the condition of the required lamp, not by the owner's description of the crack. If the lens is defective or the damage affects colour, output, security or durability, the MOT result can change.

Damage severity

Small Crack vs Serious Tail Light Lens Damage

There is no simple MOT rule stating that a crack of a certain length automatically passes or fails. The important issue is what the damage has done to the lamp. A hairline mark that has not opened the lens is very different from a split lens with missing plastic, exposed internal components or visible white light.

Fine Surface Mark

A light surface mark or superficial stress line may have little practical effect if the lens remains intact, secure, correctly coloured and fully functional.

Lower MOT Risk

Crack Through the Lens

Once the crack has opened through the plastic, sealing and durability become concerns. Moisture can enter and the damaged section may spread or break away.

Increased MOT Risk

Missing Lens Material

A missing piece is much more significant because the lamp may no longer produce the correct colour or may expose the bulb, reflector, LEDs or internal electronics.

High MOT Risk

Crack Across Illuminated Area

Damage directly across the illuminated portion deserves particular attention because it may distort, scatter or reduce the visible light output.

Inspect Carefully

Lens Beginning to Separate

If the outer lens is lifting away from the housing or can be moved by hand, the problem is no longer just a crack; lamp security and durability are also involved.

Repair Before MOT

Crack With Water Inside

Water ingress indicates that sealing has been compromised. Standing water can damage bulb holders, terminals, reflectors and LED electronics even if the lamp currently works.

Repair Recommended
Practical pre-MOT rule

If you can see the crack opening, a section of lens is missing, the lamp changes colour, moisture is entering or the unit no longer feels secure, do not gamble on the MOT. Repair or replace the damaged lamp first.

Common question

Will a Cracked Tail Light Fail an MOT if the Light Still Works?

It can. A functioning bulb or LED is only one part of the MOT inspection. The tester also checks the condition and security of the lamp and whether it produces the required colour and adequate visible output.

This is the important answer for drivers who switch the lights on, see a red glow and assume the lamp must pass. A cracked lens can still be defective even though the electrical part of the lamp continues to operate.

Works and Still Looks Red

Correct red illumination is a positive sign, but it does not cancel out significant lens damage or an insecure lamp. The physical condition must still be acceptable.

Works but Shows White Light

If damaged red lens material allows white light from the bulb or LED source to escape, the required emitted colour can be affected and the lamp should be repaired before the test.

Works but Output Is Weak

A lamp can illuminate yet still have reduced output because of lens damage, contamination, corrosion, internal water, electrical resistance or LED failure.

MOT inspection

What Does an MOT Tester Check on a Cracked Rear Light?

The tester's decision is based on the required lamp and the defect actually present. For a cracked rear lamp, several inspection points can matter at the same time.

Is the Required Lamp Present?

The required lighting function must be fitted where the MOT rules require it. A missing or substantially destroyed lamp cannot be treated as a cosmetic lens problem.

Does It Illuminate?

The tester operates the relevant lamps and confirms that the required function works rather than remaining completely inoperative.

Is the Lens Defective?

Cracking, missing pieces and other physical deterioration are considered when judging the condition of the required rear lamp.

Is the Lamp Secure?

The lamp should remain correctly attached to the vehicle. Damage to mounting points or the housing can make the unit insecure even when the lens itself is still mostly present.

Is the Colour Correct?

Rear position lamps must produce the required red light. Damage should not allow an incorrect colour to be emitted from the required tail-light section.

Is Light Output Adequate?

Lens damage, contamination or internal deterioration can interfere with the visible output even where the light source itself continues to work.

Has the Damage Been Repaired?

A repaired lamp is judged by the result of the repair. It should remain secure, correctly coloured, effective and sufficiently durable rather than simply looking acceptable from several metres away.

Are Other Cluster Functions Affected?

The same cracked unit may contain the stop lamp, indicator, reversing lamp or fog lamp. Damage across another section of the cluster can therefore create additional MOT defects.

Is the Reflector Damaged?

Many rear clusters incorporate reflectors. Significant damage may therefore affect more than the powered lighting functions and should be checked before the MOT.

Why two similar cracks can receive different results

A crack confined to a non-critical edge of one lamp may have very different consequences from a crack that removes red lens material, makes the unit loose or crosses several lighting sections. The actual defect matters more than the word “cracked”.

Colour and visibility

How Cracked Tail Light Lenses Affect Colour and Light Output

A rear light lens does more than keep rain away from the bulb. Depending on the lamp design, it can provide the required colour, control the distribution of light and protect the reflector, bulb holder or LED electronics behind it.

Correct Rear Position Light Colour

The required tail-light function must emit red light. If a broken red lens exposes an uncoloured bulb and allows white light to show through, the lamp no longer presents the same compliant output.

Red LED Behind a Clear Lens

Some modern lamps use coloured LEDs behind clear or partly clear lenses. In those designs, a crack may not alter the colour in the same way, but lens condition, output, security and sealing still matter.

Crack Distorting the Beam

Broken or displaced lens material can scatter light unevenly. Where damage significantly reduces or distorts the useful visible output, the fact that the bulb remains illuminated does not resolve the defect.

Dirty or Clouded Internal Surface

Once moisture and dirt enter through a crack, the reflector and inner lens surfaces can deteriorate. Output may become weaker long before the electrical circuit stops working.

Check the lamp in darkness as well as daylight

Colour leakage and weak output can be easier to identify in lower light. Compare the damaged side directly with the undamaged rear lamp rather than judging brightness in isolation.

Lamp security

Can a Cracked Tail Light Fail Because the Lamp Is Loose or Missing Pieces?

Yes. A cracked tail light can become an MOT problem for more than one reason. If the lens or housing has split badly enough for the lamp to move, separate from its mounting points or lose part of the lens, the tester is no longer looking at a simple cosmetic crack.

Rear lamps should remain securely attached and capable of performing their required function. Damage to the mounting tabs, housing or outer lens can therefore matter even where the bulb or LED still illuminates correctly.

Loose Lamp Unit

If the entire rear lamp moves when touched, the housing or mounting points may be damaged. A loose unit can affect alignment, durability and electrical connections.

Repair Before MOT

Broken Mounting Tab

Plastic mounting tabs often break in minor impacts. One damaged tab may not always make the lamp obviously loose, but several broken fixings can leave the unit inadequately supported.

Check Security

Piece of Lens Missing

Missing lens material can expose the light source, change the emitted colour, allow water into the housing and make the remaining plastic more likely to break further.

High MOT Risk

Lens Lifting From Housing

A lens that is separating from the lamp body may no longer remain weatherproof or durable. Adhesive failure and impact damage are common causes.

Repair Recommended

Internal Parts Exposed

If the crack exposes the reflector, bulb holder, circuit board or LED module, the lamp is vulnerable to water, corrosion and further deterioration.

Replace or Properly Repair

Sharp Broken Plastic

Jagged pieces should be dealt with promptly. Apart from the lighting defect itself, broken plastic can worsen quickly and may present an avoidable safety hazard during normal use or maintenance.

Do Not Leave Exposed
Check the mounting points, not just the lens face

After a parking knock or low-speed collision, the visible crack may be only part of the damage. Open the boot or access panel where possible and check that the lamp fixings and surrounding body mounting points remain intact.

If the outer cover is substantially broken or partly missing, see our Broken Tail Light Cover MOT Guide for the more specific rules and repair considerations.

Moisture and sealing

Can a Cracked Tail Light Cause Water Ingress and MOT Problems?

Yes. Once a crack penetrates the outer lens or the lens begins separating from the housing, moisture can enter the lamp. Initially this may appear as light condensation, but continued water ingress can damage electrical connections, reflectors, bulb holders and LED electronics.

Water inside the lamp does not automatically mean the same thing in every case. A small amount of temporary condensation after washing the vehicle is different from standing water, droplets that remain for days or obvious entry through a damaged lens.

Light Condensation

A thin mist that clears naturally may occur in some vented lamp units and does not necessarily mean the housing is seriously damaged.

Monitor Condition

Persistent Droplets

Droplets that repeatedly reform can indicate failed sealing or a crack that is allowing moisture into the housing.

Inspect Sealing

Standing Water

Water collecting in the bottom of the lamp is a stronger sign that the unit has lost its weather sealing and may soon suffer electrical or reflector damage.

Repair Promptly

Corroded Bulb Holder

Moisture can corrode contacts and increase electrical resistance, causing intermittent operation, flickering or reduced brightness.

LED Module Damage

Many modern rear lights use sealed LED electronics. Moisture damage can lead to partial LED failure and may require replacement of the complete lamp assembly.

Reflector Deterioration

Water and contamination can dull or stain the internal reflector, reducing the useful light output even though the light source itself still works.

Drying the lamp does not repair the crack

Removing moisture may temporarily restore appearance, but if water entered through damaged plastic or failed sealing, the cause remains. The unit should be sealed correctly or replaced rather than repeatedly dried out.

Pre-MOT inspection

How to Check a Cracked Tail Light Before the MOT

A basic pre-MOT inspection can identify most obvious rear-light problems before the vehicle reaches the test station. Check the lamp both switched off and illuminated, because some defects are easier to see in only one condition.

1. Clean the Lamp

Remove road dirt so you can see the true extent of the crack, missing plastic, water marks and any previous repair.

2. Inspect the Crack Closely

Check whether the damage is only a surface mark or whether the plastic has opened completely through the lens.

3. Look for Missing Pieces

Confirm that no section of the coloured lens or outer cover has broken away.

4. Check Lamp Security

Gently check whether the complete lamp moves. It should remain firmly attached without excessive movement.

5. Switch on the Side Lights

Confirm that both rear position lamps illuminate and compare the damaged side directly with the undamaged side.

6. Check the Emitted Colour

Make sure the required rear position-light area still produces a clear red light without obvious white light escaping through a missing or broken section.

7. Compare Brightness

Check whether the damaged lamp appears significantly weaker or less visible than the equivalent lamp on the opposite side.

8. Test the Brake Lights

If the cracked cluster also contains the stop lamp, confirm that the brake-light section works correctly and that the damaged lens has not affected its colour or output.

9. Test the Indicator

Where the indicator is part of the same cluster, confirm correct operation and colour because one cracked outer lens can cross several separate lamp sections.

10. Check for Moisture

Look for condensation, droplets, staining or standing water inside the housing.

11. Inspect Previous Repairs

Check tape, adhesive or repaired plastic carefully. A repair should not change the required colour or leave the unit insecure.

12. Recheck in Lower Light

In a garage or shaded area, compare colour and brightness again. White light leakage and weak output may be easier to see away from bright daylight.

Use the opposite lamp as your comparison

On vehicles with matching left and right rear clusters, the undamaged side is a useful reference for brightness, colour and normal appearance. A noticeable difference deserves investigation before the MOT.

Temporary repairs

Will Tape Over a Cracked Tail Light Pass an MOT?

Tape is not automatically a guaranteed pass or fail. What matters is the condition of the lamp after the repair. A repair should remain secure and durable and must not prevent the lamp from producing the correct colour and adequate visible output.

This means a small, neat repair may be judged very differently from clear tape stretched over a large hole, loose adhesive hanging from the lamp or tape that changes the appearance and brightness of the required lighting function.

Correct-Coloured Repair Material

Where coloured lens material is missing, any repair must still allow the required lamp function to emit the correct colour.

Clear Tape Over Missing Red Lens

If an uncoloured bulb is behind the lens, clear tape may allow white light to escape instead of the required red output.

Poor MOT Strategy

Tape Holding the Whole Lamp On

If tape is being used because mounting points are broken and the complete lamp is loose, replacing or properly securing the unit is the stronger repair.

Repair Properly

Temporary Waterproofing

Tape can reduce water entry for a short period, but it should not be relied upon as a permanent repair where the lens has split significantly.

Lens Repair Tape

Purpose-made coloured lens repair material can provide a better temporary result than ordinary household tape, but the final lamp condition still needs to be secure, correctly coloured and effective.

Glue or Plastic Repair

A clean crack may sometimes be repaired with suitable plastic repair methods, provided the repair remains strong and does not distort the required light output.

A temporary repair should not hide a serious defect

If a large section of lens is missing, the lamp is loose, water has damaged the electronics or several lighting functions are affected, replacing the unit is usually safer and more reliable than trying to cover the damage.

Repair options

Should You Repair or Replace a Cracked Tail Light?

The right repair depends on how deep the crack is, whether any lens material is missing, whether the lamp remains sealed and whether the unit uses replaceable bulbs or integrated LED electronics.

Small Stable Crack

A fine crack with no missing material, no water entry and no effect on colour or output may sometimes be repairable using an appropriate plastic or lens-repair method.

Repair May Be Possible

Large Split Lens

A long open split is more likely to admit water and spread further. Replacement is often a more durable solution.

Replacement Often Better

Missing Lens Section

When coloured plastic is missing or the internal lamp is exposed, replacement normally gives the most reliable result.

Replace Recommended

Broken Mountings

If the housing itself is damaged and the lamp cannot be secured properly, replacing the assembly may be necessary even where the visible lens crack looks small.

Bulb-Type Rear Lamp

Older bulb-based units are often available used or aftermarket at reasonable cost, making complete replacement relatively straightforward.

Integrated LED Lamp

Modern LED clusters can be considerably more expensive because the light source and electronics may be built into a sealed assembly rather than supplied separately.

Used Genuine Lamp

A good used original lamp can be cost-effective, but check carefully for hidden cracks, damaged mounting tabs, condensation and matching part numbers before fitting it.

Aftermarket Lamp

Aftermarket replacement units can reduce cost. Fit and quality vary, so make sure the lamp mounts correctly and all required functions operate as intended.

New Genuine Lamp

A new original-equipment lamp usually gives the best fit and finish but can be expensive on newer vehicles, particularly with LED technology.

Repair the cause as well as the visible damage

If the lamp cracked because of impact damage, check the bumper corner, body mounting points and wiring at the same time. A replacement lamp fitted to distorted or broken mounting points may not sit securely or seal correctly.

Used car buying

Should You Buy a Used Car With a Cracked Tail Light?

A cracked tail light is not automatically a reason to reject a used car, but it should not be ignored. The visible crack may be a cheap repair, or it may be evidence of previous impact damage, broken mounting points, water ingress or an expensive LED lamp that requires complete replacement.

Check the Crack Closely

Determine whether the mark is superficial or whether the plastic is split completely through the lens.

Look for Missing Plastic

Missing lens material increases the likelihood of water ingress, incorrect emitted colour and further damage.

Check Lamp Security

Gently check whether the lamp moves. Broken mounting tabs can be hidden behind the body panel after a minor impact.

Look for Water Inside

Persistent moisture or standing water can indicate that the lamp has been damaged for some time and may already have electrical corrosion.

Test Every Lamp Function

Check tail lights, brake lights, indicators, reversing lights and rear fog lamps where they are incorporated into the damaged cluster.

Compare Brightness

A noticeably weaker damaged-side lamp may indicate corrosion, LED failure, a poor electrical connection or internal contamination.

Check the Surrounding Bodywork

Uneven panel gaps, cracked bumper corners or distorted lamp apertures can indicate previous impact damage rather than an isolated broken lens.

Ask Why It Has Not Been Repaired

A seller describing the damage as inexpensive should be able to explain why it has been left unrepaired. On newer LED-equipped cars the true replacement cost may be much higher than expected.

Check MOT History

Previous lighting defects, accident-related problems or repeated lamp issues can provide useful context when assessing the vehicle.

Do not price the defect as “just a bit of plastic”

On a traditional bulb lamp the repair may be inexpensive. On a modern integrated LED cluster, a cracked outer lens can mean replacing an assembly costing several hundred pounds. Establish the correct part and replacement cost before negotiating the vehicle price.

Used-car buying decision

A small isolated crack with a secure, dry and fully working lamp is very different from a loose, water-filled LED unit accompanied by distorted bodywork. Judge the complete condition rather than the crack alone.

Frequently Asked Questions

Cracked Tail Light MOT FAQs

These are the most important questions UK drivers ask about cracked rear-light lenses, tail-light MOT failures, temporary repairs and replacement decisions.

Is a cracked tail light an MOT failure?

A cracked tail light can fail an MOT. A required rear position lamp is checked for condition, security and function, and a defective lens can be recorded as a Major defect.

Will a cracked tail light fail an MOT if the light still works?

It can. A working bulb or LED does not automatically mean the lamp will pass because the tester also considers lens condition, lamp security, emitted colour and light output.

Will a small crack in a tail light pass an MOT?

A superficial mark may be less significant than an open or defective lens, but there is no simple permitted crack length. The tester assesses the actual condition of the required lamp and whether the defect affects its function, security, colour or output.

Can a hairline crack in a rear light fail an MOT?

A fine surface mark is different from a lens that has split through. If the lens is judged defective or the crack affects lamp condition, sealing, colour, security or output, it can become an MOT problem.

Will missing red plastic from a tail light fail an MOT?

Missing red lens material can cause an MOT failure if it leaves the required rear position lamp with a defective lens or allows the lamp to emit an incorrect colour.

Does a rear tail light have to be red for an MOT?

A required rear position lamp must emit red light. A damaged lens that allows white or another incorrect colour to show can therefore create an MOT defect.

Can I use red tape on a cracked tail light for an MOT?

A repair is judged by the resulting lamp condition. It must remain secure and durable and must not prevent the lamp from producing the correct colour and adequate light output. Tape is not a guaranteed MOT pass.

Will clear tape over a cracked tail light pass an MOT?

Clear tape is risky where red lens material is missing because an uncoloured light source may show white light through the damaged area. The repaired lamp still needs the correct colour, condition, security and output.

Can a loose tail light fail an MOT?

Yes. Required rear lamps are checked for security as well as function. Broken mounting tabs or a damaged housing that leaves the lamp insecure can result in an MOT defect.

Will water in a cracked tail light fail an MOT?

Water itself does not create the same result in every case, but a cracked lamp with persistent moisture or standing water can develop poor output, corrosion, electrical faults or further lens deterioration that affects the MOT.

Can condensation inside a tail light fail an MOT?

Light temporary condensation is different from significant water ingress. The MOT concern increases when moisture affects lamp operation, output, condition or the durability of the unit.

Will a dim tail light fail an MOT?

A tail light can become an MOT problem if its output is significantly reduced. Weak output may result from lens damage, corrosion, electrical resistance, incorrect bulbs or partial LED failure.

Can a cracked LED tail light fail an MOT?

Yes. LED rear lamps are still assessed for the required lighting function, condition, security, colour and output. A cracked outer lens can also allow moisture to damage integrated LED electronics.

Does a cracked brake light lens fail an MOT?

Brake light lens damage is assessed according to the stop-lamp requirements. Damage that affects the emitted light is more serious than damage that does not affect the light, so the exact defect matters.

Can a cracked indicator section fail an MOT?

Yes. If the cracked rear cluster also contains the indicator, damage can affect that separate lighting function, including its required colour and visible output.

Should I replace a cracked tail light before an MOT?

Replacement is sensible where the lens is clearly split, material is missing, the lamp is loose, water is entering, the emitted colour is wrong or the output is affected. Minor stable damage may be repairable if the final lamp remains secure and effective.

How much does a cracked tail light cost to repair in the UK?

Costs vary from inexpensive lens repair materials to several hundred pounds for complete LED lamp assemblies. Used and aftermarket lamps may reduce costs, while premium integrated LED units can cost substantially more.

Is a cracked tail light safe to drive with?

A small stable crack may not immediately stop the lamp working, but damage should be repaired promptly if the unit is loose, the required colour or output is affected, sharp plastic is exposed or water is entering the lamp.
