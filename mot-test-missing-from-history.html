MOT Test Missing From History?

An MOT test missing from your vehicle's history can make it difficult to confirm previous passes, failures, mileage readings and recorded defects. This complete UK guide explains why individual MOT tests may be absent, how to identify missing records, what evidence to prepare, who to contact and how to investigate genuine mistakes using official MOT record guidance.

Missing MOT Records

Why is an MOT test missing from vehicle history?

When an MOT test appears to be missing from a vehicle's history, the first step is to establish whether the test is genuinely absent or whether the online record is being interpreted incorrectly.

The official MOT history service records qualifying MOT tests carried out in Great Britain within its available historical coverage. However, not every vehicle has a complete online record covering its entire lifetime. A gap between displayed test dates does not automatically prove that an MOT test has been deleted or incorrectly recorded.

Incorrect registration

The registration entered may be incorrect, or the vehicle may previously have carried a different number plate. Always check that the returned vehicle details match the car being investigated.

Older test records

Online MOT history has historical coverage limits. Older vehicles may have undergone tests before the available digital records began.

Test recording problem

A test may have been completed but its expected details are not visible in the official history. The test centre and official reporting process may help establish what happened.

Different testing arrangements

Vehicles tested in Northern Ireland have separate MOT testing arrangements. Do not assume the Great Britain history service contains every test carried out elsewhere.

No test was carried out

A gap may reflect a period when the vehicle was not tested, such as when it was off the road. The gap itself does not establish why.

Vehicle identity mismatch

Incorrect registration or VIN information may make it difficult to match documents with the expected vehicle record.

Missing MOT history does not always mean a missing test

For example, a vehicle may show an MOT pass in March one year and its next recorded test in May the following year. That gap alone does not prove that a test is missing. The vehicle may have been unused, tested late or subject to circumstances that require further checking.

A stronger indication of a genuine record discrepancy is when you have a certificate or other credible evidence identifying a specific MOT test that does not appear in the official history.

Mechanic-style checking tip

Identify the exact missing test date before reporting a problem. A precise test date, registration number and certificate details are more useful than simply reporting that the history looks incomplete.

Official MOT Records

How UK MOT test records work

MOT test records provide a history of qualifying vehicle inspections. For vehicles covered by the Great Britain service, the official online history can show previous test dates, results, recorded mileage, defects and advisory information.

These records help drivers understand when vehicles were tested and what was recorded at each inspection. However, an MOT history is not the same as a complete servicing or mechanical maintenance record.

1. Vehicle identification

The test record is associated with the vehicle's recorded identity and registration details.

2. MOT inspection

An authorised tester carries out the applicable MOT inspection.

3. Test result recorded

The outcome and relevant inspection details are recorded through the official MOT testing system.

4. History checked

Drivers can use the official service to review available previous MOT results.

What information should an MOT history show?

Test date and result

The recorded date and whether the vehicle passed or failed the test.

Recorded mileage

The odometer reading recorded during the test, where applicable.

Defects and advisories

Recorded faults, failures and advisory items, according to the rules and records applicable at the time.

How far back does online MOT history go?

For cars, motorcycles and vans tested in Great Britain, the GOV.UK MOT history service generally provides records from 2005 onwards. Older paper-based tests may not be available online. The applicable record coverage can differ according to vehicle type and testing location.

Important distinction

An MOT certificate records the outcome of a particular inspection. It does not prove that the vehicle remained roadworthy throughout the period after the test. Equally, an incomplete online history does not automatically establish that a vehicle was never tested.

Missing MOT Failure

What if an MOT failure is missing from history?

An MOT failure is also part of a vehicle's testing history. A failed inspection can record defects that explain why repairs were needed before a later pass.

If you have evidence that a particular MOT failure took place but it does not appear in the official history, check the test date, registration, vehicle details and any available paperwork.

Failure followed by a pass

Compare the failed test and subsequent pass dates. The records may help explain repairs carried out between tests.

Failure with major defects

A missing failure may conceal useful information about previously recorded defects. Verify the available evidence before drawing conclusions.

Failure with dangerous defects

Dangerous defects are serious safety findings. If documents indicate such a result, confirm the vehicle's repair and testing history carefully.

Why a missing failure matters when buying a used car

Previous MOT failures can reveal recurring problems involving brakes, suspension, tyres, corrosion, steering and other safety-related components.

A later MOT pass indicates that the vehicle met the applicable test requirements at that inspection. It does not prove that every previous defect was permanently resolved or that the vehicle has been maintained correctly since.

Historical MOT Records

Why are there gaps in older MOT history?

Gaps in older MOT history are not necessarily evidence of a missing or deleted test. The online MOT history service has historical coverage limits, and a vehicle may have periods during which no MOT test was required or carried out.

For cars, motorcycles and vans tested in Great Britain, the online service generally provides MOT history from 2005 onwards. Earlier tests may have been recorded on paper rather than appearing in the current online service.

Tests before 2005

Older paper-based MOT records may not appear in the online history. An absence of earlier entries does not prove the vehicle was never tested.

Historical coverage limit

Vehicle kept off the road

A vehicle may have been unused or kept off public roads for a period. Check available documents rather than assuming a missing test.

Check vehicle history

Imported vehicles

A vehicle imported into Great Britain may have been inspected under another country's testing system. Earlier foreign inspections will not necessarily appear in the GB MOT history.

Check import records

Different testing jurisdiction

Northern Ireland uses separate MOT testing arrangements. Check the appropriate official service for tests carried out there.

Late or missed MOT tests

A gap may mean the vehicle was not tested during that period. The online dates alone cannot explain why.

Registration changes

Previous number plate changes may complicate the investigation. Confirm the current vehicle identity before comparing records.

How to investigate an unexplained MOT history gap

1. Identify the gap

Write down the last recorded test before the gap and the first test after it.

2. Check the vehicle's age

Establish when the vehicle first became subject to MOT testing and whether an exemption may apply.

3. Review supporting records

Compare service history, invoices, previous certificates and ownership information.

4. Decide whether a test is missing

Only report a specific missing test when there is a reasonable basis for believing it took place.

Important distinction

A gap in the online history is not the same as proof of an incorrect MOT record. The strongest evidence of a genuine missing test is documentation identifying a particular inspection that should appear in the official record.

Number Plate Changes

Can changing a number plate make an MOT test appear missing?

A vehicle may have carried several registration numbers during its lifetime, particularly if a private number plate has been assigned or removed.

Changing the registration number does not automatically erase the vehicle's previous MOT tests. However, a registration change can make the history more difficult to interpret if you are searching using an old plate or comparing documents issued under different registrations.

Private plate assigned

A vehicle may receive a private registration after earlier MOT tests were completed under another plate.

Original plate restored

A private plate may later be removed and the vehicle assigned another registration.

Old plate reassigned

A previous registration may subsequently be associated with another vehicle. Do not assume the returned history belongs to your car.

How to check MOT history after a registration change

Keep this issue separate from a missing test

A missing individual MOT result and a registration-history problem are not necessarily the same issue. First establish that the correct vehicle is being checked, then investigate any genuinely absent test.

Official Responsibilities

DVLA or DVSA: who deals with missing MOT tests?

DVLA and DVSA have different responsibilities. Knowing which organisation deals with the underlying problem can help prevent unnecessary delays.

In Great Britain, DVLA is responsible for vehicle registration and licensing records. DVSA oversees MOT testing and the associated testing system.

DVLA — vehicle registration

DVLA deals with vehicle registration information, V5C documents, registration number assignments and private plate transfers.

If you know which authorised MOT test centre carried out the inspection, contacting that centre can be a useful early step, particularly when the missing test was completed recently.

The garage may be able to confirm whether the vehicle was tested, identify the relevant test details or explain which official procedure applies. However, a garage cannot simply rewrite historical MOT records at will.

UK Driving Rules

Can you drive if an MOT test is missing from history?

Whether you can legally drive depends on the vehicle's current MOT status, whether it requires an MOT and whether it is roadworthy. A historical gap alone does not establish that driving is prohibited.

In Great Britain, most cars require an MOT once they reach three years old, unless an exemption applies. A vehicle that requires an MOT must normally have a valid certificate for ordinary road use.

What about driving to a pre-arranged MOT test?

Limited exceptions to the requirement for a current MOT can apply to certain journeys for a pre-arranged MOT test or specified repairs. These exceptions do not remove the requirement for the vehicle to be roadworthy.

If a vehicle has a dangerous defect, do not drive it until the defect has been repaired. Arrange recovery or suitable transport where necessary.

Important legal and safety warning

Never assume that a missing MOT entry provides an exemption from MOT rules. Confirm the vehicle's current legal position before driving. A vehicle can be unsafe or unlawful to use even if its MOT status appears valid.

Frequently Asked Questions

MOT Test Missing From History — FAQs

1. Why is an MOT test missing from my vehicle history?

An MOT test may appear missing because you are checking the wrong registration, the vehicle previously had another number plate, the test falls outside the available online history or there is a genuine record discrepancy. Confirm the vehicle identity and specific test date before reporting a missing entry.

2. Can an MOT test disappear from the official history?

A test that previously appeared but can no longer be found should be investigated. First confirm that you are checking the correct vehicle and registration details. Do not assume the test has been deliberately deleted without supporting evidence.

3. What should I do if my latest MOT pass is missing?

Check the vehicle's current MOT status using the official GOV.UK service. Compare the registration, test date and certificate details, then contact the original MOT test centre if necessary. If validity remains uncertain, obtain clarification before ordinary road use.

4. Can a failed MOT be missing from history?

If you have a failure certificate or other evidence of a particular failed MOT that does not appear online, compare the registration, test date and vehicle identity. A missing failure should be investigated through the appropriate official MOT record process.

5. How far back does online MOT history go?

For cars, motorcycles and vans tested in Great Britain, online MOT history generally goes back to 2005. Earlier paper-based records may not appear online. Coverage can differ according to vehicle type and testing location.

6. Can changing a private number plate affect MOT history?

A private registration transfer can make MOT history more difficult to interpret, particularly when older certificates show a previous registration. A number plate change does not automatically erase the vehicle's previous MOT tests.

7. Who should I contact about a missing MOT test?

Start by checking the official MOT history and gathering evidence of the missing test. The original MOT test centre may be able to confirm the inspection. In Great Britain, DVSA oversees MOT testing, and the appropriate official reporting procedure should be followed for unresolved issues.

8. Can DVLA correct a missing MOT test?

DVLA deals with vehicle registration and licensing records, while DVSA oversees MOT testing in Great Britain. A missing MOT test should be investigated through the appropriate MOT record process. Incorrect registration information may require a separate DVLA enquiry.

9. Does a missing historical MOT test invalidate my current MOT?

No, a missing historical entry does not automatically invalidate a current MOT. Check the vehicle's current MOT status separately using GOV.UK. If the latest successful test is missing or the validity is uncertain, investigate before ordinary road use.

10. Can I drive if my MOT test is not showing online?

Driving legality depends on whether the vehicle currently requires and has a valid MOT, whether an exemption applies and whether the vehicle is roadworthy. A missing older entry does not automatically prohibit driving. If current MOT validity is uncertain, confirm it before driving.

11. How long does it take to correct a missing MOT record?

There is no single guaranteed correction time for every missing MOT record. The time required depends on the type of discrepancy, the available evidence and whether further investigation is necessary.

12. Should I buy a used car with missing MOT history?

A missing MOT entry does not automatically make a used car unsuitable. However, unexplained recent gaps, inconsistent mileage, vehicle identity discrepancies or uncertain current MOT validity should be investigated before purchase. Check the documents and consider an independent vehicle inspection.

Key Takeaways

What to Remember About Missing MOT Test Records

What You Should Do

  • ✓ Check the official MOT history.
  • ✓ Confirm the vehicle registration and identity.
  • ✓ Identify the specific missing test.
  • ✓ Gather certificates and supporting evidence.
  • ✓ Follow the appropriate official reporting process.

What You Should Never Assume

  • ✓ Every historical gap means a deleted test.
  • ✓ A missing older test invalidates the current MOT.
  • ✓ A garage receipt automatically proves MOT validity.
  • ✓ A registration change permanently removes MOT history.
  • ✓ An MOT pass guarantees ongoing roadworthiness.
Final MOT record advice

The most useful starting point is to establish whether a particular test is genuinely missing. Check the correct vehicle, compare official records with available paperwork and separate historical record problems from questions about current MOT validity.

Final Thoughts

Understanding and Resolving Missing MOT History

A complete and accurate MOT history helps drivers and used car buyers understand previous test results, mileage readings and recorded defects. When a test appears to be missing, it is important to investigate the evidence rather than immediately assuming that a record has been deleted.

Start with the official MOT history and current status services. Confirm the vehicle identity, identify the relevant test date and compare any available certificates or garage documents. If a genuine discrepancy remains, follow the official reporting procedure appropriate to the problem.
