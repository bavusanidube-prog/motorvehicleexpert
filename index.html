Diagnostic Process

Diagnose the Fault Before Replacing Parts

A fault code, warning light or symptom is the beginning of diagnosis, not proof that a particular component has failed. A proper diagnostic process compares the driver's symptoms with mechanical checks, electrical testing, scan-tool information and live vehicle data.

1. Confirm the symptom

Establish exactly when the fault occurs: cold or hot, idle or acceleration, low speed or motorway speed, uphill or under load.

2. Scan the vehicle

Read stored and pending fault codes and review available freeze-frame information before clearing anything.

3. Test the system

Check wiring, pressures, voltages, vacuum, mechanical condition and live-data values relevant to the suspected system.

4. Verify the repair

After repair, confirm that the original symptom is gone and recheck fault codes and system operation before considering the diagnosis complete.

Mechanic principle

The MOT assesses specific safety and environmental requirements at the time of the test. It does not confirm that every mechanical component is healthy or that the vehicle will remain fault-free until the next test.
