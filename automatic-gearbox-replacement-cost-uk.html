Quick Answer

How Much Does an Automatic Gearbox Replacement Cost in the UK?

A complete automatic gearbox replacement in the UK can cost from roughly £1,500–£3,000+ using some suitable used or lower-cost reconditioned units, while many professionally reconditioned fitted replacements can reach £2,500–£5,000+. Complex premium, DCT, DSG, CVT or new replacement transmissions can exceed £4,000–£6,000+.

These are broad planning ranges rather than fixed quotations. Transmission design, vehicle make and model, unit availability, labour time, fluid quantity, coding, adaptations and additional damage can move the final price substantially.

What Does a Complete Automatic Gearbox Replacement Cost Include?

The price of replacing an automatic gearbox is not simply the cost of purchasing another transmission. The final fitted bill can include the gearbox itself, removal of the failed unit, installation, transmission fluid, filters, seals, programming, adaptations and sometimes additional cooling-system or torque-converter work.

This is why two quotations for what appears to be the same gearbox replacement can differ by thousands of pounds. One may quote only the replacement unit, while another includes the complete job required to return the vehicle to service.

How Much Labour Is Involved in Replacing an Automatic Gearbox?

Gearbox replacement is labour intensive because the transmission must be disconnected from the engine and drivetrain and removed from the vehicle before another unit can be installed.

Labour time varies with vehicle design. Front-wheel-drive cars, four-wheel-drive systems, large SUVs and tightly packaged premium vehicles can require significantly different procedures.

How Much Does a Conventional Automatic Gearbox Cost to Replace?

Conventional automatic transmissions use hydraulic pressure, planetary gearsets, internal clutch packs and a torque converter. Replacement cost varies enormously because a relatively simple automatic fitted to a common family car is very different from a modern eight, nine or ten-speed transmission used in a premium or high-performance vehicle.

Before replacing the complete gearbox, the diagnosis should establish whether the fault is actually inside the transmission. Low fluid, external leaks, valve-body faults, solenoids, electrical problems and torque-converter faults can sometimes be repaired without replacing the entire unit.

Diagnose Before Spending

How Should an Automatic Gearbox Be Diagnosed Before Replacement?

Complete gearbox replacement should be the result of diagnosis, not the starting assumption. The purpose of testing is to determine whether the complete transmission is genuinely beyond economical repair or whether a smaller electrical, hydraulic, fluid or mechanical fault is responsible.

1. Confirm the Complaint

Establish exactly what the gearbox does, when it happens and whether the fault changes between cold and hot operation.

2. Identify the Gearbox

Confirm the transmission code and design before applying service data, fluid specifications or replacement options.

3. Scan All Relevant Modules

Record transmission and powertrain fault codes before anything is cleared.

4. Check Live Data

Compare requested and actual operation, temperatures, speed signals, ratios and other available transmission data.

5. Check Fluid & Leaks

Verify fluid level and condition using the correct procedure and investigate any evidence of external leakage.

6. Road Test

Reproduce delayed engagement, slipping, shuddering, harsh shifts or abnormal noises safely while observing transmission behaviour.

7. Test the Suspected System

Electrical, hydraulic or mechanical testing should follow the evidence rather than replacing components by guesswork.

8. Compare Repair Options

Once the fault is proven, compare targeted repair, rebuilding, reconditioned replacement and complete new-unit replacement.

One gearbox fault code is not proof that the complete transmission has failed

Fault codes describe conditions detected by the control system. Wiring faults, low voltage, incorrect fluid level, sensor problems, solenoid faults and hydraulic-pressure issues can all affect gearbox operation. Major replacement should be supported by diagnostic evidence.

Repair Decision

Should You Repair or Replace an Automatic Gearbox?

The best option depends on the confirmed fault, the condition of the existing transmission, the cost of repair, the quality of available replacement units and the value and overall condition of the vehicle.

Replacement should not automatically be treated as the default solution. If the original gearbox has an isolated valve-body, mechatronic, torque-converter, sensor or external fluid fault, a targeted repair may cost substantially less than replacing the whole unit.

Repair Existing Gearbox

Repair May Make More Sense When:

  • ✓ The fault has been clearly identified
  • ✓ Internal hard parts are still serviceable
  • ✓ Contamination is limited
  • ✓ Valve-body, converter or mechatronic repair is possible
  • ✓ The specialist offers a worthwhile warranty
Replace Gearbox

Replacement May Make More Sense When:

  • ✓ Multiple internal components are badly damaged
  • ✓ Metal or friction debris has circulated widely
  • ✓ Rebuild cost approaches replacement cost
  • ✓ A strong reconditioned replacement is available
  • ✓ Previous repair attempts have repeatedly failed

Compare the replacement cost with the vehicle's realistic market value, overall condition, maintenance history and the cost of buying another vehicle of similar quality.

The MOT is not a full assessment of internal automatic gearbox condition. Testers do not dismantle the transmission or decide whether the gearbox should be repaired, rebuilt or replaced.

However, related defects can still be relevant. Significant fluid leakage, insecure drivetrain components or other applicable faults may affect the test depending on their condition and severity.

Frequently Asked Questions

Automatic Gearbox Replacement Cost FAQs

These answers cover the most common questions UK drivers ask when a garage recommends replacing an automatic transmission.

How much does an automatic gearbox replacement cost in the UK?

A complete automatic gearbox replacement can cost roughly £1,500–£3,000+ using some suitable used units, while many professionally reconditioned fitted transmissions can cost around £2,500–£5,000+. Complex premium, DCT, DSG, CVT or new replacement gearboxes can exceed £4,000–£7,000 depending on the vehicle and work required.

How much does a used automatic gearbox cost to fit?

A used automatic gearbox can commonly cost around £500–£1,800+ for the unit itself, with a fitted total of roughly £1,500–£3,000+ once labour, fluid, seals and any necessary programming are included. Exact prices depend on the vehicle and gearbox.

How much does a reconditioned automatic gearbox cost?

A reconditioned automatic gearbox may cost around £1,500–£3,500+ as a supplied unit, while a professionally fitted replacement can commonly reach around £2,500–£5,000+ depending on the gearbox, vehicle, installation work and warranty.

How much does a new automatic gearbox cost?

A new automatic gearbox can cost around £3,000–£6,000+ before fitting on some vehicles, while a complete fitted job can reach £4,000–£7,000+ or more. Premium and specialist transmissions can exceed these broad planning ranges.

What should be included in a gearbox replacement quote?

A complete quote should state the condition of the replacement gearbox and whether removal, installation, correct transmission fluid, filters, seals, programming, adaptations, VAT and warranty are included. Torque-converter and cooler-system work should also be clarified where relevant.

How much does the labour cost to replace an automatic gearbox?

Automatic gearbox removal and installation can cost roughly £500–£1,500+ depending on vehicle layout, drivetrain design, gearbox size and labour rate. Complex premium or specialist vehicles can cost more.

Does a replacement automatic gearbox need programming?

Some replacement automatic transmissions or associated control modules require coding, programming, basic settings or adaptation procedures before normal operation. The requirements depend on the particular gearbox and vehicle.

Does a replacement gearbox need new transmission fluid?

Yes, the replacement transmission must be filled to the correct level using the fluid specification and procedure required for that gearbox. Filters, sump assemblies and installation seals may also need replacement depending on the design.

Is a used automatic gearbox worth fitting?

A used gearbox can make financial sense on an older or lower-value vehicle, but its mileage, servicing history and internal wear may be uncertain. Compatibility and warranty should be checked, and the risk of paying installation labour again if it fails should be considered.

Is a reconditioned gearbox better than a used gearbox?

A properly reconditioned gearbox can offer greater certainty because it should have been dismantled, inspected and rebuilt to a defined standard. A used gearbox is normally installed with its existing internal wear, so warranty and remaining life can be less predictable.

How much does a DCT or DSG gearbox replacement cost?

A used DCT or DSG replacement may cost around £1,500–£3,500+ fitted, while a reconditioned unit can reach around £2,500–£5,000+. New or particularly complex dual-clutch transmissions can exceed £4,000–£7,000 depending on the vehicle.

How much does a CVT gearbox replacement cost?

A used CVT may cost around £1,500–£3,500+ fitted, while a professionally reconditioned replacement can reach around £2,500–£5,000+. New CVTs and complex installations can exceed £4,000–£7,000 depending on the vehicle and transmission.

Should I use a main dealer or gearbox specialist for replacement?

A main dealer can offer manufacturer software, technical support and access to new genuine units, while an automatic gearbox specialist may offer rebuilding and reconditioned replacement options. Compare the diagnosis, replacement specification, complete fitted price and warranty.

What warranty should a replacement automatic gearbox have?

Warranty terms vary, so check the written duration, mileage limit, parts coverage, labour coverage, servicing requirements and exclusions. Labour coverage is particularly important because removing and refitting an automatic gearbox can itself be expensive.

Does a slipping automatic gearbox always need replacing?

No. Slipping can result from low fluid, hydraulic-pressure problems, valve-body faults, torque-converter issues or internal clutch wear. The actual cause should be diagnosed before complete gearbox replacement is authorised.

Is it worth replacing an automatic gearbox on an older car?

It can be. Compare the replacement cost with the vehicle's realistic value, overall condition, known maintenance history, replacement warranty and the cost and risk of buying another vehicle. Market value alone should not be the only deciding factor.

Will needing a replacement automatic gearbox fail an MOT?

The MOT is not a complete assessment of internal automatic gearbox condition, so needing gearbox repair or replacement does not by itself describe the MOT outcome. Related defects such as significant fluid leakage or applicable drivetrain defects can still be MOT-relevant.

Should I buy a used car that needs a replacement gearbox?

Only if the purchase price leaves sufficient margin for a properly diagnosed and fully fitted gearbox replacement, including labour, fluid, programming and warranty. If the deal only makes sense using the cheapest possible gearbox estimate, the financial risk is high.
