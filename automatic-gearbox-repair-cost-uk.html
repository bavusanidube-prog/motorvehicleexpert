Automatic Gearbox Repair Cost UK

Automatic gearbox repair costs in the UK can range from a relatively inexpensive fluid, seal or electrical repair to several thousand pounds for major internal damage. This guide explains what different automatic transmission faults can cost, why quotes vary so widely, when specialist diagnosis is worthwhile and how to decide whether repairing the gearbox makes financial sense.

Quick answer

How much does an automatic gearbox repair cost in the UK?

There is no single automatic gearbox repair price because the same symptom can be caused by anything from low or contaminated transmission fluid to major internal mechanical damage. A minor service, seal or electrical repair may cost a few hundred pounds, while valve-body, mechatronic, torque-converter and internal transmission repairs can move well into four figures. A complete rebuild or replacement can cost several thousand pounds on some vehicles.

Minor gearbox work

Often hundreds, not thousands

Fluid servicing, some external seals, wiring faults, sensors and relatively accessible components can be substantially cheaper than dismantling or replacing the transmission.

Specialist component repair

Can reach four figures
Specialist diagnosis

Valve bodies, mechatronic units, torque converters and some clutch-related automatic transmission faults can involve expensive parts, specialist labour, programming or gearbox removal.

Major internal failure

Potentially several thousand pounds
Compare repair options

Severe clutch-pack, bearing, gear, belt, chain or internal hydraulic damage can require dismantling, rebuilding or replacing the transmission. At this stage the value of the vehicle becomes an important part of the decision.

Mechanic insight:

The most important number is not the first repair estimate — it is the cost of fixing the fault that has actually been proven. A proper gearbox diagnosis may include a road test, diagnostic fault codes, live transmission data, fluid level and condition checks, leak inspection and manufacturer-specific testing before major parts are authorised.

Why online gearbox prices vary so much

A quote described simply as a “gearbox repair” may refer to very different work. One vehicle may only need servicing or an external repair, while another needs the transmission removed and rebuilt. Gearbox design, vehicle make and model, parts availability, programming requirements, labour time and the amount of internal damage all affect the final bill.

Professional Diagnosis

How Should an Automatic Gearbox Be Diagnosed Before Repair?

A major automatic gearbox repair should follow evidence. The aim is to identify whether the fault is electrical, fluid-related, hydraulic, converter-related, clutch-related or mechanical before expensive parts are authorised.

1. Confirm Symptoms

Establish whether the fault occurs cold, hot, under load, during a particular shift or only when selecting Drive or Reverse.

2. Read Fault Codes

Scan the transmission and related powertrain modules before codes or adaptation data are cleared.

3. Check Live Data

Compare requested gear, actual ratio, speeds, temperatures, converter slip and pressure-related information where available.

4. Inspect Fluid

Check the correct fluid level and assess condition according to the gearbox manufacturer's procedure.

5. Check for Leaks

Trace any fluid loss before assuming the transmission simply needs topping up.

6. Road Test

Reproduce the reported fault safely while monitoring transmission operation.

7. Electrical Tests

Verify wiring, connectors, supply voltage, grounds and control circuits where the evidence points to an electrical problem.

8. Hydraulic / Internal Tests

Move toward pressure testing, valve-body diagnosis or internal inspection only when the earlier evidence supports that route.

Never approve a £3,000 gearbox because one fault code named a solenoid

Fault codes identify the circuit or operating condition detected by the control system. They do not automatically prove which physical component has failed. Electrical, hydraulic and mechanical testing should support a major repair decision.

Repair Decision

Should You Repair or Replace an Automatic Gearbox?

An automatic gearbox fault does not automatically mean the complete transmission should be replaced. In many cases the most economical repair is to correct the specific failed component while retaining the rest of the gearbox.

Replacement becomes more attractive when there is extensive internal damage, multiple worn components, severe contamination, an uneconomical rebuild quotation or a suitable replacement unit with a strong warranty.

Repair Existing Gearbox

Repair May Make More Sense When:

  • ✓ Internal mechanical condition is otherwise good
  • ✓ The gearbox has not been heavily contaminated
  • ✓ A specialist can repair the original unit
  • ✓ The repair carries a worthwhile warranty

  • ✓ Metal or friction debris has circulated through the gearbox
  • ✓ Previous repairs have repeatedly failed
  • ✓ Rebuilding costs approach replacement cost

Financial Decision

Is an Automatic Gearbox Worth Repairing?

Whether a gearbox repair makes financial sense depends on more than the repair bill alone. The condition and value of the entire vehicle should be considered alongside the cost of replacing it with another car of comparable quality.

A £2,500 gearbox repair can make sense on a well-maintained vehicle worth considerably more than the repair, while the same bill may be difficult to justify on an older car that also needs major suspension, corrosion, engine or emissions work.

Before paying for a major gearbox repair, check whether the vehicle, transmission or previous repair is still covered by any applicable manufacturer, dealer, aftermarket or repair warranty.

Coverage varies significantly. Some policies include internal transmission components but exclude wear items, diagnostic charges, fluids, consequential damage or faults linked to missed servicing. Read the actual terms rather than assuming “gearbox cover” means every transmission repair will be paid for.

Keep the Invoice
Repair-Specific Protection

If the gearbox was previously rebuilt or repaired, check the original invoice and written warranty before authorising further work elsewhere.

Contact the warranty provider before authorising major work

Some warranty providers require prior approval, specific diagnostic evidence or inspection before repairs begin. Authorising dismantling or replacement first can complicate a later claim.

Will normal car insurance pay for an automatic gearbox repair?

Ordinary motor insurance is generally intended for insured events rather than routine mechanical wear or breakdown. Whether gearbox damage is covered depends on the circumstances and the wording of the individual policy. For example, transmission damage associated with a covered accident is a different situation from a gearbox that has simply worn internally.

Keep evidence of servicing and diagnosis

Service invoices, gearbox-fluid records, diagnostic reports and previous repair documentation can be useful when warranty coverage depends on maintenance history or the cause of failure.

UK MOT

Will an Automatic Gearbox Problem Fail an MOT?

The MOT is not a general assessment of automatic gearbox condition, and the test does not dismantle the transmission or judge whether a gearbox is financially worth repairing.

However, a vehicle with gearbox trouble can also have defects that fall within areas inspected during the MOT. A serious fluid leak, insecure drivetrain component or another applicable defect may still affect the test depending on its condition and the relevant inspection criteria.

An MOT pass does not prove the automatic gearbox is healthy

A vehicle can pass an MOT and still have expensive transmission problems such as slipping, delayed engagement, shuddering, valve-body wear or internal gearbox damage. Treat MOT condition and gearbox condition as separate assessments.

Used-Car Buying Risk

Should You Buy a Used Car That Needs an Automatic Gearbox Repair?

A used car with a known automatic gearbox fault can sometimes be bought cheaply enough to justify repair, but it is one of the higher-risk used-car purchases because the final repair cost may not be known until proper diagnosis or dismantling has taken place.

Statements such as “probably only needs a sensor”, “just needs gearbox oil” or “easy solenoid fix” should not be treated as a diagnosis unless there is evidence supporting them.

Before Buying a Car With a Suspected Automatic Gearbox Fault

  • ✓ Identify the exact gearbox fitted to the vehicle.
  • ✓ Ask when the fault first appeared and whether it is getting worse.
  • ✓ Test the vehicle from genuinely cold where possible.
  • ✓ Check Drive and Reverse engagement.
  • ✓ Check light-throttle and moderate-load shifts.
  • ✓ Look for RPM flare, shuddering, harsh shifts and unusual noises.
  • ✓ Inspect underneath for transmission-fluid leakage.
  • ✓ Scan the transmission control system before codes are cleared.
  • ✓ Ask for gearbox servicing and previous repair invoices.
  • ✓ Obtain a specialist repair estimate before agreeing the purchase price.
Price the car assuming the fault may be worse than the seller claims

If the purchase only makes financial sense when the gearbox requires the cheapest possible repair, the risk is high. Allow enough margin for diagnosis and a substantially more expensive repair outcome.

Frequently Asked Questions

Automatic Gearbox Repair Cost FAQs

These answers cover the most common questions UK drivers ask before approving automatic transmission diagnosis, repair or rebuilding.

How much does an automatic gearbox repair cost in the UK?

Automatic gearbox repair cost varies from a few hundred pounds for some fluid, leak, electrical or serviceable component repairs to several thousand pounds for major internal damage, rebuilding or replacement. The exact cost depends on the gearbox type, confirmed fault, labour required and parts needed.

How much does automatic gearbox diagnosis cost?

A basic diagnostic scan may cost around £50–£100+, while more detailed specialist diagnosis can commonly cost around £80–£200+ or more where extended road testing, electrical work, hydraulic testing or manufacturer-specific procedures are needed.

How much does an automatic gearbox fluid service cost?

A basic automatic gearbox fluid change may cost around £150–£300+, while a service involving specialist fluid, a filter or integrated sump assembly may cost around £250–£500+ depending on the transmission.

How much does an automatic gearbox oil leak cost to repair?

Accessible sump, pipe or external seal leaks may cost a few hundred pounds to repair, while leaks requiring driveshaft or gearbox removal can cost considerably more. The bill can rise sharply if low fluid has already caused internal transmission damage.

How much does an automatic gearbox solenoid repair cost?

An individually serviceable solenoid repair may cost around £250–£700+, while larger solenoid packs or hydraulic assemblies can cost around £500–£1,200+ or more. Wiring and hydraulic faults should be ruled out before a solenoid is replaced.

How much does an automatic gearbox valve body repair cost?

Valve-body repairs can commonly cost around £500–£1,200+, while a replacement assembly can cost around £800–£1,800+ depending on the transmission, electronics, solenoids and any programming or adaptation required.

How much does a torque converter repair cost?

Torque-converter work can commonly cost around £700–£1,500+, with the total increasing where gearbox removal, cooler work, contamination or additional transmission repairs are required.

How much does a gearbox mechatronic repair cost?

A repairable mechatronic unit may cost around £600–£1,500+ to repair, while replacement can cost around £1,000–£2,500+ or more. Programming, adaptations and transmission-specific procedures can add to the final bill.

How much does a DSG or DCT gearbox repair cost?

DCT or DSG repair costs vary widely. Diagnosis and servicing may cost a few hundred pounds, clutch or mechatronic repairs can move into four figures, and significant internal damage can cost £1,500–£4,000+ or more depending on the gearbox and vehicle.

How much does a CVT gearbox repair cost?

CVT servicing or relatively minor hydraulic repairs may cost hundreds of pounds, while valve-body, bearing, belt, chain, pulley or major internal repairs can cost £1,000–£4,000+ or more depending on the transmission.

How much does an automatic gearbox rebuild cost?

A complete automatic gearbox rebuild commonly moves into the £1,500–£4,000+ range, while complex or premium transmissions can exceed £3,000–£6,000. The exact price depends on which internal parts are damaged and what the rebuild quotation includes.

Is a reconditioned automatic gearbox cheaper than a new gearbox?

It often can be. A professionally reconditioned transmission may avoid the cost of a brand-new unit, but installation, fluid, programming and other associated work must still be included when comparing quotations.

Should I use a main dealer or automatic gearbox specialist?

Main dealers can be useful for warranty, software, recall and manufacturer-specific support, while gearbox specialists may offer internal rebuilding, valve-body, torque-converter and reconditioning options. Compare the diagnosis, repair scope and warranty rather than labour rate alone.

Does a slipping automatic gearbox always need rebuilding?

No. Slipping can result from low fluid, pressure-control faults, valve-body problems, torque-converter issues or internal clutch wear. The gearbox should be diagnosed before a complete rebuild is authorised.

Is it worth repairing an automatic gearbox on an older car?

It can be. Consider the vehicle's realistic value, overall condition, repair warranty, future maintenance requirements and the cost and risk of buying a replacement car. Vehicle value alone should not be the only deciding factor.

Can an automatic gearbox repair be covered by a warranty?

It may be covered by a manufacturer, dealer, aftermarket or previous repair warranty depending on the terms. Check claim limits, exclusions, servicing requirements and approval procedures before authorising major work.

Will an automatic gearbox problem fail an MOT?

The MOT is not a complete automatic gearbox health assessment, so slipping or poor shift quality does not by itself describe an MOT result. However, related defects such as significant fluid leakage or applicable transmission and driveline defects can still be MOT-relevant.

Should I buy a used car that needs an automatic gearbox repair?

Only if the purchase price leaves enough margin for a professionally diagnosed repair and a more expensive outcome than the seller predicts. A car that only makes financial sense if the cheapest possible gearbox repair solves it is a high-risk purchase.
