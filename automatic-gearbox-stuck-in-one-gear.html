Automatic Gearbox Stuck in One Gear: Causes, Limp Mode, Diagnosis & Repair Costs UK

If your automatic gearbox stays in one fixed gear and refuses normal upshifts or downshifts, the transmission may be operating in a protective limp or fail-safe mode, or it may have an electrical, hydraulic or internal fault that physically prevents another ratio being selected. The exact gear, warning messages, restart behaviour and temperature pattern can quickly narrow the cause.

Why Is My Automatic Gearbox Stuck in One Gear?

An automatic gearbox that stays in one fixed ratio is often operating in limp or fail-safe mode. The transmission control module can deliberately restrict the gearbox to one usable gear after detecting a fault with speed information, hydraulic pressure, solenoids, gear-ratio monitoring or another important control system.

However, fixed-ratio operation is not always electronic protection. A valve-body fault, low fluid, failed solenoid, internal pressure loss or damaged clutch or geartrain can also prevent the gearbox from achieving another ratio.

Workshop diagnostic process

How Should an Automatic Gearbox Stuck in One Gear Be Diagnosed?

A proper diagnosis should establish whether the gearbox is deliberately operating in fail-safe or is mechanically unable to achieve another ratio. That distinction should be made before expensive gearbox parts are replaced.

1. Confirm the Fixed-Gear Symptom

Verify that the transmission genuinely remains in one ratio rather than simply missing one individual shift.

2. Identify the Gearbox Type

Confirm conventional automatic, DCT, CVT or another transmission design.

3. Record Which Gear Is Active

Use road behaviour and live data to determine the fixed ratio.

4. Check Warning Messages

Note gearbox warnings, flashing selector displays and other dashboard messages before switching the car off.

5. Scan Before Clearing Codes

Read transmission, engine and network faults while the evidence is still available.

6. Check Restart Behaviour

Record whether ignition cycling temporarily restores normal shifting.

7. Compare Cold and Hot

Reproduce the fault under the same temperature conditions reported by the driver.

8. Check Battery & Charging Voltage

Confirm stable system voltage before chasing electronic transmission faults.

9. Inspect Fluid & Leaks

Verify fluid level, condition and specification using the correct manufacturer procedure.

10. Review Speed-Sensor Data

Compare engine, gearbox input and gearbox output speeds for plausibility.

11. Compare Requested vs Actual Gear

Determine whether the TCM is deliberately requesting the fixed gear or unsuccessfully asking for another ratio.

12. Check Selector / Range Data

Confirm that the control module sees the correct driver range and manual-mode requests.

13. Test Solenoid Commands

Confirm electrical command and hydraulic response where suitable test procedures are available.

14. Assess Valve-Body / Pressure Operation

Prove whether hydraulic pressure is reaching the clutch circuit needed for the missing ratio.

15. Assess Internal Gearbox Condition

Only after control and hydraulic faults are excluded should internal clutch, seal or geartrain damage be condemned.

The final diagnosis should answer one key question

Is the transmission staying in one gear because the TCM has chosen that gear for protection, or because the gearbox cannot physically achieve another ratio? That answer determines the next repair step.

Safety decision

Is It Safe to Drive With an Automatic Gearbox Stuck in One Gear?

It depends on which ratio the gearbox is stuck in, whether the car can accelerate predictably, and whether there are additional symptoms such as slipping, overheating, severe banging or loss of drive.

A fixed emergency ratio may allow limited movement to a safe place, but it should not be treated as normal driving. A high fixed gear can make pull-away dangerously slow, while a low fixed gear can force engine RPM too high at normal road speed.

UK MOT rules

Will an Automatic Gearbox Stuck in One Gear Fail an MOT?

A gearbox being stuck in one fixed ratio is not itself tested as a dedicated internal automatic gearbox-performance item during the MOT. The MOT does not assess TCM fail-safe strategy, solenoid timing, clutch-application logic or whether the transmission changes through all forward ratios.

However, related driveline, mounting and severe fluid-leak defects can still be MOT failures where they fall within the inspection.

Repair decision

Does a Gearbox Stuck in One Gear Need Replacing?

Not necessarily. Fixed-ratio operation often begins because the control system has detected a fault and deliberately entered fail-safe. The trigger may be a sensor, electrical circuit, solenoid or hydraulic problem rather than complete transmission failure.

Frequently Asked Questions

Automatic Gearbox Stuck in One Gear FAQs

Why is my automatic gearbox stuck in one gear?

Common causes include limp or fail-safe mode, input or output speed sensor faults, shift-solenoid problems, valve-body wear, selector faults, electrical issues, low gearbox fluid, hydraulic pressure loss and internal transmission damage.

Why is my automatic gearbox stuck in 2nd gear?

Some transmissions use 2nd gear as an emergency or fail-safe ratio after detecting a fault. A missing 2–3 shift caused by a solenoid, hydraulic or clutch problem can create a similar symptom.

Why is my automatic gearbox stuck in 3rd gear?

Some automatic transmissions use a middle ratio such as 3rd gear as a fallback gear in fail-safe mode. Speed-sensor, solenoid, valve-body and internal faults can all trigger this behaviour.

Why is my gearbox stuck in a high gear?

A high fixed ratio can result from fail-safe operation or a fault preventing lower gears from being selected. The car may feel very sluggish from rest because it has less mechanical advantage.

Why is my gearbox stuck in a low gear?

The transmission may be unable or unwilling to upshift because of limp mode, sensor faults, solenoids, valve-body problems or internal damage. Engine RPM can become unusually high as road speed rises.

Why does restarting the car fix the gearbox temporarily?

Switching the ignition off can reset the current fail-safe state and temporarily restore normal shifting. The original fault often remains stored and can trigger limp mode again.

Why does the gearbox get stuck in one gear only when hot?

Hot-only fixed-ratio faults can involve heat-sensitive solenoids, valve-body leakage, internal hydraulic leakage or protective strategies triggered by excessive transmission temperature.

Why does the gearbox get stuck in one gear when cold?

Cold-only faults can involve incorrect or degraded fluid, slow hydraulic response, sticky valves or electrical components that behave differently at low temperature.

Is a gearbox stuck in one gear always in limp mode?

No. Limp mode is common, but a hydraulic or mechanical fault can also stop the gearbox achieving another ratio even when the control module requests the shift.

Can a speed sensor leave the gearbox stuck in one gear?

Yes. The transmission control module uses input and output speed signals to calculate ratio and verify shifts. Missing or implausible data can trigger fixed-ratio fail-safe operation.

Can a shift solenoid cause the gearbox to stay in one gear?

Yes. A sticking or electrically faulty solenoid can prevent the hydraulic circuit required for another ratio from operating correctly and may also trigger limp mode.

Can valve-body wear cause fixed-gear operation?

Yes. Worn or sticking hydraulic valves can prevent the transmission applying the correct clutch, cause wrong-gear starts or trigger fail-safe operation.

Can low automatic gearbox fluid make it stay in one gear?

Yes. Low fluid can reduce hydraulic pressure, cause failed shifts and generate ratio faults severe enough to trigger protective fixed-gear operation.

Is it safe to drive with an automatic gearbox stuck in one gear?

Limited movement may be possible if the vehicle accelerates and decelerates predictably, but persistent fixed-ratio operation, excessive engine RPM, severe slipping, overheating or loss of drive are reasons to stop normal driving and arrange diagnosis or recovery.

Will an automatic gearbox stuck in one gear fail an MOT?

Fixed-ratio operation is not itself tested as a standalone internal automatic gearbox-performance item. However, serious transmission shaft, joint, bearing, mounting or excessive fluid-leak defects can fail the MOT.

Does a gearbox stuck in one gear need replacing?

Not necessarily. Sensor, wiring, solenoid and some valve-body faults can often be repaired without replacing the complete transmission. Replacement becomes more likely where severe internal damage is proven.

How much does it cost to fix a gearbox stuck in one gear in the UK?

Diagnosis may cost around £50–£120+, speed-sensor work around £100–£350+, selector faults around £120–£400+, solenoid repairs around £200–£700+ and valve-body repairs around £500–£1,500+. Internal gearbox rebuilds can exceed £1,200–£3,500.

What should a garage check before replacing a gearbox stuck in one gear?

Diagnosis should confirm the fixed ratio, read fault codes before clearing them, compare requested and actual gear, check restart and temperature behaviour, verify battery voltage, inspect fluid and leaks, review speed and selector data and test solenoid, valve-body and hydraulic operation before internal damage is condemned.
