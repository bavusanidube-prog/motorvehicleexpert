UK roadside safety and tyre changing guide

How to Change a Tyre Safely in the UK

A flat tyre can leave you stranded at the roadside, but changing a wheel is only safe in the right location. This complete UK guide explains where to stop, what equipment you need, how to fit a spare wheel correctly, when to call breakdown recovery and what the Highway Code says about motorway emergencies.

Critical roadside safety warning

Never attempt a wheel change on a motorway hard shoulder, emergency area or live traffic lane. Follow the Highway Code breakdown procedure and seek recovery assistance. On other roads, only attempt the job when you can park completely clear of moving traffic on firm, level ground and work without putting anyone at risk.

Quick Answer

How Do You Change a Car Tyre Safely?

If your car is parked in a genuinely safe, level location away from moving traffic, you can replace a damaged wheel with a compatible spare using the correct tools and manufacturer's procedure.

Secure the car, apply the parking brake, select Park or an appropriate gear, chock the wheels, loosen the wheel fixings slightly, lift the vehicle at its approved jacking point, remove the damaged wheel, fit the spare, lower the vehicle and tighten the fixings to the manufacturer's specified torque.

In everyday language this is called changing a tyre. Technically, the roadside procedure changes the complete wheel-and-tyre assembly. Removing a tyre from its wheel rim requires specialist equipment.

Where is it safe?

On firm, level ground, completely clear of moving traffic, with enough space to work safely.

Assess the location

Where is it unsafe?

Motorways, live traffic lanes, narrow verges, bends, blind hills and unstable ground.

Do not attempt

What is essential?

A compatible spare, correct jack, wheel brace, locking wheel nut key if fitted, and accurate torque information.

Check equipment
Mechanic's Tip

Before lifting the car, read the vehicle handbook. Jacking points, wheel-fixing arrangements, spare-wheel restrictions and tightening torque differ between models. Never rely on a generic torque figure or guess where the jack should go.

1. Choose a safe stopping place

Where Should You Stop If You Get a Flat Tyre?

The location determines whether a wheel change is sensible at all. A flat tyre does not justify putting yourself or your passengers in danger.

Stopping Location Safety Guide

Location Wheel change? Recommended action
Private driveway Potentially suitable Check that the surface is firm, level and unobstructed.
Quiet, level car park Potentially suitable Choose a space away from moving vehicles and pedestrians.
Residential street Only if genuinely safe Do not work in the traffic lane or where approaching vehicles create a risk.
Busy A-road Usually unsuitable Seek a safer location or breakdown recovery.
Blind bend or hill crest No Do not work where approaching drivers have restricted visibility.
Soft or sloping verge No Unstable ground can allow the jack or vehicle to move.
Motorway hard shoulder No Follow motorway breakdown guidance and call recovery.
Motorway emergency area No Use the emergency telephone and follow operator advice.
Motorway live lane Never Stay belted inside if you cannot safely get clear and call 999 for police.
Never Jack Up a Car on Unsafe Ground

Avoid slopes, loose gravel, soft soil, deep potholes and uneven surfaces. A vehicle can slip or fall from a jack, causing serious injury.

2. Safety before tools

Roadside Safety: Hazards, Passengers and Warning Triangles

If your vehicle is stopped because of a puncture, warn other road users where appropriate and assess whether everyone can remain safely away from traffic.

1. Stop Safely

Move completely clear of the road if possible. Do not stop where visibility is restricted.

2. Warn Other Drivers

Use hazard warning lights if the vehicle is causing an obstruction. Use sidelights when required in darkness or poor visibility.

3. Protect Passengers

Keep passengers away from moving traffic and the area where the wheel will be changed.

4. Assess the Risk

If you cannot work without entering the road or standing near moving vehicles, call recovery instead.

Should You Use a Warning Triangle?

Highway Code Rule 276 advises placing a warning triangle at least 45 metres behind a broken-down vehicle on the same side of the road where appropriate, but only if it is safe to place and retrieve it.

Never Use a Warning Triangle on a Motorway

Warning triangles must not be placed on motorways. Do not walk into traffic or expose yourself to danger to position one on another road.

High-visibility clothing can help other drivers see you, but it does not make an unsafe wheel-changing location safe.

3. Equipment

What Tools Do You Need to Change a Car Wheel?

Modern cars do not all carry the same puncture equipment. Some have a full-size spare wheel, some have a temporary space saver and others are supplied with a tyre repair kit.

Compatible Spare Wheel

A full-size or temporary spare approved for your vehicle.

Vehicle Jack

The manufacturer's approved jack, suitable for the vehicle and intended lifting point.

Wheel Brace

The correct socket or wheel brace for the wheel bolts or nuts.

Locking Wheel Nut Key

Required if the vehicle has locking wheel fixings.

Wheel Chocks

Suitable chocks to help prevent movement while the vehicle is lifted.

Torque Wrench

Needed to tighten wheel fixings accurately to the manufacturer's specification.

Where Is the Spare Wheel and Jack Stored?

Depending on the model, the spare and tools may be under the boot floor, behind a side panel or mounted beneath the vehicle. Some vehicles have no spare wheel at all.

Check the handbook before you need the equipment. Confirm that the jack, brace, locking key and spare-wheel retaining mechanism are present and working.

Mechanic's Tip

Check the locking wheel nut key when buying a used car. Missing keys can turn a straightforward wheel replacement into a recovery or specialist removal job.

4. Preparation

Before You Start Changing the Wheel

Only continue if the location is safe, the vehicle is stable and you are confident that the equipment is correct.

  • ✓Confirm that the spare wheel is approved for the vehicle.
  • ✓Check that the spare is not visibly damaged or flat.
  • ✓Read the vehicle's jacking and wheel-changing instructions.
  • ✓Check the required wheel-fixing torque.
  • ✓Confirm that the correct locking key is available.
  • ✓Make sure the ground is level, firm and clear of traffic.
  • ✓Keep passengers away from the work area.
  • ✓Do not proceed if the vehicle is unstable or the jack is damaged.
Important Jack Safety

A vehicle's emergency jack is designed for the specified wheel-changing procedure, not for working underneath the car. Never put any part of your body beneath a vehicle supported only by a jack.

5. Step-by-step procedure

How to Change a Car Wheel Safely: Step-by-Step

The following sequence applies to a conventional passenger car with a manufacturer-approved spare wheel and emergency jack. Always follow your vehicle's specific instructions where they differ.

Step 1: Secure the Vehicle

Switch off the engine, apply the parking brake and select Park on an automatic or the gear recommended in the handbook for a manual. Use suitable wheel chocks as instructed to prevent movement.

Step 2: Prepare the Wheel Fixings

Remove any wheel trim as instructed. Locate the locking wheel nut key if required. Confirm that the brace fits the wheel bolts or nuts correctly.

Step 3: Loosen the Fixings

With the tyre still on the ground, loosen each fixing slightly using the correct tool. Do not remove the bolts or nuts yet.

Step 4: Position the Jack

Locate the manufacturer's approved jacking point nearest the damaged wheel. Position the jack exactly as the handbook specifies.

Step 5: Lift the Vehicle

Raise the vehicle only enough to provide the clearance required to remove the damaged wheel and fit the spare. Stop if the jack tilts or the vehicle moves.

Step 6: Remove the Damaged Wheel

Remove the loosened fixings and carefully lift the wheel clear of the hub. Wheels can be heavy, so avoid straining your back or placing fingers beneath the tyre.

Step 7: Fit the Spare Wheel

Align the spare with the hub and fit the correct wheel fixings by hand first. Follow any special instructions for temporary spare wheels or different wheel-fixing designs.

Step 8: Tighten Lightly

Tighten the fixings evenly in the manufacturer's recommended sequence while the wheel remains lifted. Do not attempt final torque tightening with the wheel freely rotating.

Step 9: Lower the Vehicle

Lower the vehicle carefully until the tyre is supporting the vehicle as required for final tightening. Remove the jack when the vehicle is fully supported.

Step 10: Torque the Fixings

Use a suitable torque wrench and the manufacturer's specified tightening sequence and torque. Never substitute an assumed universal figure.

Step 11: Check the Spare

Check inflation pressure, visible condition, clearance and any temporary spare restrictions. If you cannot confirm the wheel is safe, arrange assistance.

Step 12: Stow Everything

Secure the damaged wheel, jack, tools, locking key and wheel chocks so they cannot move around inside the vehicle.

Why Should You Loosen the Wheel Nuts Before Jacking?

Loosening the fixings while the tyre is on the ground helps prevent the wheel rotating when force is applied. Attempting to loosen tight fixings after lifting can disturb the vehicle and increase the risk of it slipping from the jack.

How Tight Should the Wheel Nuts Be?

The correct torque depends on the vehicle, wheel and fixing specification. Use the manufacturer's figure and the appropriate torque wrench.

Do Not Guess the Tightening Torque

Under-tightened fixings can loosen. Over-tightening can damage threads, wheel components or fixings. If the correct torque cannot be achieved or verified, do not assume the wheel is safe for normal driving.

Mechanic's Tip

Start wheel bolts or nuts by hand to reduce the risk of cross-threading. If a fixing does not turn smoothly, stop and investigate rather than forcing it with the wheel brace.

6. Motorway emergency

Flat Tyre on a UK Motorway: What Should You Do?

A motorway puncture is a breakdown safety situation, not a normal wheel-changing opportunity. High-speed traffic makes even simple repairs dangerous.

Motorway Breakdown Procedure

1. Go Left

If possible, leave the motorway at the next exit or enter a service area. Otherwise, move into an emergency area or hard shoulder if you can do so safely.

2. Warn Traffic

Switch on hazard warning lights. If stopped at the left, position the vehicle as far left as possible and turn the wheels left as advised by the Highway Code.

3. Get Safe

If safe, exit on the side away from traffic. Move behind a safety barrier where available, checking for drops and other hazards.

4. Get Help

Contact breakdown recovery or use the emergency telephone. Stay well away from moving traffic and do not attempt repairs.

What If You Cannot Reach the Hard Shoulder?

If you cannot reach a place of relative safety and cannot exit the vehicle safely to get well away from moving traffic, remain inside with your seat belt fastened, keep hazard lights on and call 999 immediately for the police.

What If You Stop in a Motorway Emergency Area?

Use the emergency telephone and follow the operator's instructions. Under Highway Code Rule 278, drivers leaving an emergency area must use the emergency telephone and follow the operator's advice before rejoining the motorway.

Motorway Rules: Never Attempt a Wheel Change

Do not change a wheel on the hard shoulder, in an emergency area or in a live lane. Do not place a warning triangle on a motorway. Never retrieve debris from the carriageway yourself.

Official guidance: The Highway Code — Breakdowns and Incidents, Rules 275–280.

7. Spare wheel options

Full-Size Spare vs Space Saver vs Tyre Repair Kit

Not every spare-wheel solution allows the same driving speed or distance. Before travelling, identify exactly what equipment your vehicle has.

Spare Wheel and Repair Kit Comparison

Equipment Main purpose Important restriction
Full-size spare Replaces the damaged wheel. Must be compatible, correctly inflated and approved for the vehicle.
Temporary space saver Short-term mobility to reach assistance. Observe its marked speed limit, vehicle instructions and permitted usage.
Tyre repair kit Temporarily seals certain punctures and inflates the tyre. Not suitable for every puncture, especially major damage or sidewall damage.
No spare or repair kit Requires alternative assistance. Arrange recovery or another suitable solution.

How Fast Can You Drive on a Space Saver?

Many temporary spare wheels are marked with a maximum speed of 50 mph (80 km/h), but you must follow the exact limit printed on the wheel and stated in your vehicle handbook.

A space saver can alter handling, braking and stability-system behaviour. It should only be used within the manufacturer's restrictions and replaced with a suitable normal wheel as soon as practical.

When Will a Tyre Repair Kit Not Work?

Repair kits generally have limitations. A large cut, sidewall damage, damaged wheel rim or tyre that has been driven on while severely underinflated may make repair unsuitable.

Mechanic's Tip

A tyre inflated with sealant still needs professional assessment. Tell the tyre technician that sealant was used, because it can affect inspection and repair procedures.

9. Aftercare

What Should You Check After Changing a Wheel?

The job is not complete simply because the spare wheel is fitted. Incorrect tyre pressure, loose fixings or overlooked spare-wheel restrictions can create further problems.

Check Tyre Pressure

Inflate the spare to the manufacturer's specified pressure. Temporary spares may require a substantially different pressure from normal tyres.

Confirm Wheel Torque

Ensure all fixings have been tightened to the correct specification with suitable equipment.

Follow Spare Restrictions

Observe any maximum speed, distance or vehicle-specific restrictions.

Arrange Tyre Inspection

Have the damaged tyre professionally assessed for repair or replacement as soon as practical.

Should Wheel Nuts Be Retightened After 50 Miles?

Some wheel-fitting procedures and tyre specialists recommend checking wheel-fixing torque after approximately 50 miles, while others specify different intervals or procedures. Follow the vehicle manufacturer's requirements and the fitting professional's instructions.

A later torque check is not a substitute for correctly tightening the wheel before driving. The wheel must be properly secured from the outset.

What If the Car Vibrates After Fitting the Spare?

Stop safely and arrange inspection if you experience severe vibration, abnormal noises, pulling or other unexpected behaviour. Do not assume that the symptoms are normal simply because a temporary spare has been fitted.

Mechanic's Tip

Once the original wheel is repaired or replaced, check that the spare, jack, locking key and wheel brace are returned to their proper storage positions. This avoids discovering missing equipment during the next puncture.

10. UK legal and MOT advice

Can a Damaged Tyre or Incorrect Spare Wheel Fail an MOT?

Tyres are assessed against applicable MOT requirements covering tread depth, condition, suitability and other relevant defects. A damaged or unsuitable tyre can result in an MOT failure.

For most passenger cars, the legal minimum tread depth is 1.6 mm across the central three-quarters of the tread around its entire circumference.

Tyre Problems and MOT Relevance

Tyre condition Potential issue Action
Tread below legal minimum Illegal tyre condition and MOT failure. Replace before road use.
Exposed cords Serious safety defect. Do not drive; arrange replacement or recovery.
Bulge or serious structural damage Potentially dangerous tyre failure risk. Arrange professional assessment without further road use if unsafe.
Incorrect wheel or tyre fitment May create clearance, compatibility or roadworthiness problems. Confirm manufacturer approval and correct fitment.
Temporary spare fitted Subject to applicable vehicle and MOT requirements. Follow manufacturer restrictions and restore the correct normal wheel promptly.
MOT Safety Reminder

Passing an MOT does not guarantee that a tyre remains safe until the next test. Drivers must maintain roadworthy tyres between inspections.

Frequently Asked Questions

How to Change a Tyre Safely: FAQs

Can I change a tyre on a UK motorway hard shoulder?

No. Do not attempt roadside repairs on a motorway hard shoulder. Follow the Highway Code breakdown procedure and contact recovery.

Can I change a tyre in a motorway emergency area?

No. Move to safety, use the emergency telephone and follow the operator's instructions. Do not attempt a wheel change in the emergency area.

Where is the safest place to change a car wheel?

A firm, level location completely away from moving traffic, such as a suitable private driveway or quiet car park, provided there is enough space and the vehicle can be secured.

Should I loosen wheel nuts before jacking up the car?

Yes, where instructed by the manufacturer. Loosen the wheel fixings slightly while the tyre is still on the ground, but do not remove them until the vehicle has been safely lifted.

Where should I place the jack?

Use only the manufacturer's approved jacking point and follow the vehicle handbook. Incorrect positioning can damage the car or allow it to slip from the jack.

How tight should wheel nuts be?

Wheel bolts or nuts must be tightened to the vehicle manufacturer's specified torque using suitable equipment. There is no single correct torque for every car.

Do I need to retighten wheel nuts after 50 miles?

Some manufacturers and tyre-fitting procedures recommend a torque check after approximately 50 miles, while others specify different requirements. Follow the relevant instructions. The wheel must be correctly tightened before the vehicle is driven.

How fast can I drive on a space saver?

Many temporary spare wheels have a maximum speed of 50 mph, but always follow the restriction marked on the wheel and specified in your vehicle handbook.

Can I use a warning triangle on a motorway?

No. Warning triangles must not be placed on motorways. On other roads, follow Highway Code guidance and only use one when it is safe to place and retrieve.

What if I cannot find my locking wheel nut key?

Do not force the wheel fixing or damage the wheel. Contact breakdown recovery or a suitable specialist for assistance.

Can a tyre repair kit fix a sidewall puncture?

Most emergency sealant kits are not suitable for sidewall damage. Follow the kit instructions and arrange professional tyre assessment or recovery where necessary.

Can I change a wheel on a sloping road?

No. Sloping or unstable ground can allow the vehicle or jack to move. Find a safe, level location or arrange recovery.

Can a damaged tyre fail an MOT?

Yes. Tyres with insufficient tread, exposed cords, serious damage or other applicable defects can fail an MOT. The vehicle must remain roadworthy between tests.

What should I do if I get a puncture in a motorway live lane?

If you cannot reach a place of relative safety and cannot exit safely to get well away from traffic, remain inside with your seat belt on, keep hazard lights on and call 999 immediately for the police.

Final Thoughts

Knowing When Not to Change a Wheel Is Just as Important

Changing a wheel is a useful practical skill, but it must never take priority over roadside safety. A suitable stopping location, correct tools, compatible spare wheel and proper tightening procedure are essential.

On motorways and other dangerous roads, the safer decision is to follow official breakdown guidance and obtain professional assistance rather than attempting repairs beside moving traffic.

Before your next journey, check whether your car has a spare wheel, space saver or repair kit. Confirm that the locking wheel nut key is present and that you know where the manufacturer's jacking points are located.
