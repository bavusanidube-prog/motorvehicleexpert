Do MOT Advisories Affect Insurance?

An MOT advisory does not automatically invalidate your car insurance. However, advisories can still matter if they relate to roadworthiness, safety or a known defect that later contributes to an accident. The key difference is between a car that has advisories but remains roadworthy, and a car that has become unsafe or illegal because the advisory was ignored.

Quick answer

Do MOT advisories invalidate insurance?

No, not automatically. An MOT advisory means the vehicle passed its MOT, but the tester recorded an item that was worn, deteriorating, corroded, leaking or close to becoming a defect. That is different from a vehicle failing its MOT or being driven in a dangerous condition.

However, insurance is not only about having an MOT certificate. Most policies expect the vehicle to be kept in a roadworthy condition. If a known advisory becomes a serious defect and contributes to an accident, it may create problems during a claim.

The practical answer is simple: advisories do not normally cancel insurance by themselves, but safety-related advisories should be fixed before they become illegal, dangerous or linked to an accident.

Best answer

When can an advisory become an insurance problem?

An advisory can become an insurance problem if it shows you knew about a safety issue and then ignored it until the vehicle became unsafe. For example, tyres close to the legal limit, corroded brake pipes, worn brakes, structural corrosion, suspension play or leaking shock absorbers can all affect safety if they worsen.

If a crash happens and the defect is relevant to the accident, the insurer may look at whether the vehicle was roadworthy and whether the defect contributed to the loss.

This does not mean every advisory ruins your cover. It means you should treat advisories as written evidence of a known issue and deal with the serious ones properly.

Insurance validity

Is Your Insurance Valid If Your Car Has MOT Advisories?

Usually yes, provided the car is still roadworthy, has a valid MOT where required, and you have answered your insurer’s questions honestly.

Usually OK

Advisory but roadworthy

If your car passed the MOT and the advisory is minor or monitored properly, the existence of the advisory alone should not automatically cancel your insurance.

Risk increases

Advisory ignored until unsafe

If a safety advisory worsens and the car becomes unroadworthy, the risk increases. This is especially important with tyres, brakes, steering, suspension and corrosion.

High risk

Dangerous or illegal defect

If the car is driven with an illegal tyre, dangerous brake fault, serious steering issue or unsafe structural defect, insurance and legal risk become much more serious.

The key insurance word is “roadworthy”

Insurance policies commonly require the vehicle to be maintained in a roadworthy condition. The Financial Ombudsman says that where a claim has been affected because a vehicle was not roadworthy, it will look for evidence that the vehicle condition caused or contributed to the loss. :contentReference[oaicite:1]{index=1}

That means an advisory alone is not the same as an automatic claim refusal. The important questions are whether the car was safe, whether the defect had worsened, whether you knew about it and whether it was connected to the accident or claim.

Declaring advisories

Do you need to tell your insurer about MOT advisories?

Most drivers are not asked to report every MOT advisory as soon as it appears. However, you must answer your insurer’s questions honestly. If your insurer asks whether the car has known defects, is modified, is roadworthy or has unrepaired safety issues, you should not hide important information.

Policy wording varies, so the safest advice is to read your policy documents and contact your insurer if you are unsure. This is especially sensible if the advisory involves a safety-critical system or if the vehicle is used for business, commuting, towing or high mileage.

If the advisory is minor and repaired promptly, it is much less likely to become an issue.

Keep proof

What evidence should you keep?

  • MOT certificate and advisory details.
  • Invoices for repairs completed after the MOT.
  • Garage inspection notes showing the vehicle is safe.
  • Tyre tread depth readings if tyres were advised.
  • Brake inspection notes if brakes were advised.
  • Photos before and after corrosion or repair work.
  • Service history and maintenance records.
  • Messages from your insurer if you ask for clarification.
Claim risk

Could an MOT Advisory Affect an Insurance Claim?

It can, but usually only where the advisory is relevant to roadworthiness or the claim. A minor advisory that has nothing to do with the accident is very different from a known dangerous defect that contributes to a crash.

Low claim risk

Advisory unrelated to the accident

Example: a number plate advisory is unlikely to matter in a rear-end collision unless it is directly relevant to the claim circumstances.

Medium claim risk

Known defect not repaired

Example: low brake pads or a shock absorber leak was advised, then later the vehicle is involved in an accident where braking or control is questioned.

High claim risk

Vehicle not roadworthy

Example: tyres are illegal, brakes are unsafe or structural corrosion has become severe. This can raise roadworthiness and claim concerns.

How an insurer may look at it

  • Was the car roadworthy at the time of the incident?
  • Was the MOT valid?
  • Was the advisory still present or had it been repaired?
  • Had the advisory worsened into a dangerous or illegal defect?
  • Did the defect contribute to the accident or damage?
  • Did the driver know about the defect?
  • Was the vehicle being maintained properly?
  • Is there evidence from a garage, engineer or accident report?

Roadworthiness

Why a valid MOT is not the whole answer

A valid MOT shows that the vehicle met the MOT standard at the time of the test. It does not guarantee the car will remain safe for the whole year. Tyres can wear below the legal limit, brakes can deteriorate, suspension can develop play and corrosion can spread after the MOT.

That is why advisories matter. They are recorded warnings. If the advisory is safety-related, it should be treated as a maintenance priority, not as something to ignore until next year.

Failed MOT

What if the car has failed MOT?

A failed MOT is much more serious than an advisory. If the vehicle has failed due to major or dangerous defects, driving it can create legal, safety and insurance problems. A dangerous defect should not be driven until repaired.

If your vehicle has failed its MOT, follow the rules carefully and do not confuse a failed MOT with a passed MOT that has advisories.

Repair priority

What Should You Fix First To Protect Yourself?

If you are worried about insurance or claim risk, prioritise advisories that affect roadworthiness and accident risk.

First

Tyres, brakes and steering

Fix tyres close to the legal limit, brake pipe corrosion, low brake pads, worn discs, brake fluid issues and steering play before cosmetic items.

Second

Suspension and shock absorbers

Repair worn bushes, arms, ball joints, leaking shocks and components causing poor handling or uneven tyre wear.

Third

Corrosion, lights and visibility

Inspect structural rust, fix poor lights, replace wipers and deal with visibility issues before they become MOT failures or safety risks.

Best practical protection

  • Keep the MOT certificate and advisory list.
  • Repair high-risk advisories early.
  • Keep invoices for any work done.
  • Ask garages to write what was inspected and what was safe.
  • Check tyres, brakes, lights, oil and coolant regularly.
  • Do not drive with known dangerous defects.
  • Read your policy wording if unsure.
  • Contact your insurer if the issue is serious or unusual.
Used car buying

Should You Buy a Car With MOT Advisories If You Need Insurance?

Yes, you can insure a car with MOT advisories, but you should understand the advisories before buying. Insurance is not the only concern. The real issue is repair cost and roadworthiness.

Acceptable

Minor advisories

Simple items such as wipers, number plates or minor maintenance advisories may be acceptable if the price is fair.

Negotiate

Repairable wear items

Tyres, brake pads, discs, suspension parts and shock absorbers should be priced before you buy.

Be cautious

Safety or corrosion advisories

Brake pipes, structural corrosion, steering, suspension and repeated advisories need careful inspection before buying.

Used car buyer checklist

  • Check MOT history before buying.
  • Look for repeated advisories.
  • Ask whether advisories were repaired after the MOT.
  • Get invoices, not just verbal reassurance.
  • Price safety-related repairs before agreeing the value.
  • Inspect tyres, brakes, suspension and rust carefully.
  • Do not rely only on “it has a full MOT”.
  • Arrange insurance with accurate vehicle information.

Decision guide

Declare, Repair or Monitor?

Use this simple decision guide if you are worried about MOT advisories and insurance.

Declare or ask

Contact your insurer if...

  • The policy asks about known defects.
  • The car may not be roadworthy.
  • The advisory is serious or safety-related.
  • You use the vehicle for business or towing.
  • You are unsure what your policy requires.
  • The vehicle has unusual or repeated defects.
Repair

Repair quickly if...

  • Tyres are close to illegal.
  • Brakes, steering or suspension are affected.
  • Brake pipes are corroded.
  • Structural rust is present.
  • Lights or wipers affect safety.
  • The defect could contribute to an accident.
Monitor

Monitor only if...

  • A garage confirms the item is minor.
  • The vehicle is roadworthy.
  • The issue is not safety-critical.
  • You keep checking it regularly.
  • You have proof of inspection.
  • You plan repair before it worsens.

Frequently asked questions

FAQs About MOT Advisories and Insurance

Straight answers to common UK driver questions about advisories, insurance, claims, roadworthiness and repairs.

Do MOT advisories affect insurance?

Not automatically. A car can usually be insured with advisories if it remains roadworthy, but safety-related advisories should not be ignored.

Do I need to tell my insurer about advisories?

Usually not every advisory automatically, but you must answer insurer questions honestly and check your policy if it refers to known defects or roadworthiness.

Can an insurer refuse a claim because of an advisory?

They may question a claim if the vehicle was unroadworthy or the known defect contributed to the accident. An unrelated minor advisory is different.

Is my insurance valid with a valid MOT and advisories?

Generally yes, if the vehicle is roadworthy and your policy conditions are met. A valid MOT is not permission to ignore dangerous defects.

Which advisories should I fix first?

Prioritise tyres, brakes, steering, suspension, brake pipes, structural corrosion, shock absorbers, lights and visibility issues.

Can tyre advisories affect insurance claims?

They can matter if the tyre later becomes illegal or unsafe and contributes to a loss of control, braking issue or accident.

Can brake advisories affect insurance claims?

They may matter if braking performance is relevant to the accident and a known brake defect was ignored.

Should I keep repair invoices?

Yes. Invoices and inspection notes help show that you acted responsibly after advisories were recorded.
