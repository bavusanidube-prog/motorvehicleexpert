Do MOT Advisories Affect Insurance?

An MOT advisory does not automatically invalidate your car insurance. The important questions are whether the vehicle remains roadworthy, whether you have answered your insurer's questions accurately and whether a known defect later becomes relevant to an accident or claim. This guide explains where MOT advisories, insurance and vehicle roadworthiness actually meet.

Quick Answer

Do MOT Advisories Affect Car Insurance?

Not automatically. An MOT advisory means the vehicle passed the MOT but the tester recorded an item that should be monitored or repaired. The existence of that advisory does not, by itself, mean the insurance policy has become invalid.

The more important insurance issue is the actual condition of the vehicle. A car can have advisories and remain roadworthy, while a fault that was only advisory-level at the MOT can later deteriorate until the car becomes unsafe or illegal.

Insurance questions also need to be separated from MOT terminology. Consumer insurance law requires you to take reasonable care not to make a misrepresentation when answering an insurer's questions. Your own policy may also contain conditions about roadworthiness, maintenance, modifications or information that must be reported.

MOT pass does not equal a twelve-month roadworthiness guarantee

The MOT records the condition against the test requirements when the vehicle is inspected. Tyres, brakes, suspension, steering and other components can deteriorate afterwards, so the driver still has to keep the vehicle safe to use.

Do not ignore a safety advisory because the car still has an MOT

If an advised fault develops into poor braking, illegal tyres, steering problems, serious structural deterioration or another unsafe condition, deal with the current defect rather than relying on the earlier MOT pass.

Best practical protection: inspect, repair and keep evidence

Where an advisory concerns a safety-related system, have it assessed, repair it when necessary and retain the invoice or inspection record. That improves vehicle safety and creates a clear maintenance history.

MOT Terminology

What Does an MOT Advisory Actually Mean?

An MOT advisory is a recorded observation about an item that has not reached the applicable MOT rejection threshold but may require monitoring or repair. It is not the same thing as a Major or Dangerous MOT defect.

This distinction is fundamental when discussing insurance. An advisory does not automatically mean the vehicle is defective to the point of being unsafe, nor does it automatically mean the insurance policy has been breached.

However, an advisory is still useful evidence that deterioration or another condition was identified on a particular date. If that condition later becomes substantially worse, the relevant question is the vehicle's current condition, not simply the fact that the earlier MOT resulted in a pass.

Insurance Validity

Is Your Insurance Still Valid With MOT Advisories?

In general, the presence of an MOT advisory does not automatically cancel a motor insurance policy. A vehicle can pass an MOT with advisories and remain roadworthy.

Insurance validity is broader than the MOT result. Your cover depends on the insurance contract, the information supplied to the insurer, compliance with relevant policy conditions and the circumstances of any claim.

That is why it is misleading to say either “advisories never matter to insurance” or “an advisory invalidates your policy”. Neither blanket statement accurately reflects how motor insurance works.

Consumer Disclosure

Do You Need to Tell Your Insurer About MOT Advisories?

There is no useful blanket rule saying that every UK motorist must automatically telephone their insurer whenever an MOT advisory is recorded.

For consumer insurance, the key statutory duty when entering into or varying a policy is to take reasonable care not to make a misrepresentation to the insurer. In practical terms, that means answering the insurer's questions carefully and accurately.

Your policy may also contain continuing notification requirements during the policy period. Those requirements vary between insurers and products, so the actual policy wording matters.

If an insurer asks about a relevant vehicle defect or another specific circumstance, deliberately giving an inaccurate answer is very different from simply having an MOT advisory on the vehicle's record.

The wording may deal separately with roadworthiness, maintenance, modifications, changes in vehicle use, ownership details and information supplied when the policy was taken out or renewed.

Do not interpret a roadworthiness clause as meaning that any cosmetic defect or every MOT advisory automatically removes insurance cover. The actual vehicle condition, wording of the policy and circumstances of any claim still matter.

Keep the documents that show what you were told

Do not rely on another driver's policy

An insurer's terms can differ by product and customer. Advice that was correct for somebody else's policy may not describe the conditions attached to yours.

Important Distinction

MOT Advisories vs Vehicle Modifications

An MOT advisory and a vehicle modification are fundamentally different things and should not be treated as interchangeable insurance terms.

An advisory records the condition of something inspected on the vehicle. A modification is an alteration to the vehicle's original or declared specification. The insurance questions and policy obligations surrounding those two situations can therefore be very different.

MOT Advisory

Condition or deterioration

Examples include tyre wear approaching a limit, corrosion, deteriorated brake components, suspension wear or another item that the tester considers worth recording.

The issue is primarily the condition of an existing vehicle component.

Modification

Change to specification

Examples can include different alloy wheels, altered suspension, engine remapping, body modifications or other changes from the vehicle's standard or previously declared specification.

Insurers may specifically ask about modifications because they can change risk, value, performance or repair costs.

A modified car can also have MOT advisories

These categories can exist at the same time. For example, a vehicle may have declared aftermarket suspension while also receiving an MOT advisory because one of its suspension components is worn.

Do not use an MOT pass as proof that a modification has been accepted by your insurer

MOT compliance and insurance disclosure are separate matters. A modification may pass the MOT yet still be something the insurer needs to know about under its questions or policy terms.

Separate the questions

Ask: “Is the vehicle roadworthy?”, “Has it passed the applicable MOT requirements?” and “Have I complied with my insurer's information and modification requirements?” These are connected questions, but they are not the same question.

Roadworthiness

Why Roadworthiness Matters More Than the Advisory Itself

The most important question after an MOT advisory is not simply whether the advisory exists. It is whether the vehicle remains safe and roadworthy in its current condition.

GOV.UK makes clear that the driver is responsible for ensuring the vehicle is safe to drive. A current MOT certificate does not remove that responsibility and does not guarantee that every component will remain roadworthy until the certificate expires.

This matters because an advisory may describe deterioration that was below the MOT rejection threshold on test day but which can continue developing afterwards. Tyres can wear further, brake components can deteriorate, suspension play can increase and corrosion can spread.

Treat advisories as maintenance intelligence

A useful advisory gives you advance warning of an area that may need attention. Acting on that information before the defect becomes serious is normally safer and often cheaper.

Important Distinction

Valid MOT vs Roadworthy Car

A valid MOT certificate and a roadworthy vehicle are related, but they are not the same thing.

The MOT is a statutory inspection against prescribed standards at the time of the test. Roadworthiness is an ongoing responsibility and depends on the vehicle's actual condition whenever it is driven.

MOT Certificate

Snapshot at inspection

An MOT pass shows that the vehicle met the applicable test requirements when it was examined.

It does not guarantee that tyres, brakes, suspension, lights or other components will remain in the same condition for the rest of the year.

Roadworthiness

Continuous responsibility

The vehicle must remain safe to use after the MOT. Wear, deterioration and damage occurring later can make a previously compliant vehicle unroadworthy.

This is why maintenance and pre-drive checks continue to matter after an MOT pass.

“It passed its MOT” is not a defence against obvious deterioration

If a tyre is now illegal, a brake fault has become dangerous or another defect has materially worsened, the earlier test result does not restore the vehicle to a safe condition.

Claim Investigation

Can an MOT Advisory Affect an Insurance Claim?

It potentially can, but the important distinction is between an advisory merely existing and a vehicle defect being relevant to the loss.

The existence of an advisory does not prove that an accident was caused by that defect. A claim investigation may instead consider the vehicle's condition at the time of the incident, whether the advised defect had deteriorated and whether there is evidence connecting that condition to what happened.

This is consistent with the Financial Ombudsman Service approach to roadworthiness disputes. Where a claim has been affected because an insurer says the vehicle was not roadworthy, the Ombudsman says it looks for evidence that the loss or damage was caused because of that condition.

Advisory history can be evidence, but it is not automatic proof of causation

An MOT record may help establish that deterioration had previously been observed. It does not, by itself, establish how severe the defect was later or whether that defect caused the accident.

Avoid absolute claims about rejected insurance

Whether a claim can be reduced, refused or otherwise affected depends on the policy, relevant law, the circumstances and the evidence. It should never be presented as an automatic consequence of an MOT advisory.

Cause of Loss

Did the Vehicle Defect Actually Cause or Contribute to the Loss?

This is one of the most important questions in an insurance dispute involving roadworthiness.

A vehicle can have pre-existing wear or an MOT advisory without that condition having caused the accident. Conversely, a deteriorated safety-critical component may become highly relevant where the mechanical evidence shows it contributed to loss of control, braking or another event.

Example 1

Unrelated deterioration

A minor exhaust corrosion advisory would not normally explain why a stationary vehicle was struck by another driver.

Little causal link
Example 2

Possible relevance

An unrepaired tyre-wear advisory could become relevant if the tyre later became unsafe and the vehicle lost grip in circumstances where tyre condition is disputed.

Requires evidence
Example 3

Strong mechanical connection

A serious braking defect may be much more relevant where an engineer finds that reduced braking performance materially contributed to the collision.

Financial Ombudsman approach

In roadworthiness disputes the Ombudsman says it looks for evidence that the loss or damage was caused because the vehicle was not in a roadworthy condition. This is why causation should not be replaced with a simple “advisory equals rejected claim” rule.

Repair Priority

Which MOT Advisories Should You Repair First?

Repair priority should be based primarily on safety, current condition and the likelihood of deterioration, not on whether you think an insurer might eventually ask about the advisory.

An advisory involving tyres, brakes, steering or structural strength deserves more urgent attention than minor cosmetic deterioration because failure of those systems can directly affect vehicle control and roadworthiness.

The most useful approach is to identify exactly what has been advised, inspect its current condition and then decide whether it can reasonably be monitored or should be repaired immediately.

MOT Failure

MOT Advisory vs Failed MOT: What Is the Difference?

An MOT advisory should not be confused with a failed MOT. They have different meanings and different consequences.

GOV.UK states that a vehicle fails its MOT when Major or Dangerous defects are recorded. Minor defects and advisories can appear on a vehicle that still receives an MOT pass.

Can you drive after an MOT failure?

GOV.UK says you can take a vehicle away after an MOT failure only if your existing MOT certificate is still valid and the failed test did not list any Dangerous defects.

Even where those conditions are met, the vehicle must still satisfy minimum roadworthiness standards. This means a current certificate does not provide permission to drive a vehicle that is actually unsafe.

Dangerous Defects

Why Dangerous MOT Defects Are Different From Advisories

A Dangerous defect is at the opposite end of the MOT severity scale from an ordinary advisory.

GOV.UK describes a Dangerous defect as a condition posing a direct and immediate risk to road safety or having a serious environmental impact. The official instruction is not to drive the vehicle until it has been repaired.

That makes it inappropriate to discuss a Dangerous defect as if the driver merely has to decide whether to notify an insurer about an MOT advisory. The immediate priority is making the vehicle safe.

Insurance relevance comes after immediate safety

A Dangerous MOT defect may also create insurance questions because the vehicle's roadworthiness is now clearly important. But the first decision should not be whether to telephone the insurer before fixing the car. The first decision is to stop treating the vehicle as safe for normal road use.

Do not knowingly continue using a vehicle with a Dangerous MOT defect

GOV.UK warns that driving a vehicle that has failed its MOT because of a Dangerous defect can lead to a fine of up to £2,500, three penalty points and a driving ban.

An old valid MOT does not override a Dangerous result

GOV.UK specifically says that a vehicle can only be taken away after a failed MOT where the existing MOT remains valid and no Dangerous defects were listed.

When an immediate advisory report may not be necessary

If the vehicle simply receives an ordinary advisory, remains roadworthy, no relevant policy question or notification condition applies and the item is being appropriately maintained, there is no sound basis for telling every driver that the advisory must automatically be reported.

Used Car Buying

Should You Buy a Used Car With MOT Advisories?

MOT advisories do not automatically make a used car a bad purchase. What matters is what has been advised, whether the defect has been repaired, whether it keeps returning and how much work the vehicle is likely to need after you buy it.

For insurance purposes, the important point is that you arrange cover using accurate information about the vehicle and then keep the car roadworthy. The presence of an old advisory in the MOT history is not the same thing as buying an unsafe vehicle today.

For buying risk, however, advisories are extremely useful. They can expose developing maintenance costs, repeated neglect and faults that may become future MOT failures.

Frequently Asked Questions

MOT Advisories and Insurance FAQs

Straight answers to the most common questions UK drivers ask about MOT advisories, car insurance, roadworthiness, claims and used car buying.

Do MOT advisories affect car insurance?

An MOT advisory does not automatically invalidate car insurance. The important issues are whether the vehicle remains roadworthy, whether any policy questions have been answered accurately and whether a known defect later becomes relevant to a claim.

Do I have to tell my insurer about every MOT advisory?

There is no universal rule requiring every driver to volunteer every MOT advisory. For consumer insurance you should take reasonable care to answer the insurer's questions accurately and should also follow any notification requirements contained in your policy.

Can I still be insured if my car has MOT advisories?

Yes. A vehicle can have MOT advisories and still remain insured and roadworthy. An advisory records an issue to monitor or repair and is different from an automatic declaration that the vehicle is unsafe.

Can an MOT advisory invalidate my insurance?

An advisory by itself does not automatically invalidate a motor insurance policy. Problems are more likely to arise where policy conditions have been breached, inaccurate information has been supplied or the vehicle has become unroadworthy.

Can an MOT advisory affect an insurance claim?

Potentially. If a defect connected with an advisory has worsened and is relevant to the accident or damage, the vehicle's roadworthiness and the relationship between that defect and the loss may be investigated.

Can an insurer reject a claim because my car was unroadworthy?

Roadworthiness can be relevant to a motor insurance claim. Whether an insurer can reduce or refuse a claim depends on the policy, the circumstances and applicable insurance law, including whether the vehicle condition was relevant to the loss.

Does a valid MOT prove my car is roadworthy?

No. A vehicle can become unsafe after passing its MOT. Drivers remain responsible for ensuring the vehicle is roadworthy whenever it is used, even while a current MOT certificate remains valid.

What is the difference between an MOT advisory and an MOT failure?

An advisory identifies an issue that should be monitored or repaired but did not prevent the vehicle receiving an MOT pass. Major and dangerous defects result in an MOT failure and have different driving and safety implications.

Which MOT advisories are most important for insurance and roadworthiness?

Advisories involving tyres, brakes, steering, suspension, structural corrosion, brake pipes, visibility or other safety-critical systems deserve particular attention because deterioration can affect vehicle control or roadworthiness.

Can a tyre advisory affect an insurance claim?

It may become relevant if the tyre later becomes unsafe or illegal and tyre condition is connected with the circumstances of the accident. An advisory alone does not automatically determine the outcome of a claim.

Can a brake advisory affect an insurance claim?

It can become relevant where the brake defect has deteriorated and braking performance is connected with the loss. The existence of an advisory alone does not prove that the defect caused an accident.

Are MOT advisories the same as vehicle modifications for insurance?

No. An MOT advisory records the condition of an inspected item, while a modification is a change from the vehicle's standard specification. Insurers may ask separate questions about modifications and they should be answered accurately.

Should I repair MOT advisories before driving?

Repair urgency depends on the defect. Minor deterioration may be monitored, but advisories involving safety-critical systems should be inspected and repaired before they develop into unsafe, illegal or MOT-failing conditions.

Should I keep invoices after repairing an MOT advisory?

Yes. Repair invoices, inspection reports and maintenance records can provide useful evidence that an advisory was investigated or repaired and can also help with future servicing, selling or insurance questions.

Should I contact my insurer if I am unsure about an advisory?

Yes. If the policy wording is unclear or the insurer has asked about known defects, vehicle condition or another relevant issue, contacting the insurer directly is the safest way to establish what information it requires.

Can I drive a car with MOT advisories?

An MOT advisory does not automatically prohibit driving, but the vehicle must remain roadworthy. If the condition has deteriorated since the MOT, its current safety and legality matter more than the wording on an earlier certificate.

Should I buy a used car with MOT advisories?

It can still be a reasonable purchase if the advisories are properly assessed and any expected repairs are reflected in the price. Repeated safety-related advisories or evidence of poor maintenance justify greater caution.

Can insurers see a vehicle's MOT history?

MOT history for vehicles tested in Great Britain can be checked using the official GOV.UK service with the vehicle registration number. Drivers should therefore treat the MOT record as part of the vehicle's documented history rather than assuming advisories are private.
